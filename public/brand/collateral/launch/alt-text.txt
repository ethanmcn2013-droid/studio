L — Asked and answered
  [L01] Signal Studio, asked and answered — asked “We already have a group chat” A chat is a record of talking. A plan is a record of deciding. You need both, and only one of them can tell you what is due today.
  [L02] Signal Studio, asked and answered — asked “Could we not just use a spreadsheet?” A spreadsheet cannot tell you it is worried. It will hold the rows. It will not notice that nothing has moved on one of them for fifteen days.
  [L03] Signal Studio, asked and answered — asked “It's all in a document” A document is a photograph of a plan. True the day it was written. The version everyone has open is never the version that is true.
  [L04] Signal Studio, asked and answered — asked “The calendar has the dates in it” A date is not a job. The calendar knows the tasting is on the first. It does not know the dietary list has to go in before it.
  [L05] Signal Studio, asked and answered — asked “We tried one of the big tools” Most of them are built for software teams. You are not running a software team. You are running a Saturday, a season, or a year, with a handful of people.
  [L06] Signal Studio, asked and answered — asked “I keep it all in my head” You do, and that is the problem. It works until you are ill, on holiday, or handing it over. Then it turns out the plan was never written down.
  [L07] Signal Studio, asked and answered — asked “We have a meeting for that” Meetings happen because visibility is poor. When everybody can see the same plan, the meeting gets shorter or stops happening.
  [L08] Signal Studio, asked and answered — asked “We're too small for software” Small is exactly who this is for. Two people and a date. Not a department, not a process, not a rollout — just the thing you are actually doing.
