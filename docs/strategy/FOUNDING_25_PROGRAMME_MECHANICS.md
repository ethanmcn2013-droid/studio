# The Founding 25 · Programme mechanics

Status: draft for founder approval · 2026-08-03
Owner: founder
Tasks: E02.10 and E02.11 (VEF-2026)
Source of truth: `content/hq/decisions/venue-edition-founding-25-2026-08-03.md`

The operating rules for the programme: what things are called, when a number is
assigned, when a place is held, when it expires, when it locks, and when the
programme closes. Internal document. The venue-facing version of these rules is
the Benefits Charter and the proposal.

---

## 1 · Terminology

### The words we use

| Thing | Say | Never say |
|---|---|---|
| The programme | the Founding 25 | the Founding Fifteen, the founding cohort of 15 |
| A venue in it | a founding venue | a partner, a member, an investor, a franchisee |
| Its position | place 07/25 | slot, seat, licence number, membership number |
| The rate | €1,000 a year, held for as long as you stay | locked for life, lifetime rate, forever price |
| The product | Venue Edition | the venue platform, the venue portal product |
| The couple's access | sponsored by your venue | free, complimentary, a giveaway |
| Closing | the programme closes at 25 | while stocks last, limited time only |

### Why each banned word is banned

**Partner.** In Ireland, partnership is a legal relationship with shared
liability. A venue is a customer on an annual agreement. Calling it a partnership
in writing invites an argument nobody wants to have.

**Member, membership.** Implies a scheme with governing rules and rights of
continuation. There is no scheme. There is an annual agreement.

**Investor, stake, share.** A founding venue buys a product. It gets no interest
in Signal Studio of any kind, and any language suggesting otherwise is a problem
of a different size.

**For life, lifetime, forever, in perpetuity.** Forbidden everywhere by D-001
point 16. This exact trap already caught this price lock once: "for life" reached
shipped copy while the canonical decision never said it. The commercial substance
is a rate held on continuous renewal with no expiry clause. Say that.

**Guaranteed.** Guarantees something that depends on the service continuing to
exist. The honest word is "held".

**Exclusive.** Signal Studio grants **no territorial exclusivity**. Twenty-five
venues in one region means neighbouring venues will both be founding venues, and
some of them compete with each other. Any word implying a protected patch is a
promise that will be broken by the second signature. If a venue asks, the answer
is direct: no exclusivity, and here is who else can be in it.

**Certified, accredited, approved.** Implies an external standards body. There
isn't one.

**While stocks last, limited time, last chance.** Manufactured urgency. The
scarcity here is real, so it needs no help. Say the number of places left.

### Scarcity claims must stay true

Every published or emailed statement about places must be true when it is sent.
"25 places" means 25. "Four places left" means four. Nothing counts down for
effect. If a claim about remaining places cannot be verified against the paid
count at the moment of sending, it does not go out.

## 2 · The number

**Format:** `NN/25`, zero-padded, from `01/25` to `25/25`. Written that way
everywhere: the certificate, the founding page, the agreement schedule, the
portal, the film.

**Assigned when the first payment clears.** Not at signature, not on invoice, not
on a verbal yes. Payment is the only event that cannot be walked back, and that
is what makes 01/25 worth having.

**Ordered by the moment the payment clears**, first cleared takes the lowest
free number. If two clear in the same minute, the earlier signature date breaks
the tie. If those match too, Ethan assigns and records why.

**A number is never reused.** If a venue lapses in year three, 07/25 stays
theirs historically. The programme does not re-issue 07/25 to somebody else, and
the founding page shows the place as closed rather than open.

**A number is not a licence.** It is recognition. The agreement is what grants
the product.

### If a payment reverses after a number is assigned

The number is withdrawn and the place returns to the pool. This is the one case
where an assigned number goes back. Recorded with the date and reason, and the
venue is told in the same message that tells them the payment failed.

## 3 · The life of a place

```
Researched → Invited → Proposed → Held → Signed → Paid → Onboarded
                            │        │        │
                            │        └────────┴──→ Lapsed (place returns to the pool)
                            └──→ Expired (hold ends, place returns to the pool)
```

| State | What has happened | What the venue has | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Researched | In the universe, not contacted | nothing | no limit |
| Invited | The film and the page have been sent | nothing | no limit |
| Proposed | A proposal with a price has been sent | **a held place** | 14 days |
| Held | Inside the 14 days | a held place | the remainder |
| Signed | Agreement signed, payment not cleared | a held place, extended to payment | see below |
| Paid | Payment cleared | **the place, and the number** | while the agreement runs |
| Onboarded | Account configured, can invite a couple | the place and a working product | |
| Expired | The 14 days ran out | nothing | |
| Lapsed | Agreement ended or a renewal was missed | nothing, number retained historically | |

### Reserved

A place is reserved the moment a proposal is sent. Not at the first reply, not
at expression of interest. The proposal states the place number that would be
assigned and the date the hold ends.

### Expires

**14 days from the date of the proposal.** One hold. Ethan can extend it
personally, and an extension is recorded against the account with its new end
date so the register never disagrees with what a venue was told.

At expiry the place returns to the pool and the venue is told, in one short
message, that the hold has ended and whether places remain. A venue whose hold
expires is not out of the programme. If places remain, they can still take one.

### Locks

A place locks on payment. From that moment the number is theirs, the rate is
theirs on continuous renewal, and the place is no longer available to anyone.

**Between signature and payment**, the hold continues rather than expiring. A
signed venue that has not paid keeps its place for 14 days from the invoice date.
If that passes, Ethan decides case by case and records the decision. Nobody loses
a place to an accounts department being slow, and nobody blocks a place
indefinitely with a signature and no money.

### Closes

**The programme closes automatically when the 25th payment clears.** Not when the
25th venue signs, and not when 25 proposals are out.

At that moment, in this order:

1. Every outstanding hold is void. Every one of those venues is told the same
   day, plainly, and offered the standard €1,500 rate. Nobody finds out by
   noticing the page changed.
2. No further founding proposals go out. Cohorts still in preparation stop.
3. Every commercial surface moves to the standard rate and stops advertising
   founding places. This is E15.17 and it is a release-blocking task, not
   housekeeping.
4. The founding page becomes a record of who the 25 are, with consent, rather
   than an offer.

A venue that lapses after the programme closes does not reopen a place. Twenty-five
is the count of places ever granted, not a running occupancy figure.

## 4 · Cohorts

Outreach runs **25 venues at a time**, released one cohort after another, until
25 venues have signed and paid. Cohort 1 goes first. Cohort 2 is released on
Cohort 1's response data, not on a calendar.

Twenty-five places and 25 emails per cohort means more than 25 venues will be
contacted in total. That is intended. The proposal states plainly that places are
assigned on payment and that other venues have proposals out at the same time.
Two venues must never both believe they hold place 03/25.

**The counter is the paid count.** Held places are not "taken" for the purpose of
a public number, because a hold can expire. Internally the register tracks both:
places paid, and places held. Externally only a true statement goes out.

## 5 · Edge cases, decided

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Two venues under one owner | One place per property. A group with three qualifying venues can take three places if three are free, at €1,000 each. |
| A venue sold mid-term | The rate follows the property. The new owner inherits the agreement and the number while it runs continuously. |
| A new owner brings other venues | Those do not get the founding rate. One place, one property. |
| A venue rebrands | Nothing changes. The place and the rate stay. |
| A venue asks to skip the queue | No. Numbers follow cleared payment, and that is the whole point of them. |
| A venue asks for exclusivity in its area | No. Stated directly, before signature, never discovered after. |
| A venue wants to pay for a lower number | No. Money already decides the order; charging for position turns recognition into a product. |
| A venue misses a renewal, then returns | The founding rate does not come back. The place is gone and the standard rate applies. Said at signature, not at the lapse. |
| A hold expires, then the venue says yes | Fine, if a place is free. They take the next free number, not the one they were quoted. |
| The 25th payment clears while holds are outstanding | Holds are void, told the same day, standard rate offered. See §3. |
| Fewer than 25 venues qualify in the region | Documented as a market shortfall (E10). The programme does not lower its eligibility to reach 25. |

## 6 · What has to be true in the system

These are the operating requirements this document creates. None of them are
built by this task.

- The register records, per account: state, proposal date, hold end date, any
  extension and its reason, signature date, payment cleared date, assigned
  number.
- The paid count and the held count are separately queryable, because only one of
  them can go in an email.
- Assigning a number is a recorded event with a timestamp, not a spreadsheet
  edit.
- Closing the programme is a single deliberate action that has downstream effects
  on the commercial surfaces (E15.17), not twenty manual edits.
