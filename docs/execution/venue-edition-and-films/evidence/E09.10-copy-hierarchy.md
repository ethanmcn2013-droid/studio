# E09.10 — Venue Edition copy hierarchy, terminology and tone rules

**Version `venue-copy.v1`** · Prepared 2026-08-03 · Evidence for E09.10
**Blocks E09.11** (offer, founding-rate, privacy, Keepsake, CTA, objection and FAQ copy)
**Governs** E09.11, E09.12, E11 (all outreach assets), E12.04, E12.07, E12.08,
E12.12, E12.13, E13.08, E13.09, E14.12, E15.02.

Authority above this document: `BRAND.md` §3 and §4 for the Signal Studio voice,
and `DECISIONS.md` for every commercial and legal fact. This document does not
invent a fact. Where it names a number, a term or a promise, the decision that
ratified it is cited. Where a founder answer is still missing, it is listed in
§9 rather than guessed.

**Standing constraint that outranks everything below (D-016).** There is no
solicitor and no accountant on this programme. Nothing written under these rules
may state or imply legal approval, solicitor review, accountant verification, or
unqualified GDPR compliance. That decision was made after the risk was put to the
founder in full. It is not re-argued here or anywhere else.

## 0. What this settles, and what it does not

**Settles.** What every noun in this programme is called, what it is never
called, what each of the four audiences must understand and in what order, the
register each surface writes in, the prohibitions that hold across all of them,
and the four answers that must never be improvised on a live call.

**Does not settle.** The copy itself. E09.11 writes it. This document is the
constraint E09.11 is written inside and the checklist it is reviewed against.

**How to use it.** Writing: §1 for the words, §3 for the order, §4 for the
register. Reviewing: §5 and §6 are the pass/fail list, §10 is the check. If a
line of copy survives §5 and §6, it goes to founder review. If it does not, it
does not go anywhere.

## 1. Terminology

Column three is not advisory. A word in "never" is a copy defect wherever it
reaches a venue, a coordinator, a couple or a public viewer, in any medium
including film subtitles, alt text, error strings, invoice line items, email
subject lines and OG cards.

### 1.1 The people

| Thing | We call it | We never call it | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| The buying business | **the venue**, or its name (Glenmara House) | client · customer · account (as the business) · partner · vendor · supplier · logo · B2B customer · merchant | "Venue" is what they call themselves. "Client" and "partner" both misdescribe an annual prepaid licence. |
| The person who signs | **the venue owner**, or their name | decision-maker · buyer · principal · stakeholder · persona · prospect (front-facing) | They are an owner. Sales vocabulary in front of the person being sold to reads as sales vocabulary. |
| The person who runs it day to day | **the coordinator**, or **the wedding coordinator** | admin · operator · end user · seat · user (front-facing) · manager | Their job title. "Admin" describes a permission, not a person. |
| The people getting married | **the couple**, or their names (Mara and Finn) | end users · consumers · recipients · beneficiaries · leads · seats · members · subscribers | The couple owns the workspace (D-001.3). Every other word describes something done to them. |
| The couple, spoken to a venue | **your couples** | our couples · your users · your clients (in Signal's own voice) | The venue may say "our couples". Signal says "your couples" in conversation, **and never in a metric label** (see §5, P9). |
| The people on the public Timeline | **guests**, **family**, **suppliers** | audience · viewers (front-facing) · traffic · visitors | They never agreed to anything and are not an audience metric in copy. |

### 1.2 The thing the venue buys and gives

| Thing | We call it | We never call it | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| The couple's private space | **their wedding workspace** · **the workspace** · **their planning workspace** | portal · dashboard · platform · tool · instance · tenant · environment · app (front-facing) · account | "Account" is the venue's word (§1.3). The workspace is the couple's. Keeping them apart in language is how the privacy boundary stays legible. |
| The same thing, internally | **sponsored workspace** | — | Internal, product and contract only. Never couple-facing. |
| The act of giving it | venue-facing: **the venue gifts it**, **stands behind it** · couple-facing: **a gift from Glenmara House** | sponsored (couple-facing) · complimentary · free of charge (couple-facing) · comped · included in your package (unless the venue says so) | The couple never sees a price (D-001.2, D-001.3). "Free" and "complimentary" both point at a price that was paid by someone. |
| The commercial arrangement | **the annual Venue Edition agreement** | subscription (front-facing) · SaaS licence · seat licence · plan · tier | It is one annual prepaid agreement, not a plan with tiers. |
| The product name | **Venue Edition** | the Venue Edition product · the venue plan · the venue tier | Permitted in the agreement, the order form, programme documents and venue-facing sales material. **Not a marketing headline** — it reads as a SKU. Headlines describe what it does. |
| The four products | **Signal Tasks · Signal Timeline · Signal · Signal Notes**, per `BRAND.md` §4 | the Tasks app · our task manager · the suite of products | `BRAND.md` §4 is the authority. Conversational form inside a product drops "Signal". |

### 1.3 The venue's own surface

**D-015 Q4: the Signal Studio Account *is* the Venue Portal. One surface, two
names.** No task is renamed and no session may treat these as two products.

| Context | We call it | We never call it |
|---|---|---|
| Venue-facing copy, UI, email, film, FAQ | **your account** · **your Signal Studio account** | the portal · the dashboard · the venue dashboard · the back office · the console · the admin panel |
| Programme documents, backlog, task titles, engineering | **the Venue Portal** | — |

**The rule that matters:** the two names never appear on the same surface, and
"Venue Portal" never reaches a venue. Two names in front of one buyer is exactly
how one surface becomes two products in their head, which is the outcome D-015 Q4
exists to prevent. Default recorded as **OQ-1** in §9 for founder confirmation.

### 1.5 The Keepsake

| Thing | We call it | We never call it |
|---|---|---|

| The download | **a one-click export you own** (D-010.3) | data dump · backup · GDPR export (front-facing) · portability request |

**The Keepsake sentence, ratified and reusable verbatim (D-010.3):**

> When the planning term ends, your workspace stays open in Keepsake mode, read
> to keep, and you can export everything in one click.

Venue-facing may add "free". Couple-facing may not, because it points at a price

promised in a document, a term or a contract.** It may be omitted in a single
in-product line where the terms are one tap away, and never omitted anywhere
else.

## 3. Copy hierarchy

Four audiences. Each has a ladder: the order a person must understand things in
before the next rung means anything. Copy that starts at rung 3 fails, not
because rung 3 is wrong but because it is unreadable without rungs 1 and 2.

Each ladder carries: the one sentence that must survive if everything else is
cut, the rungs in order, what the surface never leads with, and the test that
decides whether a draft passed.

### 3.1 The venue owner — pays, signs, and can say no

**The one sentence.** *Every couple who books their wedding with you gets a
private planning workspace, given by you, for as long as your licence is
current.* (D-020, verbatim-derived.)

**The ladder.**

1. **What their couples get.** Concrete, in a sentence, no product names. A
   private place to plan the wedding, plain English, nothing to learn.
2. **Whose it is, and what that costs them in exposure.** The couple owns it.
   The venue is named on it, quietly. This rung is where trust is won or lost
   and it comes before price.
3. **What it costs and what the price does.** €1,000 a year, prepaid, VAT
   included. €500 a year less than standard, for as long as they stay. The lock
   covers the base annual agreement while it renews continuously without lapse
   (D-009.3), it follows the property and not the company (D-009.4).
4. **What they see, and in the same breath what they never see.** Invitation
   administration, activation evidence, privacy-safe aggregate adoption
   evidence. Never a couple's planning content. Never a per-couple row. Never a
   wedding date change (D-011.1). The negative half is not a caveat and never
   sits in a footnote; it is half the product.
5. **What it asks of their team.** One action to invite a couple. Nothing to
   run, nothing to learn, no training day.
6. **What happens at the end.** The couple keeps everything: Keepsake, read-only,
   one-click export. Anything already redeemed keeps its full term whatever
   happens to the licence (D-020.2, stated unprompted).
7. **What being one of 25 means.** The number on payment. One 30-minute call a
   year and a named email route (D-009.5). Requests are logged and shape the
   roadmap; nothing is built for one venue (D-014.2).

**Never leads with.** Product names. Features. Screenshots. The metric
dictionary. GDPR mechanics. The roadmap. Signal Studio's story. The films.

**The test.** A venue owner who reads only rungs 1 to 4 can explain the offer
correctly to a business partner without opening anything.

### 3.2 The coordinator — does not pay, and can kill it

The coordinator is the single most under-served audience in this programme.
They did not choose this, it landed on their desk, and their reasonable first
assumption is that it is a new system they now have to run and will be blamed
for. Every coordinator-facing line answers that assumption before anything else.

**The one sentence.** *You invite a couple in one action, and then there is
nothing to run.*

**The ladder.**

1. **What lands on their desk.** One action. Invite the couple. That is the
   whole job.
2. **What a couple will ask them, and the answer.** How do I open it, what is
   it for, do I have to use it. Three answers, ready, in their words.
3. **What they can see.** Enough to answer, "did they open it yet".
4. **What they cannot see, so they never promise wrongly.** They cannot see
   the couple's planning content. If a couple asks them to look at something
   inside it, the honest answer is "I can't see that, and neither can anyone
   here". A coordinator who over-promises on our behalf is our failure.
5. **What breaks and who to ask.** A named route. Not a support portal.

**Never leads with.** Strategy. Price. Adoption percentages. The Founding 25.
Anything that implies their performance is being measured. Aggregate adoption
evidence exists for the owner at renewal, not as a stick over a coordinator, and
no coordinator-facing surface frames it as one.

**The test.** A coordinator can do the whole job from memory after reading once,
and can correctly say what they cannot see.

### 3.3 The couple — never pays, never sees a price

**The one sentence.** *This is yours, it is private, and Glenmara House gave it
to you.*

**The ladder.**

1. **Whose it is.** Theirs. Private. Rung 1 and nothing displaces it.
2. **Who gave it, and that nothing is being asked of them.** The venue's name,
   said once, warmly, without a logo wall. No price, no commercial relationship,
   no sign-up funnel, nothing to accept beyond the terms they must actually
   accept.
3. **What to do first.** One action, today, that is useful on its own. Not a
   tour, not a setup wizard, not "complete your profile".
4. **What the venue can and cannot see.** In the couple's own words, before
   they are asked to type anything real. "Glenmara House can see that you opened
   this. They cannot see what is in it."
5. **What happens after the wedding.** Keepsake, read-only, one-click export,
   per §1.5.

**Never appears at all.** A price, in any form, including "free", "worth", "your
venue paid for", "value", "included in your package" unless the venue's own
brochure says so. The word "sponsored". Adoption metrics. An upgrade prompt. A
Signal Studio marketing message. A referral ask. A review request. Anything that
treats a couple as a growth surface.

**The test.** Read the whole couple-facing journey with the venue's name removed.
If it still reads like a gift rather than a trial, it passed. If it reads like
software they were signed up for, it failed.

### 3.4 The public Timeline viewer — a guest who agreed to nothing

The hardest audience, because they have no account, no relationship with us, and
gave no consent. They arrived because someone they love sent a link.

**The one sentence.** *This is Mara and Finn's day, and here is what you need to
know.*

**The ladder.**

1. **Whose day it is.** Names, date, nothing else above the fold.
2. **What is happening and when, and what is being asked of them.** Times,
   places, the one thing they need to do if there is one.
3. **That this is the couple's page.** Attribution is one line in the footer.
   No logo, no badge, no "powered by" in the viewport (D-011.2). The venue gets
   the same restraint. If it reads as marketing on someone's wedding page, the
   thing being sold is gone.
4. **How their own information is handled.** One quiet line, and a route to ask.
   A guest is entitled to know who holds what about them, and the page is the
   only place they will ever look.

**Never appears at all.** Sign-up prompts. Product marketing. A venue logo. A
Signal Studio call to action. Cookie theatre. Any dietary, allergy, accessibility
or health information about any named person, by hard rule and not by clause
(§5, P8). Any private planning content. The couple's contact details unless the
couple put them there deliberately.

**The test.** A guest can read the page, act on it, and leave without ever
learning what Signal Studio is or that a venue bought anything.

## 4. Tone rules, per surface

One voice, `BRAND.md` §3, tuned by distance from the wedding. The closer a
surface sits to the couple's actual day, the quieter it gets.

| Surface | Register | Person | Sentence length | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outreach email (E11) | One founder writing to one owner. Specific to that venue in the first line. | I / you | Short. Under 200 words total. | Marketing voice · a deck in an email · "quick question" · attachments |
| Proposal and landing page (E12.04) | Plain and complete. Every condition on the same page as the price. | we / you | Medium. Scannable. | Urgency · social proof theatre · a price without its conditions |
| *Limerick First* (film) | Place, warmth, restraint. Invitation, not pitch. | third person | n/a | A price on screen · a product screenshot as a hero · a hard CTA |
| *Before the Day* (film) | The couple's experience, shown. Ends on the walkthrough CTA. | third person | n/a | **A price, anywhere (D-014.1)** · invented UI · invented metrics |
| The agreement, schedule, DPA (E03) | Precise, readable, no more formal than it must be. | Signal Studio / the Venue | Long is allowed. Vague is not. | Any promise the code cannot keep · "forever" · implied legal review |
| The account (venue UI) | Calm instrument. Numbers name their source or show a dash. | you | Short. | Vanity metrics · a per-couple row · a seat count · exclamation marks |
| Invitation email to a couple | Warm, human, from the venue's world not ours. | the venue's voice, then ours | Very short. | Our branding above the venue's · a price · a funnel |
| Couple product UI | Quiet and practical. Their words, not ours. | you | Short. | Congratulation theatre · gamification · nudges to upgrade · streaks |
| System, error and empty states | `BRAND.md` §3 emotional-intelligence rule: not "Task overdue" but "This may need attention". | you | One line. | Blame · jargon · error codes without a human sentence |
| Public Timeline | Almost silent. The couple's page, not ours. | the couple's voice | Short. | Everything in §3.4 |
| Keepsake transition and end-of-term email | Honest, unsentimental, one action. | you | Short. | "Forever" · loss-aversion framing · an upsell |
| Invoice, receipt, order form | Boring on purpose. States VAT-inclusive. | neutral | n/a | A marketing line on a financial document |
| HQ, backlog, this document | Operator register. `BRAND.md` §6.6 vocabulary. | any | any | Leaking internal vocabulary onto a front-facing surface |

**Two cross-cutting rules.**

**The negative half travels with the positive half.** Every claim about what a
venue can see states, in the same block and at the same visual weight, what it
cannot. This is the product. Splitting them across a page and a FAQ is how a
trust claim becomes a trust problem.

**Silence is available.** `BRAND.md` §6.5: the dispatch only updates when there
is something to say out loud. The same applies here. A quiet surface is a
permitted outcome. Every additional sentence on a couple-facing surface has to
earn a place against the couple's attention on their own wedding.

## 5. Standing prohibitions

Numbered so a review can cite one. Each carries the rule, why, the approved
substitute, and its source. **A draft that breaks any of these does not reach
founder review.**

**P1 — Never "forever", "for life", "lifetime", "permanent", "always" or
"guaranteed", about anything.**
Why: D-001.16 forbids an unconditional technical promise that the service or a
hosted artifact will exist. The trap has already caught this programme once, in
`commercial-truth.md`, and again in D-021 where the founder's spoken words were
"for life" and the published wording deliberately was not.
Substitute, price lock: *"€500 a year less, for as long as you stay"* and *"for
as long as the agreement renews continuously without lapse"* (D-009.2, D-009.3).
Substitute, Keepsake: *"read-only, for as long as the service exists, with a
one-click export you own"* (D-010.3).
Source: D-001.16 · D-009.3 · D-010.3 · D-021.

**P2 — Never state or imply legal approval, solicitor review, or accountant
verification.**
Why: D-016. No such review exists or will be obtained. Banned constructions
include "legally reviewed", "solicitor-approved", "our legal team", "counsel has
confirmed", "reviewed by our advisors", and any use of a formal drafting register
whose only function is to imply a lawyer wrote it.
Substitute: §6, Answer 2, verbatim.
Source: D-016.3, a standing constraint checked at every founder review.

**P3 — Never claim GDPR compliance without qualification.**
Why: compliance is a continuing obligation and a contested judgement, not a
state a company can assert about itself. Banned: "GDPR compliant", "fully
compliant", "GDPR certified", "DPC approved", "audited", "100% secure",
"bank-grade", "military-grade", "your data is safe with us".
Substitute: name the artifact. *"There is a data processing agreement and a
security schedule, a privacy notice written for the couple, and a written record
of who is responsible for what."* Then §6, Answer 1.
Source: D-016.3.

**P4 — No price, in any form, on any couple-facing surface.**
Why: D-001.2 and D-001.3. The venue buys and gifts; the couple never pays and
never sees a price. Banned to a couple: the numbers, "free", "complimentary",
"worth", "value", "your venue paid for this", "normally €", "sponsored", and any
comparison implying a price existed.
Substitute: *"A gift from Glenmara House."*
Source: D-001.2, D-001.3 · extends D-014.1, which keeps price out of *Before the
Day* for the same reason.

**P5 — No numeric entitlement in any commercial document.**
Why: D-020. A venue with a current paid licence may create a workspace for any
couple with a signed booking. No seat count, no per-couple maths, no number in
the commercial terms. Fair use notifies Signal HQ and keeps issuing; it never
blocks, and a numeric pause must never appear in a document that also says
unlimited.
Substitute: *"Every couple who books their wedding with you gets a workspace,
for as long as your licence is current."*
Source: D-020.

**P6 — The words "unlimited" and "no seats" are blocked in shipped copy until
R-016's four code changes land.**
Why: R-016, verified. `entitlements-db/codes.ts` refuses a null allotment and
`OnboardVenueForm.tsx` defaults the field to **10**. The live `/venues` page
already promises "No seats. No per-couple maths." while the shipped system
computes a remaining count and raises a headroom warning in the venue's own
account. A venue sold "no seats" is currently shown a seat count.
Substitute until the code lands: the P5 sentence, which is true today and does
not use the word.
Source: R-016 · D-020 ("blocked on code").

**P7 — Never "18 months" alone.**
Why: D-010.2 and D-022. The term is 18 months from redemption **or 3 months past
the wedding date, whichever is later**, computed dynamically and recomputed on
postponement. Access can move later, never earlier. The bare figure describes a
product we deliberately do not ship, and the failure it hides, a couple losing
access before their own wedding at the venue that gifted it, is the single worst
outcome available to this product.
Substitute: *"18 months from when the couple opens it, or 3 months past the
wedding, whichever is later."*
Source: D-010.2 · D-022.

**P8 — No dietary, allergy, accessibility or health information about a named
person appears in any example, screenshot, film frame, demo fixture, template
string or public Timeline. No marketing copy states that such information flows
to the venue.**
Why: R-017, verified in the repository. `app/src/lib/templates.generated.ts:108`
ships a "Collect final dietary notes" task in the wedding template, and
`app/src/app/the-wedding/page.tsx:26` advertises *"One reply to Glenmara House,
including dietary notes."* Dietary and allergy data reveals health and routinely
reveals religious belief; accessibility requirements reveal disability. These are
Article 9 special categories, entered by the couple **about guests who have never
agreed to anything**, and a public marketing page says they flow onward to the
venue. Guest lists also routinely contain children's data.
This is a copy rule with a product fix behind it. The copy rule holds
immediately; the architectural mitigation, moving capture to a guest-facing step
where the guest supplies their own data, is owned by E03.04, E05.03 and E06.01.
Substitute on the demo page: *"One reply to Glenmara House, with the choices
confirmed."*
Source: R-017 · severity critical · open.

**P9 — No metric that resolves to one couple, and no metric labelled "your
couples".**
Why: `E09.01` §1 and `E09.02` §1. Aggregate is the product, not a presentation
choice. There is no biggest user, no per-couple row, no ranking, no leaderboard.
The denominator is always "invitations redeemed" and never "your couples",
because Signal never learns which couples hold real bookings and will not imply
that it does.
Substitute below threshold: *"Not enough activity to report yet."* Thresholds: 3
behavioural, 5 percentage (D-011.3).
Source: `E09.01` · `E09.02` · D-011.3.

**P10 — Wedding date changes are never surfaced anywhere.**
Why: D-011.1. A postponement is the couple's news to share, not a dashboard
event. Dates appear in the account only where the couple redeemed a code from
that venue.
Source: D-011.1.

**P11 — No venue logo, badge or "powered by" in the public Timeline viewport.**
Why: D-011.2. Attribution is one line in the footer. The venue gets equal
restraint. If it reads as marketing on someone's wedding page, the thing being
sold is gone.
Source: D-011.2 · D-001.15.

**P12 — Every price is stated inclusive of VAT at the prevailing rate.**
Why: D-021. €1,000 and €1,500 are both VAT-inclusive, in every agreement, order
form, invoice and page. Never "+ VAT", never "ex VAT", never a monthly
equivalent.
Source: D-021 · amends D-009 · supersedes CR-001 criterion 6 per D-023.

**P13 — No urgency, no scarcity theatre, no social proof theatre.**

## 6. The four questions, pre-approved

Every venue asks these. An improvised answer on a live call is how this
programme acquires a promise nobody decided to make. Each answer below states
positively what does exist, then plainly what does not. Each carries **conditions
of use**: the artifacts that must exist before the answer may be given as
written. Where a condition is unmet, the honest interim answer is supplied.

**The rule underneath all four.** If asked something not covered here, the answer
is *"I don't know, and I'd rather find out and tell you than guess."* That
sentence is always available and is never a weak answer in this market.

---

### Answer 1 — "Are you GDPR compliant?"

**Spoken, roughly 30 seconds.**

> Nobody can hand you a GDPR certificate, because there isn't one. What I can
> hand you is the paperwork. There is a written record of who is responsible for
> what, a data processing agreement and a security schedule, and a privacy
> notice written for the couple in plain English. The reporting you see is
> aggregate only, with small numbers held back so nothing points at one couple,
> and I never see a couple's planning content. You get all of it before you sign,
> not after. If you have someone who reads this sort of thing, I would rather
> they read it now.

**Written, for the FAQ or an email reply.**

> There is no such thing as GDPR certification, so no honest supplier can claim
> it. Here is what exists instead.
>
> **What exists.** A written record of who is responsible for what, and for which
> data. A data processing agreement and a security schedule. A privacy notice
> written for the couple. Reporting that is aggregate only: no per-couple row, no
> ranking, no planning content, with small numbers held back so a figure can
> never point at one couple. A one-click export the couple owns, and deletion
> controls. Data minimisation as a rule rather than a setting.
>
> **What does not exist.** No certification, because there is none to hold. No
> external audit. No approval from the Data Protection Commission, which does not
> approve suppliers. Compliance is a continuing obligation for both of us, not a
> badge either of us can hold up, and I will not describe it as one.
>
> **What you can do.** Read the agreement and the privacy notice before you sign,
> and have your own adviser read them. I would rather that happened now than
> after twenty-five venues have signed the same document.

**Never says.** "We are GDPR compliant" · "fully compliant" · "GDPR certified" ·
"audited" · "approved by the DPC" · "your data is 100% safe" · anything implying
a lawyer wrote or approved the answer (P2).

**Conditions of use.** May be given as written once E03.01 (role map), E03.04
(couple privacy notice) and E03.05 (DPA and security schedule) exist as drafts a
venue could actually be sent, **and** the R-017 special-category position is
decided rather than open. Until then the honest answer is: *"The paperwork is
being written now and you will get all of it before you sign. I would rather show
you a finished document than describe one."*

### Answer 3 — "Are you insured?"

**Status today: no policy is recorded anywhere in this programme.** The answer
below therefore has two versions, and **version B is the only one that may be
used until a certificate exists.** Using version A without a certificate in hand
would be exactly the kind of claim P2 and P3 exist to prevent.

**Version A, usable only when a policy is in force and a certificate exists.**

> Yes. Professional indemnity and cyber cover with [insurer], to a limit of
> [limit]. I can send you the certificate.

Nothing beyond the named cover, the named limit and the certificate. No "fully
insured", no "covered", no adjective.

**Version B, the honest answer today.**

> Not yet, and I would rather say so than be vague about it. Here is what stands
> in its place while cover is arranged. The agreement sets out plainly what
> Signal Studio is responsible for and what it is not. You are prepaying €1,000
> including VAT for one year, so your maximum commercial exposure is that figure
> and it is on the page in front of you. Every couple's workspace has a one-click
> export they own, so nothing anyone has written is trapped with me. I will tell

**Never says.** "Fully insured" · "we're covered" · "we have insurance" without a
named policy · a limit that is not on a certificate · any implication that cover
exists because it has been discussed.

**Conditions of use, and the flag that goes with them.** Version B is honest but
it is the weakest of the four answers, and it is weak on the question a venue

### Answer 4 — "Where is our data held?"

**Spoken.**

> Two different things, and they are worth keeping apart. Your account with me
> holds your venue details, your invitations and the aggregate figures you see.
> The couples' planning content sits in the Signal Studio production database,
> and I do not read it. Everything runs on EU-hosted infrastructure with a named
> set of suppliers, and the full list, with what each one does, is in the
> security schedule attached to the agreement. If the question behind the
> question is whether I can pull your couples' notes up on a screen, the answer
> is no, and that is a design decision rather than a policy one.

**Written.**

> **What is held, and where.** Your account record, your invitations and the
> aggregate adoption figures live in the Signal Studio production database. The
> couples' planning content lives in the same production system, in workspaces
> the couple owns, and Signal Studio staff do not read planning content in the
> ordinary course.
>
> **Who else is involved.** A named list of suppliers, each with a single job:
> hosting and compute, the database, sign-in, email delivery, error reporting
> with personal data stripped before it is sent, payment processing, and file
> storage. The complete list, what each one holds, where it holds it, and the
> legal basis for any transfer outside the EU, is in the security schedule
> attached to the agreement. It is a list, not a paragraph, and it is kept
> current.
>
> **What you can get out, and how quickly.** Every couple has a one-click export
> they own. Your own account data is exportable on request. Deletion controls are
> in the agreement with defined timeframes.
>
> **What does not exist.** No on-premise option. No separate database per venue.
> No choice of hosting region. And no guarantee that this service exists
> indefinitely, which is exactly why the export is one click rather than a
> support request.

**Verified as at 2026-08-03, from the `app` dependency manifest.** The processors
in the shipped system are: Vercel (hosting and compute), Turso / libSQL
(databases), Clerk (accounts and sign-in), Resend (email delivery), Sentry (error
reports, with personal data scrubbed before send), Stripe (payment), and Vercel
Blob (file storage). **The region and the transfer basis for each of these are
not yet verified in this programme.** E03.05 verifies them and supplies the
security schedule. Until it does, P15 applies: no region, no country and no
transfer claim is stated on a call, in an email, on a page or in a document.

**Never says.** "Your data never leaves Ireland" · "EU only" · "we don't use any
US providers" · "end-to-end encrypted" unless it is · a named data centre · a
region that has not been checked in the console this quarter.

**Conditions of use.** The written version may be sent once E03.05 exists. The
spoken version may be used now **with the sentence "everything runs on EU-hosted
infrastructure" removed** until E03.05 verifies it, leaving: *"the full list of
suppliers, with what each one does and where, is in the security schedule
attached to the agreement."*

## 10. The check

Before any Venue Edition copy reaches founder review, it passes all six.

1. **Audience.** Which of the four ladders is this, and does it start at rung 1.
2. **Terminology.** Every noun in §1's "we call it" column. Nothing from a
   "never" column, in any medium including alt text, subtitles and error strings.
3. **Prohibitions.** All fifteen, P1 to P15. P1, P2, P3, P4, P7 and P8 are the
   ones that recur; check them twice.
4. **Brand.** `BRAND.md` §3: no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no banned words,
   declarative, verbs over nouns, plain English.
5. **The negative half.** Every claim about what a venue sees carries what it
   does not see, in the same block, at the same weight.
6. **Source.** Every number, term, date and promise traces to a ratified decision
   in `DECISIONS.md`. If it does not trace, it does not ship, and it becomes a
   founder question instead of a sentence.

Failing any one of these is a defect, not a preference, and it is fixed before
founder review rather than raised at it.
