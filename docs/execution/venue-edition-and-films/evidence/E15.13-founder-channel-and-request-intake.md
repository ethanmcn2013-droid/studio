## 1. The two commitments this task delivers

**Founder access (D-009 point 5):** one 30-minute call per venue per year, plus a
named email route. **Not standing access.** The bound is the point: twenty-five
unbounded relationships become a full-time job, which is R-006 in its most
predictable form.

**Founding requests (D-014 point 2):** requests are logged and shape the roadmap.
Nothing is built for one venue. This is stated explicitly in the Benefits Charter
so the boundary is a documented benefit rather than a refusal produced later.

## 2. What only a human can do

Take the call. Answer the email. Say no to a request in a way that keeps the
relationship.

### Step 2 — The states, defined once

Every request moves through exactly these, and **silence is not a state**:

| State | Means | Leaves within |
|---|---|---|
| `received` | logged, venue acknowledged | 2 working days |
| `triaged` | categorised, and the venue told what happens next | 5 working days |
| `on_roadmap` | it shapes the roadmap; no date promised | reviewed monthly |
| `declined` | answered with the standing answer | closed |
| `delivered` | shipped for everyone, never for one venue | closed |

### Step 3 — The standing answer, written once and reused

> Requests from founding venues are logged and they shape what gets built. We do
> not build for one venue. If enough of you ask for the same thing, it moves up.

Say it at handoff, put it in the Benefits Charter, and reuse it verbatim. A
boundary explained once at the start reads as a policy. The same boundary
explained for the first time in response to a request reads as a refusal.

### Step 4 — The call, offered and recorded

- **Do:** offer the 30-minute call once a year, per venue. Record whether it was
  offered and whether it was used.
- **Why record it:** the commitment has to be evidenceable at renewal, and a
  venue who never used the call still received the offer.
- **Fail:** access that becomes continuous. The bound protects both sides.

### Step 5 — The monthly rollup

- **Do:** once a month, group open requests by what was asked for and count how
  many venues asked.
- **Expect:** a short list where the count is the signal.
- **Fail:** a rollup that becomes a backlog. It feeds the roadmap; it is not the
  roadmap.

## 4. The stop rules

- **Stop** if a request is about to produce venue-specific code, configuration or
  copy. That is the line D-014 point 2 draws and it does not have an exception.
- **Stop** if a request sits in `received` past its two days. The mechanism has
  failed at the first step and everything after it is theatre.

## 6. What I would tell the founder

This is the only task in E15 whose infrastructure is finished and whose procedure
was missing, which is the opposite of the usual problem. The table is well shaped
and nobody had written down what to do with it.

The one thing worth deciding now is the response time in step 2. A stated two
days that is met beats an unstated ambition. The boundary in D-014 does not erode
because somebody overrules it; it erodes because a request sits unanswered long
enough that saying yes becomes the fastest way to close it.
