## 1. The risk this task is really about

**The sweep.** I-002 records what happened the last time a commercial position
changed: a full-workspace inventory found **roughly 140 files** carrying the
retired position, not the two the issue originally recorded, and the previous
July change had reached sixteen gated files and stopped.

So the failure mode at closure is not forgetting to close. It is closing on the
pages you remember and leaving EUR 1,000 live on a deck, a demo script, an atlas
entry or an operator todo that a venue eventually finds.

I-002 also lists, in section C of its ledger, what was deliberately not fixed
last time and who owns it: three public decks, the lender-facing loan pack, the
film and demo scripts, the atlas entries, and the P0 operator todo
`planning-period-commercial-ratification`, which still states "the founding 15"
as evidenced fact while gating checkout. **Those are the files that will carry
EUR 1,000 past closure unless section C is cleared first.**

## 2. What only a human can do

Confirm the twenty-fifth cleared payment, and take the change-control decision if
the pool cannot supply 25.

## 3. The script

### Step 1 — Close on cleared payment, not on signature

- **Trigger:** the twenty-fifth **cleared payment** recorded through
  `recordAnnualPrepayment`, evidenced by
  `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM sponsors WHERE founding_number IS NOT NULL` returning 25.
- **Fail:** closing on the twenty-fifth signature. D-009 point 6 is explicit and
  the whole meaning of 01/25 rests on it.

### Step 2 — Inventory before editing

- **Do:** run the same full-workspace inventory method that found roughly 140
  files, not the sixteen-file method that missed them. Produce the list first,
  then edit.
- **Fail:** starting from memory.

### Step 3 — Clear I-002 section C first

- **Do:** the three public decks, the loan pack, the film and demo scripts, the
  atlas entries, and `planning-period-commercial-ratification`. These are known,
  named and already owed. Closing the offer on top of them buries them.

### Step 4 — Switch every forward-facing surface

- **Do:** every venue-facing price statement moves to **EUR 1,500 inclusive of
  VAT at the prevailing rate** (D-021). The machine contract gets a new version.
- **Fail:** any surface stating EUR 1,000 as available after closure.

### Step 5 — The enforcement scripts must pass against the new position

- **Run:**
  ```bash
  cd studio
  node scripts/check-venue-edition-contract.mjs
  node scripts/check-venue-copy.mjs --report
  pnpm test
  ```
- **Expect:** exit 0 on all three.
- **Result 2026-08-04, against the pre-closure position:** studio `pnpm test`
  exits 0 with 742 tests and 0 failures, and the copy check reports
  `[venue-copy] ok — 16 rules, 92 patterns, 29 surfaces swept (0 cross-repo, not
  checked here), 30 pinned hit(s), 21 citation(s), 6 recorded exception(s)`.
  **Note the parenthesis: cross-repo surfaces are not checked here.** The app's
  couple-facing copy is outside this sweep and has to be checked separately.
- **Fail:** a green check that was re-pointed at the new figures without anyone
  reading what it now asserts. The gate criterion for the commercial gate makes
  exactly this point about the enforcement script.

### Step 6 — Prove the twenty-five are untouched

- **Run:** for every founding venue, read the full
  `sponsor_price_agreements` history and the next renewal price.
- **Expect:** every past term reads EUR 1,000; the next renewal is priced at
  EUR 1,000.
- **Why it works:** every term is its own append-only row, and the module says
  so: *"Changing the constants tomorrow cannot reach a term that has already been
  paid for."* Prove it anyway. A guarantee that has never been checked is a
  comment.
- **Fail:** any founding venue reading EUR 1,500 anywhere. That is the promise
  breaking, and the whole programme rests on it.

### Step 7 — The lock wording does not change

- **Check:** no surface says forever, for life, or in perpetuity. D-021 is
  explicit that although the founder's words were "for life", the **published
  wording stays as ratified in D-009 point 3**, because an unconditional
  technical promise is forbidden and the same trap already caught this price lock
  once.
- **Also:** the rate follows the property, not the company. It survives rebrand
  and ownership change while the agreement runs continuously, and it does not
  transfer to additional properties a new owner brings (D-009 point 4).

### Step 8 — Prepare the first renewal cycle

- **Do:** run `venueRenewalWorklist` and confirm each venue approaching renewal
  has one action.
- **Result 2026-08-04:** the worklist and its states are covered by passing tests
  (*"lists paying venues with a derived state and one action each"*, *"reports a
  lapsed venue as lapsed and a venue inside grace as not lapsed"*). **It has
  never run against a real term.** No renewal falls due before 2027, so it will
  remain untested against reality for a year after this task completes. Say that
  out loud rather than letting the green tests imply otherwise.

### Step 9 — Record the closure as a dated decision

- **Do:** the offer closing is a commercial fact other documents depend on.
  Record it as a decision with a date, not as a state change.
