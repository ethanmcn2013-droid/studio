## 2. Drafting decisions, stated before the schedule

### 2.1 This is a schedule, not a second agreement

Three documents already carry these rules: the Benefits Charter, the Programme Mechanics
and the machine contract `commercial-terms.v2.json`. A fourth written in fresh words is
how a contract and a website end up saying different things, and this programme is already
unpicking that in several places.

So the schedule below is the operative text, and it says so. It cross-references the
agreement rather than repeating it, and it names the two strategy documents as
descriptions rather than incorporating them.

### 2.2 The charter is not incorporated by reference, and that is a deliberate departure

`FOUNDING_25_BENEFITS_CHARTER.md` still carries **"Status: draft for founder approval ·
2026-08-03"** in its own header, and E02.08, the task that produced it, is recorded done.
Incorporating it by reference would make an unapproved document contractual, and would
put the six benefits in two places at once.

**The schedule therefore states the six benefits operatively, with their boundaries in the
same paragraph**, and the charter is named at paragraph 12 as the plain-English version
that is not part of the agreement. That keeps every boundary intact, removes the drift
risk, and does not depend on an unapproved document. It is a departure from the obvious
reading of this task and is raised as founder question 1 rather than made quietly.

### 2.3 One decision point, because E02.04 is backlog

What a founding venue is offered if it comes back after a lapse has three answers on the
record and none of them is ratified:

| Source | Position |
|---|---|
| `FOUNDING_25_BENEFITS_CHARTER.md` lines 35 to 37 | Coming back means coming back at the standard rate. |
| `FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS.md` line 198 | "The founding rate does not come back. The place is gone and the standard rate applies." |
| `src/lib/venue-billing.ts:276` `foundingRateOnReturn()` | `settled: false`. "What is offered afterwards has never been decided." |

Both documents are deliverables of tasks recorded done and approved, and both describe
themselves as unapproved. The code refused to follow either. That is carried as decision
point A below, not resolved here.

**What is NOT a decision point.** That a lapse ends the founding rate is ratified
(D-009 point 3, E02.03) and is stated flatly at paragraph 3.4. The notice period and the
late-payment window that decide *when* a lapse can happen are marked blanks in the parent
agreement at clauses 5.5 to 5.7, and this schedule does not restate them.

# SCHEDULE 1 · Founding Venue schedule

**Draft. No solicitor has reviewed these documents, and no accountant has verified the
financial or VAT position.**

**Version:** 0.1 draft · **Prepared:** 2026-08-04 · **Governing decisions:** D-009,
D-010, D-014, D-016, D-020, D-021, D-027, D-033

This schedule forms part of the Venue Edition agreement between Signal Studio and the
venue named on the order form. Words used here have the meaning the agreement gives them.
Where this schedule and the agreement differ, this schedule wins, as the agreement says at
clause 18.2.

---

## 1 · What this schedule is

**1.1** The venue named on the order form holds a place in the **Founding 25**, the first
twenty-five venues to take Venue Edition.

**1.2** This schedule sets out the rate that goes with that place, the conditions on which
it is held, what the place is, and what a founding venue gets.

**1.3** Everything else about the service is in the agreement. This schedule does not
repeat it.

## 2 · Your rate

**2.1** Your rate is **€1,000 a year for your venue, paid in advance**, inclusive of VAT
at the prevailing rate. It replaces the €1,500 in clause 4.1 of the agreement.

**2.2 In one line: €500 a year less, for as long as you stay.**

**2.3** The price above is the amount your venue pays. VAT at the prevailing rate comes
out of it, not on top of it, exactly as clause 4.2 of the agreement says.

**2.4** Your rate is the same whether your venue holds twenty weddings a year or two
hundred and fifty. The wedding count we ask for after you sign, under clause 3.6 of the
agreement, never sets your price and never changes at renewal.

## 3 · How long your rate is held, and what ends it

**3.1 What the rate covers.** The base annual Venue Edition agreement. That is what is
held, and nothing beyond it.

**3.2 The condition.** Your rate is held for as long as that agreement renews
continuously, without a lapse. There is no clause that expires it into a higher number,
no review date that resets it, and no end date on it.

**3.3 What is not covered.** If Signal Studio later offers a product that is materially
separate from Venue Edition, it is priced separately and your held rate does not cover it.
Clause 6.4 of the agreement says the same thing and is the operative text.

**3.4 What ends it.** A lapse. If your agreement is cancelled, or a renewal is not paid
and the agreement ends, your rate ends with it and your place is gone. **This is written
here so you read it before you sign, and not for the first time on the day it happens.**

**3.5** When the agreement ends, clause 3.4 of the agreement holds without exception:
every couple who has already opened a workspace keeps it for their full term and keeps
their Keepsake. Only new invitations stop.

**3.6 Coming back later.**

> **[ DECISION POINT A ]** What is a founding venue offered if it lapses and later wants
> to come back?
> **Drafted to:** the standard rate, with restoration available from Ethan personally, in
> writing, with the reason recorded.
> **Why that reading:** two documents produced by approved tasks both say a returning
> venue comes back at the standard rate, so drafting anything else would reopen them. The
> restoration exception is added because the code currently refuses to answer at all, and
> a rule with no human exception in a twenty-five-relationship programme will be broken
> the first time it is unfair.
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.04-continuous-renewal-and-lapse.md` section 6, recommendation
> 5. Not ratified.
>
> **The clause, if approved:** *"If your agreement lapses and you later want to come back,
> you come back at the standard rate. Your place is not reopened. Ethan can restore a
> founding rate personally, in writing, and if he does, the reason is recorded."*

**3.7 If your venue is sold or changes hands**, your rate follows the property under
clause 11 of the agreement. It is not repeated here.

## 4 · Your number

**4.1** Founding places are numbered **01/25 through 25/25**, written that way everywhere:
on the certificate, on the founding page, on your order form and in our records.

**4.2 Your number is assigned when your first payment clears.** Not when you sign, not
when you are invoiced, and not on a verbal yes. Payment is the only step that cannot be
walked back, which is what makes the number worth having.

**4.3** Numbers are assigned in the order payments clear. Whoever clears first takes the
lowest free number. If two clear in the same minute, the earlier signature date decides.
If those match too, Ethan assigns and records why.

**4.4** Other venues have proposals out at the same time as yours. The number on your
order form is the number that would be assigned if your payment cleared next. If a payment
clears before yours, you take the next free number, and we tell you which one on the day it
is assigned.

**4.5 A number is never reused.** If your agreement lapses in year three, your number stays
yours in the record. It is not given to anyone else, and the founding page shows the place
as closed rather than open.

**4.6 If a payment reverses after a number is assigned**, the number is withdrawn and the
place returns to the pool. This is the one case where an assigned number goes back. It is
recorded with the date and the reason, and you are told in the same message that tells you
the payment failed.

**4.7 A number is not a licence.** It is recognition. The agreement is what grants the
product.

## 5 · How long a place is held

**5.1** A place is held for **14 days from the date of your proposal**. The proposal states
the place number and the date the hold ends.

**5.2** It is one hold. **Only Ethan can extend it**, personally, and an extension is
recorded against your account with its new end date so our register never disagrees with
what you were told.

**5.3** If the hold ends, the place returns to the pool and we tell you in one short
message whether places remain. A venue whose hold ends is not out of the programme. If a
place is free, you can still take one, and it will be the next free number rather than the
one you were quoted.

**5.4 Between signature and payment**, the hold continues rather than ending. A signed
venue keeps its place for 14 days from the invoice date. If that passes, Ethan decides case
by case and records the decision. Nobody loses a place because an accounts department was
slow, and nobody holds a place indefinitely with a signature and no money.

## 6 · When the programme closes

**6.1** The Founding 25 closes when the **twenty-fifth payment clears**. Not when the
twenty-fifth venue signs, and not when twenty-five proposals are out.

**6.2** At that moment, every hold still outstanding is void. Every one of those venues is
told the same day, plainly, and offered the standard €1,500 rate. Nobody finds out by
noticing a page changed.

**6.3** There is no twenty-sixth place, no second founding round, and no later reopening at
the founding rate.

**6.4** A venue that lapses after the programme closes does not reopen a place. Twenty-five
is the number of places ever granted, not a running count of how many are occupied.

## 7 · What a founding venue gets

Everything in clause 3 of the agreement applies to you as it does to every venue. The six
things below are what a founding place adds. Each one carries its boundary in the same
paragraph, on purpose.

**7.1 The rate.** €1,000 a year, on the conditions in paragraph 3.
**Where it stops:** paragraph 3.4. A lapse ends it.

**7.2 Your number.** A place in the Founding 25, numbered as paragraph 4 describes.
**Where it stops:** paragraph 6. The programme closes at twenty-five paid venues.

**7.3 Access to Ethan.** One 30-minute call a year, plus an email route that reaches him
directly, with a reply within one working day. The call is yours to use on whatever is
useful.
**Where it stops:** it is one call a year and an email address. It is not a retainer, not
an account manager and not a standing arrangement. Signal Studio is one person, and the
boundary is what keeps the reply times honest across every venue that has one. Clause 10
of the agreement is the operative text.

**7.4 First look.** You see new work before it ships, and you can say what should change
while it still can be changed.
**Where it stops: first look is not a veto.** Twenty-five venues will not agree with each
other, and a product designed by committee is worse for all of them.

**7.5 Your requests, on the record.** Every request from a founding venue is written down
and goes into the roadmap ahead of requests from anywhere else. You can ask what happened
to yours and get a straight answer, including no.
**Where it stops, and this is stated now rather than discovered later: nothing is built for
one venue.** A request that only helps you does not get built. A request that helps you and
would help the next twenty-four does, and that is the test. Work for a single venue sits
outside this agreement, as clause 6.2 of the agreement says.

**7.6 Recognition.** Your venue's name on the founding page and the founding map, and a
numbered certificate for the wall.
**Where it stops:** paragraph 8, and it is a choice rather than a condition.

## 8 · Recognition is a separate consent

**8.1** Using your venue's name and logo in the founding programme, on the founding page
and on the map, is a **separate written consent**. It is asked for on its own, not buried
in this schedule, and it is not part of signing the agreement.

**8.2 You can withdraw it at any time**, in writing, and we take the name and logo down.

**8.3 Nothing about your rate, your number or your place depends on giving it or keeping
it.** A founding venue that says no to recognition is a founding venue on identical terms.

**8.4** Recognition is your venue's name and logo in the founding programme and nothing
else. It is not a joint marketing commitment, a case study, or an endorsement of Signal
Studio, and none of those is assumed from having a founding place.

**8.5** Your certificate is sent once. Reprints are not included, as clause 6.2 of the
agreement says.

## 9 · What a founding place is not

**9.1 It is not a protected area.** Signal Studio does not give any venue a protected area
or a defined patch. Other venues near you, including venues you compete with, can be
founding venues too. **This is said before you sign, and it is never something you discover
afterwards.** If you want to know who else can be in it, ask and we will tell you.

**9.2 It is not a stake in Signal Studio.** A founding place buys a product on an annual
agreement. It gives you no interest in the business of any kind, and no share in anything
it earns.

**9.3 It is not a joint enterprise.** Clause 17.1 of the agreement sets out what the
relationship is.

**9.4 It is not a scheme with rules of continuation.** There is no committee, no vote and
no right to remain beyond the agreement itself.

**9.5 It carries no accreditation.** There is no external standards body behind Venue
Edition and no certificate of quality attached to a founding place. The certificate in
paragraph 7.6 records a number, not a standard.

**9.6 It is not a promise that the service lasts.** Clause 14.1 of the agreement says what
Signal Studio does not promise, and clause 7.4 says what happens if Venue Edition stops.

## 10 · Places and how they are described

**10.1** Any statement about how many places remain is true at the moment it is sent. It is
counted from payments that have cleared, never from proposals that are out.

**10.2** There is no countdown, no deadline invented for effect, and no claim about places
that cannot be checked against the paid count at the moment of sending.

**10.3** The same applies to you under clause 12 of the agreement. If you describe your
place to couples or to anyone else, it is described the way this schedule describes it.

## 11 · What we ask of you

**11.1 Describe it the way it is**, using the approved copy pack, as clause 12 of the
agreement requires. Nothing about the product is permanent or lasts indefinitely, and it
must never be described that way.

**11.2 Tell us when it is wrong.** A founding venue that stays quiet about a problem is
worth less to the programme than one that says so on the call.

## 12 · Two documents that are not part of this

**12.1** The **Founding 25 Benefits Charter** and the **Founding 25 Programme Mechanics**
are plain-English descriptions written to be read before signing. They are not part of the
agreement.

**12.2** This schedule is the operative text on everything it covers. Where a description
and this schedule differ, this schedule wins, and clause 18.3 of the agreement says the
same.

3. **The mechanics document contradicts itself and this schedule follows the code.**
   `FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS.md` lines 105 to 106, the state diagram, says a lapse
   sends the place back to the pool. Line 198 says the place is gone, section 2 says a

   rest of the document. Confirm, and the diagram needs a one-line correction in another
   task's deliverable.
4. **Paragraph 8.1, recognition consent.** It covers name and logo in the founding
   programme. D-027 point 3 separately says no logo appears in a couple's workspace, and
   clause 9.2 of the agreement carries that. Confirm the two are meant to be different, so
   nobody later reads the recognition consent as permission to put a logo in front of a
   couple.
5. **Paragraph 7.3, "a reply within one working day."** Its only source is the unapproved
   Benefits Charter. D-009 point 5 ratifies "a named email route" and states no reply time,
   while E02.09 AC1 requires one to be stated. This is a commitment to 25 venues from one
   person, and nothing measures it. Keep one working day, change it, or state the route
   without a time. Same question as E03.02 founder question 7, and it should get one answer
   rather than two.

## 4. Open legal points

For the professional review D-023 funds. Each is a question.

   continuous renewal, with no expiry clause, enforceable as drafted, and what happens to
