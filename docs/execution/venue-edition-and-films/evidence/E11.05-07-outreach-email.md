# Part A · E11.05 — The founder introduction email

## 1. What this email is for, and what it is not for

The email has exactly one job: get the venue owner to open the film. It does not
sell. It does not book a meeting. It does not explain the product. It carries
enough for a person to decide whether the next sixty seconds are worth spending.

D-013 fixed the channel: email, a personalised film, a landing page, no visits,
no letters (with D-018 closing the letters question). D-017 fixed the cadence: 25
venues per cohort, sequentially, until 25 have signed and paid. This is the first
of four touches.

The tone rule for this surface, from `E09.10` §4, is the whole brief in one line:

> One founder writing to one owner. Specific to that venue in the first line.
> I / you. Short. Under 200 words total. Never: marketing voice, a deck in an
> email, "quick question", attachments.

## 2. Which rungs of the ladder this email climbs

`E09.10` §3.1 sets seven rungs for the venue owner, in order. An email under 200
words cannot carry seven rungs, and a first email that tries reads as a brochure.

| Rung | In the email | Where it lands instead |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · What their couples get | **Yes**, one sentence | — |
| 2 · Whose it is, and the venue's exposure | **Yes**, one sentence, before price | — |
| 3 · What it costs and what the price does | **Yes**, with the per-wedding arithmetic | Fuller conditions on the private page |
| 4 · What they see, and never see | **Half** — the negative half only | The private page carries both halves at equal weight |
| 5 · What it asks of their team | No | The film and the private page |
| 6 · What happens at the end | **Yes**, the survival sentence, stated unprompted | Full Keepsake sentence on the private page |
| 7 · What being one of 25 means | No | The private page, then the proposal |

**Why rung 4 appears as half a rung.** `E09.10` §4 requires that every claim about
what a venue can see travels in the same block, at the same weight, as what it
cannot. The way to obey that rule inside 200 words is to make no positive
visibility claim at all. The email says only *you never see what they write*. The
private page carries both halves in full. An email that said "you see when
invitations are opened" would owe the negative half in the same breath and would
blow the word count doing it.

**Why the survival sentence is in a first cold email at all.** D-020 point 2
requires it stated unprompted in the outreach email and in the agreement. It is
also the single most useful sentence in the email commercially: it is the answer
to "what happens to my couples if this company disappears", given before the
owner has to ask, which is the only version of that answer worth anything.

## 3. The email

### Subject line

**Use:** `A founding place for {Venue Name}`

Two alternates, if the primary underperforms across a full cohort:

- `{Venue Name} and the Founding 25`
- `Something I built for {Venue Name} couples`

**Rules.** The venue's name is in it. Under 45 characters. No "quick question",
no "Re:" on a first send, no false thread, no urgency, no question mark
gimmick, no emoji, no brackets. **Pick one and hold it for the whole cohort.**
Splitting subject lines across 25 sends makes the cohort uncomparable at the
weekly review (E11.15) and 25 is far too small a sample to learn anything from
the split.

### Sender and shape

| Field | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| From name | `Ethan McNamara` | A person wrote this. "Signal Studio" in the from-name makes it a campaign. |
| From address | `ethan@signalstudio.ie` | D-013 point 3 fixes the sending domain. `hello@` is the published suite address (`BRAND.md` §4) and reads as a shared mailbox. |
| Reply-to | same | No reply-to redirection. Redirection is a bulk-mail signature. |
| Format | Plain text, or minimal HTML with no images and no CSS | R-010 mitigation, verbatim: "plain, short HTML with a real signature". |
| Images | **None.** No logo, no banner, no signature image | Blocked images leave holes, and image-heavy cold mail filters badly. |
| Attachments | **None. Ever.** See Part B. | D-013 point 1 and E11.07 |
| Links | **Exactly one**, on `signalstudio.ie` | See §7. No shorteners, no redirector, no tracking domain. |
| Total weight | Under 50KB | A cold email that weighs more than a page of text is not a letter. |

### The body

```text
Subject: A founding place for {Venue Name}

Hi {First name},

{Venue-specific opening. One or two sentences, thirty words maximum. See §4.}

Every couple who books their wedding with you gets a private planning
workspace. You give it to them.

It belongs to the couple, not to me and not to you. Your name sits quietly
on it. You never see what they write inside it.

It is €1,000 a year, prepaid, VAT included, for the first 25 venues in
Limerick and the surrounding counties. That is €500 a year less than
standard, for as long as you stay. Across 40 weddings that is €25 a wedding.

If you ever stop, every workspace already opened keeps its full term, and
the couple keeps everything in it to read and to export. Only new
invitations stop.

I made a short film for {Venue Name}. It runs under a minute and your venue
is in it by name.

{film_url}

If it is not for you, say so and I will leave it there.

Ethan McNamara
Signal Studio
ethan@signalstudio.ie
```

**Word count: 148 words written, plus a venue-specific opening of up to 30
words. 178 at its longest**, excluding the greeting, the URL and the signature.
The cap in `E09.10` §4 is 200.

### Merge fields

| Field | Source | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `{First name}` | E10.07, verified at E10.08 | First name only. No title, no surname, no "Dear". If the first name is not verified, the send does not go. |
| `{Venue Name}` | E10.04 | The name the venue uses for itself, not its legal entity name. Appears three times: subject, opening, film line. |
| `{Venue-specific opening}` | E10.11, hand-written per venue | §4 governs it. No default, no fallback, no generic version. |
| `{film_url}` | E13.16 | One URL, on `signalstudio.ie`. §7 governs it. |

**A send with any unfilled field is not a send.** The pre-send check in §9 is
mechanical: an unresolved `{` anywhere in the drafted message stops it. "Hi ,"
reaching a founding venue costs more than the whole cohort is worth.

## 4. The venue-specific opening

This is the only part of the email that is written by hand, and it is the part
that decides whether the rest gets read. It is also the part most likely to go
wrong, because the obvious way to write it is flattery and the second most
obvious way is a guess about their business presented to them as fact.

**Its source is E10.11**, "one honest, venue-specific reason each account belongs
in the founding outreach", drawn from the research in E10.09 and E10.10. If
E10.11's line for a venue is not good enough to open an email with, the research
is not finished and that venue is not ready to contact.

### The five rules

1. **It must be something only a person who actually looked would know.** Not the
   county. Not that they do weddings. Something specific to how they run them.
2. **It is about their wedding operation, not their building.** Every venue in
   this cohort has been told their house is beautiful. None of them has been told
   something true about how their couples plan.
3. **No flattery, and no compliment as the opening move.** "I love what you have
   built" is what every supplier email opens with, and it is what makes the next
   sentence unreadable.
4. **No guess about their problems, stated as fact.** The old draft's
   `[venue_pain_guess]` slot ("I imagine the last month before a wedding still
   creates repeated questions") tells an owner what their own business feels
   like. Ask it later, on the call (E11.09). Never assert it in a first email.
5. **Verifiable and dated.** Websites change. The opening cites something checked
   within 30 days of the send, and the check date is recorded against the account
   (E10.14 already carries contact-verification dates; the same field carries
   this).

### What is forbidden in the opening

No named couple. No named real wedding. No quote from a review. No reference to a
photograph of an identifiable person from their website or social accounts. No
mention of another venue in the cohort, by name or by hint (E10.14 governs public
naming and consent). No claim about their volume, their prices or their bookings.

### Worked examples

Using the synthetic demo venue only (D-012.1: Glenmara House, Mara and Finn are
the approved fictional example; P14 forbids presenting any of it as real).

**Good.**

> Your planning pack sets out the twelve weeks before the day week by week,
> which is more structure than most venues give a couple in writing.

> You publish a supplier list and a run-of-day template rather than leaving
> couples to invent both, and that tells me your coordinator is carrying the
> planning year, not just the day.

> Glenmara House runs its own tastings and its own final-numbers deadline
> rather than pushing both to the couple's planner.

**Bad, with the reason.**

| Line | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Glenmara House is one of the most beautiful venues in the county." | Flattery. Rule 3. Also true of everyone in the cohort, so it says nothing. |
| "I imagine the last month before a wedding creates a lot of repeated questions for your team." | Rule 4. Telling an owner what their own operation feels like. |
| "I saw you had a wedding last Saturday." | Watching. Reads as surveillance, and it names a real couple's day. |
| "As a leading Limerick wedding venue…" | Marketing voice in the first line. `BRAND.md` §3. |
| "Just a quick question about your wedding planning process." | Banned by `E09.10` §4 explicitly, and it is not a question. |

### Three deliberate omissions, each of which looks like an oversight

**No "your place is held for 14 days".** D-009 point 7 holds a place for 14 days
**from proposal**, not from first contact. Putting a hold in the first email
invents a term that was never ratified, and it is urgency theatre, which P13
bans outright.

**No meeting ask.** The old sequence closed with "Would it be worth a short
conversation?" Two asks in one email means the reader chooses the cheaper one,
and the film is the thing that was built. The film carries the next step.

**No product names, no features, no screenshot, no deck, no PDF.** `E09.10` §3.1:
"Never leads with. Product names. Features. Screenshots."

### One judgement call, flagged rather than buried

The email says *"the couple keeps everything in it to read and to export"* rather
than naming Keepsake. `E09.10` §1.5 requires that wherever Keepsake is promised
in a document, a term or a contract, the qualifier **"for as long as the service
exists"** appears with it, and permits omission only in a single in-product line
where the terms are one tap away.

A cold email is not that in-product line. So the choice was between spending 20
words on the full ratified sentence, or stating the substance without the proper
noun and putting the full sentence on the private page, one click away. This
draft takes the second route. The alternative, if the founder prefers Keepsake
named at first contact:

> If you ever stop, every workspace already opened keeps its full term. After
> that it stays open in Keepsake mode, read-only with a one-click export the
> couple owns, for as long as the service exists. Only new invitations stop.

That is 40 words instead of 27 and pushes the body to 187. It still fits.
**This is founder decision FD-A in §11.**

# Part B · E11.07 — Delivering the film and the page

## 6. The rule

**Nothing is attached. Ever.** No film file, no PDF, no deck, no calendar invite,
no image. The whole message weighs under 50KB and carries one link.

Four independent reasons, any one of which is sufficient:

1. **Deliverability.** R-010 is already critical: 25 near-identical cold,
   link-carrying emails from a domain whose DKIM is pending. A 10MB video
   attachment on top of that is the last input a spam filter needs. R-010's own
   mitigation list says it in as many words: "link to the film, never attach it".
2. **It would not arrive.** Common corporate mailbox limits sit around 10 to 25MB
   including encoding overhead, and many venue mailboxes are hosted on plans
   tighter than that. A bounce is the good outcome. Silent stripping is the
   likely one.
3. **An attachment is a dead end.** It cannot be updated, it cannot carry a next
   step, it cannot be corrected after sending, and it tells you nothing about
   whether it was ever opened. D-013 point 3 permits exactly one signal: film
   link clicks. An attachment produces none.
4. **It is the wrong object.** The film is an invitation to a page, and the page
   is where the offer lives. Sending the film alone strips it of everything the
   venue needs next.

## 7. The delivery chain

One link. One page. One place everything lives.

```
Email  ──▶  signalstudio.ie/v/{venue-slug}-{token}      the private venue page
                 │
                 ├── the film, click to play, poster frame, captions
                 ├── the offer in full, both halves of what the venue sees
                 ├── the Keepsake sentence, complete, with its qualifier
                 └── one action: reply to Ethan, or take a time
```

### 7.1 The link

**Shape:** `https://signalstudio.ie/v/glenmara-house-7Kq2mXvR4nBt9wDs`

- **The venue's own name is in the path.** The recipient sees their name in the
  URL before they click. That is personalisation doing real work, and it reads
  legitimate in a way a bare token does not.
- **The token is unguessable and does the security.** At least 16 random
  characters from a URL-safe alphabet, generated per venue, never sequential,
  never derived from the venue name, cohort number or account ID. Without it,
  `/v/glenmara-house` is guessable and the whole cohort can be enumerated by
  anyone who receives one email.
- **On `signalstudio.ie` directly.** No link shortener, no redirect hop, no
  third-party tracking domain. Shorteners hide the destination, are a well-known
  spam signal, and would break the one tracking event that is permitted by making
  the click land somewhere other than the page.
- **No query string.** Everything the page needs is resolved server-side from the
  token. The existing tracking contract in `studio/src/lib/tracking.ts` stays for
  public marketing surfaces; it is not used here, because a private page for a
  named business does not need its identifiers in a URL that gets forwarded,
  pasted into chat threads and logged by every proxy on the way.
- **The link text is the visible URL**, not "click here" and not a styled button.
  Buttons in a plain founder email look like a campaign; a visible URL survives
  plain-text conversion.

### 7.2 The page

| Property | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Route | `/v/{slug}-{token}`, in `studio` | Same origin as the film, same domain as the sender. |
| Indexing | `noindex, nofollow`, excluded from the sitemap, `/v/` disallowed in `robots.ts` | A private invitation must not become a search result naming a venue that has not agreed to anything (E10.14). |
| Login | **None** | A password on a cold-email click is where the funnel ends. The token is the access. |
| Film playback | Poster frame, click to play, no autoplay | Autoplay on a cold click reads as an ad, plays to a viewer who has already scrolled, and burns mobile data before consent. |
| Captions | A `.vtt` track, and legible on-screen text in the film itself | Most first views are muted. This is not an accessibility extra; it is whether the film works at all. |
| Analytics | Server-side page view only. No third-party script, no pixel, no session recording | D-013 point 3. See §7.4. |
| Cookies | None that require a banner | Nothing to consent to means no consent banner between a venue and the film. |
| Video source | One `.mp4` per venue, served from object storage over CDN | §7.3. |
| Fallback | The offer is complete in text and stills **below** the film | The page must work with video blocked. See §8. |
| Second reader | The page names the venue, never the recipient | It gets forwarded to a partner or an owner. It has to make sense to someone who did not get the email. |
| Lifetime | The token does not expire during the programme. When the cohort closes, `/v/*` redirects to `/venues` | A venue returning in four months must not hit a 404. |

**The page has two states on one URL.** This is a recommendation to E12.03, which
owns the parameterised private proposal page.

- **State 1, invitation.** What the first email links to: the film, the offer in
  full, one action.
- **State 2, proposal.** After a conversation, the same URL gains the order form,
  the named conditions, the 14-day hold date and the founding number.

The venue never loses the thread, never gets a second link, and never has to
search their inbox for which of two URLs was the current one. One link per venue,
for the life of the relationship.

### 7.3 The film file

| Item | Spec | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Container and codec | MP4, H.264 High, AAC audio | The only combination that plays everywhere without a second encode. |
| Resolution | 1920×1080 | |
| Target size | **Under 12MB** for a 40-second film | Roughly 2.2 Mbps video plus 128 kbps audio. 25 films is then about 300MB total. |
| Poster | JPEG, under 200KB, showing the venue name | It is the first frame the owner sees, and it is what renders if the film never loads. |
| Captions | `.vtt`, one per venue | |
| Storage | Object storage with CDN delivery, **not** the repository's `public/` directory | 25 files in `public/` is 300MB in git, redeployed on every build, forever. |
| Naming | Keyed to the token, not the venue name | A guessable file URL defeats the token. |

**Hosting, assessed against the zero budget (D-015 Q5).**

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Vercel Blob** | **Recommended.** Same platform, CDN-backed, no new vendor, no new trust surface. It is not currently a dependency of `studio`, and the plan's storage and bandwidth allowance must be checked against roughly 300MB stored plus a few hundred views before it is adopted. Recorded as assumption A-3. |
| YouTube, unlisted | Rejected. "Unlisted" is not private. It puts Google's chrome, its recommendations and its tracking on a page whose entire subject is that the venue's couples are not being watched. |
| Vimeo, private | Rejected on budget. Domain-locked private hosting is a paid tier. |
| `public/` in the repo | Rejected. 300MB of binary in git, shipped on every deploy. |
| Direct link to the file | Rejected. It opens a bare player with no offer, no next step and no context, and it is the one thing a raw `.mp4` URL can never be talked out of. |

### 7.4 Tracking

D-013 point 3 is exact: **film link clicks only. No open pixels.** Open tracking
on a cold email to people you want a trust relationship with is a bad trade, and
that reasoning applies with equal force to anything else that watches a reader.

**Recorded, server-side, on the page render:**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Venue account ID | Stable ID. The CRM owns contact records (`PROJECT.md` §21). |
| Cohort and touch | `cohort_1`, `touch_1` |
| First view timestamp | |
| Repeat view count | A second and third view from a venue is the strongest signal available in this system. |

**Not recorded, and not built:** open pixels. Read receipts. Third-party
analytics on `/v/*`. Session recording. Heatmaps. Scroll depth. IP-derived
location. Anything that identifies an individual reader rather than the venue.

**One boundary the founder should rule on.** "Play started" and "watched to the
end" are film events, not link clicks. They would be genuinely useful and they
are arguably inside the spirit of "film link clicks". The conservative reading of
D-013 is page view only, and this draft takes it. **This is founder decision FD-B
in §11.**

## 8. What goes wrong, and what happens then

| Failure | Response, built in advance |
|---|---|
| The email lands in Promotions or Junk | R-010, still open and still critical. DKIM, SPF and DMARC complete first (E11.04). Warm the domain with real sends for two weeks. Stagger the 25 across three to five days. Plain text, one link, no images. Check inbox placement against a seed set before the first real send. |
| Video is blocked by corporate policy, or the film simply will not play | **The page never depends on the film.** The offer is complete in text and stills below it. This is why the film is not the destination. |
| The recipient has left the venue | The page names the venue, not the person. A forward to the successor still reads correctly. |
| The email is forwarded to an owner or a business partner | Intended. The page repeats the offer in full for a reader who never saw the email. Nothing on it depends on context from the message. |
| The venue asks for something to show their owner | Send the same link. Never the file. If a download is genuinely needed, the page offers one; it is still not an attachment. |
| The token leaks, or is pasted publicly | Nothing on the page is confidential to Signal Studio. It names one venue and one offer. No other venue in the cohort is named or hinted at, and the film's other 24 pins stay anonymous (E13.05). Rotate the token, redirect the old one. |
| A venue replies "no" or "stop" | The address goes on the suppression list immediately and nothing further is sent from any cohort. E11.04 owns the mechanism. D-013 point 4 already stops at four touches; an explicit no stops at one. |
| The film is wrong: mispronounced name, wrong county, wrong pin | Fix the render, re-upload against the same token, tell them plainly. The URL does not change, which is the reason the link points at a page rather than at a file. |
| A venue comes back in four months | The token still resolves. After the programme closes, `/v/*` redirects to `/venues` rather than 404ing. |

## 9. Before the first send

Mechanical. Every line is checkable by someone who was not in the room.

**Per cohort**

- [ ] DKIM live on `signalstudio.ie`, with SPF and DMARC verified alongside it (E11.04, `docs/DKIM_SETUP.md`). **This gates all sending, D-013 point 3.**
- [ ] Domain warmed with real sending for two weeks (R-010).
- [ ] Inbox placement checked against a seed set across Gmail, Outlook and at least one hosted-business mailbox.
- [ ] Suppression list live and checked against the cohort (E11.04).
- [ ] The 25 sends scheduled across three to five days, not one (R-010).
- [ ] Cohort 1 locked (E10.12).

**Per venue**

- [ ] Contact verified within 30 days: name, role, direct address, still employed (E10.08).
- [ ] The venue-specific opening written by hand, checked against §4's five rules and the forbidden list.
- [ ] Every merge field resolved. No `{` survives anywhere in the drafted message.
- [ ] The film rendered and watched end to end by a human, with the venue name and county correct and the name pronounced correctly (E13.17).
- [ ] The private page loads on a phone, on a laptop, and with video blocked.
- [ ] The film plays from the page, and the poster and captions are present.
- [ ] The link opens on the first click, on the same domain, with no redirect hop.
- [ ] Nothing attached. Message under 50KB.
- [ ] The send is logged against venue, cohort and touch (E11.01).

## 11. Two founder decisions

**FD-A — Keepsake, named or described in the first email.**
The draft says *"the couple keeps everything in it to read and to export"*, and
