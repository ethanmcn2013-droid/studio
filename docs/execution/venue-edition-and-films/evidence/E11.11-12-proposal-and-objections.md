## Standing constraints, restated because they are the ones this document could break

**D-016.** There is no solicitor and no accountant. Claude and Codex draft and
review everything. Nothing here states or implies legal approval, solicitor
review, accountant verification, or unqualified GDPR compliance. That decision
was made after the risk was put to the founder in full. It is not re-argued here.

**P1.** The words "forever", "for life", "lifetime", "permanent", "always",
"guaranteed" and "in perpetuity" appear nowhere in any asset below, about
anything, including the price lock and the Keepsake.

**P12.** Every price is stated inclusive of VAT at the prevailing rate. Never
"+ VAT", never "ex VAT", never a monthly equivalent.

**P13.** No urgency, no scarcity theatre, no social proof theatre. The place is
held 14 days. That is a fact, stated once, plainly.

**P8.** No dietary, allergy, accessibility or health information about a named
person appears in any example, screenshot, demo fixture or asset below, and no
line here says such information flows to the venue.

Two clocks run and they are not the same clock. The **hold** is 14 days from the
date of the proposal. The **payment window** is 14 days from the invoice date and
only starts once the order form is returned. A venue never loses a place because
an accounts department is slow (`FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS.md` §3).

linked page, never as an attachment alone. E12.09 designs it; the words are

### 2.1 Why the proposal is built in that order

It runs the venue-owner ladder from `E09.10` §3.1 without skipping a rung: what
their couples get, whose it is, what it costs, what they see and in the same
breath what they never see, what it asks of the team, what happens at the end,
what being one of 25 means. Price sits at rung 3, after trust and before
mechanics, because a venue owner who does not yet believe the privacy boundary
has no way to judge whether €1,000 is cheap.

The negative half travels with the positive half, in the same block, at the same
weight. "What you see" and "What you never see" are two headings of equal size on
the same page. Splitting them across a proposal and an FAQ is how a trust claim
becomes a trust problem.

### 2.2 What is deliberately not in the proposal

- **Product names.** Signal Tasks, Signal Timeline, Signal, Signal Notes do not
  appear. A venue owner is buying an outcome for their couples, not four
  products. The names arrive in onboarding, when they are useful.
- **Screenshots.** The walkthrough already showed the product live. A screenshot
  in a proposal is a weaker version of something they have already seen.
- **A second call to action.** One ask. Complete the order form.
- **Any reference to the films.** They did their job before this document existed.
- **Insurance, legal review, GDPR, data location.** Each has an answer, each is
  honest, and each is given when asked. None of them belongs unprompted in a
  document whose job is to be readable in ninety seconds. §9 carries them.

### 2.3 The number sentence, and why it is worded that way

`FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS.md` §3 requires the proposal to state the place
number that would be assigned. §4 requires that two venues never both believe
they hold place 03/25. Those two rules pull against each other when six proposals
are open at once.

The resolution: state the paid count, state the next free number, and state in
the same sentence that numbers follow cleared payment and other proposals are
open. That satisfies both rules and is true at the moment of sending. **The paid
count is verified against the register at the moment of sending, never carried
over from the previous proposal** (mechanics §1, scarcity claims must stay true).

Recorded as **OQ-4** in §17 for founder confirmation.

### 4.1 The fourth confirmation box, and why it stays

A tick box that says "no solicitor has reviewed this" is an unusual thing to put
in front of a buyer, and the instinct is to move it into small print. It stays
where it is, at the same weight as the others, for three reasons.

1. It is what D-016 requires in substance. A programme that decides to draft its
   own legal documents and then makes that hard to notice has made the decision
   twice: once honestly and once not.
2. It is the strongest thing on the page. Twenty-five venues signing a document
   whose author told them plainly what it is not, is a better commercial position
   than twenty-five venues who were allowed to assume.
3. It converts the worst version of a future conversation into a signed
   acknowledgement, and it does so before the money moves rather than after.

The founding rate is €1,000 including VAT for one year. A venue's own solicitor
may cost more than the contract. That is the venue's call to make with the facts
rather than around them (`E09.10` §6, Answer 2).

### 4.2 The publicity box is separate on purpose

D-010.1: venues are asked for logo and name usage, couples are asked for nothing.
D-013.5: publicity consent is always separate, opt-in and revocable. Putting it in
the same block as the commercial confirmations would make it feel like a term of
the deal. It is not, and the line says so.

## 6. The same-day follow-up email

Sent the same day as the walkthrough, ideally within two hours. `E09.10` §4:
one founder writing to one owner, short, no marketing voice, no deck in an email.

**Subject.** `Venue Edition for [venue_name]`

If the walkthrough came off a live thread, reply in that thread and keep its
subject. A new subject line reads as a campaign.

---

> Hi [first_name],
>
> Thanks for the time this morning.
>
> The proposal is below and attached. Everything is on one page: what your couples
> get, what you see, what you never see, and what it costs.
>
> The three documents you would be signing are attached as well: the agreement and
> the founding schedule, the data processing agreement and security schedule, and
> the privacy notice written for couples. I would rather you had all of it now than
> after you had decided.
>
> Two things from the call I said I would put in writing.
>
> [answered_question_1]
>
> [answered_question_2]
>
> Your place is held until [hold_end_date]. If you need longer, say so and I will
> move it.
>
> Ethan

---

**`[answered_question_1]` and `[answered_question_2]` are not optional.** Every
walkthrough produces at least one question the founder answered less well than he
wanted to, or said he would check. Writing two of them down does three things: it
proves the call was listened to, it puts the answer in a form the owner can
forward to a partner, and it stops a half-answer becoming a promise nobody
decided to make. If a question was genuinely not answered, the line is *"I said I
would check this and I have not yet. I will come back on it by [date]."*

**Never in this email.** An apology for the length of the call. "Just checking".
"Circling back". A second ask. A calendar link. A summary of the product they
just watched. Anything about the films. Any word from the never-list in
`E09.10` §2.

## 7. The hold clock

One hold, 14 days, from the date of the proposal. D-009.7 and mechanics §3. The
messages below are the entire set. Nothing else is sent inside the hold.

### 7.1 What the hold actually is

A place is reserved the moment the proposal is sent. Not at the first reply, not
at an expression of interest. That is generous and it is deliberate: it means the
sentence "your place is held" is true when it is written, and it means the
founder cannot use the hold as a pressure device, because it was already given.

### 7.2 Day 10, one factual note

> Hi [first_name],
>
> Your place is held until [hold_end_date], so this is the point where I would
> normally check whether you need anything else from me.
>
> If it is a question I have not answered, send it. If it is timing, I can move
> the date. If it is a no, that is a completely fine answer and I would rather
> know.
>
> Ethan

**This message is a judgement, not a ratified rule.** Mechanics §3 defines the
proposal, the expiry and the extension, and is silent on anything between. A
mid-hold note risks reading as urgency, which P13 forbids. It is included because
the alternative is a fortnight of silence ending in an expiry message, which
reads worse and converts worse. It is written so that "no" is the easiest of the
three replies to send. Recorded as **OQ-5** in §17.

### 7.3 Day 14, the hold ends

Sent on the day, not after. Mechanics §3: *"the venue is told, in one short
message, that the hold has ended and whether places remain."*

> Hi [first_name],
>
> The 14 days are up, so I am releasing the place rather than leaving it sitting
> against your name.
>
> [places_remain_sentence]
>
> Nothing about this is final. If it comes back around, write to me.
>
> Ethan

`[places_remain_sentence]` is one of exactly two lines, and which one is used is
checked against the paid count at the moment of sending:

- *"There are still founding places, so if this becomes relevant in the next few
  months there is likely still one."*
- *"The founding places are gone. The standard rate is €1,500 a year, prepaid,
  including VAT, and everything else in the proposal is the same."*

A venue whose hold expires is not out of the programme. If a place is free later
they take the next free number, not the one they were quoted (mechanics §5).

### 7.4 The order form is returned

> Hi [first_name],
>
> Got it, thank you.
>
> The invoice is attached. Your place stays yours while it is with you, so there is
> no clock on this beyond your own accounts process.
>
> Your number is assigned when the payment clears. I will tell you what it is the
> day it happens.
>
> Ethan

Between signature and payment the hold continues rather than expiring. A signed
venue keeps its place for 14 days from the invoice date, and past that the founder
decides case by case and records the decision (mechanics §3).

### 7.5 The payment clears

The one moment in this sequence that is allowed to feel like something.

> Hi [first_name],
>
> Payment cleared this morning. You are [assigned_number] of the Founding 25.
>
> [venue_name] is the [ordinal] venue to back this, and the rate is yours for as
> long as the agreement runs.
>
> Next: I will set your account up and send you one thing to do. It takes about ten
> minutes and then you can invite your first couple.
>
> Ethan

No exclamation marks. No "congratulations". No certificate in this email; the
certificate is E12.11 and arrives on its own, after onboarding, where it is not
competing with an operational instruction.

### 7.6 The programme closes while a hold is open

Mechanics §3: when the 25th payment clears, every outstanding hold is void, and
every one of those venues is told the same day. Nobody finds out by noticing the
page changed.

> Hi [first_name],
>
> The 25th founding venue paid this morning, so the founding places are gone. Your
> hold went with them and I would rather tell you today than let you find out from
> the website.
>
> The offer itself has not changed. Venue Edition is €1,500 a year, prepaid,
> including VAT. Everything in the proposal about what your couples get, what you
> see and what you never see is exactly the same.
>
> If that still works, the order form is attached and I will send a fresh
> proposal at the standard rate. If it does not, I understand, and thank you for
> the time you gave it.
>
> Ethan

# E11.12 · the objection library

---

## 9. How an answer is built

Four moves, in order. Any answer that skips move 3 is a sales answer, and a venue
owner can hear the difference.

1. **Take it as true.** The objection is usually correct about something. Say
   which part.
2. **The fact.** One, specific, traceable to a decision or a verified behaviour.
3. **The boundary.** What is not being claimed. This is the move that buys the
   rest of the answer.
4. **The next move.** Small, and theirs to decline.

**The rule underneath all of it** (`E09.10` §6): if asked something not covered
here, the answer is *"I don't know, and I'd rather find out and tell you than
guess."* That sentence is always available and is never a weak answer in this
market.

**Three things an answer never does.** It never rebuts. It never says "great
question". It never answers a second objection the venue did not raise, which is
how one doubt becomes three.

**On quoting.** Objection headings below carry the venue's own words, including
words from the `E09.10` §2 never-list. That is a record of what was said. **No
answer uses them.** "White label", "seats", "compliant" and "forever" appear in
quotation marks in a heading and never in a reply.

## 10. Price and money

**A1 · "A thousand euro for something our couples might not use."**
That is the real risk and it is worth being plain about. Nobody has run this at
scale yet, so I cannot show you an adoption number from another venue. What I can
tell you is what you would see: which invitations were opened, and adoption across
your couples as a group. If it turns out your couples do not open it, you will
know that inside a season, from your own account, and you can decide not to renew.
I would rather you found that out from real figures than from me predicting.

**A2 · "Why don't you charge the couples?"**
Because the moment a couple sees a price, it stops being something you gave them
and becomes something they were sold. That is the whole product. They never see a
price, they never enter a card, and there is no upgrade prompt anywhere in it.

**A3 · "Can we pay monthly?"**
No. It is one annual payment, prepaid. Monthly billing means a payment
relationship that can fail quietly in month seven, and a couple's planning year
going dark because a card expired. One payment a year is the only version where
that cannot happen.

**A4 · "Can we try it with one couple first?"**
Not as a paid trial, because the price does not scale down to one couple and
pricing it per couple is the thing I am deliberately not doing. What I can do is
show you the whole thing working with a real upcoming wedding of yours during the
walkthrough, before you pay anything. If you have already had the walkthrough, the
honest answer is that the next step is the year.

**A5 · "What if it doesn't work out. Do we get money back?"**
**[GATED · E02.07.]** Cancellation and refunds are not ratified. Do not improvise
a refund position. The interim answer: *"I am not going to make that up on a
call. It is written into the agreement and you will have the agreement before you
sign anything. If it does not say what you need it to say, tell me then."*

**A6 · "Will the price go up?"**
Not yours. €1,000 is €500 a year less than standard, and it holds for as long as
your agreement renews continuously without lapse. It covers the base annual
agreement. If it ever lapses, the founding rate does not come back and the

## 11. Adoption

**B1 · "What if our couples just don't use it?"**
Then you have spent €1,000 and learned something in one season, and the couples

From your account. Invitations sent, invitations opened, and adoption across your

**B6 · "How many couples typically use it?"**
I do not know, and I am not going to invent a figure. This is the founding group,

## 12. Privacy and data

The family the brief is most specific about: **answerable with what is actually
true, not with a compliance claim.** Every answer below is either a verified
behaviour, a ratified decision, or an honest statement of what is not yet known.

**C1 · "Can we see what our couples are planning?"**
No. Not "you can, but we ask you not to". You cannot, and neither can I in the
ordinary course. You see which invitations you sent, which were opened, and
adoption across your couples as a group. There is no per-couple row, no ranking,
no list of who is behind, and no view of anyone's plan. This is the part of the
product a couple is actually being given, so it is not something I would trade for
a feature request.

**C2 · "Are you GDPR compliant?"**
Use `E09.10` §6 Answer 1, verbatim, spoken or written. **Conditions of use apply:**
the full answer is available only once E03.01, E03.04 and E03.05 exist as drafts a
venue could be sent, **and** R-017 is decided. Until then, the interim answer is
the one in that document: *"The paperwork is being written now and you will get
all of it before you sign. I would rather show you a finished document than
describe one."* Never "we are GDPR compliant", never "fully compliant", never
"certified", never "audited", never "approved by the DPC".

**C3 · "Our guests' details end up in this. Who is responsible for that?"**
Both of us, in different roles, and the written record of which is which is part
of what you get before you sign. In practice the couple decides what goes in
their workspace, you never see it, and I do not read it. The part I will not
gloss: your guests never agreed to anything, and a guest is entitled to know who
holds what about them. That is why the shared page carries a line telling them
how to ask, and why the answer to "what do you hold about my guests" cannot be a
paragraph of reassurance.

**C4 · "We collect dietary and allergy information from guests. Does this handle
that?"**
Not the way it should, yet, and I would rather tell you than sell you a version
of that answer.

Today the wedding template includes a task about collecting final dietary notes,
and the couple types it in. That is health information about your guests, and
sometimes it reveals their religion. Accessibility requirements reveal disability.
It is being entered by someone the guest never gave permission to.

I am changing where that information is captured, so the guest supplies it
themselves at the reply step rather than the couple typing it about them. That is
better law and a better product, and it is being done before this goes anywhere
near a real wedding. Until it is done, the honest instruction is not to route
allergy or accessibility information through the workspace, and none of it appears
on any page a guest can open.

**Conditions of use.** This answer is given when asked and is never volunteered as
a lead. It is accurate as at 2026-08-03 and it changes the day the R-017 fix
lands, at which point this entry is rewritten rather than quietly kept. Source:
R-017, verified at `app/src/lib/templates.generated.ts:108` and
`app/src/app/the-wedding/page.tsx:26`.

**C5 · "There will be photographs of our guests on a public page."**
There can be, and the couple decides that, not you and not me. What is verified
today: the shared page sits behind a link that is not indexed by search engines,
the couple can revoke that link and revocation takes effect immediately, and a
link can carry an expiry. What is not on it, by a hard rule rather than a clause,
is any dietary, allergy, accessibility or health information about a named person.
There is also no venue logo and no Signal Studio branding in the page itself; the
attribution is one line in the footer, and that restraint applies to me as much as
to you.
*Verified 2026-08-03: `app/src/app/share/[token]/page.tsx:11` sets
`robots: { index: false, follow: false }`;
`app/src/server/db/queries.ts:685` refuses a revoked link, an expired link and an
archived workspace.*

**C6 · "Where is our data actually held?"**
Use `E09.10` §6 Answer 4. **P15 applies:** no region, no country and no transfer
claim is stated on a call, in an email, on a page or in a document until E03.05
has verified that specific claim. Spoken now, the sentence "everything runs on
EU-hosted infrastructure" is removed, leaving: *"the full list of suppliers, with

**C7 · "What happens if you have a breach?"**
There is a written procedure and defined timeframes in the agreement, and you get
it before you sign. What I will not do is tell you it cannot happen, or use a
phrase like "bank-grade". If it happened, you would hear it from me, quickly, with
what is known and what is not, and I would rather commit to that in writing than
describe how secure everything is.

**C8 · "What happens to all of it if we stop paying?"**
Any couple who has already opened their workspace keeps it, for their full term,
and then keeps the Keepsake. Nothing is taken from them because you stopped
paying, and I say that unprompted because a venue should know it before it is
weighing a renewal. What stops is new invitations. For your own account data,
deletion controls and timeframes are in the agreement.

**C9 · "Can we delete a couple's workspace? A booking fell through."**
You can release it, which is not the same thing. The couple keeps their content,
your name and branding come off within 24 hours, and the place goes back so you
can invite someone else. You cannot delete their planning, because it was never
yours to delete.

**C10 · "Do you sell the data, or use it to train anything?"**
No, on both. **Conditions of use:** this may be given as a flat "no" only once
that commitment is written into the agreement and the privacy notice (E03.04,

be able to point at it."*

**C11 · "Can we get a list of which of our couples are using it?"**
No, and that one is deliberate. You get the total, not the names. If you could

a couple about a gift, and then it is not a gift.

**C12 · "One of our couples postponed. Will that show up in our account?"**
No. Wedding date changes are never surfaced anywhere. A postponement is the
couple's news to share with you when they are ready, not something you find out
from a screen.
