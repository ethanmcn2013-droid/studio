# E05.10 — who the couple can bring in, and what they get

Evidence for E05.10. Written 2026-08-03 by WP-08 in worktree `_wt-wp08` (app,
branch `claude/wp08-couple`). Criteria written before assessment.

---

## The matrix

Four relationships the task names, one more added because the couple will hit it
and the register already worries about it, and the venue, which is the row that
must never move.

| Relationship | What they should see | What they should do | What ships today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Spouse** | Everything | Everything | `member`: full read-write on every task in the workspace. Notes and Timeline: nothing. | **partly met** |
| **Wedding planner** | Everything operational | Edit tasks, dates, suppliers. Not billing or private notes. | `member`, identical to the spouse. | **not met** |
| **Family member** | The parts they are helping with | Comment, complete their own items | `member`, identical to the spouse. | **not met** |
| **Collaborator / supplier** | One project or a named set of tasks | Update their own items only | `member`, identical to the spouse. A florist invited to see one task can read the honeymoon budget. | **not met** |
| **Second owner** | Everything | Everything, including inviting and removing | `owner`, and it exists. | **met** |
| **The venue** | **Nothing** | **Nothing** | No path. Proven by test below. | **met** |

Two roles exist and that is all: `workspace_members.role` is
`$type<"owner" | "member">` (`src/server/db/schema.ts:467`),
`pending_invites.role` is `$type<"member" | "owner">` (`:933`), and
`src/lib/members.ts:14` types the client-side shape the same way. The five
richer roles in `docs/COLLABORATION_LOOP.md:74-78` were never built; that
passage is aspirational prose, not a description of the product.

## Criterion-by-criterion

### 1 and 2. The matrix exists and is checked — **MET**

Above. Four of six rows are not met, and the shape of the gap is now a decision
with a cost rather than an open worry.

### 3. A sponsorship confers no membership, in either direction — **MET**

Behavioural. A workspace is seeded exactly as redemption leaves it: owner
membership, `workspace_sponsorships` row with `sponsor_id = 'sponsor-glenmara'`,
`sponsor_ref = 'glenmara-house'`, status active. Membership is exactly one row.
No `workspace_members.user_id` matches `sponsor%`. Accepting a spouse invitation
afterwards leaves `workspace_sponsorships.status` and `consented_metadata`
untouched.

Structurally, the table's own header comment states it
(`src/server/db/schema.ts:354-356`): *"Nothing in this row grants membership,
and private content must never be copied into `consentedMetadata`."* The only
writer, `src/server/actions/planning.ts:906-917`, copies exactly two fields.

### 4. A venue-shaped role is clamped to member — **MET**

`workspace_members.role` carries **no SQL check constraint**, so the clamp in
code is the only thing standing between a tampered or migrated invite row and a
real grant. The test drives six values through the real accept SQL —
`venue`, `sponsor`, `coordinator`, `planner`, `admin`, and the empty string —
and every one lands as `member`. The final role set in the workspace is exactly
`["member", "owner"]`.

The clamps themselves, asserted on source:
- `src/server/actions/settings.ts:348-349` on write:
  `inviteRole === "owner" ? "owner" : "member"`.
- `src/server/actions/settings.ts:585` on accept:
  `invite.role === "owner" ? "owner" : "member"` — a second, independent clamp,
  which is why criterion 4 passes even against a hand-written database row.

### 5. A sponsor reference is not a token — **MET**

`sponsor-glenmara`, `glenmara-house` and `spon-1` are each fed to the accept
path. All three return `missing` and membership is unchanged. Invite tokens are
32 CSPRNG bytes base64url (`settings.ts:481-490`); a sponsor slug cannot
collide with one by construction, and the test proves the lookup refuses.

### 6. No sponsor-facing read reaches private content — **MET**

`listSponsorActivationDTOs` (`src/server/planning/queries.ts:225-245`) is
asserted to contain none of `workspaceMembers`, `workspace_members`,
`pendingInvites`, `pending_invites`, `tasks`, `notes`, `comments`,
`attachments`, `shareLinks`. `src/lib/planning/sponsorship.ts` contains neither
`workspaceMembers` nor the string `role`.

**This closes a gap the recon flagged**: the recon noted that the app-side
sponsor DTO allowlist had no regression test at the boundary and recommended one
for E05.10. It now has one.

Worth stating plainly: `listSponsorActivationDTOs` has **zero production
callers**. It is dormant code a future venue portal will reach for, which is
exactly why pinning its allowlist now is worth more than pinning it later.

### 7. No price, plan or seat count on the invitation path — **MET, after a fix**

**Found and fixed.** `src/app/invite/[token]/page.tsx` rendered, unconditionally
and on every invitation including a sponsored wedding one:

> One workspace, every view, the daily digest. Three editing guests on Free,
> unlimited members on Team. No card, no trial.

That is a seat count ("three editing guests") and a plan pitch ("on Free", "on
Team") shown to the spouse, planner or parent of a couple whose venue paid for
the workspace. It violates D-020 ("no seat count anywhere") and the standing
"the couple never sees a price" constraint. It also names a tier, "Team", that
is not one of the five tiers the product has.

**This is a fourth price surface. The E05-E06 audit found three** — Notes,
billing settings, and the Timeline workspace-cap message — and did not reach the
invitation path.

Replaced with:

> Accepting adds you to this workspace. You will see the same views, tasks and
> daily briefing the owner sees, and you can edit them.

Declarative, no plan, no price, no seat count, no em dash, no exclamation mark,
sentence case. The test asserts the absence of `editing guests`, `on Free`,
`on Team`, `seats`, `seat`, `pricing`, `Upgrade`, `€`, `No card` and `trial`
from the rendered file with comments stripped. It failed against the shipped
copy before the fix and passes after, which is the fail-before receipt.

**Passing evidence recorded alongside it.** The three equivalent strings in
`src/components/app/settings/sections/members.tsx:249, 291, 304` are correctly
gated: they render only when `showCounter` or `isCapped`, both of which derive
from `memberCapacity.max !== null`, which is null for a wedding workspace. A
sponsored couple sees none of them. That gating is right and was left alone.

### 8. No member cap on a sponsored workspace — **MET**

`getMemberCapacity` (`src/server/db/membership.ts:105-114`) puts `wedding` in
the unlimited set and returns `max: isUnlimited ? null : FREE_WORKSPACE_MEMBER_CAP`.
Unlimited is an absent cap, never a large number, which is what D-020 requires.

The entitlement is workspace-scoped, which is what makes this work:
`src/server/actions/comp.ts:235-244` writes `workspaceId: ws` on redemption, and
`getWorkspaceTier` (`membership.ts:35`) reads per-workspace rows. Had redemption
written a user-level entitlement, `getWorkspaceTier` would have returned `free`
and a sponsored couple would have been capped at four and shown an upgrade
prompt. It does not. Recorded because it is one line away from being false.

### 9. The invitee is told truthfully what accepting grants — **MET**

The replacement copy says the invitee can edit. Given that `member` is
unrestricted read-write, that is the honest sentence. It is not a substitute for
real roles; it is the minimum owed to someone being handed the keys.

## What the invitation path does well, and was not touched

Recorded because "build only the gap" means saying what is already right.

- 256 bits of CSPRNG entropy per token (`settings.ts:481-490`), replacing an
  earlier `Math.random()` implementation.
- Seven-day expiry; one-per-hour resend cooldown (`settings.ts:414-420`).
- Email match required before the invited address is even revealed
  (`invite/[token]/page.tsx`), so the page cannot enumerate addresses.
- Owner-only invite and role change (`settings.ts:351-354`, `:220`).
- Refusal to remove or demote the last owner (`settings.ts:185-192`, `:222-238`).
- Audit rows for `inviteSent` and `inviteAccepted` in `workspace_events`, with
  no email address in the payload (`settings.ts:465-472`).
- Idempotent accept: `INSERT OR IGNORE` (`settings.ts:590`), so a double accept
  yields one membership row.

## What "invite your spouse" actually delivers, across the four products

The row that most needs stating, because the matrix above is about Tasks and the
couple will not experience it that way.

| Product | Scoping | An invited spouse gets |
|---|---|---|
| Tasks | `workspace_members` (`schema.ts:459-478`) | Everything |
| Signal | `workspace_members` join (`signal/lib/planning-periods/scope.ts:105-111`, `briefing/tasks-db-source.ts:126-128`) | The shared wedding briefing |
| Notes | per user (`eq(notes.userId, userId)`, `notes/server/actions/notes.ts:257`) | **Nothing** |
| Timeline | per owner (`eq(workspaces.ownerUserId, userId)`, `timeline-queries.ts:167`) | **Nothing** |

So two people cannot co-plan in Notes or Timeline today, whatever role they are
given. That is not an E05.10 defect to fix inside the invitation path; it is a
fact about what the invitation buys, and it belongs in front of the founder
alongside the roles decision.

## Risk raised

`workspace_members.role` has no database-level check constraint. Both clamps are
in application code and both are tested, but a migration, a script, or a direct
write could seat an arbitrary role string. Adding
`CHECK (role IN ('owner','member'))` is a one-statement migration; it is offered
as an option rather than taken, because a schema change on a shared production
table is not a thing to do unilaterally seventeen days before UI freeze.
