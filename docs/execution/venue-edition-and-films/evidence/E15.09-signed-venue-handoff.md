## 1. What only a human can do

All of it. A handoff is a conversation and a signature. What this document
contributes is the template, the four sentences that must be said out loud, and
the rehearsal that R-024 asks for.

## 2. The handoff record, one page per venue

| Field | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Venue account id | venue universe | never a name typed by hand |
| Signature date | the agreement | |
| Cleared payment date | the bank | **not** the invoice date, **not** signature |
| Plan | `founding` or `paid` | founding assigns a number |
| Founding number | `sponsors.founding_number` | read from the database, never from memory |
| Named contacts | the venue | who signs, who speaks to couples, who to email |
| Annual wedding count | the venue, **after signature** | sets the ceiling, never the price |
| Promised outside standard terms | verbatim | see section 4 |
| Configuration decisions | this handoff | feeds E15.10 |

## 3. The four sentences that are said out loud

These are ratified positions that a venue will otherwise learn later, at the
worst moment. Say them at handoff, unprompted.

1. **Entitlement.** Every couple who books their wedding with you gets a
   workspace, for as long as your licence is current. No seat count, no
   per-couple maths, nothing for your coordinator to track. (D-020)
2. **Survival.** Any workspace already redeemed keeps its full term plus
   Keepsake, whatever happens to the licence. Only new issuance stops.
   (D-020 point 2)
3. **Release.** If a couple cancels or moves venue, you can release the
   workspace, the couple keeps their content, and your name comes off within
   24 hours. (D-020 point 3)
4. **The wedding count.** This sets your issuance ceiling, it never sets your
   price, and it never changes at renewal. (D-020 point 4)

## 4. The verbatim column, and why it is the point of the whole task

Write down what was promised in the room, in the words used, before the memory
of it softens. Then do one of two things with each item: honour it, or correct
it with the venue **before configuration**. There is no third option, and an item
left in the column unresolved becomes a dispute at renewal.

Two things a founder is most likely to have promised that the product cannot do:

- **A logo in the couple's workspace.** D-027 point 3 fixes launch branding at
  the venue's name only. If it was promised, correct it now.
- **A venue login.** R-043: there is no venue-authenticated route. D-027 point 4
  makes the portal at launch invitation administration only.

## 5. The rehearsal (R-024)

- **Do:** run the whole handoff against a rehearsal venue before the first real
  one. Fill the template. Say the four sentences to an empty room. Configure the
  account. Time it.
- **Expect:** to find something. The rehearsal is worthless if it finds nothing,
  which usually means it was not run properly.
- **Record:** what broke, what took longer than expected, and what you did not
  know the answer to.
- **Result 2026-08-04:** **not run.** No rehearsal venue exists and no venue has
  signed.

## 6. The stop rules

- **Stop** if the cleared payment date is unknown. The founding number depends on
  it and D-009 point 6 assigns on cleared payment, explicitly not on signature.
- **Stop** if anything in the verbatim column is unresolved at configuration.
- **Stop** if the agreement does not carry the marketing-controls clause and the
  prohibited-claims list (R-020). It cannot be added afterwards.

## 8. What I would tell the founder

The handoff is the cheapest insurance in E15 and the one most likely to be
skipped, because on the day a venue says yes the last thing anyone wants is a
form. Fill it in anyway, on the call, while the venue is still on the phone.
Everything in section 3 is easier to say at handoff than to explain at renewal.
