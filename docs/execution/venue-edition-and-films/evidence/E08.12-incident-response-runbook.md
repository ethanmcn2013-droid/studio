# E08.12 — incident response runbook

**Task:** E08.12 · **Written:** 2026-08-03 · **Reviewed:** never — see §7
**Scope:** Signal Studio, the `app` and `studio` production deployments, and the
Turso databases behind them.

---

## §0 · The honest preamble

**There is one person.** Ethan McNamara is the responder for every scenario
below. There is no rota, no second contact, no paid on-call, no escalation path
and no monitoring service that will wake anyone. The budget is zero and stays
zero (D-015 Q5), and paging tooling costs money.

That is recorded here as an **accepted limitation**, not solved by writing it
down. Its practical consequences:

- Detection is whatever Sentry emails, plus whatever a venue or a couple tells
  you. Assume you find out late.
- Mean time to respond is "when Ethan next looks at a phone".
- If Ethan is unreachable, nothing happens. There is no answer to that today,
  and §6 asks for one.

**Nothing in this document is legal advice.** The Article 33 timings below are
taken from the text of the GDPR. No solicitor reviewed them (D-016). If a real
breach happens, the first call is to a solicitor, and this runbook's job is to
have preserved the evidence and stopped the bleeding before that call.

---

## §1 · The first five minutes, whatever it is

1. **Write down the time.** UTC. Every clock below runs from a moment you will
   otherwise misremember.
2. **Stop the bleeding before you understand it.** Containment beats diagnosis.
   A page taken down can go back up; data that continued leaking for an hour
   while you read logs cannot be recalled.
3. **Do not delete anything.** Not a log, not a row, not a deployment. If this
   turns out to be reportable, what you deleted is the evidence.
4. **Open one file** and keep a running timeline in it: what you saw, what you
   did, what time. This becomes the incident record and, if it is reportable,
   part of the notification.

## §2 · Breach affecting couple data

**Person:** Ethan McNamara · **Channel:** direct, and in writing to the couple ·
**Clock:** contain immediately · assess within 24 hours · **72 hours to notify
the DPC from the moment you become aware** (GDPR Article 33).

### What this looks like

One couple's workspace visible to another. A published page reachable that
should not be. A share link resolving after revocation. A venue seeing content
rather than counts. A guest list indexed by a search engine.

### Contain

```bash
# Unpublish a specific workspace, or revoke a specific share link — the
# smallest action that stops the disclosure.
# If the exposure is a whole surface rather than one workspace, take the
# surface down: promote a deployment without it (rollback §2), or set
# NEXT_PUBLIC_SIGNAL_LAUNCH_STATE=pre and redeploy to close the site.
```

- [ ] Disclosure stopped. Confirmed in incognito, from outside.
- [ ] Time of containment written down.

### Assess

- **Whose data?** The couple's own, or third parties named in it — guests,
  family, suppliers? The second is worse and is the more likely case, because a
  wedding workspace is mostly other people's names.
- **Any Article 9 data?** Dietary notes, allergies, accessibility requirements.
  These reveal health and routinely reveal religious belief. The shipped wedding
  template instructs couples to collect them (R-017), so assume yes until you
  have looked.
- **Any children's data?** Guest lists routinely contain it.
- **How many, and for how long?** Reconstruct from Vercel logs, not memory.

### Notify

- **The couple, first, in plain language.** What was exposed, to whom, for how
  long, what you have done, and what they can do. Not a template. Not "we take
  privacy seriously". They will forgive an early honest message and will not
  forgive a late polished one.
- **The venue, only if the venue is affected**, and never with couple content
  in the message.
- **The Data Protection Commission** at `dataprotection.ie` — the breach
  notification form is on that site — **within 72 hours of becoming aware**,
  unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the people involved.
  That judgement is a legal one and this document does not make it. If in doubt,
  notify: a notification that turns out to be unnecessary costs an afternoon.
- **Get a solicitor on the phone the same day.** D-016 means there is no
  retained one. That decision was made for the drafting of documents, not for
  the handling of a live breach.

## §3 · Breach affecting venue data

**Person:** Ethan McNamara · **Channel:** phone the venue, then confirm in
writing · **Clock:** contain immediately · venue told within 24 hours.

### What this looks like

One venue seeing another venue's numbers, agreement or founding position. The
Signal HQ password reaching a venue. A venue's own metrics reaching a couple.

### The specific one to fear

**The Signal HQ password is one shared secret that opens every HQ room** —
Today, Money, Company, Vault, Atlas, Decks, Founders Circle and the Data Room
(R-043, F-05). If it reaches a venue, or leaks, the blast radius is the founder's
entire operating surface, not one venue's view.

```
1. Rotate SIGNAL_HQ_PASSWORD in the studio Vercel project.
2. Redeploy studio. The v2 token is HMAC'd from a key derived from the
   password, so rotation invalidates every live session at once.
3. There is NO way to revoke one holder without rotating for everyone, and no
   audit trail that attributes any past action to a named person. Assume every
   HQ room was readable for the whole exposure window.
```

- [ ] Password rotated and studio redeployed
- [ ] Exposure window written down
- [ ] Venue told, by phone, before they find out another way

Venue data is commercial, not personal, so Article 33 usually does not apply —
but a venue's contact people are individuals, so check before assuming.

## §4 · Total outage

**Person:** Ethan McNamara · **Channel:** status note on the marketing site if
it is up · **Clock:** promote a previous deployment within 15 minutes.

```bash
vercel ls                            # find the last known-good deployment
vercel inspect <deployment-url>      # confirm the commit
vercel promote <deployment-url>
```

- [ ] Confirm which repository is down. `app` and `studio` are separate
      projects and separate deployments.
- [ ] Check whether the database is the cause rather than the code:
      `pnpm db:status -- --environment=production --confirm-production=tasks`
- [ ] If `TASKS_DATABASE_URL` is unset or wrong, the app **throws on boot** by
      design. That looks like a total outage and is a five-minute fix.
- [ ] Full procedure: `E08.12-rollback-procedure.md` §2.

An outage during planning is an inconvenience. An outage on a couple's wedding
week is not. If you know a sponsored wedding is within seven days, that couple
gets a message from you personally, whatever the cause.

## §5 · Silent data corruption

**Person:** Ethan McNamara · **Channel:** none until you know the scope ·
**Clock:** stop writes within the hour · scope before you restore.

This is the worst category because nothing alarms. It surfaces as a number that
looks wrong, a workspace missing a task, a venue's count that does not match
what they know.

```bash
# 1. Scope it. 27 checks in app, 21 in studio, each proven to catch a specific
#    corruption by creating it.
node scripts/db/integrity-check.mjs --environment=production        # app
node scripts/check-entitlements-integrity.mjs --environment=production  # studio

# 2. Do NOT restore yet. Establish when it started, or you will restore to a
#    point that is already corrupt.

# 3. Only when you know the window:
node scripts/db/restore-verify.mjs --backup=<path>   # prove the backup restores
#    then follow E08.12-rollback-procedure.md §3.
```

- [ ] Integrity check run, output saved
- [ ] Corruption window established from evidence, not from a guess
- [ ] Restore point chosen **before** the window opens
- [ ] Data loss window written down and, if any couple wrote in it, that couple
      told

**The audit ledger cannot be quietly repaired.** It is append-only at the
database level, enforced by triggers, and that is on purpose. If the ledger is
wrong, the correction is a new appended line, not an edit.

## §6 · A wrongly issued or wrongly expired entitlement

**Person:** Ethan McNamara · **Channel:** the couple directly, and the venue
only if the venue caused it · **Clock:** same day.

### A couple has lost access, or is about to

**Fix this first and ask questions afterwards.** A sponsored couple losing
Signal Studio before their wedding, in public, at the venue that gifted it, is
the single worst outcome this product can produce.

- Access is computed as `max(redemption + 548 days, wedding date + 90 days)`
  (D-022). It recomputes on a wedding-date change and **extends** — it must
  never shorten.
- If a couple is short, the wedding date is almost certainly missing or wrong.
  Set it correctly; the term recomputes.
- **Never** shorten a term to correct an administrative error.

### A code went to the wrong couple

- Do not revoke silently. Talk to the couple who has it.
- The couple keeps their content in Keepsake regardless.

### A founding number was assigned wrongly

- Withdraw it: `withdrawFoundingNumber` records the date and reason in the audit
  ledger and returns the place to the pool. It is never reused.
- **Two venues told they hold the same number is not recoverable.** The unique
  index makes it impossible at the database level. If it somehow happened, the
  conversation is with both venues, personally, the same day.

### A venue's licence lapsed

- Every already-redeemed couple keeps their full term plus Keepsake
  (D-020 point 2). Only new issuance stops.
- Venue branding and the venue's name come off released workspaces within 24
  hours.
- **No message to a couple may imply they can lose access because a venue did
  not pay.** They cannot, and saying it would make the promise look conditional.

## §7 · What has not been done, and it matters

- [ ] **This runbook has never been walked through as a tabletop.** The `data`
      release gate requires exactly that, and it has not happened. It is an
      hour: read §2 aloud, decide who you would call, find the DPC form, check
      you can actually log into Vercel from a phone.
- [ ] **The rollback in §4 has never been executed.** See
      `E08.12-rollback-procedure.md` §6.
- [ ] **There is no second contact.** Every scenario names one person. If Ethan
      is unreachable, no scenario has a responder. Naming a second person — even
      someone who can only take a phone call and post a status note — is the
      single cheapest improvement available to this document.
- [ ] **There is no alerting.** Sentry emails on a new error class. Nothing
      watches for a disclosure, a corrupted row, or a share link resolving after
      revocation. Detection is manual.
