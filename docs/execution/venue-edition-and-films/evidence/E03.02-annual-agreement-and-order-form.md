## 1. Acceptance criteria for E03.02

Written before drafting, per the audit-first rule.

1. A DRAFT status block heads the deliverable, naming what it is, what it is not, and
   carrying the E09.10 Answer 2 sentence verbatim.
2. The price is stated as €1,500 standard and €1,000 for the Founding 25, annually
   prepaid, both inclusive of VAT at the prevailing rate (D-021). No formulation that
   puts VAT on top of the price appears anywhere.
3. The founding lock is expressed only as covering the base annual Venue Edition
   agreement, held for as long as it renews continuously without a lapse (D-009 point 3,
   E02.03). No permanence wording appears in the operative text.
4. The initial term is one year, renewing on the same terms, matching what the live
   `/venues/questions` page already promises (D-023).
5. Change of control follows the property, survives rebrand and ownership change while
   the agreement runs continuously, and does not extend to additional properties a new
   owner brings (D-009 point 4, E02.05).
6. Entitlement carries no number: every couple with a signed booking at the venue, while
   the licence is current (D-020). The survival sentence appears without being asked for.
7. The couple access term is stated as eighteen months from the day the couple opens the
   workspace, or three months past the wedding, whichever is later (D-022). Never a bare
   figure.
8. A marketing-controls clause exists, with a named prohibited-claims list and a
   correction route (R-020).
9. Venue branding is the venue's name only (D-027 point 3).
10. Founder access is bounded exactly as D-009 point 5 and E02.09, and is stated not to
    be a retainer or an account manager.
11. Every clause that cannot be settled because E02.04, E02.06 or E02.07 remains backlog
    is carried as a visibly marked decision point, drafted to the safer reading, with the
    reading named and its reason given. None is chosen silently.
12. Every data-protection reference points at the E03.04 instrument rather than restating
    it.
13. The order form is a separate object from the agreement: it carries identity, price,
    place number, term rule and a signature block, and it references the agreement rather
    than repeating it.

## 3. Reading guide for the decision points

Eight decision points appear in the agreement, marked like this:

> **[ DECISION POINT n ]** the question · **Drafted to:** the reading taken ·
> **Why that reading:** the reason · **Source:** the brief that sets out the options.

Each one is drafted to the reading that costs the venue less, on the principle that an
error found later is correctable at renewal under D-023 if it favoured the venue, and is
a broken promise if it did not. Nothing is left blank except where the code or a ratified
decision forbids a number.

## 1. Who this is between

**1.1** This agreement is between:

**Signal Studio**, a business carried on by **Ethan McNamara** of Limerick, Ireland,
contactable at hello@signalstudio.ie ("Signal Studio", "we", "us"); and

**the venue named on the order form** ("you", "your venue").

**1.2** Signal Studio is a sole trader today and is not an incorporated company. If
Signal Studio incorporates, we will tell you in writing and this agreement transfers to
the company on the same terms. Nothing about your price, your term or your place changes
because of it.

**1.3** The order form, this agreement and any schedule attached to it are the whole of
what is agreed between us. They are listed in order of precedence at clause 18.

## 2. What Venue Edition is

**2.1** Venue Edition lets your venue give every couple who books a wedding with you a
private Signal Studio planning workspace, at no cost to the couple.

**2.2** You send an invitation. The couple opens the workspace themselves. From that
moment the workspace is theirs, not yours.

**2.3** You do not see inside a couple's planning at any time. You see invitations,
whether they were opened, and aggregate adoption figures with small numbers held back.
That boundary is a term of this agreement. How it is enforced, and what the limits of that
enforcement are, is set out in the data-protection instrument named in clause 13 rather
than claimed here.

## 3. What you get

**3.1 Who is covered.** Every couple who books their wedding with you gets a workspace,
for as long as your licence is current. There is no number in this agreement, nothing for
your coordinator to count and nothing to reconcile.

**3.2 What the couple gets.** A private planning workspace with your venue's name on it,
free to them, with the tools Signal Studio ships from time to time.

**3.3 How long the couple keeps it.** Eighteen months from the day the couple opens the
workspace, or three months past the wedding date, whichever is later. A long engagement
does not run out before the day.

**3.4 What happens to a couple if your agreement ends.** Every couple who has already
opened a workspace keeps it, for their full term, and keeps their Keepsake afterwards.
Only new invitations stop. A problem with your licence is never a couple's problem.

**3.5 Releasing a workspace.** If a couple cancels or moves to another venue, you can
release the workspace. The couple keeps their content. Your venue's name comes off within
24 hours.

**3.6 Your wedding count.** After you sign, we ask roughly how many weddings you hold in
a year. That sets an internal issuance ceiling that tells us when to look at an account.
It never sets your price and it never changes at renewal. If issuance passes the ceiling,
we are notified and invitations keep issuing.

**3.7 Keepsake.** When a couple's access term ends, their workspace becomes a free,
read-only Keepsake for as long as the service exists, with a one-click export the couple
owns. There is no storage promise attached to it. The couple-facing terms name the
Keepsake rules in full.

**3.8 Support.** A couple with a problem contacts Signal Studio, not you. There is no
admin panel for your team to run and no support queue for you to staff.

## 4. What you pay

**4.1 The rate.** €1,500 a year for your venue, paid in advance, for each twelve-month
term.

**4.2 VAT.** Your price is the amount you pay. VAT at the prevailing rate comes out of
it, not on top of it. Both the standard rate and the founding rate are inclusive of VAT
at the prevailing rate. Any other tax or duty that applies to your own business is yours.

**4.3 Whether Signal Studio is an accountable person for VAT has not been confirmed with
Revenue.** A written enquiry is prepared and has not yet been sent. If the answer changes
the VAT position, your price does not change, because your price is the amount you pay.

**4.4 If you are one of the Founding 25**, Schedule 1 replaces the rate in clause 4.1 with
€1,000 a year and sets out the conditions on which that rate is held. Schedule 1 prevails
over this clause.

**4.5 Invoicing.** We invoice you when your signed order form reaches us.

> **[ DECISION POINT 5 ]** How long does a venue have to pay the first invoice?
> **Drafted to:** payable within **14 days** of the invoice date.
> **Why that reading:** it is already the operative number elsewhere. E02.11 mechanics
> section 3 holds a signed venue's place for 14 days from the invoice date, so a
> different payment period would put two numbers in the same fortnight that disagree.
> The live proposal copy at `src/lib/venue-proposal.ts:284` already tells a venue
> *"I will invoice you, and the place stays yours while the invoice is with you."*
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.07-prepayment-invoicing-and-renewal-dates.md` section 7,
> recommendation 3. Not ratified.

**4.6 Payment must be the exact amount of your plan.** A payment that is not the plan
amount is not treated as payment of it, and we will come back to you rather than apply
it.

## 5. Your term

**5.1 The initial term is one year.** It runs for 365 days. If it is renewed, it renews
for further one-year terms on the same terms.

**5.2** For the first three founding agreements, the one-year initial term and the
renewable-on-the-same-terms wording are a deliberate commercial guardrail. They exist so
that a drafting error found early is correctable at the first renewal rather than fixed
in place for years. This is stated openly rather than left as internal reasoning.

> **[ DECISION POINT 6 ]** When does the term start?
> **Drafted to:** the day your payment clears.
> **Why that reading:** it is one indisputable date, it is the same event that assigns a
> founding number under D-009 point 6, and it is what the code already records. The
> alternative, starting the term when the venue is first able to issue an invitation,
> creates a disputable date judged by the person who benefits from calling it early,
> and R-024 records that the first onboarding is unrehearsed.
> **Cost of this reading, stated rather than hidden:** a venue that pays in September and
> is not configured until October has paid for a month it could not use.
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.07-prepayment-invoicing-and-renewal-dates.md` section 6,
> option A. Not ratified.

**5.3 You are told your renewal date on the day you pay**, in writing, so you have it a
full year in advance.

**5.4 Renewal is not automatic.** There is no automatic charge and no stored card for
Venue Edition. Your agreement renews when you are invoiced and you pay. This is stated
plainly because the general Signal Studio terms say otherwise and are wrong for a venue
today. See clause 18.2.

**5.5 Renewal notices.**

> **[ DECISION POINT 1 ]** How much notice before a term ends, and how many notices?
> **Drafted to:** a **marked blank**. The clause below reads
> *"We will write to you [NOTICE PERIOD] before your term ends, with the renewal invoice
> and the date it is due."*
> **Why a blank rather than a number:** `src/lib/venue-billing.ts:98`
> (`renewalPolicyPublicationRefusal`) refuses publication of the proposed numbers in an
> agreement, an order form, a renewal email or the portal while
> `VENUE_RENEWAL_POLICY.ratified` is `false`. Writing a number here would make it binding
> by writing it down, which is exactly what that refusal exists to prevent.
> **What is proposed and not approved:** 60 days and again at 30 days, both recorded as
> delivered.
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.04-continuous-renewal-and-lapse.md` section 6. Not ratified.

**5.6 If a renewal is late.**

> **[ DECISION POINT 2 ]** How long after a term ends before the agreement counts as
> lapsed, and does anything visible change during that window?
> **Drafted to:** a **marked blank** for the period, and to the safer reading on
> behaviour: **nothing visible changes** during it. Invitations keep issuing, your name
> stays where it is, and your rate holds.
> **Why that reading on behaviour:** D-020 point 2 already establishes that a licence
> problem is never a couple's problem. Turning invitations off during a grace window
> punishes couples who booked a wedding for a delay in a finance inbox, which applies the
> same principle one step earlier.
> **What is proposed and not approved:** 60 days.
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.04-continuous-renewal-and-lapse.md` section 6. Not ratified.

**5.7 What a lapse is.**

> **[ DECISION POINT 3 ]** Is a lapse the passing of a date, or a decision recorded on or
> after that date?
> **Drafted to:** a **recorded decision**, taken on or after the end of the late-payment
> window, with the venue told before it happens rather than afterwards. Drafted with the
> further condition that **the founding rate is not lost where Signal Studio cannot show
> the renewal notice was delivered**.
> **Why that reading:** renewal notices are sent by one person by hand and there is no
> email sender in the studio repository. A rule that costs a venue €500 a year for a step
> Signal Studio failed to take is indefensible, and this condition closes that case
> without weakening the rule in any case where the notice did go out.
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.04-continuous-renewal-and-lapse.md` section 6, recommendations
> 3 and 4. Not ratified.

**5.8 The clause as it reads today, with the blanks in place.**

> **Renewal.** Your agreement runs for twelve months from the day your payment clears. We
> will write to you [NOTICE PERIOD] before it ends, with the renewal invoice and the date
> it is due.
>
> **If a renewal is late.** Your agreement does not end on the renewal date. You have a
> further [GRACE PERIOD], and during that time nothing changes: your couples keep
> receiving invitations, your name stays where it is, and your rate holds.
>
> **If it is not paid.** After that, the agreement can end, and a founding rate ends with
> it. We will tell you before that happens, not afterwards. If we cannot show that we sent
> you the renewal notice, your rate is not affected.
>
> **If your agreement ends.** Every couple who has already opened a workspace keeps it,
> for their full term, with their Keepsake. Only new invitations stop.

**5.9 Renewal invoicing.**

> **[ DECISION POINT 7 ]** When is the renewal invoice issued and how long is it payable?
> **Drafted to:** issued with the first renewal notice, **payable within 30 days**.
> **Why that reading:** 30 days is ordinary practice for Irish business-to-business
> invoicing, and there is no place to protect at renewal, so the 14-day figure that exists
> to match the place hold has no work to do here. If the proposed 60-day first notice is
> approved, the invoice is in your hands two months before the money is due.
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.07-prepayment-invoicing-and-renewal-dates.md` section 7,
> recommendation 4. Not ratified.

## 6. What your price does not cover

> **[ DECISION POINT 4 ]** Does the agreement state what the price does not include, and
> at what length?
> **Drafted to:** a **short list of four exclusions plus a tax line**, rather than a full
> exclusion schedule or silence.
> **Why that reading:** every item below is either already decided elsewhere or is
> something a venue would otherwise reasonably assume. None of them is a charge Signal
> Studio intends to make, which is what keeps the list short. A page of exclusions on a
> €1,000 agreement reads as a product with hidden costs, and silence means the first
> correction a venue hears is the first time it feels sold to.
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.06-what-the-price-does-not-include.md` section 5, option A.
> Not ratified.

**6.1 What your price covers.** The annual Venue Edition agreement, for one venue, for
twelve months. Everything in clause 3 is in it at no extra charge.

**6.2 What it does not cover.**

1. **A second venue.** One place, one property. A group with three venues takes three
   agreements, at the same rate each while places remain.
2. **Work built for one venue.** Your requests are logged and shape what gets built.
   Nothing is built for a single venue, and bespoke work sits outside this agreement.
3. **Anything anyone else charges you.** Your own bank or card fees, your own internet
   and devices, and any other service you choose to use alongside Venue Edition.
4. **Printed materials beyond the ones we send.** Reprints, extra printed collateral and
   signage are not included.

**6.3 Tax.** As clause 4.2.

**6.4 If we ever sell something else.** If Signal Studio later offers a product that is
materially separate from Venue Edition, it is priced separately, and a held rate under
Schedule 1 does not cover it. Nothing like that exists today.

**6.5 A couple's own purchases.** A couple who buys a Signal Studio plan of their own does
so on their own terms. You are not charged for it and you receive nothing from it.

## 7. Ending the agreement

**7.1 If you decide not to continue**, do nothing. Your agreement ends at the end of the
year you have paid for, and you keep the service until then.

> **[ DECISION POINT 8 ]** What is refunded, in each direction?
> **Drafted to:** three rules. (a) A venue that simply does not renew owes nothing and is
> owed nothing. (b) A venue that ends its agreement part way through a term it has already
> paid for is **not refunded** for that term and keeps the service to the end of it.
> (c) **If Signal Studio withdraws Venue Edition or stops operating during a term you have
> paid for, the unused part of that term is refunded pro rata.**
> **Why that reading:** (c) is the clause that makes taking a year's money up front
> defensible. It costs nothing while nothing has been sold, it is close to what the live
> `/terms` page already promises, and it is the honest counterweight to R-013, which
> records that there is no professional indemnity behind any of this. (b) is the ordinary
> consequence of annual prepayment and is stated so it is not discovered later.
> **Source:** `evidence/E02.07-prepayment-invoicing-and-renewal-dates.md` section 7,
> recommendation 5. Not ratified.

**7.2 If either of us breaks this agreement** and does not put it right within 30 days of
being asked in writing, the other can end it by written notice.

**7.3 Whatever ends this agreement**, clause 3.4 holds. Every couple who has already
opened a workspace keeps it for their full term and keeps their Keepsake.

**7.4 If Signal Studio stops offering Venue Edition**, we will tell every venue in
writing, we will honour clause 3.4, and the refund position in decision point 8 applies.

## 8. What you agree to do

**8.1** Give a workspace only to a couple with a signed booking at your venue.

**8.2** Keep the invitation route to yourself and your own staff. Invitation links are not
for publication and not for resale.

**8.3** Describe the product the way clause 12 requires.

**8.4** Tell us when something is wrong. Signal Studio is one person, and a venue that
stays quiet about a problem gets a worse product.

## 9. Your name in the couple's workspace

**9.1** Your venue's name appears at the top of each sponsored workspace, in one quiet
line. The couple's plan stays the thing in focus.

**9.2** Nothing else of yours appears. No logo, no mark, no text you write, and no shared
branding of any kind. This is the position at launch and it is stated here so it is not
inferred from anything else you have been shown.

**9.3** No sales asset, page, film or email may say otherwise. If you have been told
otherwise anywhere, this clause is what is true.

## 10. Access to Ethan

**10.1** One 30-minute call per venue per year, plus an email route that reaches Ethan
directly. Email gets a reply within one working day.

**10.2** That is what it is: one call a year and an email address. It is not a retainer,
not an account manager and not a standing arrangement. Signal Studio is one person, and
the boundary is what keeps the reply times honest across every venue that has one.

**10.3** Founding venues have the same access under Schedule 1. It is set out there so it

## 11. If your venue is sold or changes hands

**11.1** Your rate follows the property, not the company. If the venue is sold, rebranded,
or changes operator, the agreement and the rate stay with the venue for as long as the
agreement keeps running without a break.

**11.2** It does not travel to other properties a new owner already holds, or later
acquires. One place, one property.

**11.3** Tell us within 30 days of a change of ownership or operator so the record matches
the venue.

## 12. How you describe Venue Edition

**12.1 Why this clause exists.** Signal Studio takes care with what it promises couples. A
claim Signal Studio never made is still a claim somebody has to keep, and a claim made by
a venue to a couple folds into that venue's own contract with the couple. This clause is
here from version one for that reason.

**12.2 The approved copy pack.** You describe Venue Edition to couples using the approved
copy pack we supply, on your website, in your brochure and at a show-around. If you want
to say something the pack does not cover, ask and we will write it with you.

**12.3 What must never be said.** Not by you, not by your staff, and not in anything
printed with your name on it:

1. That the workspace, the Keepsake or the service lasts **forever**, is for life, is

2. That anything about it is **guaranteed**.
3. Any storage promise, or any claim that content will be kept for a stated period beyond
   what the couple-facing terms say.
4. Any claim that the product complies with the GDPR, is certified, is audited, or has
   been approved by any authority.
5. Any claim that a solicitor, an accountant or any professional adviser reviewed, drafted

6. Any claim that your venue has a protected area, or that no other venue near you can be

7. Any price other than the one in your order form, and any statement that VAT is added on

**12.4 Correction.** If we tell you in writing that something you have published is
outside this clause, you correct or remove it within **10 working days**. If it is on a
printed item already in circulation, you stop issuing it and correct the next print run.

**12.5 If it is not corrected**, we can suspend new invitations until it is, and clause
7.2 is available. We would rather send you the right wording.

**12.6 No protected area.** Signal Studio does not give any venue a protected area. Other
venues near you, including venues you compete with, can be founding venues too. This is
said before you sign, and it is never something you discover afterwards.

**12.7 Your name in our marketing** is a separate, written consent under Schedule 1

## 13. Data protection

**13.1** Signal Studio and your venue each handle personal data about couples, and the
split of responsibility between us is set out in the **Venue Edition data-protection
instrument (E03.04)**, which is attached to this agreement and forms part of it.

**13.2 This agreement does not restate that instrument**, on purpose. If the two are ever
read together, the instrument is the operative text on every data-protection question, and
this clause exists so that the two documents cannot drift into saying different things.

**13.3** The instrument covers, and this agreement does not: the role each of us holds for
each processing operation; the joint arrangement covering invitation and redemption and
where it ends; the fact that a couple can bring a data-protection request to either of us
and neither can point at the other; the list of suppliers involved and what each one does;
the security position; and what each of us does if there is a personal-data breach.

**13.4** Two things are stated here as well as there, because they are commercial promises
and not only legal ones. **No workspace content passes to your venue at any time.** And
**the aggregate figures you see have small numbers held back**, so a figure can never point
at one couple, including where you would be able to work out who it was.

**13.5** Signal Studio does not claim compliance with the GDPR, and holds no certificate,
audit or approval from any authority. Neither may you.

## 14. What Signal Studio does not promise

**14.1** That the service exists indefinitely. Venue Edition is a service, and clause 7.4
says what happens if it stops.

**14.2** That any document supplied under this agreement was reviewed by a solicitor or an

financial or VAT position.

**14.3** That professional indemnity or cyber insurance is in place. There is no policy
today. We will tell you the day there is one rather than wait to be asked.

**14.4** Uninterrupted service. We aim for the service to be available and we will tell you

## 5. Open legal points

These are the brief for the professional review D-023 funds at €3,000 of cleared founding
revenue. Each is a question, not a finding.

1. Is a written promise to 25 businesses of a rate held indefinitely on continuous
   renewal, with no expiry clause, enforceable as drafted under Irish law, and what
   happens to it on assignment or insolvency?
2. Does clause 15's cap at one year's fees survive on a contract whose largest realistic
   exposure is a personal-data claim rather than a commercial one, and is a cap of that
   size enforceable at all against a data-protection liability?
3. Does clause 11's rule that the rate follows the property rather than the company work
   in Irish law where a venue is sold by share transfer rather than by asset transfer, and
   does clause 17.2 accidentally block the transfer clause 11 intends to allow?
4. Does stating a price as inclusive of VAT, before Revenue has confirmed whether Signal
   Studio is an accountable person, create an exposure the agreement should disclose
   rather than absorb?
5. Is clause 12 enforceable as a marketing-controls clause against a venue's own
   commercial practices under the Consumer Protection Act 2007, and does clause 12.5's
   suspension remedy create a risk that suspending invitations breaches clause 3.1?
6. Is the venue a joint controller of adoption analytics, or an independent controller of
   the copy it receives, and does clause 13's pointer to E03.04 correctly leave that
   question where the instrument decides it?
7. May a venue reshare a couple's published page in its own marketing? If yes, the venue
   is an independent controller for that republication and clause 12 has to say so.
8. Does clause 18.2's precedence over the published general terms actually displace them
   for a venue, given that the venue's own staff will also be users of Signal Studio
   products under those terms?
