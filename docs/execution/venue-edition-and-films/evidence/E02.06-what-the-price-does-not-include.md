# E02.06 decision brief — what the price does not include, and why this task's title is out of date

**Prepared:** 2026-08-04, Wave 3 (WP-18). **Decision owner:** Ethan McNamara.
**Blocks:** E03.02 (the annual agreement), E03.03 (the founding schedule).
**Status:** open. Recommendation below. Nothing applied, no state written.

**Drafted under D-016.** No solicitor and no accountant has read this, and none
will. Nothing here is a legal opinion, and nothing in it claims a confirmed VAT
position.

---

## 1. The trap in the title, stated first

The task reads *"Exclude VAT, taxes, optional future add-ons, third-party
charges and materially separate products from the locked base price."*

**The first word cannot be done any more.** D-021, approved 2026-08-03, ratified
that €1,000 and €1,500 are both **VAT-inclusive**: *"Every agreement, order
form, invoice and page states the price as inclusive of VAT at the prevailing
rate."* It explicitly supersedes the VAT-exclusive drafting rule that R-018
proposed and that this title was written from.

The machine contract already carries the ratified position:

```json
"vat": {
  "basis": "inclusive",
  "publicStatement": "inclusive of VAT at the prevailing rate",
  "accountablePersonStatus": "unconfirmed"
}
```

So writing "the price excludes VAT" into any agreement would now contradict an
approved decision, the machine contract and four live venue-facing pages.

**The title is not rewritten.** Imported titles never are. The mismatch is
recorded here and belongs in a change request against E02.06's title, alongside
the equivalent one already raised for E03.12 under D-016.

**What the task can still legitimately do**, and it is worth doing: state what
the price does **not** include. Optional future add-ons, third-party charges,
materials, materially separate products, and any tax other than the VAT that is
already inside the number. That is the remaining four fifths of the title and it
is entirely unwritten.

## 2. Two different statements, currently confused

The title runs together two things that are not the same.

**Scope of the lock.** What the held €1,000 rate covers. **Already written**, in
`FOUNDING_25_BENEFITS_CHARTER.md:37`: *"Products that are materially separate
from Venue Edition are not covered by it."* Ratified through E02.03 and encoded
as `lockScope: "base_annual_agreement"`.

**Scope of supply.** What the €1,000 or €1,500 actually buys this year.
**Nowhere.** Verified:


Not one venue-facing surface says where the price stops. The charter says where
the *lock* stops. A venue reading it does not draw that distinction, and neither
did this task's title.

## 3. What is already promised as included

Any exclusion has to survive this list, because these are already published.

From `FOUNDING_25_BENEFITS_CHARTER.md` "What every venue gets, founding or not",
and from the live `/venues` page:

- Every couple who books a wedding gets a workspace, unlimited, while the licence
  is current (D-020).
- Each couple keeps it for 18 months from opening, or 3 months past the wedding,
  whichever is later (D-022).
- A redeemed workspace survives whatever happens to the licence, with Keepsake.
- The venue's name at the top of the workspace. Name only (D-027 point 3).
- The activation record. No admin panel, nothing for the venue's team to run.
- Support for couples goes to Signal Studio, not to the venue.

And for the Founding 25 specifically: the number, one 30-minute call a year plus
an email route, first look at new work, requests logged on the roadmap, and the
name on the founding page and map plus a numbered certificate.

**Every exclusion proposed in §5 was checked against this list. None of them
removes anything on it.**

## 4. The two options

### Option A. A short list of what the price does not include, in the agreement and the charter (recommended)

Four lines in the agreement, and the same four in plain English in the Benefits
Charter so the venue reads them before signature rather than after.

**Cost.** A venue reads four sentences about limits at the point of sale. That
is a real cost in a document whose job is to build trust, and it is the reason
to keep the list to four rather than fourteen.

### Option B. A full exclusion schedule in the agreement

Standard commercial drafting. Comprehensive, and it covers cases nobody has
thought of.

**Cost.** At €1,000 a year, a page of exclusions reads as a product with hidden
costs, which is the opposite of the thing being sold. It also invites the venue
to ask what each excluded item costs, and the honest answer to most of them is
that the product does not exist, which is a worse conversation than not having
raised it.

### Option C. Say nothing. Leave the charter's lock sentence to do the work

Cheapest, and it is the status quo.

**Cost.** The charter sentence is about the lock. A venue that assumes a printed
keepsake, a second property, or a bespoke integration is included has read
nothing that says otherwise, and the first time it is corrected is the first
time it feels sold to.

## 5. Recommendation

**Option A.** Four exclusions plus one clarification. Wording below is
venue-facing and written to brand voice.

> **What your price covers.** The annual Venue Edition agreement, for one venue,
> for twelve months. Everything in the charter under "what every venue gets" is
> in it, at no extra charge.
>
> **What it does not cover.**
>
> 1. **A second venue.** One place, one property. A group with three venues takes
>    three agreements, at the same rate each while places remain.
> 2. **Work built for one venue.** Requests are logged and shape the roadmap.
>    Nothing is built for a single venue, and bespoke work is outside the
>    agreement.
> 3. **Anything anyone else charges you.** Your own bank or card fees, your own
>    internet and devices, and any other service you choose to use alongside
>    Venue Edition.
> 4. **Printed materials beyond the ones we send.** Your certificate is sent
>    once. Reprints, extra printed collateral and signage are not included.
>
> **Tax.** Your price is the amount you pay. VAT at the prevailing rate comes out
> of it, not on top of it. Any other tax or duty that applies to your business is
> yours.
>
> **If we ever sell something else.** If Signal Studio later offers a product
> that is materially separate from Venue Edition, it is priced separately and
> your held rate does not cover it. Nothing like that exists today.

**One clarification, not an exclusion, and worth its own line:** a couple who
buys a Signal Studio plan of their own does so on their own terms. The venue is
not charged for it and does not receive anything from it. This closes a real
ambiguity in a sponsored model and costs nothing to say.

**Reasoning.** Every item on the list is either already decided elsewhere (one
place per property, nothing built for one venue) or is a thing a venue would
otherwise reasonably assume. None of them is a charge Signal Studio intends to
levy, which is what keeps the list short and honest. Item 3 and the tax line are
the two that stop a €1,000 price being read as covering more than it does. The
"if we ever sell something else" line is the charter's existing lock sentence
translated into something a venue can act on, and it names no product that does
not exist.

## 6. The edge nobody has decided, raised rather than answered

**The personalised film.** The Limerick First founding invitation film includes
per-venue personalisation and cohort renders (E13 `personalisation_pipeline`,
`cohort_1_renders`). A venue that receives a film with its own name in it during
outreach may reasonably assume it can use that film on its own website and
social channels.

Nothing anywhere states whether it can. That matters twice over: it is a
question about scope of supply, which is this task, and it is a question about
what 25 untrained venues publish about the product, which is R-020.

**This brief does not answer it.** It needs an answer before Cohort 1 is
contacted, because the film goes out before the agreement does.
