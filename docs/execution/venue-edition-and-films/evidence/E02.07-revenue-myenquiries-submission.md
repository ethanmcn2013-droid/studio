# Revenue MyEnquiries submission — VAT position (E02.07, R-018)

**Send this. It is free, and it is the one place in this project where you can
get a written answer from an actual authority at zero cost.**

How: sign in to ROS or myAccount → **MyEnquiries** → *Add a new Enquiry* →
Enquiry relates to: **VAT** → More specifically: **VAT registration** (if you
are not yet registered) or **General VAT queries** (if you are).

Before sending, fill the three bracketed placeholders in section 1. Everything
else is ready.

> **Two things to know.** Revenue answers on the facts you give, so the facts
> below are stated precisely and completely — a vague enquiry gets a vague
> reply. And their answer applies to the facts as stated, which is why the
> service list is exhaustive rather than "and others". Attach their reply to
> E02.07 as evidence when it comes back.

## Subject line

```
VAT — accountable person status, prepayment tax point, and OSS for a pre-revenue Irish software business
```

---

## Enquiry body — copy from here

```
Dear Revenue,

I am seeking guidance on my VAT position before I begin trading. I am
pre-revenue and have not yet issued an invoice, so I would like to establish
the correct treatment before contracts are signed rather than afterwards.

1. MY SITUATION

I operate [SOLE TRADER / A LIMITED COMPANY — state which; if a company, give the
registered name and CRO number]. I am based in [COUNTY], Ireland. My tax
reference number is [PPSN / TAX REFERENCE].

I am not currently registered for VAT.

I am building a software service (a wedding-planning application). I intend to
sell it on two lines:

(a) A business line. Irish wedding venues pay an annual subscription in advance
    for the right to gift the software to couples who book weddings with them.
    The price is EUR 1,000 or EUR 1,500 per venue per year, stated to the
    customer as inclusive of VAT. Payment is taken up front for a twelve-month
    period. I expect up to 25 venues in the first year, so approximately
    EUR 25,000 in the first twelve months, and the payments would arrive in a
    compressed window of a few months rather than evenly across the year.

(b) A consumer line. Individual consumers may buy subscriptions directly at
    approximately EUR 12 per month, EUR 89 once off, or EUR 100 per year. These
    customers may be in Ireland or in other EU member states. Volumes in the
    first year are expected to be small but I cannot predict them precisely.

I purchase business services from suppliers established outside Ireland. To the
best of my knowledge the current list is: Vercel Inc (hosting, United States),
Clerk Inc (authentication, United States), Turso / ChiselStrike Inc (database,
United States), Stripe (payments, Ireland/United States), Resend (email, United
States), Sentry / Functional Software Inc (error monitoring, United States),
Upstash Inc (caching, United States), and Anthropic PBC (AI services, United
States). These are business-to-business supplies of services received by me in
Ireland. Current spend across these is in the region of EUR 100 to EUR 300 per
month and will rise as the business grows.

2. MY QUESTIONS

Question 1 — Am I already obliged to register?
Does my receipt of business-to-business services from suppliers established
outside the State, where the place of supply is Ireland under the general B2B
place-of-supply rule, make me an accountable person obliged to register for VAT
and to self-account under the reverse charge, irrespective of my own turnover
and irrespective of the registration thresholds for supplies I make? If so, from
what point does that obligation arise, and does it also mean that VAT becomes
chargeable on my own supplies from the outset?

Question 2 — When does VAT fall due on an annual prepayment?
Where a customer pays in advance for a service supplied over the following
twelve months, when does the tax point arise? Is VAT due in full in the VAT
period in which the payment is received, or is it spread across the period over
which the service is delivered?

Question 3 — Do the two lines aggregate for threshold purposes?
For the purpose of the registration thresholds for supplies of services, are the
business line in 1(a) and the consumer line in 1(b) aggregated into a single
turnover figure, or assessed separately?

Question 4 — Do I need to use OSS?
For the consumer subscriptions supplied to customers in other EU member states,
do these fall within the rules for telecommunications, broadcasting and
electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons, and does the EUR 10,000
threshold for cross-border supplies of such services apply to me? If I exceed
it, is the One Stop Shop the correct mechanism, and can I opt in before I exceed
it?

Question 5 — VAT-inclusive pricing on a fixed multi-year price.
I intend to quote my prices to venues as inclusive of VAT, and I intend to
guarantee the first 25 venues that their price will not increase for as long as
they remain customers. If I am or become obliged to charge VAT, I understand
that I would account for VAT out of that inclusive price. Is that correct, and
is there anything in that arrangement I should be aware of — in particular if
the standard rate of VAT were to change during the life of those agreements?

Question 6 — Correct rate.
What is the correct VAT rate for a software subscription supplied
electronically, on both the business and the consumer lines?

3. WHAT I WOULD FIND MOST USEFUL

If registration is already required under Question 1, I would be grateful to
know that clearly, as it changes the pricing I put in front of customers before
I sign any agreement.

Thank you for your assistance.

[YOUR NAME]
[CONTACT EMAIL]
[PHONE]
```

## What this does not cover

This is a VAT enquiry. It does not touch corporation tax, revenue recognition on
deferred income, or the CRO annual return — all of which are also unadvised
under D-016, and none of which Revenue will answer in a VAT enquiry. The
deferred-income schedule (one row per venue, revenue released monthly) is
recorded as a separate item under R-014 and is built before the first invoice.
