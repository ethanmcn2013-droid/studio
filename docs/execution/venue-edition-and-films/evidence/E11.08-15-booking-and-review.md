# Part 1 · E11.08 · The booking flow and meeting-confirmation sequence

## 1. What this is, and what it is not

This is the stretch between a venue replying and a venue sitting on a call. It
starts the moment somebody writes back and ends the moment the walkthrough
begins. It is a small piece of work that decides whether the expensive pieces
either side of it, the films and the proposal, ever get used.

It is not the discovery structure (E11.09). It is not the walkthrough itself
(E11.10). It is not the proposal or the order form (E11.11). It is not the
objection library (E11.12). Those four are still in the backlog. This document
defines the interface to each of them and writes none of them.

**Where it sits in the life of a place.** The states below are taken verbatim
from `studio/docs/strategy/FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS.md` §3 rather than
invented here. E11.01 and E11.02 should adopt the same words when the CRM stages
get built, so the register, the mechanics document and the review all agree.

```
Researched → Invited → Replied → Booked → Walkthrough held → Proposed → Held
                                                                          │
                                                          Signed → Paid → Onboarded
```

**Replied** and **Booked** are new. They sit between Invited and Proposed, and
they are the only two states this document owns. Nothing before Replied and
nothing after Walkthrough held is decided here.

## 2. The route a venue uses to book. Founder decision FD-A.

Three routes were considered against a zero budget, a UI freeze on 2026-08-20
and a copy freeze on 2026-08-21.

| Route | What it costs | What it risks |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Reply and pick a slot.** Ethan offers three specific times in the reply. The venue picks one. Ethan sends a calendar invite with a video link. | One extra email each way. No new tool, no new page, no new third party. | One additional round trip per venue. |
| **(b) A free scheduling link.** A booking page on a free tier, dropped into the reply. | Nothing in money. A third party receives the venue contact's name and email, which needs a stated position and a line in the notice. A booking page is a commercial surface and has to clear copy freeze. | A new processor and a new public surface, both added inside three weeks of freeze. |
| **(c) A booking page on signalstudio.ie.** Built in-house. | Real build time against a UI freeze on 2026-08-20. | Competes directly with product work that is release-blocking. Booking is not. |

**Recommendation: (a), for Cohort 1.** The constraint on this project is founder
time in the call, not founder time arranging the call. A venue that has watched
its own film and asked for a conversation is worth one more email. Route (a) adds
no third party, no page before freeze and no new privacy disclosure, and it is
the only one of the three that can be running tomorrow.

**Review it after Cohort 1, on this trigger and no other:** if more than one
venue in a cohort asks for a call and then fails to land on a time within two
exchanges, the booking step is losing meetings and route (b) earns its
disclosure work. Until that happens, it does not.

**What route (a) assumes.** Ethan has a working calendar that can send an invite
with a video link, at no cost. Nothing in this document provisions one.

## 3. One meeting, not two. Founder decision FD-C.

The film ends on a walkthrough call to action (D-014 clause 1) and the landing
page carries the price. A venue that books has usually already seen the number.
Discovery as a separate first meeting would spend a second slot of a venue
owner's attention to learn things the first ten minutes of the walkthrough
surfaces anyway.

**Recommendation: one call, 30 minutes.** Discovery folds into its opening.
E11.09 writes the questions that go in those opening minutes. E11.10 writes what
happens after them. This document books the 30 minutes and gets the right people
into it.

**A naming collision to head off now.** D-009 clause 5 gives every founding venue
one 30-minute founder call per year as a benefit of the programme. That is a
different thing from the 30-minute sales walkthrough. They must never be counted
against each other, and no confirmation email should describe the sales call in
words that make it sound like the benefit being spent.

## 4. Calendar rules and capacity. Founder decision FD-B.

The founder is the constraint (R-006). A cohort of 25 that lands well can eat a
build week, and the build week is release-blocking while the calls are not.

**Recommendation:**

- **Six venue calls a week, maximum**, in two blocks of three on set days.
- **15 minutes of buffer** after each call. The follow-up gets written while the
  call is still fresh, which is also what E11.11 depends on.
- **No calls on a cohort send day.** Sending 25 emails and taking calls on the
  same day means neither gets attention.
- **Overflow is offered the following week**, in writing, with two specific
  times. A venue told "next week, Tuesday or Thursday" waits happily. A venue
  told "I will come back to you" does not.

More demand than the ceiling is a good problem, and the weekly review records it
as one rather than quietly absorbing it.

## 5. The sequence

Six messages, at most. Every one of them is short.

| Step | When | Message |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The venue replies asking to talk | **The slot offer.** Three specific times. |
| 2 | Same day as the venue picks | **The confirmation**, plus the calendar invite. |
| 3 | The day before | **The reminder.** Two lines. |
| 4 | The call | Owned by E11.09 and E11.10. |
| 5 | Same day as the call | Handover to E11.11. This document ends here. |
| R | Any time | **Reschedule** or **missed call**, below. |

### Step 1 · The slot offer

```
Subject: A 30 minute walkthrough

Thanks for coming back to me.

Thirty minutes is enough to show you the whole thing working. I share my
screen and walk one wedding from the booking through to the day, and you
tell me whether it would help your couples.

Three times that work at my end:

  Tuesday 12 August, 10:00
  Wednesday 13 August, 14:30
  Thursday 14 August, 09:30

Tell me which one and I will send the invite. If none of them suit, give
me two that do.

Ethan
Signal Studio
```

Rules for this message. Three times, never a vague offer of availability. Real
dates, inside the next seven working days. No attachment. No second pitch: the
film already did that, and repeating it here reads as nerves.

### Step 2 · The confirmation

```
Subject: Confirmed, Wednesday 13 August at 14:30

That is in the diary. The invite has the video link in it.

Thirty minutes. I share my screen and walk one wedding from booking to
the day. No slides.

Two things worth saying before we talk:

If price is likely to come up, it helps to have whoever signs off on
spend on the call. If that is not practical, no problem, I will put the
numbers in writing afterwards.

Bring one upcoming wedding you are happy to talk about in general terms.
The shape of it is all I need. No names, no couple details.

If you need to move it, reply here.

Ethan
```

Three jobs, and it does all three without sounding like it is doing any of them.
It sets the length so the call does not overrun. It raises the approval question
early, in a way a coordinator can answer without embarrassment. And it asks for
nothing about a real couple beyond the shape of a wedding, which is the first
time the venue sees the privacy posture behave rather than be described.

**The last line is a rule, not a flourish.** No document, invite or confirmation
in this sequence tells a venue that its data, or a couple's data, is compliant,
approved or reviewed. If the subject comes up, section 8 governs.

### The calendar invite

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Title | `Signal Studio · Venue Edition walkthrough · <venue name>` |
| Length | 30 minutes |
| Location | Video link |
| Description | Two lines. What the 30 minutes covers, and the line about whoever signs off on spend. Nothing else. |

No marketing copy in a calendar entry. It sits in somebody's week for days and
its job is to say what the meeting is.

### Step 3 · The reminder

```
Subject: Tomorrow at 14:30

Still good for tomorrow at 14:30. The video link is in the invite.

Thirty minutes, screen share, one wedding start to finish. If tomorrow
no longer works, reply and we will move it.

Ethan
```

One reminder, the day before. Never two. Never on the morning of the call, which
reads as chasing.

### Reschedule

```
Subject: Moved to Thursday 14 August at 09:30

Moved. New invite sent.

Ethan
```

Rescheduling is free and is never treated as a signal of anything. A venue owner
in August has weddings on.

### Missed call

Wait ten minutes on the call, then send this and leave.

```
Subject: Missed you today

I was on at 14:30 and did not want to leave you chasing it. These
things happen in a venue.

If you still want to see it, name a time and I will send a new invite.

Ethan
```

**Second missed call: stop.** Mark the account `later` and leave it alone. That
is the same posture as the D-013 stopping rule, applied to meetings instead of
emails. Nobody is pursued into a third attempt.

## 6. The branches that are not a booking

| The venue says | What happens |
|---|---|
| "Just send me the information." | Not a booking. Hands to E11.11 for the written route, and the account stays at Replied. It does not become Booked because a PDF was sent. |
| The reply comes from a coordinator who cannot approve spend | Hold the walkthrough anyway. A coordinator who wants this is the strongest thing available. The proposal still goes to whoever approves spend, and the confirmation line in step 2 is how that person gets named without an awkward question. |
| "What does it cost?" before any call | Answer it plainly and in full, in the reply, then offer the slots. EUR 1,000 a year for the Founding 25, inclusive of VAT, EUR 1,500 standard. Never defer a price to the call. A venue that has to sit through 30 minutes to hear a number remembers the 30 minutes. |
| "Not now, try us later" | Record the month. Stop. This is not a lost venue. |
| "No thanks" | Thank them and stop. No nurture sequence. |
| Out of scope under D-012 | Say so honestly and do not book. Below roughly 20 weddings a year, EUR 1,000 cannot be justified to them, and a venue that cannot justify it will not renew. |

## 7. What is recorded, and where

Every booking produces facts. None of them live in this project tree.

| Fact | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Venue name, contact name, role, email | Signal HQ and the private register only. `private/venues.csv` is gitignored (PROJECT.md §21). |
| Call booked, held, missed, rescheduled | The private register. |
| Whether someone who approves spend was on the call | The private register. |
| Objections raised | The private register, then counted into the weekly review. |
| Counts of any of the above | `PROJECT_STATE.json` `commercial.*`, count-only, and only through `project-control.mjs commercial`. |

**No personal recipient data reaches a generated report or a committed session
file.** `validate` fails if contact data appears in the commercial tracker, and
that check is the backstop, not the rule.

## 8. Two things that must never be improvised on a call

**Guest data.** R-017 is open and rated critical. Dietary and allergy notes are a
first-class object in the shipped product: `app/src/lib/templates.generated.ts`
line 108 ships a "Collect final dietary notes" task in the wedding template, and
`app/src/app/the-wedding/page.tsx` line 26 says "One reply to Glenmara House,
including dietary notes". That data reveals health, routinely reveals religious
belief, and accessibility requirements reveal disability. It is entered by the
couple about guests who agreed to nothing, and guest lists routinely include
children. There is no settled answer to that question yet.

So: if a venue asks where guest dietary or accessibility information goes, who
can see it, or what happens to it, **the answer does not get invented on the
call.** Say that it is a fair question, that the answer is being written down
properly rather than improvised, and that it will come in writing. Then record
the question in the weekly review, because a venue asking it is the strongest
possible argument for finishing R-017 before Cohort 1 goes out.

**A hand-off constraint to E11.10.** While R-017 is open, the walkthrough should
not lead with the dietary-notes flow. E11.10 owns that sequence and this
document does not rewrite it, but the constraint is recorded here so it is not
discovered live in front of a venue.

**Compliance and legal wording.** No call, email, invite or follow-up in this
sequence says compliant, approved, reviewed, certified or legally checked. D-016
forbids it and it is checked at every founder review. The honest sentences are
available: what the venue can see, what it cannot see, and what is written down.

# Part 2 · E11.15 · The weekly conversion review

## 11. Where it lives

**Friday morning, inside the existing weekly operating review, inside the Friday
brief.** D-008 clause 4 chose that deliberately and it is not reopened here.
E01.12 already built the weekly review: `PC status weekly` generates six
sections, `templates/WEEKLY_REVIEW.md` is copied to
`sessions/weekly/<YYYY-MM-DD>-review.md`, and WORKFLOWS.md §12 is the procedure.

The conversion review is **one block inside that document**, placed directly
after section 5 (Pipeline), where the Founding 25 counters already sit. It is
not a second meeting, not a second file and not a second cadence.

**When no cohort is live, the block is one line.** "No cohort live. Nothing to
review." A review that manufactures content on a week with no outreach in it
teaches everyone to skim the weeks that matter.

**The one decision this block exists to produce:** release the next cohort, or
do not. Everything in it is either evidence for that decision or a change that
has to happen before the next 25 emails go out.

## 12. What the tool can and cannot give it

Stated plainly, because half of this review has no machine source and pretending
otherwise would put invented numbers in front of the founder.

**Already in `PROJECT_STATE.json` `commercial`:** `invitationsIssued`,
`responses`, `qualifiedMeetings`, `demonstrations`, `proposals`,
`signedAgreements`, `paidAgreements`, `configuredVenueAccounts`,
`onboardedVenues`, `firstCoupleInvitations`, `firstCoupleActivations`,
`foundingPlacesAvailable`, `cohortReady`.

**A gap worth fixing cheaply.** `renderWeekly` section 5 prints researched,
invitations, responses, proposals, signed, paid and onboarded. It does **not**
print `qualifiedMeetings` or `demonstrations`, which already exist in state and
are exactly the two numbers a conversion review turns on. Adding two rows to the
existing table is a small change and it is recommended.

**Not in state at all, and deliberately not added yet:** holds live, holds
expiring, missed calls, objections by category, close-lost reasons.

**Recommendation, FD-E: leave those five as written lines for Cohort 1.** A
counter nobody updates is worse than a sentence somebody wrote on Friday, and
counters designed before a single cohort has run would be guesses about what
matters. Revisit once Cohort 1 has completed its four touches, with real
material to design against.

## 13. The seven sections

Every number obeys three rules. **Activity is never summed with outcome**
(E01.12). **No fabricated precision** (PROJECT.md §11 clause 6). **A number with
no source shows a dash rather than a flattering guess** (BRAND.md §5.1).

### 13.1 Cohort

*Answers: where is the live cohort, and is it finished.*

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Cohort number and release date | Private register |
| Venues contacted | `commercial.invitationsIssued` |
| Touches complete, of four | Private register |
| Days since release | Computed from the release date |
| **Places paid** | `commercial.paidAgreements`, of 25 |
| Places held right now | Written line |
| Places free | 25 minus paid, minus held |

Places paid is the outcome row. Places held is not, because a hold expires.
Anything published or emailed about places left has to be true at the moment it
is sent (mechanics §1).

### 13.2 Channel

*Answers: did the email actually arrive, and did anyone watch the film.*

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Sent, delivered, bounced | Sending platform |
| Spam complaints | Sending platform |
| Replies, of which substantive | Private register |
| Film link clicks | The tracked signal, `touch` and `venue` per `VENUE_TRACKING_AND_CONVERSION.md` |
| Unsubscribes and suppressions | E11.04 suppression list |

**There is no open rate and there never will be.** D-013 clause 3 refused open
pixels on a cold email to people you want a trust relationship with. Film link
clicks are the only intent signal in the system. If this section ever grows an
open rate, something was added that a decision forbids.

Any bounce or complaint number above zero is read against R-010, not shrugged
at. The domain is the asset.

### 13.3 Meeting quality

*Answers: were these real conversations, or were they polite.*

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Calls booked · held · missed · rescheduled | Private register |
| Second misses, now marked later | Private register |
| `qualifiedMeetings` and `demonstrations` | `commercial`, once section 5 prints them |

**Quality is three binary facts, not a rating.**

1. Was someone who approves spend on the call.
2. Did the walkthrough finish, or did it run out of time.
3. Was a next step agreed, with a date.

Three out of three is a good call. Zero out of three with a warm feeling is not
a good call, and the whole point of writing it as three facts is that it stops
warmth from being mistaken for progress.

### 13.4 Objections

*Answers: what is the same thing everybody says.*

Every objection raised this week, in the venue's own words where they are worth
keeping, then grouped. Categories from the E11.12 library once it exists, and
until then: **price · adoption · privacy and data · support · existing tools ·
implementation · product maturity · no perceived need**.

The number that matters is not how many objections were raised. It is **which
one repeated**. One objection that turns up in most calls is not a sales problem
to be handled better, it is a fact about the offer or the product, and it goes
to E11.12 and possibly to a change request.

**Two objections escalate on sight rather than being logged.**

- Anything about guest data: dietary, allergy, accessibility, children. R-017 is
  open and critical. Section 8 governs the answer, and the question is recorded
  here so its frequency is visible.
- A venue repeating a promise Signal Studio never made: forever, guaranteed,
  legally approved, GDPR compliant, exclusive in their area. Correct it in
  writing the same day and record it. R-020 is the reason this line exists, and
  a venue saying it back is the earliest possible warning.

### 13.5 Proposals

*Answers: what is live, and what runs out.*

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Proposals sent | `commercial.proposals` |
| Holds live | Written line |
| **Holds expiring in the next seven days**, with dates | Written line |
| Holds expired this week | Written line |
| Holds extended, with the new date and the reason | Written line |

Holds are 14 days, once, extendable by Ethan personally (D-009 clause 7), and an
extension is recorded against the account so the register never disagrees with
what a venue was told. A hold that expires without anyone noticing is the
cheapest lost venue available, which is why the expiring row sits in a document
that is read every Friday.

### 13.6 Wins

*Answers: what actually happened.*

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Signed this week | `commercial.signedAgreements` |
| **Paid this week, and the place numbers assigned** | `commercial.paidAgreements` |
| **Onboarded this week** | `commercial.onboardedVenues` |
| Close-lost this week, with reason | Written line |

Paid and onboarded are the two bold rows, matching section 5 of the weekly
review. A signature is not a win until the money clears, because the number is
assigned on payment and nothing else (D-009 clause 6).

**Close-lost reasons, proposed. Founder decision FD-D.** A controlled list, so
the same thing is not recorded four ways across a year:

price · no perceived need · timing, revisit month recorded · already committed
elsewhere · privacy or data concern · could not reach whoever approves spend ·
out of scope under D-012

**No reply after four touches is not on that list.** It is `later`, and it is
counted separately. E11.14 is already Done and approved and its criterion covers
slot mechanics rather than this list, so this is new material rather than a
reopening. It needs a nod before it is used.

### 13.7 Next-cohort changes

*Answers: what changes before the next 25 emails go out.*

**Three items maximum**, each with an owner and a date, matching the discipline
already in `templates/WEEKLY_REVIEW.md`. Anything longer is a backlog and there
is already one of those.

Then the decision, written as one of three words.

**Release** the next cohort when all five hold:

1. The live cohort has completed its four touches, or the remaining touches are
   scheduled and need no attention.
2. Every venue that asked for a call has been offered specific times.
3. Deliverability held. No complaints, bounces inside what the register
   expected, sending domain not degraded.
4. Nothing learned this cohort changes the film, the page or the price. If
   something does, it changes first, and if it touches the offer it goes through
   a change request rather than an edit.
5. There is calendar room for the calls the next 25 could produce **on top of**
   the calls the live cohort is still producing.

**Hold** when any of those fail, and say which.

**Stop and change the offer** on one condition: a full cohort completed four
touches and produced no substantive reply. Twenty-five researched venues in the
target ring, contacted with a film made for them, producing nothing, is not a
follow-up problem. It is the list, the offer or the film, and the answer is a
change request, not another cohort.

## 15. What this review does not do

It changes no state. Same rule as the weekly review it sits inside: a decision
taken here exists only once it is recorded with `approve`, `gate`, `target`,
`commercial <field> <value>`, or a change request. Discussing something is not
recording it.

It carries no venue names, no contact names and no email addresses in the
committed file. Names live in the private register and in Signal HQ. The review
carries counts and, where a venue has to be identified, the account reference.

It never reports a percentage it cannot source. A conversion rate on a
denominator of four venues is a story, not a number, and PROJECT.md §11 clause 6
already forbids it.

## 18. Assumptions made, stated so they can be corrected

1. **E11.09, E11.10, E11.11 and E11.12 are unwritten.** This document defines the

2. **E11.01 and E11.02 are unwritten**, so the stage vocabulary here is taken
   from `FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS.md` §3 rather than invented. Replied and
   Booked are the two states added.
3. **Ethan has a working calendar that sends invites with a video link at no
   cost.** Route (a) depends on it. Nothing here provisions a tool.
4. **"The Friday brief" is the existing weekly ritual** named in D-008 clause 4
   and WORKFLOWS.md §12, and the conversion block is appended inside the same
   generated document rather than created as a new one.
5. **The private register exists or will exist** at `private/venues.csv`,
   gitignored, per PROJECT.md §21. Every field this document routes there depends
   on that being true.
6. **No reply rate, meeting rate or conversion rate is forecast anywhere in this
