## 1. Why this instrument, and why not the other two

CR-001 required that the instrument be chosen after the role map rather than
before it, from three candidates. The role map is now approved at v0.2. The
choice, and the reasoning, on the record.

### 1.1 Processor standard contractual clauses are ruled out

The controller-to-processor standard contractual clauses at Commission
Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/915 presuppose that one party processes personal
data **on behalf of and on the documented instructions of** the other.

Role map §1 concludes, per processing operation, that **Signal Studio is a
controller of every operation in its map and a processor of none**. The lead
argument is essential means: Signal Studio decides the retention periods, the
access model, which fields reach a sponsor, which fields reach the open web,
whether an indexable publication mode exists at all, and which category of data
the product asks a couple to collect. Nobody instructs it. Role map §1 also
records why "processor" is the most attractive and least accurate label available
to this business, and that roles follow the factual circumstances rather than the
label in an agreement.

Signing a processor instrument across a relationship that is not one would
therefore be **wrong on its face rather than protective**, which is the exact
harm CR-001 was written to prevent. Ruled out, and recorded as ruled out.

### 1.2 A single controller-to-controller sharing agreement is not enough on its own

The venue is an independent controller for its own operations: its staff records,
its booking relationship with the couple, and anything it collects directly from
a guest under its own catering relationship (role map row 2, Note c). For those,
a controller-to-controller sharing arrangement is the right shape.

But role map row 3 concludes that **venue invitation and couple redemption is
joint controllership**, because both parties pursue a shared commercial purpose in
activation, both benefit from it, and the venue supplies the identifiers. That is
the converging decision the case law looks for. Article 26 applies to that stage
and an arrangement is required **either way**, whichever lawful basis wins (role
map §12 item 2).

### 1.3 The instrument is therefore both, at different stages

> **This agreement is a data protection agreement in two parts: an Article 26
> joint-controller arrangement covering the invitation and redemption stage, and
> a controller-to-controller data-sharing arrangement covering everything else the
> venue gives or receives.**

It is drafted so that it does not fall apart on either reading of role map §12
item 5, which records as unresolved whether the venue is a joint controller of
adoption reporting or an independent controller of the copy it receives. See §5,
Decision point A.

**Nothing in this agreement is capable of changing the parties' actual roles.**
Where the facts and this document disagree, the facts win. That sentence stays in
the executed version.

## 4. The operative clauses

### 4.1 Venue data and venue staff data

1. Signal Studio processes the Venue's commercial and billing data as controller,
   to perform the Venue Edition agreement, to meet Irish Revenue record-keeping
   obligations, and for the security and integrity of its own systems.
2. Where the Venue supplies personal data about its own staff (a named contact, a
   coordinator, a signatory), the Venue confirms it has a lawful basis for doing
   so and has given those people whatever information its own privacy notice
   requires. Signal Studio does not rely on the Venue's confirmation as a
   substitute for its own transparency duty and will provide its own information
   to those people on request.
3. Financial and transaction records are retained for six years to meet Irish
   Revenue record-keeping obligations. Every other category is governed by
   `evidence/E03.11-retention-deletion-and-discontinuation.md`, which states
   plainly which retention rules are enforced in code today and which are not.

**Decision point, marked:** the Venue Edition agreement's clauses on invoicing,
renewal and payment failure rest on E02.04, E02.06 and E02.07, which are still
backlog. This agreement does not settle any of them and does not need to; it
touches billing data only as a category, not as a commercial term.

### 4.2 The couple's content, and the hard boundary

> **No workspace content passes to the Venue at any time.**

This is the single most important sentence in this agreement and it is the one
sentence the product actually enforces server-side.

1. The Venue never receives the couple's planning content: not task titles, not
   descriptions, not notes, not Timeline content, not attachments, not
   collaborator identities, not private dates.
2. The list of fields the Venue may receive is the sponsor allowlist in
   `studio/docs/privacy-permission-matrix.md`, and that list is **exhaustive**.
   Anything not on it is excluded by default.
3. Enforcement is server-side. Interface visibility is never an authorisation
   control, and the permission matrix says so in its own words.
4. A Venue request to see a couple's data is refused, with no discretion, on
   every occasion. Role map Scenario 3 records this as the clearest answer in the
   map and gives the reasoning: there is no lawful basis, it contradicts what the
   couple was told, and it breaches purpose limitation.

**One qualification, stated rather than hidden.** If a couple publishes their
workspace to the open surface at `/p`, the Venue can read what any member of the
public can read, by finding the page. Clause 4.2 is a refusal to disclose. It is
not a technical guarantee of confidentiality once the couple has published.
D-033 decided R-031 as Option B: a wedding workspace ships `noindex` by default
and search visibility is an explicit couple opt-in. Role map §13 Finding 5 records
that **the opt-in has no storage yet**, so today the default is the entire
behaviour.

### 4.3 The Article 26 arrangement: invitation and redemption

**Stage covered.** From the moment the Venue issues an invitation to a couple,
until the moment that couple redeems it. Not before, not after.

**The boundary, as three clauses.** Role map §7 requires this to be drafted as
three clauses rather than the single sentence an earlier draft used, because the
single sentence would be false on the face of the product.

> **(a) Joint controllership ends at redemption.** From the moment a couple
> redeems, the Venue is not a joint controller of the couple's workspace or of
> anything in it.
>
> **(b) No workspace content passes to the Venue at any time**, before or after
> redemption. Enforced server-side. The sponsor allowlist is exhaustive.
>
> **(c) Suppressed activation reporting continues after redemption**, under the
> role and basis recorded in §4.4 and subject to the suppression controls in
> §4.5. Clause (a) ends the joint role over the workspace. It does not end the
> reporting, and this agreement says so rather than leaving the earlier sentence
> to be discovered as false.

**Allocation of responsibilities under Article 26(1).**

| Responsibility | Who discharges it |
|---|---|
| Article 13 information to the couple at the point of invitation | The Venue, using the supplied approved copy pack, at the moment it invites |
| Article 13 information to the couple at signup and redemption | Signal Studio, in the couple-facing privacy notice (E03.05) |
| Telling the couple that activation data about them goes to the Venue | Signal Studio, at signup. **This disclosure does not exist today.** Role map §7 records it as owed and missing; it is Decision point C |
| Responding to a rights request about the invitation stage | Signal Studio, as single point of contact, whichever party receives it |
| Security of the invitation mechanism | Signal Studio (Schedule 1) |
| Accuracy of the identifiers supplied to create an invitation | The Venue |
| Confirming the couple has a signed booking at the Venue | The Venue |

**Article 26(3), and it cuts both ways.**

> A couple may exercise their rights against either party. "The Venue holds the
> relationship" is not a defence available to Signal Studio, and "Signal Studio
> runs the software" is not a defence available to the Venue. Each party will
> forward any request it receives to the other within two working days and will
> not tell the person to go elsewhere.

**Article 26(2), the essence.** The essence of this arrangement must be made
available to the couples it concerns. See Decision point D: there is no stable
public URL for it today.

**Lawful basis at this stage is not asserted by this agreement.** Role map §12
item 2 records it as unresolved between Article 6(1)(f) for both parties and
Article 6(1)(b) inside the Venue's booking relationship.

> **The safer reading, taken and named.** This agreement is drafted as though
> Article 6(1)(f) applies, because that reading carries the heavier requirements:
> a written legitimate interests assessment, a working Article 21 objection route,
> and a balancing test that must actually be documented. Building for 6(1)(f) also
> satisfies 6(1)(b) if that reading wins; building for 6(1)(b) alone would leave
> the apparatus missing if it loses. The assessment is written at
> `evidence/E03.11-legitimate-interests-assessments.md`, LIA-2. **The Venue must
> record its own lawful basis for the invitation and tell Signal Studio in
> writing which it relies on**, so that the two records do not contradict each
> other in front of a regulator.

### 4.4 Adoption and activation reporting after redemption

**A correction to the record, carried here so it is not missed.** Role map §13
Finding 1 establishes that venue-facing reporting is **live today**, not planned:
`studio/src/lib/account/live/project-venue-usage.ts` projects it and the Account
surfaces render it. Two consequences follow. Its Article 30 record is required
now rather than at launch. And the suppression controls it depends on were
defective when the role map was written, and have since been fixed and
mutation-tested.

**What the Venue receives.** Aggregate adoption and activation evidence only,
subject to §4.5. No per-couple row. No ranking. No last-active. No task counts.
No content signal of any kind. No metric labelled "your couples".

**Aggregate is not anonymous, and this agreement says so.** The Venue knows
exactly which couples it invited, so it holds the re-identification key. Under
Recital 26 anonymity is measured against means reasonably likely to be used by
the controller **or by another person**, and the Venue is that other person.
Aggregation over a set the recipient already holds the key to is presentation,
not anonymisation (role map §7). The Venue therefore receives **personal data**
and its obligations under this agreement apply to it.

**Decision point A is the role label for this stage.** See §5.

### 4.5 Suppression, and the commercial cost the Venue is told about before signing

1. A behavioural count is withheld when the value is below three, **or** when the
   population minus the value is below three, independent of population size. The
   complement of a small number identifies people just as precisely as the number
   does.
2. A rate is withheld below five eligible activations, and the denominator travels
   with the value.
3. A withheld cell reads as "not enough activity to report yet". It never reads
   as "fewer than 3", because that is itself a disclosure.
4. No filter combination may narrow a view to a single couple.
5. The suppression rules are covered by regression tests, not by query discipline.

> **Stated to the Venue before signature, not discovered afterwards:** a Venue
> paying for this service will see suppressed cells in a report it paid for. That
> is the control working. It is not a fault and it will not be removed on request.

### 4.6 The Venue's own obligations

1. **Purpose limitation.** The Venue uses the data it receives only to administer
   its invitations and to understand adoption at its own property. It does not
   use it to profile couples, to target marketing at them, to enrich its own
   records about them, or for any purpose not named in this agreement.
2. **No onward disclosure.** The Venue does not pass what it receives to any third
   party, including a marketing agency, a photographer, a wedding directory or a
   parent group, without a separate written agreement.
3. **Security.** The Venue keeps its access credentials confidential and does not
   share them. See Decision point B on what "access" means at launch.
4. **Its own transparency.** The Venue gives its own couples the information its
   own privacy notice requires about the invitation, using the supplied approved
   copy pack.
5. **Cooperation.** The Venue assists Signal Studio in responding to a rights
   request or a breach, and does so without charge.
6. **Marketing controls.** The Venue describes the product only from the supplied
   approved copy pack. The prohibited-claims list, and the correction right that
   goes with it, live in the Venue Edition agreement (E03.02) under R-020. They
   are not restated here.
7. **Republication of a couple's page.** Whether a Venue may reshare a couple's
   published page in its own marketing is **unresolved** (role map §12 item 10,
   founder decision 14). See Decision point F. Until it is decided, this agreement
   does not permit it.

### 4.7 Special-category data, stated without a condition being claimed

This clause exists because the honest position is uncomfortable and hiding it
would be worse.

1. The couple's private workspace is an **unstructured free-text store**. Nothing
   constrains what a couple types into a task title, a description or a note.
2. Signal Studio's own shipped wedding template contains a task instructing the
   couple to collect information about guests' food requirements. The canonical
   source is `studio/src/lib/templates/wedding-planning-workspace/tasks.ts:69`,
   generated into `app/src/lib/templates.generated.ts:108`.
3. Information of that kind reveals health, and routinely reveals religious
   belief. Requirements about physical access reveal disability. Those are
   Article 9 special categories, and they concern guests who have agreed to
   nothing.
4. **No Article 9(2) condition is claimed for this data, because none is
   available.** Role map §4 runs the analysis: explicit consent is the only
   realistic candidate and the guest is never asked; the couple cannot give it on
   the guest's behalf. Article 9 is a prohibition lifted only by a listed
   condition, and a contract clause does not lift it.
5. **What this agreement does instead**, and it is harm reduction rather than a
   claimed compliance:
   - **No egress to the Venue, ever.** Clause 4.2 covers it, and the Venue
     receiving none of this data is the single most effective control available.
   - **No publication.** A hard rule at title and tag level, in code, with a
     regression test, per role map §4(c). Recorded as required; see the security
     schedule for what is and is not built today.
   - **The shortest retention of any category in the system**, per
     `evidence/E03.11-retention-deletion-and-discontinuation.md`.
   - **A documented position with a stated end date**, recorded as an accepted
     exposure and not as a resolved one.
6. **There is no structured dietary, allergy or accessibility field anywhere in
   the system, and this agreement does not create one.** Role map §4 records that
   removing a field would be a no-op because there is no field, and that designing
   one is a different product decision. R-017 remains open. Whether the template
   instruction is removed, reworded or kept with guidance is a founder decision
   (D-035), not a drafting one, and this agreement does not pre-empt it.

### 4.8 Records of processing

Article 30(5)'s under-250 exemption does not apply here. It falls away where
special-category data is processed and the processing is not occasional, and role
map §10.6 records both as applying. Role map §13 Finding 1 adds that the
venue-facing reporting operation is live, so its record is due now.

> **Clause.** Signal Studio maintains a record of processing activities covering
> the operations in this agreement. **This record does not exist today.** No
> Article 30 record was found anywhere in either repository. It is required and it
> is not written. Recorded as a gap, not as a control.

## 5. Decision points. None of these is chosen quietly.

Each one is a place where the role map records a question as open, or where the
product does not yet support the clause. Each states the safer reading this draft
took and why. Each needs a founder answer, and several need a build.

### Decision point A: the Venue's role in adoption reporting

**Open:** role map §12 item 5. Joint controller of the analytics, or independent
controller of the copy it receives.

**Safer reading taken.** Draft the substance so both readings produce the same
obligations, and label the stage only where a label is unavoidable. Concretely,
this agreement requires the Venue to do the things **both** readings need:

- to record its own lawful basis for holding the reporting (needed if it is an
  independent controller);
- to give its own couples information about the invitation (needed either way);
- to accept Article 26(3) single-point-of-contact handling for the invitation
  stage (needed under the joint reading, and harmless under the other);
- to accept purpose limitation and no onward disclosure (needed either way).

**Why that way.** Role map §12 item 5 says both readings converge in practice once
suppression is a tested control, and advises implementing the controls first and
deciding the label with the Article 26 drafting. The controls are now implemented
and mutation-tested. The label is the only thing left, and getting it wrong in
writing is worse than leaving it unlabelled.

**Founder decision needed:** confirm this shape, or pick a label.

### Decision point B: what "the Venue's access" means at launch

**The problem, verified.** R-043: there is no venue-authenticated route anywhere
in the product. The only guard on every Account surface is one shared password,
and that same password opens every Signal HQ room. D-027 point 4 makes the portal
at launch "invitation administration only", which implies an administrator, and
there is none.

**Safer reading taken.** This draft does **not** promise the Venue a login. It
describes the Venue as receiving invitation administration and reporting, without
stating the mechanism, which is true whether the answer is a portal account or
evidence sent by Signal Studio. `evidence/E07.16-venue-identity-lab.md` sets out
four options with costs and recommends Option C for 1 September: Signal HQ
administers and the Venue receives evidence rather than a login.

**Founder decision needed:** if a Venue is ever handed the shared Signal HQ
password, clause 4.6.3 becomes a promise the product cannot keep and the blast
radius is the founder's whole operating surface. Confirm Option C, or fund the
alternative before the first Venue is contacted.

### Decision point C: the Article 13 disclosure that does not exist

**The gap, from role map §7:** couples must be told, at signup, that activation
data about them goes to the Venue. That disclosure does not exist on any surface
today.

**Safer reading taken.** This agreement states the obligation as Signal Studio's,
and states plainly that it is not yet discharged. It is not drafted as though the
disclosure exists.

**Founder decision needed:** it is a paragraph in the couple-facing privacy notice
(E03.05) and it should land before the first couple redeems, not before the first
Venue signs.

### Decision point D: where the essence of the Article 26 arrangement is published

**Article 26(2)** requires the essence of the arrangement to be made available to
the data subjects it concerns. There is no stable public URL for it today.

**Safer reading taken.** This agreement records the obligation and names the
requirement: a short plain-English summary of who does what, at a permanent URL,
linked from the couple-facing privacy notice.

**Founder decision needed:** approve a URL, or accept that the requirement is
unmet and record it as an accepted exposure with a date.

### Decision point E: a postal address for data protection correspondence

The live app privacy notice carries a literal placeholder,
`[NEEDS-REVIEW: add postal address once entity is formed]`. A controller that
cannot be written to is a real gap, and it also bites the venue relationship,
because a Venue's own adviser will ask for one.

**Safer reading taken.** This draft names `hello@signalstudio.ie` as the working
contact route, because it is verified to exist and is already published on the
live security page as the route for security reports. It does not invent a postal
address.

**Founder decision needed:** publish an address, or record the decision not to and
the reason.

### Decision point F: may a Venue reshare a couple's published page

**Open:** role map §12 item 10 and founder decision 14. If the answer is yes, the
Venue becomes an independent controller for that republication, needs its own
basis, and the couple has to have agreed. It also intersects with R-020, because
a republished page is a place a Venue's own words appear next to a couple's.

**Safer reading taken.** This agreement does **not** permit it. Silence would be
read as permission by a Venue that wanted to, so the draft says no rather than
nothing. Reversing a no later costs an amendment; withdrawing an implied yes costs
a couple's page coming down.

### Decision point G: the lawful basis at the invitation stage

Covered in §4.3. Drafted to Article 6(1)(f), the heavier reading, with the
assessment written and the objection route required. Named there rather than
repeated here.

## 7. Personal data breach

### 7.1 Correcting the record

Role map §10.5 states that no breach procedure exists. **That is now out of
date.** `evidence/E08.12-incident-response-runbook.md` exists, sets the Article 33
clock at 72 hours from awareness to the Data Protection Commission, names the
notification route at `dataprotection.ie`, and carries its own §0 recording the
limitation that matters: there is one responder, no rota, no second contact, no
paid on-call and no alerting.

This agreement cites that runbook rather than restating it, so the two cannot
drift.

### 7.2 Allocation

| | |
|---|---|
| **Signal Studio** | Assesses any breach affecting personal data in its systems, decides whether it is notifiable, and makes the Article 33 notification to the DPC where it is the controller. Communicates to affected data subjects under Article 34 where required. Owns the 72-hour clock. |
| **The Venue** | Reports any breach it becomes aware of that affects data covered by this agreement to Signal Studio **without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours of becoming aware**, in writing, with what it knows at that moment rather than waiting to know more. Does not notify the DPC or any data subject about a joint operation without telling Signal Studio first, and does not delay its own notification if Signal Studio is unreachable. |
| **Both** | Cooperate without charge on assessment, containment and communication. Neither publishes an account of a breach naming the other without giving the other reasonable notice. |

**The 24-hour figure is chosen, not derived.** The Regulation sets 72 hours on the
controller's notification to the supervisory authority and does not set an
internal figure between joint controllers. Twenty-four hours leaves Signal Studio
two thirds of the clock to assess and notify. If a Venue negotiates it, the
consequence is arithmetic and can be explained.

### 7.3 Two limitations stated to the Venue rather than discovered by it

1. **Detection is manual.** Error monitoring is integrated in the application code
   and **not provisioned** (`studio/docs/INFRASTRUCTURE.md`, "Provisioned
   deliberately later"). There is no alerting of any kind. Signal Studio is likely
   to learn of a problem from a person rather than from a system.
2. **There is one responder.** The runbook records this as an accepted limitation
   rather than a solved problem, and it is not softened here.

Neither sentence is comfortable in a document sent to a customer. Both are true,
and D-016 forbids the alternative.

## 8. Rights requests

1. Whichever party receives a request, **Signal Studio answers it** for anything
   in its systems. The Venue does not answer on Signal Studio's behalf and does
   not tell the person to contact Signal Studio instead of forwarding it.
2. A request from a person who is not an account holder (a guest, a family member,
   a supplier named by a couple) runs to Signal Studio directly, not through the
   couple and not through the Venue. Role map §9 states this as Signal Studio's
   responsibility.
3. **A removal route for a non-account third party does not exist on any surface
   today.** Role map §5 Consequence 5 and founder decision 1 record it as the
   cheapest and highest-value item in the whole map: a controller name, a
   monitored contact address, one sentence of Article 14 notice, and a
   "remove me from this page" link that resolves without an account, on every
   published surface. This agreement does not describe it as if it existed.
4. Response handling follows role map Scenario 1, including restriction under
   Article 18 on receipt, acting unilaterally on any published surface regardless
   of the couple, and executing the erasure itself if the couple does not act
   within the deadline.

## 9. Term, termination and what happens to data

1. This agreement runs for as long as the Venue Edition agreement runs, and
   survives it for as long as either party still holds data covered by it.
2. On termination, the Venue deletes or returns the personal data it received
   under this agreement, except what it must keep for its own legal obligations,
   and confirms in writing which it did.
3. **The survival sentence, stated unprompted** (D-020 point 2): any workspace a
