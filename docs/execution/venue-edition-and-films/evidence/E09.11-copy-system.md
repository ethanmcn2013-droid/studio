founding rate, privacy, collaboration, Keepsake, CTA, objection and FAQ. Where
an approved string already exists in a Done document, this document points at it
and does not rewrite it. Where none exists, it writes one. It also supplies the
venue-distributable copy pack and the prohibited-claims list that R-020 requires,

E09.02 §9.1 to §9.10 leaves open: ten founder calls on metric definitions, none of
which is closed here, and none of which may be closed by a sentence appearing in
copy. The visual design of any of this. The film scripts. The four commercial
conflicts recorded as D-003 to D-006.

**How to use it.** §2 is the audit: what already existed and what did not. §3 to
§10 are the eight blocks. §11 is the pack. §12 is the sweep and its real numbers.
§13 is the defect register: things this audit found that belong to other tasks.
§14 is what the founder has to answer.

**Nothing in this document is approved.** E09.11 is `in_progress`. Copy is
finalised when the founder says so, not when it is written.

## 3. Block 1 — the offer

**Canonical, and every other surface derives from it.** D-020's venue-facing line,
ratified, verbatim:

> Every couple who books their wedding with you gets a workspace, for as long as
> your licence is current.

**Three lengths. A surface picks one. It does not write a fourth.**

**One line**, for a subject line, an OG description, a film end card, a CTA lead:

> A private place to plan, for every couple who books with you.

**One paragraph**, for a landing page hero, an email body, a card:

> Every couple who books their wedding with you gets a private place to plan it.
> Notes, the actual work, a plan they can share with family and suppliers, and a
> short note in the morning when something needs attention. Your name is on it,
> quietly. Nothing for your team to run.

**One page.** `E11.11-12` §2, verbatim. It is the finalised long-form offer and it
runs the E09.10 §3.1 ladder without skipping a rung. It is not restated here.

### 3.1 The survival sentence, stated unprompted

D-020 point 2. It appears wherever the end of the agreement is described, and it
is never held back for the objection.

> Any workspace a couple has already opened keeps its full term, and its
> Keepsake, whatever happens to your agreement. Only new invitations stop.

**Why unprompted.** A venue that has to ask what happens to its couples if it
stops paying has already decided we would not have said. The sentence costs one
line and it is the strongest thing in the offer.

### 3.2 What the offer never does

- **Names the four products.** A venue owner is buying an outcome for their
  couples. "Signal Tasks, Signal Timeline, Signal, Signal Notes" arrives in
  onboarding, when it is useful. `E11.11-12` §2.2 already holds this line.
- **Asks the venue for its wedding count before signature.** D-020. Signal
  computes the per-wedding figure and shows it as a scale, never as a question.
- **Carries a number of couples.** P5. There is no entitlement figure.
- **Leads with price.** Rung 3, after trust. E09.10 §3.1.

## 4. Block 2 — the founding rate

**The commercial summary in `E11.11-12` §3 is the finalised block.** Every row
traces. It is not restated here, because two copies of a price table is how two
prices happen.

What this document adds is the sentence set and the refusal set, because the rate
is the one place in the programme where the correct sentence and the forbidden
sentence sound almost identical, and the trap has already caught this programme
once (R-008, D-021: the founder's spoken words were "for life" and the published
wording deliberately was not).

### 4.1 The four permitted forms

Any of these may be used. Nothing else may.

> €1,000 a year, prepaid, including VAT at the prevailing rate.

> €500 a year less than the standard rate, for as long as you stay.

> The founding rate holds for as long as the agreement renews continuously
> without lapse.

> It follows the property. It survives a sale, an operator change and a rebrand.
> It does not travel to other venues a new owner brings.

### 4.2 The refusal set

Each of these is a sentence someone will reach for on a call because it is
shorter and warmer. Each is forbidden, with the substitute alongside.

| Never say | Say instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Locked in for life" · "your price is locked forever" · "you'll never pay more" | "For as long as the agreement renews continuously without lapse." | D-009.3 · P1 · R-008 |
| "€1,000 + VAT" · "€1,000 ex VAT" · "from €1,000" · "about €83 a month" | "€1,000 a year, prepaid, including VAT at the prevailing rate." | D-021 · P12 |
| "a discount" · "an offer" · "a deal" · "20% off" · "promotional rate" | "the founding rate" | D-009.2 · E09.10 §1.6 |
| "limited time" · "while places last" · "only a few left" · "act now" | "Your place is held for 14 days." Stated once. | D-009.7 · P13 |
| "join the 12 venues already signed" | Nothing. No venue has signed. It is also untrue. | P13 |
| "guaranteed for the life of your business" | "for as long as you stay" | P1 |

### 4.3 The one arithmetic sentence

D-020 permits Signal to compute the per-wedding figure and forbids asking the
venue for its wedding count first. The permitted form, from `E11.11-12` §2:

> The price does not change with the number of weddings you run. For scale only:
> across 20 weddings a year that is €50 a wedding, across 40 it is €25, across 80
> it is €12.50.

"For scale only" is load-bearing. Without it the sentence reads as a per-wedding
price, which is the pricing model D-020 abolished.

## 5. Block 3 — privacy

The negative half travels with the positive half, in the same block, at the same
weight. E09.10 §4. This block is written so that a surface cannot take half of it.

### 5.1 The venue-facing privacy block, reusable verbatim

> **What you see.** Which invitations you sent, and which couples opened them.
> Adoption figures across your couples as a group. Small numbers are held back so
> a figure can never point at one couple.
>
> **What you never see.** What any couple is planning. There is no per-couple row,
> no ranking and no list of who is behind. If a couple changes their wedding date,
> that does not appear anywhere. A postponement is their news to share.
>
> **At launch.** At launch you see your invitations and who opened them. Adoption
> figures follow later in the year.

**The third part is required, not decorative.** D-032 R4. A surface that takes
this block verbatim takes all three parts. Taking the first two and dropping the
third describes the product at renewal and calls it the product on day one, which
is the concealment §5.1a exists to prevent.

**Three things and no fourth.** D-001.4 fixes the venue-visible list at exactly
invitation administration, activation evidence and aggregate adoption evidence.
A fourth thing named in copy is a copy defect and probably a product defect
(E09.10 §1.4). The FAQ in §10 is written against this, and the sweep enforces it.

**One narrowing the copy must respect.** D-027 point 4, ratified 2026-08-03: the
portal at launch is invitation administration only, and aggregate adoption
evidence follows after 1 September. So the second sentence of "What you see" is a
claim about the product at renewal, not at launch. §5.1a fixes what every surface
must say about that, and makes it required rather than optional.

### 5.1a The launch-scope sentence · required, not optional

**Ratified by D-032 R4**, 2026-08-03: the launch-scope sentence goes in writing on
the commercial surfaces rather than only into the call. This section is that
instruction in a form a surface owner can build against.

**The final form. One sentence pair. A surface uses it verbatim and does not
write a variant.**

> At launch you see your invitations and who opened them. Adoption figures follow
> later in the year.

**Why verbatim and why no variants.** The whole point of the sentence is that a
venue reads the same thing everywhere. Two paraphrases of a scope limit read as
two different scopes, and the second one a venue meets is the one that sounds
like a walk-back.

**Why "later in the year" and not a date.** D-027 point 4 says adoption evidence
follows after 1 September. It commits to no month. A date in copy would be a
promise the programme has not made, and a missed one would cost more than the
vagueness does.

**Where it must appear.** The rule first, because the list will go stale and the
rule will not: **any surface that tells a venue what it sees must carry it.** The
surfaces that do so today, each verified by reading it on 2026-08-03:

| Surface | Where | Owner | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| The FAQ answer to "Can we see the couple's workspace?" | §10.2 of this document | E09.11 | **Carries it.** Applied here |
| The reusable venue-facing privacy block | §5.1 above | E09.11 | **Carries it**, through this section |
| The venue copy pack | `E09.11-venue-copy-pack.md` §3 | E09.11 | **Carries it.** Applied |
| The private venue page, both states | `E13.16-link-and-destination-contract.md` §3.1 | E12.03 / WP-13 | **Required.** Added to that document's must-carry list |
| The one-page proposal, the "What you see" block | `E11.11-12-proposal-and-objections.md` §2, line 106 | E11.11 | **Does not carry it.** Boundary request. It reads "and adoption figures across your couples as a group" in the present tense, which is untrue on 1 September |
| The live public Venue Edition page | `studio/src/app/venues/page.tsx:118` | E12.04 / WP-13 | **Does not carry it, and is worse than silent.** The line names a fourth visible thing and a consent layer D-027 point 4 leaves unwired. §13 defect 4 |
| The walkthrough script, Beat 5 | `E11.09-10-discovery-and-demo.md` | E11.09 / E11.10 | **Does not carry it.** Boundary request. The beat shows the reports panel and says "how the group as a whole is using it" in the present tense |

**One surface checked and deliberately not on the list.**
`studio/docs/strategy/FOUNDING_25_BENEFITS_CHARTER.md:123` says *"You see
invitations, activation and entitlement state."* That is accurate at launch and
promises no aggregate figures, so it needs nothing. It acquires this requirement
the moment it acquires an adoption claim.

**The argument, stated once so a surface owner does not have to be persuaded
again.** It is the same argument E09.02 §9.9 makes about hiding an empty metric
row. A venue that discovers at launch that the adoption figures are not there yet
reads it as concealment, and it will be right that nobody told it. The sentence
costs sixteen words and it is cheaper than the conversation it prevents.

**What the sentence must never turn into.** It is a scope statement, not an
apology and not a roadmap promise. Banned: "coming soon", "in the next release",
"we're working on", "phase two", any version number, any month, any feature
name, and any framing that invites the venue to ask when.

**One thing the sentence does not license.** It says nothing about a consent
layer, and no surface may describe a couple consenting to share a specific field
with a venue. That mechanism is unwired at launch (D-027 point 4) and naming it
would put a fourth thing in front of the venue (D-001 point 4). That is exactly
the defect at `venues/page.tsx:118`.

### 5.2 The coordinator-facing privacy block

The coordinator is the person a couple will actually ask, and a coordinator who
over-promises on our behalf is our failure (E09.10 §3.2 rung 4).

> You can see that a couple opened their workspace. You cannot see what is in it.
> If a couple asks you to look at something inside their plan, the honest answer
> is that you cannot see it, and neither can anyone else here.

### 5.3 The couple-facing privacy block

E09.10 §3.3 rung 4, in the couple's own words, before they are asked to type
anything real.

> This is yours. Glenmara House can see that you opened it. They cannot see what
> is in it.

Nothing is added to that. No reassurance stack, no security adjectives, no badge.
P3 forbids the adjectives and the product does not support them.

### 5.4 The refusal set for privacy

| Never say | Why |
|---|---|
| "GDPR compliant" · "fully compliant" · "GDPR certified" · "DPC approved" | P3. There is no certification to hold. Use E09.10 §6 Answer 1. |
| "bank-grade security" · "military-grade encryption" · "100% secure" · "your data is safe with us" | P3. None of them is a claim this system can support, and two of them are not claims any system can support. |
| "your data never leaves Ireland" · "EU only" · "we don't use US providers" | P15. The processors are known; their regions and transfer bases are not verified in this programme until E03.05. |
| "end-to-end encrypted" | It is not. |
| "reviewed by our legal team" · "solicitor-approved" · "professionally drafted" | P2 · D-016. Use E09.10 §6 Answer 2, which is the one answer of the four fully supported today. |
| "analytics" · "insights" · "usage data" · "engagement metrics" · "reporting on your couples" | E09.10 §1.4. The permitted words are invitation administration, activation evidence, aggregate adoption evidence. |
| "your couples" as a denominator label | P9. The denominator is "invitations redeemed". Signal never learns which couples hold real bookings. |

## 6. Block 4 — collaboration

> **This section is the collaboration contract, and it is an amendment to E09.10.**
> Ratified by **D-032 R2**, 2026-08-03. The amendment is recorded in
> `change-requests/CR-004.md`, approved on the same date. E09.10 is Done and
> founder-approved, so it is amended by change request and **not one line of it is
> edited**. Collaboration is now the eighth governed block of `venue-copy.v1`, and
> everything below carries the same authority as a section written inside E09.10:
> §6.2 extends its §1 terminology, §6.3 extends its §3 hierarchy, §6.4 extends its
> §5 prohibitions, and §6.3's objection form extends its §6 pre-approved answers.
>
> One line is still wanted in E09.10's own header so that a session reading it
> learns the eighth block exists. E09.10 is outside this package's file column, so
> that line is a boundary request rather than an edit. CR-004's closing section
> carries its exact wording.

**This block had no contract of any kind.** E09.10 declares at line 5 that it
blocks "offer, founding-rate, privacy, Keepsake, CTA, objection and FAQ copy",
which is seven, while the E09.11 title names eight. A search for "collaborat"
across the whole of E09.10 returns nothing: no terminology row, no ladder, no
register. The only appearances across E09.01 and E09.02 are on the never-list,
where `collaborators`, `collaboratorCount`, `members`, `memberList`, `invitees`,
`inviteEmail`, `assignee` and `assigneeName` are all forbidden in a venue-facing
payload.

So the block of the eight sitting closest to a privacy boundary is the one with
no words. It is written here. The omission was raised as OQ-5 in §14; D-032 R2
answered it, and CR-004 is the amendment that carries the answer.

### 6.1 The determination this block rests on

**A couple sharing their plan is a couple's act, and the venue is not a party to
it.** Everything below follows from that one line.

There is no venue-side sharing control, no venue view of who a couple shared
with, and no route by which a venue becomes a participant in a couple's workspace
by any action the venue can take. The venue's only relationship to collaboration
is that it has none, and the copy says so positively rather than leaving it to be
inferred.

### 6.2 Terminology

| Thing | We call it | We never call it | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| The people a couple brings in | **the people they share it with** · **their family** · **their suppliers** · **their own planner** | collaborators · team · team members · users · seats · members · invitees · contributors | Every one of those is a payload-forbidden key (`E09.01` §6.2) or a word that describes a licence rather than a person. |
| The act | **share it** · **send it to someone** · **give someone access** | invite a collaborator · add a user · provision access · onboard someone · grant permissions | "Share" is the word the product already uses and the word a couple would say. |
| What they get | **a link they can open** · **read-only** · **they can leave a comment** | share link (as a noun) · magic link · public link · guest seat | E09.10 §1.6 already bans "share link" as a noun. |
| The people who see a published Timeline | **guests** · **family** · **suppliers** | audience · viewers · traffic · visitors | E09.10 §1.1. They agreed to nothing and are not an audience metric in copy. |
| The venue's relationship to any of it | **none** | venue access · venue collaboration · shared with your venue · your venue's view | There is no such thing and copy that names it invents a product. |

### 6.3 The copy

**Couple-facing, in product.** Quiet and practical, their words not ours.

> Share this with anyone you like. They can read it without an account.

**Couple-facing, the privacy line that travels with it.** This is the one that
must never be dropped, because it is the sentence that keeps the venue out of the
couple's head.

> Glenmara House does not see who you share this with.

**Venue-facing.**

> A couple can share their plan with their family, their suppliers or their own
> planner. You do not see who they shared it with, and you do not see what is in
> it. If a couple wants you to have something, they send it to you the same way
> they send it to anyone else.

**Coordinator-facing.**

> If a couple shares their plan with you, you get a link, the same as their
> florist. Nothing arrives in a venue dashboard. If you did not get a link, you
> cannot see it, and nobody here can.

**Objection form, for the library.** *"So we can see the plan if the couple lets
us?"*

> Only in the ordinary way. They send you a link and you read what is on it. There
> is no setting on our side that turns a couple's workspace on for you, and there
> is no request you can make to me that does it either. If that ever changes it
> will be because a couple chose it, one couple at a time.

### 6.4 Four refusals, each grounded in verified product behaviour

These are the places where warm copy would say something the code does not do.

**1. No security adjective attaches to a shared link.** `E03.01-gdpr-role-map.md`
records at lines 250 to 257 that the Tasks share-link token is stored in
plaintext as the primary key and resolved
by raw equality, while the Timeline audience path stores a hash with a
constant-time compare. One surface is hashed and the control was asserted across
all of them. Until that is uniform, copy says what a link does, never how safe it
is. Banned: "secure link", "private link", "encrypted", "only people you send it
to".

**2. Publishing is workspace-level, not per item.** Verified at
`app/src/server/db/queries.ts:181` (`getPublishedWorkspaceBySlug` maps every task
in the workspace) and `app/src/lib/public-task.ts` (the public allowlist carries
`title` and `tags`). One toggle publishes everything. Banned: "choose which parts
to share", "pick what guests see", "share just the timeline". Permitted: *"When
you publish, the whole plan goes. Titles and tags are visible. Descriptions are
not."*

**3. A published page is findable.** `app/src/app/robots.ts` disallows `/app`,
`/s`, `/share`, `/redeem`, `/welcome` and `/api`. It does not disallow `/p`, and
`/p/[slug]` sets no `noindex`. A published workspace page is crawlable. Banned:
"only the people you send it to", "private link", "unlisted". Permitted: *"A
published page can be found by anyone with the address, including a search
engine. Keep it to what you would be happy for a guest to read."*

**4. No dietary, allergy, accessibility or health information about a named
person appears in any collaboration example.** P8, hard rule. This block is where
the temptation is strongest, because "send the dietary list to the venue" is the
single most natural wedding-coordination example in existence. It is forbidden
in every example, screenshot, film frame, demo fixture, template string and
public Timeline, and no marketing copy states that such information flows to the
venue. Substitute: *"with the choices confirmed"*.

## 7. Block 5 — Keepsake

**What existed.** One ratified sentence, E09.10 §1.5, and a lifecycle model at
`studio/src/lib/venue-lifecycle.ts`. `E11.09-10` records F-5: no Keepsake surface
exists in `app/src`. So the promise is real, the words for it were one line, and
the product does not have it yet.

**The copy rule that follows from that.** Keepsake is described in the future
tense on any surface that describes the product as it is today, and in the
present tense only in the agreement, the proposal and the commercial summary,
which describe what is being bought. A venue reading a walkthrough must not be
shown a Keepsake screen that does not exist. `E11.09-10` Beat 6 already handles
this correctly: it is spoken, not shown.

### 7.1 The sentence, ratified and reusable verbatim (D-010.3)

> When the planning term ends, your workspace stays open in Keepsake mode, read
> to keep, and you can export everything in one click.

## 11. The venue copy pack, and the prohibited-claims list

**R-020 is the risk that 25 untrained venues will describe this product to
couples, and that a venue saying "yours forever" is a commercial practice under
the Consumer Protection Act 2007 which folds into the venue's own contract with
the couple.**

The mitigation R-020 records is an automated check that polices Signal Studio's
own strings. That is necessary and insufficient: the strings a venue says are not
in our repository. So the mitigation shipped here is two artifacts, and an
extension of the check so that the first of them is enforced.

1. **`E09.11-venue-copy-pack.md`** — the approved pack. Sentences a venue may use
   with a couple, in their own words, with the named prohibited-claims list
   alongside. It is written to be handed over, printed, and pinned above a desk.
2. **`copy/prohibited-claims.v1.json`** — the same prohibitions, machine-readable.
   It is the single source: the pack renders it, and the sweep enforces it, so the
   two cannot drift.

**What the pack cannot do.** It cannot stop a venue improvising, and no artifact
can. What it can do is make the correct sentence the easiest one to reach for and
put the forbidden ones in writing before the first venue is on a call with a
couple. The residual risk stays open in RAID and is not closed by this document.
