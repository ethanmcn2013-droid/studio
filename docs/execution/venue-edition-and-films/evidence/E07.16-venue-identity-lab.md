**Release:** 2026-09-01 (29 days) · **UI freeze:** 2026-08-20 (17 days) · **Copy freeze:** 2026-08-21

## Part 1 — Acceptance criteria, written before looking at the code

E07.16 reads: *"Complete Account, organisation settings, members, roles, support
history and reporting preferences."* Under D-027 point 4 the portal at launch is
invitation administration only, so these criteria describe the Account **as an
administration surface**, not as an adoption-evidence surface.

### E07.16 criteria

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| AC-1 | A named principal can reach the Account surface for exactly one venue, and the venue that principal may reach is resolved on the server from the session, never from a client-supplied identifier. |
| AC-2 | Organisation identity shows the venue's name, its edition, its term start and end, its renewal date and its account standing, every value read from `sponsors`, none hard-coded. |
| AC-3 | Venue branding on every Account surface is **the venue's name only**. No logo, no venue-written message, no free-text field a venue could write into (D-027 point 3). |
| AC-4 | Members and roles are either (a) live, backed by a persisted membership record, with role changes audited, or (b) **absent by decision**, with the surface stating that Signal Studio administers the account and no role control rendered. A role selector that governs nothing satisfies neither. |
| AC-5 | The capability matrix in `docs/venue-portal/ROLES_AND_PERMISSIONS.md` and the shipped capability list in `lib/account/roles.ts` agree, or the divergence is recorded as a decision with a date. |
| AC-6 | Support and request history is either the venue's real `sponsor_requests` rows or an explicit "nothing recorded" state. Illustrative rows never render on live data. |
| AC-7 | Reporting preferences are either persisted and honoured, or the control is absent. A control that saves to component state and says so in small print is not a preference. |
| AC-8 | No Account payload, export or page contains note, task, project, briefing, comment, attachment, collaborator or private Timeline content, and no plaintext licence code, proved by a test over the payload rather than by review. |
| AC-9 | Every mutation reachable from the Account writes an audit event naming a real actor. A mutation attributable only to "whoever held the credential" fails this criterion — that is the standing position in the `hq-per-operator-identity` operator todo, and it applies to a venue principal exactly as it applies to an operator. |

### E07.18 identity-gate criteria (the part of E07.18 that E07.16 blocks)

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| AC-G1 | Each role in the shipped matrix is exercised against each capability by an authorisation test that asserts a **request outcome**, not a pure function's return value. |
| AC-G2 | A request naming another venue's identifier returns not found, and changing a client-supplied venue identifier cannot change the server-resolved venue context. |
| AC-G3 | Withdrawing a principal's access ends its existing sessions, proved by a test. |
| AC-G4 | The venue-visible audit history is a real read of `entitlement_events` scoped to that venue, with no operator-only rows and no other venue's rows. |

### Two criteria the product gate already carries, restated so they are not lost

- **Gate criterion (product, item 11):** *"The Account and the Portal are demonstrably one surface. A single authenticated session reaches invitation administration, adoption evidence, account standing, renewal date and support history without a second login or a second product."* (`evidence/gates/product.json`)
- **Gate criterion (product, item 4):** the privacy boundary is a test, not a promise.

**Gate item 11 does not say whose session it is.** As written it is satisfied by
an operator session on a screen share. That ambiguity is load-bearing for the
decision in Part 7 and is flagged as an open question rather than resolved here.

### What is genuinely built, and should be recorded as evidence rather than rediscovered

This audit found substantially more than the handoff implied. Recorded as
candidate evidence, not as completion:

1. **A complete invitation lifecycle.** `lib/account/invitations/lifecycle.ts` (617 lines) defines six states and nine actions with a transition table, destructive-action confirm words, and a documented substrate decision: `sponsor_activations` carries the invitation, `license_codes` carries redemption truth, and the ledger outranks the record (`:6-27`). It refuses to coerce a missing timestamp into a decision (`:29-33`).
2. **A working invitation store.** `lib/account/invitations/store.ts` (638 lines) is the first reader and first writer of `sponsor_activations`, joined to `license_codes` by `sha256(code)` (`:35-43`). It never mints and never sends, and says so.
3. **Server-side search, filter and paging over the venue's whole set**, fixing the truncation defect where the panel filtered forty rows and reported the result as the total (`actions.ts:95-101`).
4. **One reviewable copy source for the distribution kit** (`invitations/copy.ts`, 304 lines) bound to D-027 point 3, D-020, D-009 point 3, D-016, D-021 and D-010, with the `welcomeLinkFor` bug fixed — the old constant pointed every couple at an invalid-code page (`:35-45`).
5. **The F-2 venue-picker leak is fixed.** `account-review.tsx:246-254` labels venues by founding place or opaque id, never by name, because consent to public naming is unknown for every account.
6. **R-016's legacy NULL-allotment case is fixed.** `project-venue-access.ts:237-246` renders `unavailable` rather than zero, and `:183-191` gives that case its own next action instead of "headroom is exhausted".

**That is E07.08, E07.09 and E07.10 substantially built.** They are built for an
operator. Whether that counts depends entirely on the question in Part 7.

## Part 3 — The finding

> **D-027 point 4 makes the portal an administration surface. There is no
> venue-authenticated route in the product, and no membership record to
> authenticate one against. Administration is fully built; the administrator
> is not.**

Supporting facts, each verified:

- Studio carries **no Clerk SDK**. Its `package.json` dependencies are eleven packages and none is `@clerk/*`. It reads the shared `__session` cookie as a raw string to decide a marketing redirect (`proxy.ts:84-87, 110-112`) and nothing more.
- Studio has **no mailer**. It renders seventeen email templates (`src/emails/templates/`) and sends none; the Resend client lives in `app/src/server/email.ts`.
- Clerk **Organizations are not in use** in either repository. `app/src/app/api/webhooks/clerk/route.ts:98` explicitly ignores `organization.*` events.
- `sponsor_members` appears in six documents and zero source files.
- `studio/docs/account/LIVE_HQ_PREVIEW.md:5` lists "sponsor auth" under *Still closed*, and `:49` schedules `sponsor_members` + Clerk for "Phase C". Line 3 says "complete **for this cycle**". The gating was deliberate and is documented; it was not an oversight.
- `ADR-008:128-134` states the same conclusion in the same words: *"None of this is reachable by a venue... There is no venue-facing route, because there is no `sponsor_members` table to authenticate a venue person against."*

### One fact that should change how the shared password is thought about

`/hq` holds roughly fifty routes. Among them: `cap-table`, `financial-model`,
`loan-pack`, `incorporation`, `data-room`, `crm`, `vault`, `founders-circle`.
**One password opens all of them.** No venue may ever hold it, no venue-facing
route may ever sit under `/hq`, and no option below proposes either. Stated
because "just give the venue the HQ link" is the cheapest-looking answer in the
room and it is catastrophic.

## Part 5 — Four options

Costs are working days for one Claude Code lane, stated as estimates and **not**
as approved effort points (D-015 Q3 sets points when a specification is written).
Seventeen days remain to UI freeze, and WP-05 also owes E07.01, .02, .03, .07,
.17 and .18 in that window.

---

### Option A — the venue link. No account, no password, no member record.

**What a venue does on day one.** The coordinator opens a bookmark. There is no
sign-in page, no password to lose and nobody to add. The page shows this venue's
invitations, their state, and the next couple to issue for. Closing the tab ends
nothing; the link is the key.

**The mechanism.** A signed, venue-scoped, expiring token in the path:
`signalstudio.ie/a/<token>`. `createHmac` already exists in `lib/entitlements-db/pure.ts:1`,
so no dependency is added. The token payload is a sponsor id, an issue time, an
expiry and a capability set. A new proxy branch verifies it before the route
renders; `/hq` is untouched. Signal HQ issues, rotates and revokes links from the
Access console, and every issue writes an `export` audit event because bytes that
open a venue's register have left the building.

**Cost to build against 2026-08-20.** Three to five days. Token mint and verify
plus tests (1), the public route and proxy branch (1), lifting the existing
panels out of `/hq` chrome into a venue-facing shell (1–2), copy pass and
accessibility (1). No migration. No new dependency. No mailer.

**Cost to operate across 25 venues.** Low and lumpy. Twenty-five links to issue
once. Rotation when a coordinator leaves, which for wedding venues is a real and
recurring event. Every rotation is an operator action and a message to the venue.

**How it fails.** *It fails by being forwarded.* A coordinator sends the link to
a supplier, a colleague, a WhatsApp group. Anyone holding the URL is the venue.
If the surface can reveal plaintext codes — and `revealInvitationLink` in
`store.ts` does exactly that — a forwarded link hands out live sponsored access.
The second failure is quieter and worse: **the audit ledger can only say "the
holder of link 7"**, which is precisely the objection the `hq-per-operator-identity`
todo raises against the shared HQ password, reproduced on the venue side. It
fails AC-9 for the same reason and by the same argument.

**Both failures are removable by narrowing the link to read-only** — no reveal,
no mutation, no request. Then a forwarded link exposes a venue's own invitation
states and nothing else, and there is no mutation to misattribute. That variant
is A′ and it is materially safer than A.

**What it forecloses.** Nothing structurally. A token is not a schema.
`sponsor_members` can land later and the link can become a sign-in hint. It does
foreclose *per-person* accountability inside a venue, permanently, for as long as
it is the only mechanism.

**Consequences.** `sponsor_members`: unbuilt, and the specification stands
untouched. Capability matrix: collapses to one capability set per link, so
`roles.ts` keeps governing nothing and AC-5's divergence must be closed by
decision rather than by code. Audit history: satisfiable for AC-G4 (the venue's
own events, scoped by sponsor id) with the actor column reading as a link rather
than a person.

**E07 tasks.** Satisfies the venue-visible half of E07.02 and E07.09. Leaves
E07.16's members and roles open by decision. Leaves AC-9 and AC-G1/G3 open.

### Option B — a real venue account, with members and roles. *(The bold one.)*

**What a venue does on day one.** The owner receives an invitation to Signal
Studio, sets a password, signs in at `signalstudio.ie/account`, and adds the
coordinator as a Manager. When that coordinator leaves, the owner removes her
and her sessions end. The venue owns its own access, and Signal Studio is not in
the loop.

**The mechanism.** Build `sponsor_members` exactly as specified at
`ROLES_AND_PERMISSIONS.md:37-61`: opaque Clerk subject id, role, status,
invited-by, five timestamps, no email stored. Add `@clerk/nextjs` to studio —
the shared instance already sets `__session` on `*.signalstudio.ie`, so studio
can verify a real session rather than sniff a cookie. Resolve one active sponsor
context server-side per request using the pattern already at `actions.ts:70-76`.
Add a `venue` actor kind to `guard.ts` so venue mutations enter the same ledger
and the same blast-radius envelope operators pass through.

**Cost to build against 2026-08-20.** Eight to fourteen days, and that is the
honest range, not a hedge. Table plus migration plus a migration test (2). Clerk
in studio, new env keys across three environments, a new proxy layer that must
not disturb `hqGate` or the marketing redirect (2). Membership CRUD with the
one-owner floor and single-use sponsor-scoped invitations (2). Member invitation
delivery, which studio cannot do today because it has no mailer (1–2). Venue
actor kind and audit verbs in `entitlements-db`, outside this package's column
(1). The seven authorisation-test classes the doc itself lists at
`ROLES_AND_PERMISSIONS.md:80-90` (2). Venue-facing shell, empty and error states,
accessibility (2). **Against seventeen days that also owe six other E07 tasks,
this does not fit, and a partial version of it is worse than either neighbour:
half an auth system is an auth system with a hole.**

**Cost to operate across 25 venues.** Highest of the four, and it arrives on day
one rather than later. Password resets, wrong-person-invited, owner-left-and-took-the-account,
"we never got the email". Every one of those is a support conversation with a
customer who paid a thousand euro and expected patronage rather than software.

**How it fails.** It fails by not being ready. A membership system shipped in the
last week before a UI freeze, with no soak time, guarding the surface where
sponsored access is handed out, is where a real access-control bug lands. There
is a second, subtler failure: **the venue coordinator and the couple share one
Clerk identity space.** A coordinator who is planning her own wedding holds one
identity in two roles, and nothing in the current model separates them.

**What it forecloses.** Almost nothing — it is the destination. It forecloses
choosing A or C later, because once venues have accounts, taking them away is a
downgrade a customer notices.

**Consequences.** `sponsor_members`: built. Capability matrix: becomes real, and
AC-5's divergence must be closed *before* the build rather than after, because
the doc and the code disagree about whether a viewer may export. Audit history:
fully satisfiable, per person.

**E07 tasks.** Satisfies E07.16 outright, satisfies AC-1 through AC-9 and AC-G1
through AC-G4, and unlocks E07.18. It is the only option that does.

### Option C — Signal HQ administers. The venue receives evidence, not a login.

**What a venue does on day one.** Nothing. The codes arrive with a short note.
The coordinator hands one to each couple as they book. When more are needed she
emails Ethan and they arrive. Once a period she receives a one-page record of
what was issued and what was taken up, with her venue's name on it. There is
nothing to log into because there is nothing for her to run.

**The mechanism.** No new identity at all. The already-built invitation
administration stays where it is, operated by a named HQ operator through the
existing fail-closed actor path (`actions.ts:47-59`). The venue-facing artifact
is generated from the live snapshot through the existing export route, which
already runs the privacy contract over its own output (`download/route.ts:48, 67`).
Venue-initiated actions become `sponsor_requests` rows, a table that already
exists and is already migrated.

**Cost to build against 2026-08-20.** Two to three days. A venue-facing evidence
artifact with a name-only header and copy that passes the venue-copy sweep (1–2).
A stated "Signal Studio administers this account" surface so AC-4 is satisfied by
decision rather than by an inert button, and removal of the reporting-preference
control that saves nothing (0.5). Copy and accessibility pass (0.5).

**Cost to operate across 25 venues.** This is the number that decides between C
and everything else, so it is worked rather than asserted. Twenty-five venues at
roughly 40–80 weddings each is 1,000–2,000 couples a year, and the largest in the
45-minute ring run 150–250 on their own (D-020, R-021). **If codes were issued
one at a time by Ethan, that is 4 to 8 interruptions per working day and Option C
is indefensible.** They are not: codes are minted in batches and handed over as a
pack (`venue-codes-ready` template, `scripts/issue-codes.ts`), so the venue hands
them out itself and only *replenishment* returns to HQ. At batches sized to a
quarter, steady state is roughly 25 top-ups per quarter, about two per working
week, each a few minutes. **That is sustainable for one person and it stops being
sustainable somewhere past 60–80 venues** — which is beyond the Founding 25 and
beyond this release.

**How it fails.** It fails at the demo, not in operation. A venue asks "can we
see it?" and the honest answer is "I'll show you" rather than "here's your
login". Under D-020's ratified sales sentence — *nothing for your coordinator to
track* — that answer is on-message. Under E07.02's title, "Redesign Overview so
'Invite a couple' is the principal action", it is a venue-facing Overview that no
venue reaches. The second failure is a single point of person: Ethan is the
administration path, and Ethan taking a week off is the outage.

**What it forecloses.** Nothing. Every other option remains available at any
time, and A′ in particular is a two-day follow-on from here.

**Consequences.** `sponsor_members`: unbuilt, specification intact, explicitly
deferred with a date. Capability matrix: recorded as a specification for a
surface that does not exist yet, and `roles.ts` is honestly described as a
fixture-review construct. Audit history: already complete and already correct —
one authenticated principal, named, fail-closed, every action in
`entitlement_events`. **Option C is the only option where AC-9 is already
satisfied rather than newly at risk.**

**E07 tasks.** Records E07.08, E07.09 and E07.10 as substantially built (for an
operator). Answers E07.16's members, roles and preferences by decision. Makes
E07.18's permissions and audit criteria satisfiable, because a single named
principal is a permission model that can actually be tested. Leaves E07.02's
venue-facing Overview open, and leaves AC-1 open by design.

### Wildcard W — delete the invitation. One venue link the couple redeems.

**What a venue does on day one.** It pastes one line into the booking
confirmation it already sends: *your planning workspace is here*, with a link.
That is the entire integration, and it is done once rather than per couple. The
venue never issues, never tracks, never runs out and never asks for more.

**The mechanism.** One durable per-venue URL, `signalstudio.ie/w/<venue-slug>`.
The couple identifies itself at redemption, which it already does — redemption
hands off to Clerk sign-up in `app` today (`redeem/[code]/page.tsx:128`). The
couple supplies its booking reference at that step; the column already exists
(`sponsor_activations.sponsor_local_reference`, `schema.ts:283-284`). The
per-couple code object disappears. D-020 point 1 already ratifies the control
that makes this survivable: **fair use notifies, never blocks** — issuance above
the internal ceiling alerts Signal HQ and keeps issuing.

**Cost to build against 2026-08-20.** Four to six days, and one of them is in
another package's column. Public venue route and slug resolution (1). A
redemption path that mints against a venue rather than consuming a code — this is
`app`'s redeem flow and `entitlements-db/codes.ts`, both outside WP-05 (2).
Booking-reference capture at redemption (1). Ceiling alerting on an open link,
which is not the same as ceiling alerting on operator-driven minting (1).

**Cost to operate across 25 venues.** Lowest of the four by a wide margin.
Twenty-five links, issued once. No replenishment, no rotation, no per-couple
action by anyone.

**How it fails.** *It fails by leaking.* A booking confirmation gets forwarded,
posted to a wedding forum, or indexed. Every stranger who follows it gets
eighteen months of the full suite. At the €1,000 founding rate against a
break-even of roughly 94 couples (D-020, R-021), a link that leaks to a few
hundred people is a materially expensive year. The booking reference is the
control, and a booking reference is guessable unless it is checked against
something, which the venue would then have to supply — reintroducing exactly the
work this option deletes.

**What it forecloses.** The invitation object, and with it E07.08's lifecycle,
E07.09's stale-invitation alerts and the delivery-state ladder. That work is
built. Choosing W after building it is a real, countable cost: 1,255 lines of
lifecycle and store, plus their tests. It also forecloses per-couple activation
evidence, because the register becomes a redemption register rather than an
invitation register — thinner, but still enough to satisfy "the venue sees
activation evidence" in D-001 point 4.

**Consequences.** `sponsor_members`: never needed. Capability matrix: never
needed. Audit history: thinner and honest — redemptions, not invitations.

**E07 tasks.** Deletes the premise of E07.08 and E07.09 rather than satisfying
them. Satisfies E07.10 with one line instead of a kit. Makes E07.16 moot.

**Why it is on the sheet.** Because it is the only option that makes D-020's
ratified sentence — *no seat count, no per-couple maths, nothing for your
coordinator to track* — literally true rather than nearly true, and because the
control it depends on is already decided rather than newly proposed.

## Part 7 — Recommendation

**Pick C for 1 September. Add A′ as a two-day follow-on if the first venue
conversation shows C reads as thin. Do not start B before the freeze.**

The reasoning, in the order it actually matters.

**1. C is the only option that starts from a passing grade on the hardest
criterion.** AC-9 says a mutation must name a real actor. Option C already
satisfies it: `actions.ts:47-59` refuses to record an invitation change against
nobody, and it was written deliberately rather than inherited. A and W introduce
a principal the ledger cannot name. B introduces one it can, in the last week
before a freeze, with no soak time, on the surface that hands out sponsored
access. Seventeen days before a UI freeze is the wrong week to make the access
ledger newly uncertain.

**2. The commercial position already sold C.** D-020's ratified venue-facing
sentence is *nothing for your coordinator to track*. The live `/venues` page says
*Nothing for your team to run* (`:126`), *Light by design. We come to you, not
the other way around* (`:560`), and *we send the codes and a short note you can
pass to couples* (`:566`). It describes the activation register as *in
development* (`:361`) rather than promising a login. **C is not a retreat from
the promise. C is the promise.** B is the option that contradicts it — a venue
told nothing runs and then asked to administer members and roles has been sold
one thing and given another.

**3. C makes the audit-first instruction pay.** Under C, E07.08, E07.09 and
E07.10 become recorded evidence for work that already exists, and E07.16's
unbuilt half is answered by a decision instead of a build. Under B, the same
built work has to be re-pointed at a new principal and re-tested. D-015 Q2 calls
the audit the largest single lever on the critical path; C is the option that
pulls it.

**4. C forecloses nothing and B forecloses everything.** C keeps A, A′, B and W
all available after 1 September. B is the one door that does not open again:
accounts, once given, cannot be taken back without the customer noticing.

**5. C's real weakness is honest and bounded.** It is that a venue cannot look at
its own register without asking. That weakness has a two-day fix (A′), and the
right moment to decide whether to spend those two days is after the first three
venue conversations, not now. Its second weakness — one person as the
administration path — is bounded by batch issuance and stops being tolerable
somewhere past 60–80 venues, which is beyond this release and should be recorded
as a risk with that number attached.

**What would change this recommendation.** Any one of these should reopen it:

- A founder position that a venue-reachable surface is non-negotiable for the sales conversation. Then it is A′, not B — A′ buys the demo for two days and does not touch the ledger.
- Evidence that venues will not accept a batch-and-replenish rhythm. Then it is W, because W removes replenishment entirely, and W's leak risk is cheaper to carry than a broken sales motion.
- A decision to move the release date. B becomes correct the moment there is soak time for it.

**What must be decided regardless of which option wins.** AC-5's divergence
between `ROLES_AND_PERMISSIONS.md:18-33` and `roles.ts:3-16` — whether a viewer
may export reports, and where `manage_invitations` sits in the matrix. It is a
one-line decision and it blocks any option that keeps a role model.
