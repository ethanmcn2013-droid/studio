## 0. What this settles, and what it does not

**Settles.** The shape of the one call that turns an opened film into a
proposal: how long it is, what order it runs in, what is asked, what is never
asked, how eligibility is decided without putting a number to the venue, what is
shown on screen and in what sequence, what is refused on screen, what is
recorded afterwards, and what the call hands to the proposal.

**Does not settle.** The proposal, the order form and the same-day follow-up
(E11.11). The full objection library (E11.12). The CRM stage machine and its
entry and exit criteria (E11.01, E11.02). The booking flow and confirmation
sequence (E11.08). The sending identity (E11.04).

**How to use it.** Before a call: §2 and §3. On the call: §4 is the clock, §5 is
the questions, §12 is the screen. After the call: §9. Reviewing a call that went
badly: §6, §14 and §17.

**The channel.** Every call is remote, by video or phone. There are no venue
visits and no physical leave-behinds (D-013.1, D-018). The call is booked from
the personalised film landing page, and the booking flow is E11.08.

# Part One — E11.09 · The discovery call

## 1. What the call is, and what it is not

One call, thirty minutes, with the person who can sign or the person one step
from them. It carries the discovery and the walkthrough together, because a
venue owner who has already watched a film should not be asked for a second
meeting before seeing the thing.

**It is a fit conversation with a demonstration inside it.** It is not a demo
call, not a discovery call in the sales-process sense, and not a pitch. The
founder does most of the listening in the first third and most of the talking in
the second.

**Three jobs, in order.**

1. Confirm the venue is eligible under D-012.4, using research and conversation,
   never a declared number.
2. Learn where the planning admin actually lands in this venue, in their own
   words, so the walkthrough is aimed rather than general.
3. Show one wedding end to end, state the offer with its conditions attached,
   and either offer the 14-day hold or close the conversation honestly.

**What the call is allowed to fail at.** Ending with "this is not for you" is a
successful call. A venue under the honest floor who signs anyway is a renewal
failure booked twelve months early, and the founding rate is locked, so the
mistake does not correct itself (D-009.3, R-021).

**Capacity.** Twenty-five venues per cohort (D-017). If five to eight of a cohort
book a call, that is three to four hours of founder time per cohort. The call
does not scale by being shortened; it scales by the film and the landing page
doing the qualifying work first.

## 2. Before the call — the research card

Qualification is decided on research (E10.03, E10.09, E10.11), not on the call.
The call confirms. This ordering is the whole trick: a venue that has been
researched properly is never interrogated, and a venue that has not been
researched properly cannot be rescued by better questions.

The card is one screen, read in the two minutes before dialling. It comes from
the CRM and the researched universe, never from a project file (`PROJECT.md`
§21: Signal HQ and the CRM own venue contacts).

| Field | Source | Why it is on the card |
|---|---|---|
| Venue type, against the D-012.4 in and out lists | E10.02 | The eligibility question that is settled before the call |
| Volume band: below the floor · around the floor · comfortably above · large | E10.09 | A band, never a figure. Bands are what research can honestly support |
| The evidence the band rests on | E10.09, E10.10 | So a verdict can be checked later by someone else |
| Likely buyer and secondary contact, with roles | E10.07, E10.08 | Whether the person on the call can sign |
| The one honest reason this venue belongs in the founding outreach | E10.11 | The first sentence of the call |
| The specific observation used in the outreach email | E11.05 | Continuity. The venue should recognise the sender |
| Cohort and place in it | E10.12, E10.13 | Which cohort's hold clock applies |
| Anything already said by email | CRM | So nothing is asked twice |

**If the card cannot be filled, the call is not booked.** A call taken on a
venue nobody researched is where the wedding-count question gets asked, because
the founder is trying to recover on the call what should have been settled
beforehand.

## 3. The eligibility screen, run without asking for a number

### 3.1 What D-012.4 actually says

**In:** dedicated wedding venues, country houses, castles, hotels with a real
weddings operation, barn and estate venues.

**Out:** restaurants, pubs, marquee hire, town hotels doing occasional weddings,
and anywhere under roughly twenty weddings a year. Below that, €1,000 a year
cannot be justified to them honestly, and a venue that cannot justify it will
not renew.

### 3.2 The rule that governs how the floor is used

**The roughly-twenty floor is a test Signal Studio applies to itself. It is
never a question put to the venue, never a condition of the offer, never a field
on any form before signature, and never a number in the agreement.**

Four reasons, each independently sufficient.

1. **D-020.4 already decided where the number lives.** The venue's annual
   wedding count is collected **after signature**, as an onboarding field, with
   the ratified line: *this sets your issuance ceiling, it never sets your
   price, and it never changes at renewal.* Asking for it before signature
   contradicts a ratified decision.
2. **The price does not depend on it.** D-020 rejected the recommendation to
   route high-volume venues to the standard rate. Ethan: *"I don't care if they
   have forty weddings or if they have two hundred and fifty weddings. They're
   locked in at a thousand."* A question that implies the answer affects the
   price is false.
3. **A declared number becomes a term.** A figure given on a sales call, written
   into a CRM and then referenced in a proposal is a representation, whatever
   anyone intended. The entitlement model carries no number (D-020, P5), and the
   call must not put one there through the back door.
4. **It is commercially sensitive data asked before there is a relationship.**
   Annual wedding volume is close to annual revenue for a wedding venue. A
   stranger asking for it in the first thirty minutes reads exactly as it
   sounds.

**If the venue volunteers a number,** which many will, it is heard, thanked and
recorded in the CRM as an impression with the words "volunteered, not verified,
not a term". It never appears in the proposal, the order form or the agreement,
and it never changes the price.

### 3.3 The observable proxies that answer the volume question without asking it

These are gathered in research (E10.09, E10.10) and confirmed by eye during the
call. Any four of the seven put a venue comfortably above the floor.

1. A dedicated weddings section with structured packages and a downloadable
   brochure, rather than a weddings paragraph on an events page.
2. A named wedding coordinator, wedding manager or events team, rather than
   "contact us".
3. A wedding-specific enquiry route: its own form, its own address, its own
   phone extension.
4. Published availability language that implies a booked calendar: dates for
   next year and the year after, midweek availability offered as an alternative,
   waiting-list wording.
5. Exclusive-use, multi-day or accommodation-inclusive structure. A venue that
   sells a whole weekend is running a wedding operation, not hosting occasional
   weddings.
6. A real-weddings gallery or supplier list with dated entries across recent
   months, not a single styled shoot.
7. Accommodation room count and a room-block practice, where the venue has
   rooms.

**The disqualifying signals, equally observable:** weddings framed as one of
several function types with no dedicated route; a bar or restaurant as the
primary business; hire-only operation with no fixed site; a gallery whose most
recent dated wedding is more than a year old; a single function room in a town
hotel with corporate events alongside.

### 3.4 The four conversational questions, none of which asks for a figure

Asked in the listening block (§4, movement 2), woven into the conversation
rather than delivered as a checklist. Each returns a volume band without a
number changing hands.

| Question | What the answer tells you |
|---|---|
| "Is the wedding side most of what the house does now, or one part of a wider events year?" | Whether weddings are the operation or a line in it. The single strongest signal, and it invites a proud answer rather than a defensive one |
| "Are you booking mostly Saturdays, or across the week too?" | Saturdays only caps the year near the floor. Midweek and Friday bookings put a venue well above it |
| "How far ahead are you taking bookings at the moment?" | Lead time is a demand proxy that venue owners answer freely and precisely, because they are proud of it |
| "Is there one person who carries the couples through, or does it move around the team?" | A named coordinator implies enough volume to justify one. It also identifies the person who can kill this (§5, Group C) |

**Read them together, never singly.** "Weddings are most of what we do" plus "we
are booking into 2028" plus a named coordinator is a venue comfortably above the
floor, with no figure requested and none given.

### 3.5 The verdict, and what happens on each

| Verdict | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Eligible** | Type is on the D-012.4 in-list and the band is at or above the floor | Continue the call. Offer the hold at the end |
| **Eligible, thin** | Type is in, band sits on the floor | Continue, and say the honest arithmetic out loud (§3.6). Offer the hold only if they reach for it |
| **Not eligible, type** | Restaurant, pub, marquee hire, town hotel doing occasional weddings | Say so on the call. Do not send a proposal |
| **Not eligible, volume** | Type is in, but the venue is clearly below the floor | Say so on the call. Do not send a proposal |
| **Undecidable** | Research was thin and the call did not settle it | End without a proposal, and either fix the research or drop the account. Never resolve it by asking for the count |

### 3.6 The honest-arithmetic line, for a venue on the floor

Per-wedding arithmetic is sales language, not a rule, and **Signal computes it,
the venue never supplies it** (D-020, E09.10 §1.6).

> A thousand a year, VAT included, works out around fifty euro a wedding for a
> venue your size. If that reads as expensive to you, say so now rather than in
> twelve months. I would rather have twenty-five venues who think it is obviously
> worth it than twenty-five who had to be talked into it.

### 3.7 The line when the answer is no

Said warmly, on the call, without a follow-up sequence attached. A venue told no
honestly is a referral source; a venue put into a nurture sequence after being
told no is neither.

> I do not think this is right for you, and I would rather say that than sell you
> something you would not renew. It is built for venues where weddings are the
> main event and there is enough of a run of them that a planning year is a real
> thing. If that changes, or if someone comes to mind who this does fit, my
> address is on the note I sent.

Mark the account `not_eligible` with the reason. No further touches. This is the
respectful stopping rule (D-013.4) applied earlier than the email sequence.

## 4. The call structure — thirty minutes, six movements

The clock is written down so the walkthrough cannot eat the offer, which is the
standard failure of a founder-run demo call.

| Min | Movement | Who talks | The job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | **Open and frame** | Founder | Name the reason you wrote to them. Set the shape and give permission to say no |
| 2–10 | **Their planning year** | Venue, mostly | The listening block. Eligibility confirms itself here. §5, Groups A to D |
| 10–13 | **The mechanic, three sentences** | Founder | Buyer and user split, before any screen |
| 13–25 | **The walkthrough** | Founder | Part Two, seven beats, on the canonical fixture |
| 25–28 | **The offer, with its conditions attached** | Founder | Price, lock, entitlement, term, Keepsake, the 25 |
| 28–30 | **The next step** | Both | The hold, or the honest no. What actually happens next |

**The open, said roughly like this:**

> Thanks for the time. I wrote to you because [the one honest reason from
> E10.11]. Here is the shape of the next half hour. I will ask you about your
> planning year for about ten minutes, because I do not want to show you the
> wrong thing. Then I will show you one wedding from start to finish, about ten
> minutes. Then I will tell you exactly what it costs and what it asks of your
> team, and you can tell me whether it is worth continuing. If it is not, that is
> a fine outcome and it will not cost you a follow-up sequence.

**The mechanic, in three sentences, before any screen is shared:**

> You pay once a year and you give it to every couple who books with you. They
> get a private place to plan their wedding, with your name on it quietly, and
> they never see a price or a card form. You see whether they opened it and how
> the group as a whole is using it, and you never see what is inside it.

**The offer block, said as one block so no condition arrives late:**

> A thousand euro a year, prepaid, VAT included. That is five hundred a year less
> than the standard price, for as long as you stay, and the rate holds for as
> long as the agreement renews without a gap. It follows the property rather than
> the company, so it survives a rebrand or a change of ownership. Every couple
> who books their wedding with you gets a workspace while your licence is
> current. There is no seat count and nothing for your coordinator to track. Each
> couple has it for eighteen months from when they open it, or three months past
> the wedding, whichever is later, and after that it stays open, read-only, to
> keep, with a one-click export that belongs to them. Anything already opened
> keeps its full term whatever happens between you and me. There are twenty-five
> founding places. The number is assigned when the invoice is paid, not when the
> agreement is signed.

Sources, in order: D-009.1, D-021, D-009.2, D-009.3, D-009.4, D-020, P6,
D-010.2, D-022, D-010.3, D-020.2, D-009.6.

**The close, and then silence:**

> If you want it, I will hold a founding place for fourteen days and send you the
> proposal and the agreement today, so you can read the paperwork before you
> decide rather than after. If you would rather think about it, that is fine too,
> and I will send the same documents anyway.

Then stop talking. The hold is fourteen days, once, extendable by the founder
personally (D-009.7, E11.14). No urgency, no scarcity theatre, no "places are
going" (P13).

## 5. The questions

Twelve questions exist. A good call uses seven. The listening block is eight
minutes and the founder should be talking for under two of them.

**Group A — eligibility, woven in, never listed.** The four questions in §3.4.
They are conversational and come first because they are the easiest questions a
venue owner ever gets asked.

**Group B — where the admin actually lands.**

| Question | Why | A good answer sounds like | A worrying answer sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Walk me through what happens after a couple signs the booking. What is the next thing your team sends them?" | Locates the exact seam this product sits in, and gives the walkthrough its opening frame | A welcome pack, a document, a named person taking over | "Nothing much until closer to the time." There may be no felt problem to solve |
| "Between the booking and the final month, what do couples come back to you about most?" | The repeated-question pain, in their words, not ours. These words go straight into the proposal | Timings, supplier access, final numbers, what is included | "They do not really." Either a very calm operation or a coordinator not on this call |
| "When something is agreed with a couple in a meeting or on a call, where does it live afterwards?" | Whether the planning record is scattered. The honest version of the pain, without leading | Email threads, a spreadsheet, a shared document, "in my head" | "We have a system for that." Ask what it is and listen properly. It may be a genuine incumbent, which is E11.12 territory |
| "Is there a point in the year where this reliably gets heavy?" | Seasonality, and whether they can hear their own problem described | The final month, the week of, the run-up to numbers | A shrug |

**Group C — who signs, and who can kill it.**

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| "If this is something you want, is it your call or is there someone else who would want to see it?" | Asked plainly, once, early enough to fix. Never "who is the decision-maker" |
| "Who would actually hand this to a couple? Would you want them on a call with me?" | The coordinator is the most under-served audience in this programme and the person most able to stop it (E09.10 §3.2). Offering them a call is a strong signal and costs twelve minutes (§15) |

**Group D — the two questions that predict a bad fit.**

| Question | Why | The answer that should slow the call down |
|---|---|---|
| "Have you tried anything like this before?" | A venue that has bought and abandoned a planning tool knows something worth hearing, and it is usually about adoption rather than features | "We tried something and nobody used it." Do not argue. Ask what happened and put the answer in the follow-up |
| "If your couples used this and it went well, what would you notice?" | Tests whether they can name their own outcome. A venue that cannot is buying a feeling and will not renew | Silence, or an answer about their own marketing rather than the couple's year |

## 6. Questions never asked

Each is a defect, not a preference. Numbered so a call review can cite one.

**Q-N1 — "How many weddings do you do a year?"** and every variant, including
"roughly", "ballpark", "give or take", and any form asking them to confirm a
threshold. Reason: §3.2, all four grounds. Substitute: §3.4.

**Q-N2 — Revenue, average spend per wedding, package prices, or anything that
values a booking.** Reason: commercially sensitive, and irrelevant, because the
price does not vary (D-020, R-021). Asking implies it might.

**Q-N3 — Anything about a specific couple.** Names, dates, contact details, what
they are like, whether one would make a good first test. Signal Studio never
learns which couples hold real bookings (`E09.02` §1) and the call must not be
where it starts.

**Q-N4 — Anything about guests, dietary requirements, allergies, accessibility
or health.** Hard rule, not a clause. These are Article 9 special categories
concerning people who agreed to nothing (R-017, P8). Not as an example, not as a
"you know the sort of thing", not as a demonstration prompt. If the venue raises
dietary handling, the answer is §8, Answer C.

**Q-N5 — "What is your budget for something like this?"** The price is one
number, it is published, and it does not move. Asking implies a negotiation that
does not exist.

**Q-N6 — "Who else are you looking at?"** and competitor-baiting generally.

**Q-N7 — Any question whose purpose is to manufacture urgency.** "When would you
need this in place by", asked to create a deadline rather than to plan, and
anything setting up a scarcity line later (P13).

**Q-N8 — Any question that presumes the sale.** "When you are set up, who would
send the first invitation?" is a closing technique wearing a discovery costume.
The plain version, "who would actually hand this to a couple", is in Group C and
is fine, because it does not assume the answer.

**Q-N9 — Anything asking them to evaluate Signal Studio's credibility to their
own advantage.** "What would it take to get you to yes." The offer is the offer.

## 7. Judging the call afterwards

Five signals, three states each. Deliberately not a numeric lead score: no
fabricated precision (`PROJECT.md` §11.6), and a two-digit number invented from a
thirty-minute conversation is exactly that.

| Signal | Proceed | Hold | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type and band** | In-list type, band at or above the floor, evidence recorded | Band undecided after the call | Out-list type, or clearly below the floor |
| **The admin is real and lands somewhere** | They named the repeated questions without being led | They agreed it happens but could not describe it | They described a calm operation and meant it |
| **The signer** | On the call, or named and one step away with a route | Vague about who decides | Cannot or will not say |
| **The privacy boundary** | Understood it, and did not push back on not seeing content | Asked twice, accepted it | Wanted per-couple visibility and did not let it go. This venue is buying oversight, and that product does not exist here (D-001.4) |
| **No promise was needed that cannot be kept** | True | One question went to "I will find out and tell you" | The call only stayed alive because something was implied. Reset it in writing before anything else happens |

Any single **Stop** ends the opportunity. Two **Hold** signals mean the proposal
waits for a second short conversation, usually with the coordinator.

## 8. The questions that arrive on this call

**The four questions every venue asks are already answered, verbatim, in
`E09.10-copy-hierarchy.md` §6.** They are not restated here, because two copies
of an approved answer is how one of them drifts.

- Answer 1, "Are you GDPR compliant?" Usable in its interim form until E03.01,
  E03.04 and E03.05 exist as sendable drafts and the R-017 position is decided.
- Answer 2, "Has a solicitor reviewed this?" Usable now, as written, and it is
  the only one of the four fully supported today.
- Answer 3, "Are you insured?" **Version B only.** No policy is recorded in this
  programme. Version A may not be used without a certificate in hand. This is the
  weakest of the four and the most likely to be escalated to the venue's own
  adviser (OQ-2 in `E09.10` §9).
- Answer 4, "Where is our data held?" Spoken version only, **with the EU-hosting
  sentence removed**, until E03.05 verifies regions and transfer bases (P15).

**The rule underneath all four, always available and never weak in this market:**

> I do not know, and I would rather find out and tell you than guess.

Three further questions arrive on a call that `E09.10` §6 does not cover.

**Answer A — "What happens if you stop, or if this does not work out?"**
Say it before it is asked, in the offer block. D-020.2 requires it stated
unprompted.

> Anything a couple has already opened keeps its full term, and then its
> read-only Keepsake, whatever happens between you and me. Only new invitations
> stop. And every couple has a one-click export that belongs to them, so nothing
> anyone wrote is trapped with me.

Never add a promise about how long the service itself exists (P1, D-001.16).

**Answer B — "Can we see how our couples are getting on?"**
The negative half travels with the positive half, at the same weight, in the same
breath (E09.10 §4).

> You see how many invitations went out and how many were opened, and you see how
> the group as a whole is using it. You never see a couple's planning content, you
> never see a row per couple, and when the numbers are small enough that a figure
> could point at one couple, the figure is held back rather than shown. If a
> couple asks your coordinator to look at something inside their workspace, the
> honest answer is that nobody at your venue can see it, and neither can I.

Sources: D-001.4, D-011.3, P9. And **never** "your couples" as a metric label;
the denominator is invitations redeemed (`E09.02` §1).

**Answer C — "Could it collect dietary requirements for us?"**
The most likely product request on this call, and the one that must not be
answered with a yes.

> Not in the shape you are probably picturing, and that is deliberate. Dietary
> and allergy information is health information about guests who have not agreed
> to anything, and the right place for it is a step where the guest gives their
> own answer rather than the couple typing it about them. That is being worked
> on. What I will not do is quietly route that kind of information about your
> guests through to your account because it made a demo look better.

Never promise a date. Never demonstrate it. The architectural fix is owned by
E03.04, E05.03 and E06.01 (R-017, P8).

## 9. After the call, the same day

**Recorded, in the CRM** (E11.01 owns the stages; E11.02 owns entry and exit
criteria; this section names only what discovery must produce):

1. The eligibility verdict from §3.5, and the evidence it rests on, so a later
   session can check it without asking.
2. The buyer and the coordinator as named account contacts, in the CRM only.
   Venue contacts never enter project files (`PROJECT.md` §21).
3. The admin story in the venue's own words, quoted, not paraphrased. These
   sentences are the raw material for the proposal (E11.11) and for the
   objection library (E11.12).
4. Objections raised, against the E11.12 categories.
5. Whether a hold was offered, and the date it expires.
6. Any question that got "I will find out and tell you", with the answer owed and
   a date. This is the single most important line in the record: an unanswered
   question left unrecorded becomes an implied promise.

**Never recorded:**

- Any couple's name or details, present or prospective (Q-N3).
- Anything about guests, dietary, allergy, accessibility or health (Q-N4, P8).
- A volunteered wedding count as anything other than "volunteered, not verified,
  not a term" (§3.2).
- Any figure, claim or commitment in the follow-up that was not said on the call.

**Sent the same day, per D-013 and E11.11:** the proposal page, the agreement and
the schedule, so the venue reads the paperwork before deciding rather than after
(Answer 2, E09.10 §6). Every price stated inclusive of VAT (P12). No attachments
in the first email if the sending identity's reputation is still warming (R-010).

# Part Two — E11.10 · The live walkthrough

## 10. What the walkthrough is

Ten to twelve minutes inside the thirty-minute call. **One wedding, from
invitation to Keepsake, on the canonical fixture.** Not a feature tour, not a
product map, not four products introduced one at a time.

**The ordering rule that carries the whole sequence: the venue never sees a
dashboard first.** The couple's experience is the product. The venue's account is
the proof that the product is safe. Leading with the account teaches the venue
that they bought a reporting tool, which is not what is being sold and is the
thing D-001.5 exists to prevent.

**Three standing refusals on screen.**

1. **No live venue data.** No other venue's name, account or figures appears at
   any point. See §13, gate 1, which is the reason this is stated first.
2. **No real couple, ever.** Glenmara House, Mara and Finn are synthetic
   (D-012.1). Demonstration imagery is generated and is never presented as a real
   venue, couple or photograph (D-012.2, P14). If a caption is needed: *"Example
   venue and couple."*
3. **No invented interface.** Nothing is narrated that is not on screen, and no
   screen is described as existing when it does not (§13, gate 5). A feature
   sketched in words during a demo is a promise by the time the agreement is
   read.

## 12. The seven beats

Each beat carries: the screen, the words, the refusal, and the rung of the
venue-owner ladder it answers (`E09.10` §3.1).

### Beat 0 · The frame · 30 seconds · nothing on screen yet

> Before I share this, one thing. The venue and the couple you are about to see
> are made up. Glenmara House does not exist and neither do Mara and Finn. I do
> not demonstrate on a real venue's account or a real couple's wedding, and that
> is the same reason you will not appear in anyone else's demo.

**Refusal:** the screen is not shared until this has been said. **Rung:** 2, and
it is the strongest thirty seconds in the call, because it demonstrates the
privacy position instead of claiming it.

### Beat 1 · The invitation, from the coordinator's side · 90 seconds

**Screen:** the account's access view, `/hq/account-review`, access panel. Masked
codes with their states: redeemed, issued, available, revoked.

> This is the only thing your team ever does. A couple books, and somebody sends
> them this. That is the whole job. Nothing to install, nothing to set up, no
> training day, and nobody at your venue has to learn a system.

**Refusal:** do not open settings, roles or member management. Do not say
"admin". **Rung:** 5, what it asks of their team, deliberately answered early
because it is the fear underneath the first ten minutes.

### Beat 2 · The couple opens it · 2 minutes

**Screen:** the redemption route, `/redeem/[code]`, then the couple's first view.

> This is what the couple sees. Your name is on it, once, quietly. There is no
> price anywhere, no card form, no trial, and nothing that reads as software they
> signed up for. As far as they are concerned, this is a gift from you.

**Refusal:** the word "sponsored" is never spoken in a couple-facing frame, and
neither is "free", "complimentary" or "worth" (P4). No logo wall. **Rung:** 1 and
2 together.

### Beat 3 · The planning year, as one year · 3 minutes

**Screen:** the Mara & Finn workspace. Notes, then Tasks, then the couple's own
Timeline view. Moved through as one continuous story, not as three products
introduced.

> Here is the same wedding at three moments. A conversation with the venue,
> written down. The things that came out of it, as work with dates on them. And
> then the plan itself, which is the bit they will actually show people. It is
> one year, not four apps, and the couple never has to decide which thing to
> open.

Anchor on the fixture's own milestones so the story is concrete: they said yes in
January, reserved the venue in April, the menu tasting is the live item, and the
guest numbers and the walk-through are ahead of them.

**Refusal:** **the dietary task must not be on screen** (§13, gate 2). No feature
comparison. No settings. No "and you can also". **Rung:** 1.

### Beat 4 · The thing they will share · 2 minutes

**Screen:** `/the-wedding`, the public example.

> This is the page they send to family and suppliers. No login, nothing to
> install, and it is theirs rather than ours. There is one line at the bottom
> that says where it came from. No logo, no badge, nothing across the top. If a
> guest reads it and never learns what Signal Studio is, that is the design
> working.

**This is where a venue asks for their logo on it.** The answer, ready:

> No, and I would rather explain why than just say no. The moment a wedding page
> looks like it is carrying advertising, the couple stops sending it, and a page
> nobody sends is worth nothing to you. You get the same restraint I do. Your
> name is on the thing the couple uses every week. It is not on the thing they
> send their grandmother.

**Refusal:** D-011.2 and P11 are hard. No logo, no badge, no "powered by" in the
viewport, at any price, for any venue, including a founding one. **Rung:** 2.

### Beat 5 · What you see, and what you never see · 2.5 minutes

**Screen:** the account's usage and reports panels. Show the withheld state
deliberately.

> Here is everything you see. How many invitations went out, how many were
> opened, and how the group as a whole is using it. And here is the part I want
> you to look at properly. This figure is being held back, because there are not
> enough workspaces in this group for it to be shown without pointing at one
> couple. That is not a gap in the reporting. That is the reporting working.
>
> You never see a couple's planning content. There is no row per couple, no
> ranking, and no most-active anything. If a couple asks your coordinator to look
> at something inside their workspace, nobody at your venue can, and neither can
> I.

**Refusal:** no per-couple row, no ranking, no leaderboard, no ROI figure, no
time-saved claim, no "usage is down" (P9, `VENUE_FACING_CLAIMS.md`). No wedding
date change is ever shown (D-011.1, P10). **Rung:** 4, both halves in the same
breath.

### Beat 6 · The end of the term · 1 minute · spoken, not shown

**There is no Keepsake surface in the product today** (§13, gate 5). This beat is
spoken, and it is stated as future-tense fact only where a ratified decision
supports it.

> Each couple has it for eighteen months from the day they open it, or three
> months past the wedding, whichever comes later. So a couple who opens it early
> and marries two years out does not lose it before their own wedding. After
> that, it stays open, read-only, to keep, for as long as the service exists, and
> there is a one-click export that belongs to them. And anything already opened
> keeps its full term whatever happens between you and me.

**Refusal:** never "18 months" alone (P7). Never "forever", "for life",
"lifetime", "permanent" or "guaranteed" (P1). Never a storage promise. Never
imply a screen exists for this. **Rung:** 6.

### Beat 7 · Stop

Close the screen share. Do not continue into settings, billing, the roadmap, the
other products, the films, or anything that was not on the list. The offer block
(§4) follows immediately, out loud, without a screen.

## 13. The six gates before any live walkthrough

**None of these is optional and none is cosmetic. Each is a place where the demo
would contradict the offer, break a ratified rule, or expose someone else's
data.**

**Gate 1 · The live venue list must not be on screen. Critical.**
`studio/src/app/hq/account-review/account-review.tsx:242-244` renders a selector
listing **every live venue account by name**, loaded by `listLiveVenueOptions()`.
Screen-sharing that page during the Founding 25 would show a prospect the names
of the other founding venues. Required before the first call: a presentation
route or mode that is fixture-only with the live selector absent, not merely
unselected. Recorded as F-2 in §19.

**Gate 2 · The R-017 special-category copy must be gone from every surface the
demo touches. Critical.**
`app/src/app/the-wedding/page.tsx:26` reads *"One reply to Glenmara House,
including dietary notes."* That line is on the page used in Beat 4, and it says on
a public marketing page that health-revealing information about guests flows to
the venue. `app/src/lib/templates.generated.ts:108` ships a *"Collect final
dietary notes"* task in the wedding template, so a live task list in Beat 3 can
put the same category on screen. Ratified substitute already exists: *"One reply
to Glenmara House, with the choices confirmed."* (P8). Owners: E05.03, E06.01,
E03.04.

**Gate 3 · One venue name across the fixture.** §11 and F-1. Glenmara House is
the approved venue (D-012.1). "The Orchard" appears in the suite fixture and in a
milestone title.

**Gate 4 · No seat count on screen. Critical to the offer's truth.**
R-016, verified: `entitlements-db/codes.ts` refuses a null allotment and
`OnboardVenueForm.tsx` defaults the field to 10, and the account computes a
remaining count and raises a headroom warning. The offer block says every couple
who books gets a workspace, with nothing for the coordinator to track. If a
remaining-count or headroom element is visible in Beat 1 or Beat 5, the venue is
watching the product contradict the sentence they just heard. Until R-016's four
code changes land, the demo path must avoid those elements, and the words
"unlimited" and "no seats" stay out of the spoken script (P6).

**Gate 5 · Nothing is narrated that does not exist.** Keepsake has no product
surface today, verified: no match for "keepsake" anywhere in `app/src`. Beat 6 is
spoken and stays spoken. The same rule covers anything else asked for on the
call.

**Gate 6 · The frozen clock is named once.** The fixture's today is 2026-07-16
and the real call is not. Said once, lightly, and never explained twice:

> The example is frozen to a fixed date so it looks the same every time I show
> it.

The sample label already present in the account fixture, `SAMPLE · DETERMINISTIC
REVIEW DATA`, should be visible rather than hidden. A venue seeing that the demo
data is labelled as demo data is being told something true about how this company
operates.

## 14. When it goes wrong

| What happens | What to do | What never to do |
|---|---|---|
| Screen share fails, or the product is slow | Stop trying after ninety seconds. Fall back to the film and the proposal page, finish the offer block properly, and offer a twelve-minute walkthrough later in the week | Narrate an interface they cannot see. A described screen is a promised screen |
| "Can we see a real venue's account?" | "No, and that is the answer you would want if you were the venue in question." Then move on. The refusal is the sale | Show anything real, redacted or otherwise |
| "Can we see what a couple actually writes in there?" | "I cannot, and that is not a permissions setting, it is how it is built." | Show a real workspace. Improvise a plausible example |
| "Can we put our own venue in the demo?" | "Yes, after you sign. Your account is set up with your name, your invitations and your details, and there is nothing to configure beyond that." | Build a bespoke demo for one venue. Nothing is built for one venue (D-008.1, D-014.2) |
| They go quiet during the walkthrough | Stop the tour and ask one of two questions: "Is any of this landing on something real for you?" or "Which of your couples would have found this useful?" Then listen | Fill the silence with more product |
| They ask for the demo before the questions | Allowed. Name the cost once: "Happy to. It will be more general than it would have been, so stop me when something is not how you work." | Refuse. It is their thirty minutes |
| They ask a question with no ratified answer | "I do not know, and I would rather find out and tell you than guess." Record it in §9, line 6, with a date | Improvise. This is how the programme acquires a promise nobody decided to make |
| They ask about dietary or allergy handling | §8, Answer C, verbatim in substance | Say yes. Demonstrate it. Promise a date |

## 15. Three variants

| Variant | Length | Beats | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The full call** | 30 minutes | 0 to 7 | The default. One person who can sign, or one step away |
| **The coordinator call** | 12 minutes | 0, 1, 2, 5 | A second short call after the owner is interested. It answers the coordinator's ladder (`E09.10` §3.2): what lands on their desk, what a couple will ask them, what they can see, what they cannot see so they never promise wrongly. No price, no strategy, no adoption percentages, and nothing that implies their performance is being measured |
| **Asynchronous** | none | none | The film *Before the Day* plus the proposal page do this job. A live walkthrough is never recorded and sent, because a recording made for one venue circulates without its context and freezes a product that is still changing |

   the scripted open, mechanic, offer block and close.

   non-numeric conversational questions, with seven observable proxies and five

   wedding count, revenue, specific couples, guests and special-category data,
   budget, competitors and manufactured urgency.

   current conditions of use, and three uncovered questions are answered.
6. A non-numeric fit judgement exists with an explicit stop condition.

   consistent with `PROJECT.md` §21.
8. Every price statement is VAT-inclusive, the access term is never stated as
   eighteen months alone, and no line states or implies legal, accounting or
   compliance approval.

**E11.10 — Write the live product walkthrough and demonstration sequence.**

1. A seven-beat sequence exists with the screen, the words, the refusal and the
   ladder rung for each beat, running couple-first and account-second.
2. It runs on the canonical fixture named in D-012.1, with every fixture value
   cited to a repository path.
3. Six pre-conditions are documented, each naming the file and line that makes it
   necessary, and each is verifiable by someone else.
4. The R-017 special-category exposure is a hard gate on the demo, not a caution.
5. A failure-mode table covers the seven ways the walkthrough goes wrong,
   including the two refusals that are the sale.
6. Three variants exist, including the coordinator call, written against the
   coordinator's ladder.
7. No line invents a surface, a date or a capability. Keepsake is spoken and is
   documented as unshowable today.

## 18. Open questions for the founder

**OQ-11.1 — One call or two.** The default written here is one thirty-minute
call carrying discovery and walkthrough together, on the basis that a venue owner
who watched the film should not need a second meeting to see the product.
*Answerable with: approve, or "split it into a 20-minute conversation and a
separate 30-minute walkthrough".*

**OQ-11.2 — Whether the coordinator call is offered on the first call or held
back.** Written here as offered on the first call, in Group C. It costs twelve
minutes and removes the person most able to stop this quietly. The argument
against is that it adds a step before a signature.
*Answerable with: approve, or "only after the proposal is out".*

**OQ-11.3 — Whether the honest-arithmetic line (§3.6) is said to every venue or
only to venues on the floor.** Written here as floor-only, because saying "about
fifty euro a wedding" to a venue running two hundred understates the value by a
factor of five, and the venue will do that arithmetic themselves.
*Answerable with: approve, or "say it to everyone".*

**OQ-11.4 — Insurance, again.** `E09.10` §9 OQ-2 raised this and it is unanswered.
It lands harder here than anywhere else, because §8 Answer 3 Version B is spoken
out loud to a venue owner who may put the question to their own insurer or
accountant the same afternoon. This needs an answer before the Cohort 1 email
goes out, not after the first venue asks.
*Answerable with: "arrange cover before Cohort 1", or "proceed on Version B and
accept the exposure".*
