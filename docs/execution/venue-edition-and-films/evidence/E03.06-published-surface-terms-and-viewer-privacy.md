## 2. What is true about search listing today, and what is not

**Decided (D-033, R-031 Option B).** `/p` stays the couple's artifact surface, because a
findable, unfurlable, stable link is the better keepsake and the Keepsake promise depends
on it. A **wedding** workspace is `noindex` by default, and search visibility becomes an
explicit couple opt-in.

**Built:** the `robots.ts` disallow on `/p`, the wedding `noindex` in
`generateMetadata`, and the publish confirmation panel.

**Not built:** the opt-in. Verified on 2026-08-04 in `_wt-integrate`:
`grep -rn "search_visibility_opted_in_at|searchVisibilityOptedInAt|published-search-posture"`
over `src` and `drizzle` returns nothing. There is no column, no migration and no control.

**So today the default is the whole behaviour.** A couple cannot turn search listing on. The
terms below describe that, not the intended end state.

**One interaction that has to be stated rather than hidden**, and `robots.ts` states it in
its own comment: a `Disallow` stops an obedient crawler fetching the page, which also stops
it reading the per-page `noindex`. A URL discovered from an external link can still be
listed URL-only. Closing that residual needs an `X-Robots-Tag` response header, which
`/s` has and `/p` does not, because it cannot be set per workspace from static header rules.
**Both controls are needed. Neither on its own is a lock, and the draft never calls either
one a lock.**

## 4. DRAFT — viewer privacy: what happens when you open one of these pages

> **What we record when you open a published page**
>
> **On a published workspace page, or a shared task link, or a page embedded in somebody
> else's site: nothing about you.** No cookie. Nothing written to your device. No
> third-party script. No analytics.
>
> **On a shared timeline, one thing.** So the couple can see roughly how many people have
> looked, your browser stores a random identifier for that one page. It is created in your
> browser, it is not linked to your name or your email, it disappears when you close the
> tab, and a different shared timeline gets a different identifier that cannot be matched to
> this one.
>
> If you stay on the page for at least two seconds, your browser sends us that identifier
> once. We turn it into a one-way fingerprint, count it, and delete the record after thirty
> minutes. The couple sees a number and never a person, and only once enough people have
> looked that a number cannot point at any one of them.
>
> **What we never record on these pages.** Your name. Your email. Your IP address stored
> against you. What you clicked. How far you scrolled. Where you came from. Whether you came
> back.
>
> **One thing we do not ask you for, and should tell you about.** We do not show a cookie
> banner on these pages, because we do not set cookies on them. The one piece of storage
> described above is on the shared timeline only, it exists for the count and nothing else,
> and we do not currently ask before writing it. [DP-A]

Note, and this is the section with the most verified detail behind it. Every sentence maps
to code:

- **No cookie.** `/p`, `/embed` and `/share` are not in the proxy matcher in
  `src/proxy.ts`, so Clerk middleware never runs on them. `/s` **is** in the matcher, but
  `isBareArtifactPath` short-circuits at the top of `proxy()` and returns before
  `productionProxy`, so Clerk never runs there either. No cookie write appears in
  `src/app/layout.tsx` or anywhere under `src/components/published/`, `src/app/p`,
  `src/app/embed` or `src/app/share`.
- **The one identifier.** `src/modules/timeline/app/audience/qualified-view-tracker.tsx`
  writes two `sessionStorage` keys per publication: a `crypto.randomUUID()` under
  `signal-timeline-qualified-view:v1:<publicationId>`, and a sent flag under
  `signal-timeline-qualified-view-sent:v1:<publicationId>`. `sessionStorage` is
  tab-scoped and cleared when the tab closes. `viewEndpoint` requires the first path
  segment to be `s`, so this runs on `/s` and nowhere else.
- **Two seconds.** `MIN_VISIBLE_MS = 2_000`, and
  `src/modules/timeline/lib/qualified-view.ts` requires dwell between 2000 ms and 60000 ms.
- **One-way fingerprint, per page.** `hashQualifiedViewSession` digests
  `sha256(publicationId \0 sessionId)`, so one viewer cannot be correlated across two
  publications.
- **Nothing else accepted.** `parseQualifiedViewPayload` sorts the key list and rejects
  anything that is not exactly `{sessionId, visibleForMs}`, with the message "View receipt
  contains unsupported fields". Referrers, user agents, IPs and raw tokens cannot drift in.
- **Thirty minutes.** `src/modules/timeline/server/qualified-view-store.ts` writes only
  `(publicationId, sessionHash, createdAt, expiresAt)` and deletes expired receipts
  opportunistically at a 30 minute retention. The durable artefact is a counter on the
  publication row.
- **Only once enough people have looked.** `src/modules/timeline/lib/viewer-count.ts`,
  `presentViewerCount`, returns a discriminated union and **never a number**. The floor is
  5. Counts of 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4 produce byte-identical output, asserted by serialising and
  comparing. No timestamp is rendered below the floor, and the withheld copy is asserted to
  contain no digit, because "fewer than 3" is itself a disclosure (R-027).

## 5. The analytics disclosure

### What was decided

**R-032, Option A, ratified at D-033.** No third-party analytics on any couple-facing
public surface. `/p`, `/s`, `/share` and `/embed` are excluded from GA4 entirely and
unconditionally. **D-034 item 1** extended the same exclusion to Sentry, because removing
GA4 alone did not satisfy the ratified sentence: Sentry is a third party.

### What is built, layer by layer

**Layer 1, one boundary rather than two lists.**
`src/lib/public-analytics-boundary.ts` names exactly `/p`, `/s`, `/share`, `/embed` and
matches a root exactly or as a path prefix, so `/pricing` and `/sitemap.xml` are
unaffected. It is deliberately separate from `isBareArtifactPath`, which answers a
different question and governs the proxy.

**Layer 2, the component seam.** `src/components/analytics/google-tag.tsx` evaluates
`isAnalyticsExcludedPath(pathname)` **before** the `enabled` flag, so turning GA on in a new
environment cannot turn it on for a couple's page. The order is asserted by a test.

**Layer 3, the browser Sentry gate.** `src/instrumentation-client.ts` uses the same
predicate. It suppresses transactions as well as errors, because on these surfaces the
transaction URL is itself the disclosure.

**Layer 4, the per-route CSP.** `next.config.ts` emits an **enforced**
`Content-Security-Policy` for `/p` and `/share`, and a second for `/embed` carrying
`frame-ancestors *`. The builder throws at config load if a Google host survives an edit.
The baseline site-wide CSP still ships Report-Only unless `SIGNAL_ENFORCE_CSP` is set; the
couple-facing policy does not depend on that.

### What is NOT closed, stated plainly, because a disclosure that overstates is worse than none

**Server-side Sentry is not excluded from anything.** `src/instrumentation.ts` calls
`Sentry.init` for the Node runtime and for the Edge runtime with no route test at all. A
server error or a sampled trace while rendering `/p/{slug}` is still sent to Sentry, at
`tracesSampleRate: 0.1`. `sendDefaultPii: false` and `scrubEvent` remove the user object
beyond an id, cookies, query strings and sensitive headers including `x-forwarded-for`.

**And `redactSensitiveUrl` in `src/lib/sentry-scrub.ts` redacts `/s/…`, `/share/…`,
`/invite/…`, `/redeem/…` and `/oauth/…` bearer segments. It does not redact `/p/{slug}`.**
So a server-side event from a published wedding page carries the slug that identifies it.

That is a smaller exposure than the one D-034 closed, and it is a real one. It is
**`[DP-C]`**, it belongs to the `app` repository rather than this worktree, and this document
does not claim it is fixed.

**The consequence for the disclosure copy in section 4** is that the sentence "no
third-party script and no analytics" is written about the **viewer's browser**, which is
true and verified, and the draft does not extend it into a claim about server-side error
reporting. If the founder prefers the stronger claim, `[DP-C]` has to be built first.

### The disclosure copy

> **Analytics on published pages**
>

> any kind, and no advertising or social tracking script. That is enforced in three places
> in the code so that switching it on somewhere else cannot switch it on here.

built, in four layers. **The second is "Marketing and commercial surfaces drop GA4 too,

## 6. The cookie position

**There are no cookies to consent to on the four couple-facing surfaces.** That is the
headline, it is verified, and it is unusual enough to be worth stating positively.

The remaining question is not about cookies. Regulation 5 of SI 336/2011, transposing
Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive, governs **storing information, or gaining access to
information stored, in the terminal equipment of a subscriber or user**. It is technology
neutral. `sessionStorage` is in scope exactly as a cookie is.

So the one live question is the two `sessionStorage` keys the shared timeline writes.

**The two readings, and neither is obviously right.**

| Reading | The argument | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **Not strictly necessary** | The exemption covers storage strictly necessary to provide a service *explicitly requested by the subscriber or user*. The user here is the guest, who requested a page. The counter serves the **couple**, not the guest. On that reading, consent is required. | Either ask the guest, which puts a consent interaction on a wedding page, or drop the count, or move the count to a server-side method that stores nothing on the device. |
| **Strictly necessary** | The storage exists only to stop one person being counted twice, is first-party, is tab-scoped, holds a random value with no meaning, is hashed per publication on receipt and is deleted after thirty minutes. Nothing is read back for any other purpose. | The current behaviour stands, documented, with no banner. |

**The reading this draft took: the first one.** Not because it is certainly correct, but
because it is the reading that is safer for the guest, and because the disclosure it forces
is honest either way. Section 4 therefore **says plainly that the storage happens and that
nobody is asked**, rather than asserting an exemption. That is `[DP-A]`, and it is an open
legal point.

**What the draft did not do.** It did not add a cookie banner, and it did not describe one.
Nothing in the product asks for consent on these pages today.

## 7. The framable embed, R-033, still undecided

`src/app/embed/[slug]/page.tsx` resolves through the same path as `/p`, with no token. Its
own purpose statement is that a blogger drops an iframe into a post. `next.config.ts` gives
it `frame-ancestors *` and removes `X-Frame-Options` deliberately.

**So anyone holding a slug can render a couple's published workspace inside a page the
couple has never seen, and the couple has no signal that it happened and no way to stop it
short of unpublishing.**

D-033 and D-034 did not decide this. E06.05 §7 records that nothing was enforced in the
route because no founder decision was available, and its recommendation is **no**: sponsored
wedding workspaces are not embeddable.

**This draft does not invent the answer.** The terms in section 3 describe the page as
public and reachable by anyone with the address, which is true under either outcome. If the
founder decides no, one sentence is added to the terms and the route gates on the workspace
domain. `[DP-B]`.

## 10. Open legal points

1. Is a first-party, tab-scoped, hashed, thirty-minute view receipt written to
   `sessionStorage` "strictly necessary" under Regulation 5(4) of SI 336/2011 when the
   service it enables is requested by the publisher rather than by the user whose device
   stores it?
2. Where the shared-timeline count serves the couple rather than the viewer, who is the
   controller of the viewer measurement, and does the answer change when the couple is a
   consumer inside a venue-sponsored service?
3. Does a `robots.txt` disallow plus a per-page `noindex`, without an `X-Robots-Tag` header,
   discharge anything at all, or is it only relevant to the Article 5(1)(f) and Article 25
   assessment of the measures taken?
4. May a venue reshare a couple's published page in its own marketing? If it may, the venue
   is an independent controller for that republication, it needs its own basis, and the
   couple has to have agreed. (Role map §12 item 10, founder decision 14, intersects R-020.)
5. Does making a workspace framable on any third-party site by holding a slug constitute a
   further disclosure by Signal Studio requiring its own basis, separate from the
   publication itself? (R-033.)
6. On the token-bound surfaces `/s` and `/share`, who controls the publication: Signal
   Studio alone, or Signal Studio and the couple jointly? The role map resolves this for
   `/p` on Lindqvist and records it as **UNRESOLVED** for the token-bound surfaces (§12
   item 6).
7. What does Signal Studio owe a person who asks to be removed from a published page, in
   what time, and what counts as complete removal given ISR caching, social unfurl caches
   and search-engine caches?
