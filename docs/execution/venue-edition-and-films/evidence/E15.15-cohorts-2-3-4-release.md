Read directly from `venue-universe/counts-only.md` and `05-COHORTS.md` on

| Figure | Value |

And `05-COHORTS.md` states it plainly: **"Cohort 1 is built and fills to 25.
Cohort 2 is built and comes up short. Cohorts 3 and 4 do not exist inside the
ratified ring."**

**So the target is 25 signed and paid venues out of 50 eligible accounts. That
is a 50% conversion rate on cold, founder-led outreach to hospitality
businesses.** That number is not hidden anywhere; it simply has not been said out
loud next to the target. It should be said at the go/no-go, because it changes
what M6 means: 25 venues is not a scheduling question, it is a conversion
question against a pool that runs out.

The cohort rules that produce this honesty are already enforced in code, which is
the right place for them:

- one property per operator per cohort;
- nothing whose trading status is unconfirmed goes into a cohort;
- a cohort that does not exist is not invented, and a short cohort reports as
  short.

`05-COHORTS.md` records that all three are enforced in `venue-rank.mjs` and
covered by tests, so they hold on a rebuild rather than depending on whoever runs
it next. It also records that every one of six suspected closures put to a
verification pass came back confirmed, including a lakeside estate closed for
seven years whose website still sells weddings.

## 2. What only a human can do

Release a cohort, contact the venues, and take the decision when the ring runs
out.

## 3. The script, per cohort

### Step 1 — Rebuild rather than reuse

- **Do:** rebuild the cohort from the universe at release time. Trading status
  changes; a list built six weeks ago is a list of assumptions.
- **Fail:** releasing a frozen list without re-verification.

### Step 2 — Check the three rules held

- **Expect:** no operator appears twice in the cohort; nothing unconfirmed is in
  it; if it is short, the output says short.
- **Fail:** a cohort that arrives at exactly 25 after being short a week ago
  deserves a look at what was added and why.

### Step 3 — Nobody is contacted twice

- **Do:** check the touch log from every previous cohort before sending.
- **Fail:** a second approach to a venue that already declined, sent as though it
  were a first, is the impression this programme is designed to avoid.

### Step 4 — The position is still current

- **Run:**
  ```bash
  curl -s https://signalstudio.ie/venues | grep -o "€1,000\|€1,500\|Founding 25"
  ```
- **Expect:** the ratified position, and a places-remaining statement that is
  still true.
- **Result 2026-08-04:** `Founding 25` × 8, `€1,000` × 8, `€1,500` × 2. Current.
- **Fail:** any live count of places remaining that has gone stale. Scarcity must
  be a static true statement, never a countdown that can rot.

### Step 5 — The count of signed and paid comes from the database

- **Do:** derive it from `sponsors.founding_number` assignments, per E15.16.
- **Fail:** a spreadsheet. Two sources of truth for the founding count is the
  same failure mode as two venues holding the same number, one step earlier.

### Step 6 — When the ring runs out

**This is a founder decision under change control, not an operational step.**

- **Do:** report the number reached and stop.
- **The three options:** extend the geographic boundary, lower the founding
  count, or accept a longer timeline. Each changes a ratified position and each
  goes through `/venue-change`.
- **Fail:** padding a cohort from outside the ring. `05-COHORTS.md` says why: it
  would quietly overturn D-012.
