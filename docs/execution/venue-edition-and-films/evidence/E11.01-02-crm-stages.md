# E11.01 · E11.02 — Venue Edition CRM stages, and the objective criteria for entering and leaving each one

**Stage vocabulary version: `venue-pipeline.v1`**
Prepared 2026-08-03 · Evidence for E11.01 and E11.02
Inputs to E11.04, E11.08, E11.09, E11.10, E11.11, E11.15, E10.12, E15.07, E15.15, E15.17
Governed by D-009, D-012, D-013, D-017, D-020, D-021, D-022, D-024 · bound by D-016

> **What this document is.** The single definition of the twelve Venue Edition
> sales stages, the twelve entry tests, the twelve exit tests, and the evidence
> that satisfies each one. A stage is a claim about reality. Every claim here has
> a test that can be answered yes or no by somebody who was not in the room.
>
> **What this document is not.** It is not legal, accounting or tax advice, and
> nothing in it has been reviewed by a solicitor or an accountant (D-016). No
> criterion below is a legal determination. Where a criterion touches a contract
> or a payment it describes an operating test, not a legal one.

---

## 0. The determinations, made once

Six things were ambiguous before this document. They are settled here so no later
session has to guess.

1. **The stage names are the tracker names.** The twelve stages are exactly the
   twelve `commercial` counters in `PROJECT_STATE.json`. No synonyms, no local
   variants, no "qualified" prefix in one place and not another.
2. **Counters are cumulative, not occupancy.** A counter is the number of
   accounts that have *ever reached that stage or any stage beyond it*. Not the
   number sitting in it today. This makes the funnel monotonic and makes the five
   counters that "no code path sums" (E01.02 finding 2) comparable at last.
3. **The account is the property, not the company.** A group with three wedding
   properties is three accounts, three agreements and three founding places.
   Flagged for founder confirmation in §11 because it moves the Founding 25 count.
4. **A click is not a stage.** Film link clicks are tracked (D-013 point 3) and
   they inform who to follow up first. They never move a venue forward.
5. **"Invitation" means two different things in this programme.** See §2.1. The
   collision is real, it is in shipped documents, and it is handled by qualifying
   the word every time rather than by renaming a field.
6. **The shipped CRM cannot hold this pipeline.** Verified in §3. Seven of the
   twelve stages do not exist in the database. This is E11.01's build list, not a
   documentation problem.

## 1. The rule these stages exist to enforce

`PROJECT.md` §6 and §22 state the business rule in one line, and everything below
is machinery for keeping it true:

> A researched venue is not a founding venue. An invited venue is not signed. A
> signed venue is not paid. A paid venue is not onboarded.

The failure mode this guards against is the ordinary one. Twenty-five emails go
out, twenty-five accounts sit in a pipeline looking like progress, and the number
that matters, twenty-five venues paid and able to invite a couple, has not moved
at all. Optimistic stage movement is the mechanism by which a founder lies to
himself for six weeks.

So every exit test below has the same shape: **a recorded fact with a date, or a
row in a database.** An impression is not evidence. "It went well" is not a stage
change.

## 2. The twelve stages

| # | Stage | `crm_stage` token | Tracker counter | Who may move it | What proves it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | researched | `researched` | `researchedAccountUniverse` | Claude Code | A complete row in the venue universe file |
| 2 | invited | `invited` | `invitationsIssued` | Founder | Accepted delivery of the founding invitation |
| 3 | responded | `responded` | `responses` | Founder | A human reply, logged with date and route |
| 4 | meeting | `meeting` | `qualifiedMeetings` | Founder | The meeting happened and four facts are recorded |
| 5 | demo | `demo` | `demonstrations` | Founder | Four named things were shown |
| 6 | proposal | `proposal` | `proposals` | Founder | A written proposal with a recorded expiry date |
| 7 | signed | `signed` | `signedAgreements` | Founder | An executed agreement, dated and stored |
| 8 | paid | `paid` | `paidAgreements` | Machine-checkable | `isPaidVenue()` returns true |
| 9 | configured | `configured` | `configuredVenueAccounts` | Machine-checkable | Sponsor row complete, test code round-tripped |
| 10 | onboarded | `onboarded` | `onboardedVenues` | Founder | Six recorded onboarding facts |
| 11 | first couple invited | `first_couple_invited` | `firstCoupleInvitations` | Half machine, half venue | A minted code plus the venue's confirmation it sent one |
| 12 | first couple activated | `first_couple_activated` | `firstCoupleActivations` | Machine-checkable | A redemption row exists |

Six parked states sit outside the ladder. They are in §9. They are not stages and
they never appear in a funnel.

### 2.1 Trap: two kinds of invitation, and they are counted separately

Stage 2 is a **venue invitation**: the founding email, the personalised film, the
landing page. Stage 11 is a **couple invitation**: a `license_codes` row the venue
hands to a couple who booked with it.

`PROJECT_STATE.json` calls stage 2 `invitationsIssued`. The metric dictionary
(`E09.01-event-taxonomy.md` §4) defines "Invitation issued" as the couple code.
Two documents in this programme use one word for two things, and the sales
counter and the product counter would both be right and would never agree.

**Determination.** The field names stay. Renaming a canonical state key to fix a
label is churn against a file that generates four reports. Instead:

- Every render of the sales-side counter reads **"Venue invitations sent"**.
  `project-control.mjs:687` and `:836` currently render "Invitations issued".
  Changing the label is a one-line, reversible fix and it is in the build list.
- Every venue-facing and product-facing use of the bare word "invitation" means
  the couple's code. That is the meaning a venue owner will assume, and the venue
  owner is the reader who matters.

## 3. Verified finding: the shipped CRM stops seven stages short of the outcome

`studio/src/lib/db/schema.ts:187-196` defines the whole stage vocabulary the
product has:

```ts
export const PROSPECT_STAGES = [
  "to_contact", "contacted", "replied", "demo_booked",
  "pilot_active", "not_interested", "later",
] as const;
```

Five active stages and two parked ones. `PIPELINE_STAGES` in
`studio/src/lib/hq/crm-utils.ts:211-218` ends at `pilot_active`. There is no
signed, no paid, no configured, no onboarded, no first-couple stage anywhere in
the CRM.

Four consequences, all live today:

1. **The CRM cannot express the project's completion condition.** `PROJECT.md`
   §22 closes the project on twenty-five venues signed, paid, configured,
   onboarded and able to invite a couple. The pipeline the founder will actually
   work in tops out five stages earlier.
2. **`pilot_active` is retired vocabulary still wearing the winner's colour.**
   There are no pilots in the ratified offer. `STAGE_COLORS`
   (`crm-utils.ts:224-236`) paints `pilot_active` green, `#d1fae5`, the same
   register the brand reserves for shipped. A venue that has paid nothing renders
   as won. `computeOutreachSummary` (`crm-utils.ts:446-447`) also counts
   `pilot_active` inside both `REPLIED_STAGES` and `BOOKED_STAGES`.
3. **The private venue file points at a CRM that cannot answer it.**
   `private/venues.template.csv` carries a `crm_stage` column commented "Mirrors
   the CRM stage (E11.01). The CRM is authoritative." The authority it names has
   no vocabulary for seven of the twelve values. §10 gives the column its
   controlled list.
4. **The one place the truth already lives is the sponsor ledger, not the CRM.**
   `proofgate.ts` reads paid venues from the real sponsors table and says so
   plainly: *"signed-unpaid is pipeline, not money"*. That instinct is right and
   this document generalises it.

**This is not a bug report against the CRM.** The CRM was built for a four-book
outreach motion (venue, student, school, smb) where the job ended at a booked
call. Venue Edition is the first motion in the company that runs all the way to a
delivered product. The vocabulary has to grow. §10 says how, and says how to grow
it without polluting the other three books.

## 4. Stages 1 to 6, the sales half

Each stage lists: what lets an account in, what is required to leave, what does
*not* count, and where the evidence lives.

### Stage 1 · researched

**Entry.** An account_id exists in the venue universe file. Nothing else. A name
on a list is the beginning of research, not the end of it.

**Exit, all nine required.** Every one is a filled field in the row, and the row
must pass `tools/venue-export.mjs guard`:

1. `venue_type` is an eligible type under D-012 point 4 and E10.02. Restaurants,
   pubs, marquee hire, town hotels doing occasional weddings, and anything under
   roughly twenty weddings a year are out.
2. `latitude`, `longitude` and `drive_time_ring` are verified and inside the
   45-minute ring from Limerick city centre (D-012 point 3, E10.06).
3. `cluster` is set.
4. `rank_score` is computed under E10.03.
5. `conflict_flag` is resolved or explicitly recorded: group ownership, shared
   operator, rename, duplicate (E10.05).
6. `buyer_role` is one of owner, general_manager, wedding_manager, sales_lead,
   events_lead. A role, never a person's name in this file (E10.07).
7. A contact route exists in the CRM, joined on account_id, and
   `contact_verified_on` is within 90 days (E10.08).
8. `fit_reason` carries one honest sentence about why this account belongs in the
   founding outreach (E10.11). "It is a wedding venue" is not a reason.
9. `cohort` is set to 1, 2, 3, 4 or reserve (E10.12, E10.13, E10.14).

**Does not count.** Found on Google. In a spreadsheet. Mentioned by someone.
Ninety-day-old contact details that nobody re-checked.

**Regression.** A hard bounce at stage 2 clears `contact_verified_on` and returns
the account here. So does discovering the venue is ineligible.

### Stage 2 · invited

**Entry, all four.** The account is researched, and:

1. Its cohort is locked and released. Twenty-five at a time, sequentially, until
   twenty-five have signed and paid (D-017).
2. The sending identity passes: SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured, suppression list
   live, unsubscribe handling live (E11.04). DKIM pending is a hard stop, not a
   caution (R-010).
3. This venue's personalised film render passed QA (E13.17) and its landing page
   resolves (E11.07).
4. The address is not on the suppression list.

**Exit.** The founding invitation was **accepted for delivery** by the receiving
server, with no hard bounce, and the send is logged against account_id, cohort,
touch number and date.

**Does not count.** "Sent" without delivery. Opens, because there is no open
tracking and there will not be, on purpose (D-013 point 3). Film link clicks, see
§0 point 4.

**Touch discipline.** Four touches total: film, two follow-ups, one final short
note, then stop and park as `later` (D-013 point 4, E11.13). Touches two, three
and four do not create new stage movement. They are logged against the same
stage-2 record.

### Stage 3 · responded

**Entry.** The account is invited.

**Exit.** A reply written by a person at the venue arrived by any route, and it is
logged with date, route and substance.

**Does not count.** An auto-reply. An out-of-office. A delivery receipt. An
unsubscribe, which is a parked state and a permanent one. A film link click.

**Branches.** "Send me more" and "not now" are both responses and both move the
stage. A response is contact, not interest. The distinction between a warm and a
cold response lives in the record, not in the stage, because a stage that encodes
enthusiasm is a stage the founder can talk himself into.

**Negative branch.** An explicit no moves the account to `not_interested` with a
close-lost reason from the list in §9.1. An unsubscribe moves it to `suppressed`
and adds the address to the suppression list, permanently.

### Stage 4 · meeting

The tracker counter is `qualifiedMeetings`, and the qualification is what the exit
test is for.

**Entry.** Responded, and a meeting is scheduled with a person whose role can sign
or who has direct access to the signer.

**Exit, all five.** The meeting happened, the person attended, and four facts are
recorded in the account:

1. **Who signs.** A role and a name. Not "the owner probably".
2. **Eligibility confirmed from the venue's own mouth**: it runs weddings, at a
   volume above the E10.02 floor, and it is an eligible type.
3. **The venue understands the model**: annual, prepaid, the venue pays, the
   couple never sees a price, the venue does not manage seats.
4. **A decision timeframe**, expressed as a date or a named event, not "soon".
5. The discovery structure in E11.09 was worked through, and what was said is in
   the record.

**Does not count.** A no-show. A rescheduled meeting, which stays at responded
until it happens. A meeting that ends without knowing who signs, which is a
conversation, and conversations do not increment `qualifiedMeetings`.

### Stage 5 · demo

**Entry.** Qualified meeting complete.

**Exit.** The venue was walked through the product live (E11.10), and **all four**
of these were shown, each recorded as shown:

1. The couple's private planning workspace.
2. The shared Timeline, which is the principal emotional artifact (`PROJECT.md`
   §7 point 7).
3. **The venue's own view**, showing exactly what a venue sees: invitation
   administration, activation evidence, and privacy-safe aggregate adoption
   evidence. And what it does not see, said out loud.
4. The Keepsake state: free, read-only access while the service exists, with a
   one-click export the couple owns (D-010 point 3).

Plus: attendees recorded, date recorded, and every objection raised logged into
the objection library (E11.12).

**Does not count.** A demo that skipped item 3. The privacy boundary is not a
feature of the product, it is the product, and a venue that bought without
understanding it will discover it at the worst possible moment.

**Hard rule, and it is a privacy rule as much as a sales one.** The walkthrough
runs on the synthetic demo account only: Glenmara House, Mara and Finn (D-012
point 1). No real couple's workspace is ever shown to a venue. Not with the
couple's permission, not with names blurred, not "just the structure". See §8.

### Stage 6 · proposal

**Entry.** Demo complete.

**Exit.** A written proposal was issued, and it carries all eight of these:

1. The price stated as **inclusive of VAT at the prevailing rate** (D-021).
2. The founding rate, with the ratified wording: *"EUR 500 a year less, for as
   long as you stay."* (D-009 point 2).
3. The lock's conditions: it covers the base annual agreement, it holds while the
   agreement renews continuously without lapse, it follows the property rather
   than the company, and it does not transfer to additional properties a new
   owner brings (D-009 points 3 and 4). **The words "for life" and "forever" do
   not appear.**
4. The entitlement sentence (D-020): every couple who books their wedding with
   the venue gets a workspace, for as long as the licence is current. No seat
   count, no per-couple arithmetic. **No number.**
5. The survival sentence, stated unprompted (D-020 point 2): any workspace
   already redeemed keeps its full term and its Keepsake state whatever happens
   to the licence. Only new issuance stops.
6. The couple access term (D-010 point 2): 18 months from redemption, or 3 months
   past the wedding date, whichever is later.
7. The **14-day hold**, with an explicit expiry date written on the proposal
   (D-009 point 7).
8. The order form.

And: `proposal_expires_on` is recorded on the account.

**Does not count.** A verbal price. A proposal without an expiry date, which does
not exit the stage, because the hold is the mechanism that makes a founding place
mean something.

**Expiry.** At day 14 without signature the account moves to `hold_expired`. The
hold is extendable once, by Ethan personally (D-009 point 7). Nobody else, and
never by default.

## 5. Stages 7 to 9, where money and machinery start

### Stage 7 · signed

**Entry.** A live, unexpired proposal.

**Exit, all four.**

1. An executed agreement exists, signed by the person identified as signer at
   stage 4.
2. It is dated, and the founding schedule is attached where the venue is taking a
   founding place.
3. The order form is complete.
4. It is stored in the agreed private operator location. **Never in this
   repository** (`PROJECT.md` §21, `private/README.md`).

**What signature does not do, and this is the whole point of the stage.**

- **No founding number is assigned.** The number is assigned on payment (D-009
  point 6). A signed venue has no place in the Founding 25 and must never be told
  it does.
- No sponsor row is created. No code is minted. No venue is named publicly.
- `foundingPlacesAvailable` does not decrement.

HQ already models this correctly and the language is worth keeping verbatim:
signed-unpaid is *"pipeline, not money"* (`proofgate.ts:132`).

### Stage 8 · paid

This is the first fully machine-checkable stage, and the only stage where the
database, not a person, is the authority.

**Entry.** Signed.

**Exit.** A `sponsors` row exists with all of:

| Field | Required value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `venuePlan` | `founding` or `paid` | `schema.ts:96` |
| `paidAt` | not null. Cleared funds, not an invoice | `schema.ts:105`, commented *"Null = signed, not yet money"* |
| `annualAmountCents` | the amount actually received | `schema.ts:98` |
| `foundingLocked` | set where the venue is one of the 25 | `schema.ts:100` |
| `termStartsAt`, `termEndsAt` | the prepaid annual window | `schema.ts:102-103` |

Equivalently: `isPaidVenue({ venuePlan, paidAt })` returns true
(`schema.ts:122-127`).

**On payment, and only on payment:**

- The founding number is assigned, in payment order. It is the first irreversible
  act in the whole sequence, which is exactly why D-009 point 6 puts it here.
- `foundingPlacesAvailable` decrements.
- HQ's proof gate moves. That is the one moment the brand allows a flourish
  (`BRAND.md` §5.1), and it should stay the only one.

**Does not count.** An invoice issued. A payment promised. A payment initiated. A
`venuePlan` of `pilot`, which is a fourth plan value the schema still carries
(`VENUE_PLANS = ["none", "pilot", "founding", "paid"]`) and which has no place in
the ratified offer. **Recommendation: no new `pilot` sponsor rows are created for
Venue Edition, and a pilot never becomes paid by editing the plan field.** A
pilot that decides to buy signs and pays like anyone else.

**Where the tracker and the ledger disagree, the ledger wins.** `PROJECT_STATE.json`
is hand-maintained; the sponsor ledger is the record of money received. The
tracker is corrected to match, never the reverse.

### Stage 9 · configured

**Entry.** Paid.

**Exit, all eight.** Seven are database facts and one is a round-trip test:

1. `slug` is short and stable. It appears in redemption URLs and it does not
   change afterwards.
2. `name` and `brandMeta` carry the venue name exactly as the venue confirmed it
   in writing at stage 4 or later.
3. `allotmentMode = 'unlimited'`, so `isUnlimitedSponsor()` returns true
   (`venue-allotment.ts`). This is the ratified entitlement (D-020) and it is not
   a number.
4. `annualWeddingCount` is collected, **after signature, as an onboarding field,
   never as a contract term**, with the sentence said to the venue: this sets
   your issuance ceiling, it never sets your price, and it never changes at
   renewal (D-020 point 4).
5. `fairUseCeiling` is derived from it. Crossing it alerts Signal HQ and keeps
   issuing. It has no refusal path and must never grow one (D-020 point 1).
6. `reportingTimezone` is set, or deliberately left null for Europe/Dublin.
7. Venue account access is granted to the named roles at the venue.
8. **The dry run passed.** One test code minted, redeemed end to end in an
   incognito browser, the wedding workspace reached, the venue eyebrow correct,
   no self-serve price shown anywhere in the flow, then the test code revoked.
   The steps are in `VENUE_FULFILMENT_RUNBOOK.md`. A failed dry run blocks the
   stage and blocks the cohort.

**Does not count.** `allotmentMode = 'limited'` with a generous number in
`codeAllotment`. That is a misconfigured venue whatever the number is, because
the venue was sold no seats and the portal would show it a seat count (R-016).

**Verified, and it corrects the register.** R-016 was recorded when the mint
refused a null allotment and the HQ onboarding form defaulted to ten. Both are
fixed in shipped code: `sponsors.allotmentMode` exists
(`entitlements-db/schema.ts:192`), `fairUseCeiling` exists (`:205`), the fair-use
path notifies and returns without a refusal branch (`codes.ts`), and
`OnboardVenueForm.tsx` now defaults to *"Unlimited, every booked couple"*. The
RAID entry still reads open at high severity. It should be reviewed against the
code rather than carried forward on reputation.

## 6. Stages 10 to 12, delivery

### Stage 10 · onboarded

This stage carries the project's completion condition (`PROJECT.md` §22 points 4
and 5), so it gets the hardest definition in the document.

**The test in one sentence.** The venue can issue a functioning couple invitation
without Signal Studio doing it for them.

**Entry.** Configured.

**Exit, all six, each a recorded fact with a date and a named person:**

1. **The coordinator walkthrough happened**, with the person who will actually
   send codes present. Not their manager, not a forwarded recording.
2. **The venue holds the couple email template** and knows the three rules: it
   sends from its own address, in plain text, with reply-to the venue. The trust
   belongs to the venue.
3. **The venue holds its first batch of codes** in the agreed private location.
4. **The venue has opened its own account view at least once**, and what it shows
   and does not show was explained out loud.
5. **The support boundary is stated**: Signal Studio supports product issues, the
   venue does not become support.
6. **A named person at the venue owns the invitation flow**, recorded by role and
   name in the CRM.

**Does not count.** A venue holding codes that has never opened its account view.
A venue whose only couple invitation was sent by Ethan on its behalf. A
walkthrough attended by someone who will not send the codes.

**Honest gap, stated rather than papered over.** Criterion 4 is not measurable
today. The only signal the product has is `entitlements.reached_board_at`, which
is stamped on a page render, and E09.01 §10 disqualifies a page load as evidence
of use. So criterion 4 is an **operator-recorded fact**, not a measured one, and
the record must say so. If it is ever presented as telemetry, it will be wrong.

### Stage 11 · first couple invited

**Entry.** Onboarded.

**Exit, both halves.** Minting is Signal's act. Sending is the venue's. Neither
alone is the stage.

*Machine half.* At least one `license_codes` row exists against this sponsor with
`status = 'minted'`, the Venue Edition `sourceType` and wedding `tier`, and
`durationDays` computed per D-022: `max(redemption + 548 days, wedding date + 90
days)`, and never shorter than 548.

*Venue half.* The venue confirms, by date, that it sent a code to a real couple
with a signed booking at that venue.

**The prohibition that makes the venue half necessary.** Signal Studio does not
hold, process, infer or report whether a couple has a signed booking. The venue
states it; Signal issues the code and never audits the claim (E09.01 §1 and §6.2,
permanent). So the sending fact cannot be measured and must be reported by the
venue. That is a deliberate accuracy cost paid for a privacy boundary.

**Batching.** Venues will send in batches. The stage moves on the first, not on
the batch, and the batch size is not a stage fact.

### Stage 12 · first couple activated

**Entry.** First couple invited.

**Exit.** One redemption exists: a `redemptions` row against a distinct code by a
distinct user, the `license_codes` row moved to `redeemed`, and the wedding
workspace exists.

**Does not count.** A code opened. A redemption landing page viewed.
`reached_board_at` stamped. Every one of those is a page load, and page loads are
disqualified as evidence of use (E09.01 §10).

**Privacy, and it is absolute.** The couple is never a CRM record. What enters the
CRM is a count against the venue account and a date. No couple name, no partner
name, no wedding date, no email, no guest count. Not in the CRM, not in a status
report, not in a screenshot, not in a founder note. §8 lists what may never
travel.

**What this stage unlocks.** The referral ask. Referrals are asked only after the
venue's first couple activates, never at signature (D-013 point 5, E11.14).
Publicity consent stays separate, opt-in and revocable, always, and is never
bundled with the referral ask.

## 7. Movement rules

**One stage at a time, forward, on evidence.** An account enters a stage only when
every exit test of the previous stage is satisfied and recorded.

**No skipping, and no exceptions dressed as judgement calls.** If a venue offers
to sign without a demo, the demo stage's exit tests are still worked through, on
a call, and recorded. A venue that bought without seeing what its own view shows
is a renewal problem with a twelve-month fuse.

**Counters are cumulative "ever reached this stage or beyond".** Consequences,
all of which should be asserted by `validate`:

- `paidAgreements` can never exceed `signedAgreements`.
- `onboardedVenues` can never exceed `configuredVenueAccounts`.
- Every counter is less than or equal to the one above it, all the way up.
- A monotonicity break is a data error, not a surprising quarter.

**Regression is recorded, never silent.** An account moves backwards for exactly
four reasons: a hard bounce, a lapsed hold, a cancelled agreement, a refund. The
reason and the date go in the record.

**Regression does not decrement a cumulative counter, except on fact reversal.**
Losing a venue at stage 6 does not un-invite it. But a refunded payment
decrements `paidAgreements` and returns the founding place, because the fact
itself was reversed. Record the reversal.

**A venue occupies exactly one stage.** Parked states replace the stage rather
than sitting alongside it, and the account remembers the furthest stage it
reached.

## 8. What the CRM may never hold

The CRM holds **venue businesses, and named business contacts in their
professional capacity.** It holds nothing about couples, guests, families or
suppliers.

### 8.1 Special-category data, recorded as R-017 and binding here

Dietary and allergy notes are a first-class object in the shipped product.
`app/src/lib/templates.generated.ts:108` ships a **"Collect final dietary notes"**
task in the wedding template. `app/src/app/the-wedding/page.tsx:26` advertises
*"One reply to Glenmara House, including dietary notes."* Both verified on
2026-08-03.

Dietary and allergy data reveals health and routinely reveals religious belief.
Accessibility requirements reveal disability. Guest lists routinely contain
children's data. All of it is entered by the couple **about third parties who
agreed to nothing**, and the marketing page says it flows onward to the venue.

**The binding rule for this pipeline.** None of that data may ever enter the CRM,
the commercial tracker, a status report, a proposal, a demo, a screenshot in a
sales asset, a film frame, or a founder note. Not aggregated, not anonymised, not
"just as an example". The sales motion has no legitimate reason to touch it and
every reason to stay clear of it.

**The one wedding a venue ever sees in this motion is synthetic**: Glenmara House,
Mara and Finn (D-012 point 1). Every screenshot in every sales asset comes from
that account, and that is checked before send, not after.

### 8.2 Never in a CRM record, a tracker or a report

Couple name · partner name · wedding date · guest count · guest list · dietary
note · allergy note · accessibility requirement · supplier name · budget figure ·
any task, note or Timeline content from a real workspace · any access code in
plain text · any signed contract.

### 8.3 Never in this repository

Contact names, direct emails, phone numbers, postal addresses and LinkedIn
profiles live in the CRM and join on `account_id`. They are deliberately absent
from `private/venues.template.csv`, and `tools/venue-export.mjs` fails the write
rather than warning. A second copy of contact data in a repo is how a repo share
becomes a data incident.

Generated reports are counts only. `STATUS.md` reports how many venues are
researched, invited, signed, paid and onboarded. It never names one. Venue names
are published as participants only with recorded consent (E10.14, E15.16).

## 9. Parked and terminal states

Not stages. They never appear in a funnel and they never increment a counter.

| Token | Meaning | Entered from | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `later` | Four touches used, no reply, or the venue asked for another time | 2, 3 | A date to revisit, set by the founder. Re-entry starts a new touch sequence |
| `not_interested` | An explicit no | 3 to 7 | Only on the venue's initiative |
| `suppressed` | Unsubscribed | any | **None.** Permanent, and the address goes to the suppression list |
| `hold_expired` | The 14-day proposal hold lapsed | 6 | One extension, by Ethan personally (D-009 point 7) |
| `disqualified` | Found ineligible under E10.02 after research | any | Only if the underlying facts change |
| `lapsed` | A paid venue whose agreement did not renew | 8 to 12 | Renewal, which is a new agreement, not a rewind |

### 9.1 Close-lost reasons, a fixed list

Every `not_interested` carries exactly one. A free-text field would produce
twenty-five unique reasons and no learning at all, and E11.15's weekly conversion
review depends on this list being small enough to count.

`price` · `no_perceived_need` · `existing_tool` · `privacy_concern` ·
`timing` · `no_decision_maker_access` · `venue_not_eligible` ·
`no_response_after_four_touches` · `other_recorded`

`other_recorded` requires a sentence, and more than three of them in a cohort
means the list is wrong and gets revised at the weekly review.

## 11. What this document does not settle

Four items. Three need a founder answer. One needs a RAID review.

1. **Group properties against founding places.** §0 point 3 assumes the account is
   the property, so a group signing three venues takes three of the twenty-five
   and pays three times EUR 1,000. This follows from D-009 point 4, where the rate
   follows the property rather than the company, but D-009 did not answer it
   directly and it changes what "twenty-five founding venues" means. Proceeding on
   the assumption, flagged here because it touches the founding count.
2. **Whether a venue may be invited before its film render exists.** Stage 2 entry
   test 3 requires it, which means a venue that responds late in a cohort cannot
   be re-invited quickly if its render was never produced. The alternative is a
   generic invitation, which is a materially weaker first touch. Recommendation:
   hold the requirement.
3. **Who moves stage 10.** It is the completion condition for the project, it is
   entirely operator-recorded, and it is the only stage with no machine check at
   all. Recommendation: stage 10 requires the founder, and it is reviewed at the
   weekly conversion review rather than moved silently.
4. **R-016 should be re-read against shipped code.** §5 stage 9 shows the four
   changes the mitigation demanded appear to be in place. The register still reads
   open at high severity. Left for a RAID pass rather than edited here, because
   this document is not the register.
