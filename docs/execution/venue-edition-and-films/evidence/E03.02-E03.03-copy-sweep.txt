CLASSIFICATION OF ALL FIVE HITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every one is a citation by the contract's own definition: "the line forbids the
word. A prohibited-claims list has to be able to name what it prohibits."

1. line 504, p1-permanence, "for life" and "forever"
   Clause 12.3 item 1 of the agreement. The marketing-controls clause required
   by R-020 tells the venue which words it may never use. Naming them is the
   whole function of the clause, and acceptance criterion 8 requires the list.

2. line 506, p1-permanence, "guaranteed"
   Clause 12.3 item 2. Same reason.

3. line 509, d033-programme-terms, "certified"
   Clause 12.3 item 4, forbidding the venue from claiming the product is
   certified. Same reason.

4. line 606, d033-programme-terms, "partnership"
   Clause 17.1: "It does not create a partnership, an agency or a joint
   venture." The clause exists to deny the relationship the word describes.
   D-033 and FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS section 1 ban the word because
   partnership in Ireland is a legal relationship with shared liability, which
   is precisely why the agreement has to say it is not one.

Three further hits present on the first run were removed rather than exempted,
because in each case the same meaning was available in plain words:
  - "legally approved" in the draft block, reworded to "approved by a lawyer".
  - "VAT-exclusive" in acceptance criterion 2, reworded to "puts VAT on top of
    the price".
  - "certification" in clause 13.5, reworded to "holds no certificate".
E03.03 acceptance criterion 11 was likewise reworded to name the rule ids rather
than list the banned words, which took that file from six hits to zero.
