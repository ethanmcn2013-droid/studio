## 1. What this document contains

Section 3 is the draft couple terms of service. Section 4 is the draft couple privacy
notice. Both are written for a person planning a wedding, not for a lawyer. A privacy
notice a couple will not read has failed regardless of its legal quality.

Everything outside those two sections is working material: what the drafts reconcile
against, what they deliberately do not say, the decision points collected in one place,
and the questions a solicitor has to settle.

**The couple never sees a price.** D-001 point 1. No euro figure, no rate and no
percentage appears anywhere in sections 3 or 4. The venue paid. The couple is told that
much and nothing more.

## 2. What these drafts reconcile against, and the three defects they do not inherit

A sponsored couple reaches Signal Studio through `app.signalstudio.ie`. The pages that
already govern them are `app/src/app/terms/page.tsx` and `app/src/app/privacy/page.tsx`,
both last updated 2026-05-07. Two more pages govern the marketing site:
`studio/src/app/terms/page.tsx` and `studio/src/app/privacy/page.tsx`, both 2026-07-12.

**These drafts are written to replace the two `app` pages for a Venue Edition couple, and
to be reconcilable with the two `studio` pages.** Where they contradict a live page, the
contradiction is named here rather than left for someone to find.

### Defect 1 — the governing law on the live app terms is a placeholder

`app/src/app/terms/page.tsx`, Governing law section, verbatim:

> These terms are governed by the laws of [NEEDS-REVIEW: pick state once entity formed],
> without regard to conflict-of-laws rules.

"Pick state" is United States drafting language in a document that will govern Irish
consumers. `studio/src/app/terms/page.tsx` already says Irish law and the exclusive
jurisdiction of the Irish courts. **The draft in section 3 says Irish law.** The live app
page has to be corrected or the couple is governed by a blank.

### Defect 2 — the postal address on the live app privacy page is a placeholder

`app/src/app/privacy/page.tsx`, Contact section, verbatim:

> Mailing address: [NEEDS-REVIEW: add postal address once entity is formed].

A controller's identity and contact details are the first thing an Article 13 notice
carries. The draft does not invent an address. It carries **`[BLANK-1]`**.

### Defect 3 — the live app privacy page states the wrong minimum age

`app/src/app/privacy/page.tsx:165`, verbatim:

> Tasks is for people thirteen and older.

`app/src/app/terms/page.tsx` repeats it: "You need to be thirteen or older." Section 31 of
the Data Protection Act 2018 sets the Irish digital age of consent at **16**, and the role
map §6 closes this as an error rather than an open question.
`studio/src/app/terms/page.tsx` already says 16. **The draft says 16.** The two app pages
have to be corrected.

### One further conflict, recorded, that is not this task's to resolve

`studio/src/app/terms/page.tsx`, Paid plans:

> We may change pricing for new subscribers at any time. Existing subscribers will keep
> their current price for at least the remainder of the term they paid for, and we will
> give thirty days' notice before increasing it.

That is the opposite of the D-009 founding lock. It governs the venue, not the couple, so
it is out of scope here and is raised against E03.02 and E03.03 rather than silently
reconciled.

## 3. DRAFT — Signal Studio Venue Edition, couple terms of service

> **Signal Studio, Venue Edition**
> **Terms for couples**
>
> Draft. Not in force. No solicitor has reviewed these documents, and no accountant has
> verified the financial or VAT position.
>
> Last updated [BLANK-2: the date this is first published].

> **1. Who this is for**
>
> This is for you, if your wedding venue gave you a Signal Studio workspace as part of

> party to it.

> **2. What it costs you**
>
> Nothing. Your venue has paid for your workspace. You will not be asked for a card, and
> nothing in your workspace will try to sell you an upgrade to keep what you already have.
>
> If you choose to buy something from Signal Studio later, that is a separate agreement
> and the price is on the page where you buy it.

Note. D-001 point 1 keeps every price away from the couple. The second sentence of the
first paragraph is a promise about product behaviour, and it binds the product: an upsell
placed in a gifted wedding workspace would break it. It is drafted deliberately because
the alternative is a founding venue discovering it on a couple's screen.

> **3. How long you have it**
>
> Your workspace is fully yours to use for **eighteen months from the day you open it, or
> three months past your wedding day, whichever is later**.
>
> If your wedding moves later, your access moves with it. It never moves earlier. If you
> correct your date to an earlier day, your access stays where it was.

Note. D-022, and the wording is fixed by rule P7 in `prohibited-claims.v1.json`: the bare
figure is forbidden because the failure it hides is a couple losing access before their
own wedding at the venue that gifted it. See **`[DP-3]`**: the second limb of that sentence
is a promise the product does not currently compute.

> **4. What happens after that**
>
> Your plan does not disappear. It becomes a keepsake: free, read-only, and yours to read
> for as long as Signal Studio runs. You can download a complete copy at any time, before
> or after that date, and the copy is yours to keep whatever happens to us.
>
> Nobody can promise a service will still be running in twenty years, and we are not going
> to pretend otherwise. Take the download. That is the part that does not depend on us.

Note. D-010 point 3, and rule P1 forbids the word this paragraph refuses. The read-only
policy, the export builder and its brand-voice tests exist
(`app/src/lib/keepsake/keepsake.ts` and `keepsake.test.ts`). **The download route, the
Keepsake surface and the enforcement of read-only do not exist**, per E06.11 §C11. See
**`[DP-4]`**.

> **5. The two of you**
>
> A wedding workspace is built for two people. Either of you can invite the other, and both
> of you can be owners.
>
> If you separate, or if one of you asks us to delete their account, we will not decide
> between you. What we do in that situation is set out in a separate document and we will
> point you to it before it matters. [BLANK-3: the link, once E03.10 is decided.]

Note. This is deliberately thin. The role map records the separation rule as **CANNOT
ANSWER** and E03.10 presents options rather than inventing one. A term that promised a
rule that does not exist would be worse than a term that says a rule is coming. Today,
account deletion by whoever holds `ownerUserId` destroys the shared workspace including the
other person's work (`app/src/server/account-erasure.ts`, R-023), which is why this
paragraph cannot be written any further until E03.10 is decided.

> **6. Your content is yours**
>
> Everything you write, upload and plan is yours. You keep it. We store it, show it back to
> you, show it to the people you share it with, and back it up. That is the whole of the
> permission you give us.
>
> We do not sell it. We do not use it to advertise to you. We do not train a model on it.
> We do not put it in our marketing without asking you first, specifically, for that.

Note. D-010 point 1: couples are asked for nothing by default, and any case-study use is a
separate specific opt-in. The "train a model" sentence is a commitment about our own use.
It is not a statement that no model ever sees the text: see section 4 and **`[DP-6]`**.

> **7. What we ask of you**
>
> Do not use your workspace to break the law, to harass anyone, or to store material you
> have no right to store.
>
> Do not try to get into anyone else's workspace.
>
> If you do any of that we will contact you first and explain what we saw. If it is serious
> or it continues, we will suspend the account.

> **8. The people you name in your plan**
>
> Your plan will fill up with other people: your families, your guests, the people you are
> hiring. They did not sign up to anything, and some of them will not know Signal Studio
> exists.
>
> We are the ones responsible for them, not you. If one of them contacts us and asks what
> we hold about them, or asks to be taken off a page you published, we answer them
> ourselves and we act. We will tell you when we do.
>
> Two things follow. Write about people as if they might read it, because one day one of
> them might. And if we ask you to take something out, please take it out.

Note. Role map §9 and Scenario 1. The last paragraph is a request, not a term, on purpose:
a term the couple can breach here would push the couple toward being a controller of guest
data, which is the opposite of the position §1 of the role map takes.

> **9. Sharing and publishing**
>
> You can share your plan two ways. A private link, which only works for people you send it
> to. And a published page, which anyone who has the address can open.
>
> A published page is public. There is no sign-in on it. We ask search engines not to list

Note. Every sentence there is a statement about shipped behaviour and is verified in
E03.06 §3. The wording matches the publish confirmation panel already live at
`app/src/components/app/settings/sections/workspace.tsx:624-630` rather than inventing a
second version of the same promise.

> **10. What your venue can see**

> guest list, not your photographs, not who you have hired. Not on request, and not by

Note. Role map Scenario 3, which is the strongest answer in the map, and the sponsor

> To the fullest extent Irish law allows, our total liability to you for anything arising

Note. The couple is a consumer. R-013 point 2 records that an unfair term is simply void,

Note. Role map founder decision 5, and it matches the live studio privacy page, which

> **What we hold about you**
>
> **Your account.** Your email address, your name if you gave one, and an identifier from

>
> **Your workspace.** Everything you put in it. Tasks, notes, timeline items, dates,

>

> **Support.** If you email us, we keep the email.
>
> **Reliability.** Server logs, error reports and counts of which parts of the product get
> used. Error reports have personal data stripped before they are sent.

> **Why we are allowed to hold it**
>
> For your account and your workspace, because we cannot give you the product otherwise,
> and because we have a legitimate interest in keeping it working, backed up and secure.
>
> For your invitation, because your venue and Signal Studio have a shared interest in you
> actually getting the thing you were given.
>
> For the people you name in your plan, because we have a legitimate interest in hosting
> your planning for you, balanced against their interests, which is why the section below

Note. Role map §12 items 1 and 2 both record the lawful basis as **UNRESOLVED**. The draft
does not pick one and present it as settled: it describes the interests plainly, which is
what a notice has to do either way, and the formal basis is carried as `[DP-7]` and as an
open legal point. Article 6(1)(b) is never relied on alone for anything touching a third
party, per the role map's own instruction.

>

>

> off a public page, and we will not ask you for more information about yourself than we
> already have.
>

> remove it ourselves. We write back and tell you what we did.

Note. This is the Article 14 content the live notices have none of (role map §10.4), and
the process is Scenario 1's corrected shape, not v0.1's withdrawn "route it to the couple".
The last paragraph exists because role map founder decision 1, the data-protection footer

## 7. Open legal points

These are the questions a solicitor has to settle. They are the brief for the fixed-fee
review D-023 funds at EUR 3,000 of cleared founding revenue.

1. What is the lawful basis for holding a couple's private workspace content: Article
   6(1)(f) with a written legitimate-interests assessment, or Article 6(1)(b) to provide
   the service plus 6(1)(f) for hosting, backup and security? (Role map §12 item 1.)
2. What is the lawful basis for invitation and redemption, and does it differ between
   Signal Studio and the venue: Article 6(1)(f) for both, or Article 6(1)(b) inside the
   venue's booking relationship? (Role map §12 item 2.)
3. Is there any Article 9(2) condition available for the special-category free text that
   Signal Studio's own template instructs a couple to collect, and if there is not, is a
   documented harm-reduction position with a stated end date a defensible place to stand?
   (Role map §4, R-017.)
4. Does the couple's household exemption survive the commercial-connection limb of Article
   2(2)(c), given that the service is sponsored by a venue inside a paid booking
   relationship, and if it does not, does this notice have to tell the couple they are a
   controller of their guests' data? (Role map §12 item 4.)
5. Is a liability cap of one hundred euro, in a consumer contract where the consumer paid
   nothing and a third party paid on their behalf, enforceable under the European Union
   (Unfair Terms in Consumer Contracts) Regulations, or is it void? (R-013 point 2.)
6. Can a couple's objection under Article 21 to activation reporting be honoured without
   breaching the venue agreement, and if it cannot, which one gives way?
7. How far does the Article 14(5)(b) "disproportionate effort" exception actually get
   Signal Studio for guests named by a couple, and does publishing this notice at a stable
   URL satisfy the "publicly available information" condition the exception depends on?
   (Role map §12 item 7.)
8. Does Article 30(5) record-keeping bite now, given that special-category processing is
   present and the processing is not occasional, and that the venue-facing reporting
   operation is live rather than planned? (Role map §10.6 and §13 Finding 1.)
9. Which vendors hold workspace content, in which countries, are their agreements executed
   and dated, and does any transfer need an Article 46 mechanism? (Role map §12 item 11.)
