## 1. Why this one matters more than the other two

D-009 point 3 is the whole founding offer in one sentence: the €1,000 rate is
held "for as long as the agreement renews continuously without lapse".

Everything else about the Founding 25 is decided. The number, the cohort size,
the price, the VAT basis, the change-of-control rule, the entitlement model, the
couple term. All ratified. **"Continuously" is not defined anywhere**, which
means the condition under which a venue loses €500 a year, permanently, does not
yet exist in words.

That sentence goes into 25 signed agreements. It is the sentence a venue's
solicitor reads first, and it is the sentence Signal Studio is held to.

## 2. The timing fact that shapes the whole answer

**Paid agreements today: 0 of 25** (`STATUS.md`, Founding 25 outcome table).
An annual term is 365 days from the instant payment clears
(`ANNUAL_TERM_DAYS = 365`, `annualTermEndsAtMs()`).

So the first renewal cannot fall due before roughly **September 2027**, about a
year after release. Two conclusions follow, and they pull in opposite
directions on purpose.

**Get the wording right now.** It goes into E03.02 and E03.03, which are the
next two tasks on the critical path, and the offer freeze is **2026-08-15**,
eleven days away. After the freeze this needs a change request.

**Do not build the machinery now.** Anything built for renewal in the next 29
days sits unexercised for twelve months, and the tests are the only proof it
works until then. The state machine that already exists (E08.03) is enough, and
it is parameterised, so ratifying the numbers is a one-line change with no
rework.

## 4. What is genuinely open

Five questions, no invented answers.

1. **How much notice before a term ends?** WP-07 proposed 30 days,
   `ratified: false`.
2. **How long between a term ending and the agreement counting as lapsed?**
   WP-07 proposed 30 days, `ratified: false`.
3. **What changes for the venue and its couples during that window?** Nothing
   is decided. Nothing is wired either, so today the answer is "nothing", by
   accident rather than by choice.
4. **Is a lapse a date, or a decision taken on a date?** The code makes it
   derived and refuses to let it be declared early. It does not say whether
   crossing the date is itself the lapse.
5. **What is a returning venue offered?** Conflict A.

## 5. The three real options for the grace regime

### Option 1. Thirty days notice, thirty days grace, as proposed

Ship what WP-07 already parameterised. One line changes,
`VENUE_RENEWAL_POLICY.ratified` becomes `true`, and the numbers can go into the
agreement.

**Cost.** The first renewals fall in September 2027, which is the back end of
the Irish wedding season, at the venues that run 150 to 250 weddings a year.
Thirty days of August and September at a busy venue is roughly twenty working
days for a finance function that is not the person who signed. One missed email
costs that venue €500 a year, permanently, under a lock that cannot be
re-granted. That is a foreseeable way to lose a founding reference over
administration.

### Option 2. Sixty and thirty days notice, sixty days grace, grace invisible, lapse recorded by hand (recommended)

Two notices rather than one, both recorded as delivered. Sixty days of grace
rather than thirty. Nothing the venue or any couple can see changes during
grace: invitations keep issuing, branding stays, the lock holds. Crossing the
end of grace makes a lapse **possible**, and Ethan records it, having told the
venue first.

**Cost.** Up to sixty days of service supplied without payment, to at most 25
venues, once a year. At the modelled heavy-usage cost of roughly €1,750 a year
for the largest venue, sixty days is about €290 of exposure in the worst single
case and far less in every other. Sending two notices instead of one costs one
email per venue per year. Against that, no founding venue is lost to a slow
invoice, and every lapse has a human name on it.

**Why grace stays invisible.** Turning invitations off on day 31 punishes
couples who booked a wedding for a delay in a finance inbox. D-020 point 2
already establishes the principle that a licence problem is never a couple's
problem. Extending it to grace is the same principle, applied one step earlier.

### Option 3. Publish no numbers at all

The agreement says the rate is held while the agreement renews and ends if it
does not, and never states a notice period or a grace window.

**Cost.** Silence does not protect the venue and it does not protect Signal
Studio. It puts 25 relationships entirely at Ethan's discretion, which is
exactly what writing the founding promise down was meant to remove, and it makes
the one question a venue's solicitor will ask unanswerable at signature. It also
leaves `VENUE_RENEWAL_POLICY` unratified indefinitely, so the internal worklist
keeps running on numbers nobody has agreed.

## 6. Recommendation

**Option 2, with two additions.**

1. **Notice at 60 days and again at 30 days before the term ends.** Both sent by
   hand, both recorded as delivered. Delivery is recorded, never assumed.
2. **Grace of 60 days after the term ends.** During grace the agreement has not
   lapsed, the founding lock holds, issuance continues and branding stays.
   Nothing visible changes.
3. **A lapse is a recorded decision taken on or after a date, not the date
   itself.** The end of grace makes it available. Ethan records it, and the
   venue is told before it happens, not after.
4. **The lock is not lost where Signal Studio cannot show the renewal notice was
   delivered.** Notices are sent by one person by hand and there is no sender in
   the repository. A rule that costs a venue €500 a year for a step Signal
   Studio failed to take is indefensible, and this closes it without weakening
   the condition in any case where the notice did go out.
5. **Reactivation: confirm the standard rate, and record the one exception.** Two
   approved-task documents already say a returning venue comes back at €1,500.
   Confirm that rather than reopen it, and record that Ethan may restore a
   founding rate personally, in writing, with the reason recorded, so the code
   can stop refusing and start following a decision.

**Reasoning.** Option 1 is defensible on paper and fragile in practice, and its
failure mode is the one this programme can least afford: losing a flagship
reference venue over an invoice, permanently, with a number that cannot be
re-issued. Option 3 removes the promise's only enforceable edge. Option 2 keeps
the condition real, which R-008 and D-001 point 16 require, while making an
accidental loss close to impossible. The extra sixty days cost a few hundred
euro across the whole cohort in the worst case. One lost founding venue costs
€500 a year for as long as the programme runs, plus the reference.

### The wording, if this is approved

For the agreement and the founding schedule. Plain English, no permanence term,
no em dashes.

> **Renewal.** Your agreement runs for twelve months from the date your payment
> clears. We will write to you 60 days before it ends, and again 30 days before,
> with the renewal invoice and the date it is due.
>
> **If a renewal is late.** Your agreement does not end on the renewal date. You
> have a further 60 days, and during that time nothing changes: your couples
> keep receiving invitations, your name stays where it is, and your founding
> rate holds.
>
> **If it is not paid.** After that, the agreement can end, and the founding
> rate ends with it. We will tell you before that happens, not afterwards. If we
> cannot show that we sent you the renewal notice, your rate is not affected.
>
> **If your agreement ends.** Every couple who has already opened a workspace
> keeps it, for their full term, with their Keepsake. Only new invitations stop.
> Your number stays yours in the record and your place is not given to anyone
> else. If you come back later, you come back at the standard rate.

## 7. What is needed from Ethan

Five lines.

1. Notice: **60 and 30 days**, or 30 only, or another pair.
2. Grace: **60 days**, or 30, or another number.
3. Grace is invisible to the venue and its couples: **yes** or no.
4. A lapse is a recorded decision rather than an automatic date: **yes** or no.
5. A returning venue comes back at the standard rate, with restoration available
   personally and in writing: **yes** or no.
