**What this is.** A first draft of the vendor and recipient schedule to the Signal

**What it is not.** Not legal advice. Not a reviewed instrument. Not a compliance

## 0. Two conventions, and why each one is deliberate

### 0.1 The word "sub-processor" is not used in this schedule

Role map §1 and §9 reject the label. The prefix *sub-* presupposes a processor
role **above** these vendors, held by Signal Studio, which the role map concludes
does not exist: Signal Studio is a controller of every operation in the map and a
processor of none.

`studio/content/hq/decisions/gdpr-data-lifecycle-policy.md` item 6 calls Clerk,
Stripe, Turso and Vercel "sub-processors". Role map §1 identifies that word choice
as the fingerprint of the self-image it corrects. This schedule uses **recipient**
for the general case and records each vendor's role individually in §2.

### 0.2 Roles are recorded per vendor, not applied in a block

Role map §9 records that an earlier blanket correction ("where Signal Studio is
controller, they are processors") replaced one wrong label with another. Stripe in
particular is **both**: a processor for payment processing on instruction, and an
**independent controller** for fraud, anti-money-laundering and its own regulatory
obligations under its own terms. Those two legs need different treatment.

## 1. How this list was built, and what would falsify it

Every entry was derived on 2026-08-04 from one or more of:

- the dependency manifests: `app/package.json`, `studio/package.json`;
- every `process.env.*` reference in `app/src` and `studio/src` and
  `studio/scripts`, enumerated exhaustively;
- the call sites named in each row;
- `studio/docs/INFRASTRUCTURE.md`, which is the workspace's canonical account map;
- `evidence/infrastructure-blob-and-email-auth-2026-08-03.md`, for the one
  verified region fact.

**What this method finds:** anything the code can talk to.
**What it does not find:** anything provisioned outside the repository, anything a
vendor does downstream of its own API, and any vendor whose integration was
removed from code but not from an account. A console-by-console audit would
falsify or confirm this list, and it has not been done.

**E09.10 Answer 4 carries a shorter list**, verified on 2026-08-03 from the `app`
dependency manifest: Vercel, Turso, Clerk, Resend, Sentry, Stripe and Vercel Blob.
**That list is incomplete.** It omits Anthropic, Upstash, PostHog and Google.
E09.10 needs correcting against this schedule, and that is raised in the
recommendation packet rather than done quietly here.

### 2.1 Two access paths that are not product vendors, recorded because they hold real access

Not personal-data recipients in the ordinary sense, and listed because leaving
them out would make this schedule read as more complete than it is.

- **GitHub Actions holds production database credentials.**
  `studio/docs/INFRASTRUCTURE.md` records a `db-migration-drift` workflow running
  daily at 07:00 UTC with `TASKS_DATABASE_URL` and `TASKS_AUTH_TOKEN` in Actions
  secrets. That is a scheduled job outside the application with credentials that
  reach couple planning data. It is a real access path and it should be assessed.
- **`evidence/E08.08-E08.09-observability-and-continuity.md` §5** records a
  founder decision requested and not yet taken: whether scheduled backups should
  live in GitHub Actions artifact storage. It was not built without an answer
  precisely because it would put couple planning data into Actions storage. That
  reasoning is right and it is recorded here so the decision is visible from the
  legal document as well as the engineering one.

## 3. Regions and transfers. Nothing is claimed that has not been verified.

**P15 applies:** no claim about where data is held, who processes it, or how it is
transferred, until it has been verified in this programme. A sentence promising
that nothing ever leaves one country is the easiest thing to say on a call and the
hardest to withdraw from a signed document. P15 names that exact sentence as
banned; this schedule does not reproduce it.

### 3.3 One DNS observation, stated with its limits

The sending subdomain `send.signalstudio.ie` carries an MX record
`10 feedback-smtp.eu-west-1.amazonses.com`, verified in DNS on 2026-08-03.

That establishes where **bounce and complaint feedback** is routed for Resend's
sending path. It does **not** establish where Resend stores message content, and
it is not a region claim about Resend. It is recorded because it also reveals that
Amazon Simple Email Service sits behind Resend's delivery, which a venue's adviser
would want to know and which no Signal Studio document currently says.

### 3.4 A live page already makes region claims this schedule does not

`studio/src/app/privacy/page.tsx` states: account records at Clerk (United
States); workspace and product data at Turso, "primarily Frankfurt"; error reports
at Sentry (United States); hosting at Vercel (United States and edge regions).
`studio/src/app/security/page.tsx` states that the database "is encrypted at rest"
and sits "primarily in the Frankfurt region".

**None of those has been verified in this programme.** They may well be right.
This schedule does not repeat them, does not contradict them, and records the
conflict rather than silently reconciling it. Verifying them in the provider
consoles is a bounded piece of work and is raised as a founder question.

## 4. Provisioning state, because "integrated" is not "running"

`studio/docs/INFRASTRUCTURE.md`, under "Provisioned deliberately later
(post-reset pass)", records: *"Sentry, PostHog, Upstash rate limiting, Anthropic
in production, all integrated in app code, none provisioned."*

| Recipient | Integrated in code | Provisioned |
|---|---|---|
| Vercel, Turso, Clerk, Stripe, Resend, Google Workspace | yes | yes |
| Vercel Blob | yes | yes, EU store connected; production redeploy outstanding |
| Sentry | yes | **no**. `app/src/instrumentation.ts` returns early when `SENTRY_DSN` is unset. Studio has no error reporting at all |
| Upstash | yes | **no**, and `app/src/lib/ratelimit.ts` fails open, so the documented AI burst caps are not enforced |
| PostHog | yes | **no** |
| Anthropic | yes | **no** in production |

**Why this table is in a legal document.** A schedule that lists a recipient
implies data reaches it. For four of these, the code path exists and the
credential does not. Listing them without this table would overstate the exposure.
Omitting them would understate it the moment a key is set, which is a
configuration change rather than a deploy. Both are stated.

**The obligation that follows:** provisioning any of these four is the moment a
new recipient starts receiving personal data, and under §7 the Venue must be told
before it happens. Setting an environment variable is a legal event here, not just
an operational one.

## 5. Anthropic, and the gap that is bigger than a drafting question

**This section exists because it is the single most consequential finding in this
schedule, and no privacy notice anywhere in the product mentions it.**

### 5.1 What actually goes to Anthropic

Verified at `app/src/server/actions/ai.ts` and `app/src/server/ai.ts`:

- **Draft a reply.** Sends the task title, the task description, the full
  conversation thread rendered as `Name: comment body` lines, the requesting
  user's display name, and any intent the user typed.
- **Summarise a thread.** Same conversation payload.
- **Weekly digest narration.** A snapshot built from workspace state.

That is **workspace content and participant names**. In a wedding workspace, that
is guests', family members' and suppliers' names, and whatever the couple typed
about them. Under §4.7 of the agreement, a wedding workspace will foreseeably
contain Article 9 special-category data in free text, and nothing in this path
filters for it.

Bounded, honestly: the model is only called when a user invokes one of these
actions, the calls are capped at 512 output tokens, and the whole path is inert in
production today because `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is unprovisioned.

### 5.2 Why it is a gap and not just a row

- `app/src/app/privacy/page.tsx` lists Clerk, Stripe, PostHog, Sentry, Resend and
  Vercel and then says, in terms, that that is the entire list. Anthropic is not
  on it, and neither are Turso, Vercel Blob or Upstash.
- `studio/src/app/privacy/page.tsx` lists Clerk, Turso, Vercel, Sentry, Resend,
  Stripe and Google Workspace. Anthropic is not on it, and neither are Vercel
  Blob or Upstash.
- **A privacy notice that says "that is the entire list" and omits the recipient
  that receives the most sensitive payload is a worse problem than a missing
  schedule.** It is stated here rather than fixed here, because both are live
  public pages and changing one inside a document task is not this task's call.

### 5.3 The decision this forces

Two options, and they are not equivalent:

- **(a) Name Anthropic as a recipient**, which means fixing both live privacy
  pages before the first sponsored couple redeems, and telling couples that AI
  features send workspace text to a model provider.
- **(b) Disable the AI features for sponsored wedding workspaces**, which removes
  the recipient rather than disclosing it, and is the smaller change while the key
  is unprovisioned.

This schedule does **not** choose. It is a founder decision and it is in §8.

## 6. Google Analytics, and a position that is decided but not fully shipped

D-033 decided **R-032 as Option A**: no third-party analytics on any couple-facing
public surface. `/p`, `/s`, `/share` and `/embed` are excluded from GA4 entirely
and unconditionally. D-034 extended it to Sentry on the same predicate, because
Sentry is a third party too and removing GA4 alone did not satisfy the ratified
sentence.

**Verified state on 2026-08-04:**

- On `claude/integrate-app`, `app/src/lib/public-analytics-boundary.ts` exports
  `isAnalyticsExcludedPath` over `["/p", "/s", "/share", "/embed"]`; both
  `google-tag.tsx` and `instrumentation-client.ts` gate on it, and those surfaces
  additionally receive an **enforcing** content-security policy that blocks the
  analytics hosts regardless of the report-only baseline. A contract test asserts
  each of those properties.
- On `app` `main`, that module does not exist. `GoogleTag` gates only on
  `isBareArtifactPath`, which covers `/s` alone, so `/p`, `/share` and `/embed`
  still load GA4.

The two commits are `4fca221` (R-031) and `81a49f6` (R-032). Neither is an
ancestor of `main`.

**So the decided position is real in code and not yet on the branch that ships.**
This schedule says that rather than describing the decision as if it were the
running behaviour.

## 7. Changes to this schedule

1. Signal Studio tells the Venue in writing **before** a new recipient of personal
   data is added, and the notice names what the recipient does and what data
   reaches it.
2. Provisioning a recipient that is integrated but not provisioned (§4) counts as
   adding one.
3. Signal Studio keeps this schedule current and dates every revision.
4. **This schedule replaces `studio/docs/security/subprocessors.md`**, which is a
   stub carrying candidate vendors "inferred from the stack" and no legal entity,
   region, transfer mechanism, retention or breach contact for any of them. That
   file should point here rather than continue to exist alongside it.

## 8. Points a solicitor must settle

1. Which of these recipients hold workspace content, in which countries, are their
   agreements executed and dated, and does any transfer require an Article 46
   mechanism (role map §12 item 11)?
2. Is Clerk a processor for the whole of what it does here, or does its own
   agreement carry an independent-controller leg that requires an Article 13
   disclosure of its own?
3. What is the correct Article 13 disclosure for Stripe's independent-controller
   leg (fraud and anti-money-laundering), and where must it appear?
4. Does sending workspace free text to a model provider require an Article 13
   disclosure naming the provider, a specific lawful basis of its own, or a
   restriction on the categories that may be sent, given that the text will
   foreseeably contain Article 9 data about third parties?
5. Is a privacy notice that lists recipients and says "that is the entire list"
   while omitting four of them a transparency breach that needs a correction
   notice, or is quietly correcting it sufficient?
6. Does a scheduled job in GitHub Actions holding production database credentials
   make GitHub a recipient that belongs in this schedule?
7. Is naming Amazon Simple Email Service, observed only from a DNS record behind
   Resend, required, or does naming Resend discharge the duty?
