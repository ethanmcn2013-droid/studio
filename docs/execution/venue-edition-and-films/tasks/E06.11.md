## Objective

Give the end of the couple's access term a destination: a free, read-only
Keepsake, and a download the couple owns and can open without Signal Studio.

## Business reason

The venue is buying a promise that outlives the wedding. D-010 point 3 ratified
free, read-only, indefinite Keepsake access "while the service exists", with a
one-click export. Without the export that sentence reduces to "we will keep
your wedding for as long as we happen to be around", which is not something to
put in front of twenty-five paying venues, and D-010 says so explicitly: the
export is what makes the promise honest.

Before this task, `grep -rni keepsake` across the app returned two lines, both
comments in a billing file saying the thing does not exist. The term arithmetic
was correct and shipped; the term simply had nowhere to end.

## Intended outcome

The access term, the phase it produces, and what each phase permits are
computable and tested. The export exists as a real artifact: one self-contained
HTML file a person can read in ten years with the product switched off, plus
the same content as JSON.

## Scope

The rules and the artifact. What the term is, what phase it produces, what a
phase permits, and what the couple gets to keep.

## Non-goals

- **Wiring.** No server action, no route, no UI. A download route needs the
  workspace read path and a decision about ZIP versus two files, and the
  read-only enforcement needs a call site in every mutating action in Tasks and
  Timeline. Both are real work and neither is safe to do half.
- **Deletion controls.** D-010 point 3 names export rights; deletion is
  `account-erasure.ts`'s and R-023's territory, which D-027 point 1 scheduled
  separately.
- **Storage promises.** There are none, deliberately.
- **Photographs inside the export.** The export inlines no bytes. Once
  photographs exist (E06.03) this needs revisiting, because a keepsake with no
  photographs in it is a weaker object than the page it came from.

## Implementation approach

Two pure modules, no database access, so every rule is testable at any date
without fixtures.

The lifecycle module implements `max(redemption + 548 days, wedding date + 90
days)` and returns which half of the max() won, so no surface can claim a date
is wedding-derived when it was not. It also holds every couple-facing sentence
about the term, which puts all the wording that could accidentally say
"forever" in one file that one test can police.

The export writes one self-contained HTML document. The hard requirement is
that it opens with no network: no script, no link, no img, no absolute URL, no
`@import`, no `url()`. That is asserted as a property of the output rather than
as a list of strings someone has to remember to update.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The term is `max(redemption + 548 days, wedding date + 90 days)`, never a
   flat eighteen months, and the R-015 case — books far ahead, redeems
   immediately — provably keeps access past the wedding.
2. The result names which half of the max() decided it, and no couple-facing
   sentence claims a wedding-derived date unless the wedding half won.
3. A postponement extends access automatically. A wedding date moved earlier
   never shortens a term already granted.
4. A couple is not "after the day" on the morning of their wedding: the wedding
   date is a day, not an instant.
5. The term ending produces a read-only Keepsake, not a lockout. Export,
   unpublish and delete survive it; every other write intent does not.
6. The export opens with no network access of any kind, asserted as a property
   of the document.
7. The export escapes the couple's own words: a supplier called `Rónán & Co`
   and a task titled `Order the cake <topper>` cannot break the file, and
   accented names survive intact.
8. Attribution is the venue's **name only**, once, in the footer. No logo, no
   badge, no venue-written message. An export with no venue carries no
   attribution line at all.
9. No phase of the couple-facing wording, and no line of the export, says
   "forever", "for life", "in perpetuity", a flat eighteen months, "guarantee",
   "GDPR" or "compliant", and none carries an em dash or an exclamation mark.
10. Every criterion is covered by a test registered in `package.json`'s `test`
    script.
11. What is not built is stated as not built.

## Risks

- **A policy nobody calls is not a control.** `isPermittedInKeepsake` is
  declared and tested and has zero production callers. That is the exact defect
  class recorded as R-028 and as the unregistered-test finding in
  `evidence/E05-production-blocker-app-gate.md`. It is stated here so it cannot
  be mistaken for enforcement.
- **R-015 blocks persistence** of the computed term.
- The export currently carries no photographs, because none exist.

## Decisions required

1. **Does the export ship as two files or one archive?** Two files is simpler
   and needs no zip dependency; an archive is one click. The builder returns
   files and an archive name, so either is a small wiring change.
2. **Does Keepsake keep the couple's public link alive?** A read-only workspace
   whose `/p` page stays up is a better keepsake and a longer public exposure.
   Under D-033 that page is `noindex` by default, which makes it defensible,
   but it is a decision rather than an implementation detail.
3. **Is read-only enforcement release-blocking for 1 September?** No sponsored
   couple reaches Keepsake before roughly March 2028, so the honest answer may
   be that the rules and the export ship now and enforcement follows.
