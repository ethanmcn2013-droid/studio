## Objective

Remove "founding partner" from every surface a venue can see, and install a
standing check that fails the build the next time a banned programme term
appears in venue-facing copy.

## Business reason

D-033 ratified R-042: the programme is **the Founding 25**, and a member of it
is **a founding venue**. "Partner" is on the banned programme-term list because
it describes standing and involvement the agreement does not grant, and E02.10
chose the programme terminology specifically to be legally safe. The term
currently sits on a printed card that is handed to a venue at the moment of
signature. Twenty-nine days from release, a venue reading two different names
for the same programme is the cheapest possible way to make the offer look
improvised.

The sweep is the smaller half. R-042 was found nine months after the term was
introduced, by a manual review, because the machinery meant to catch it does not
run. That is the same defect class as R-028 (a threshold enforced only in a
test) and the three unregistered privacy tests. The durable fix is the point.

## Intended outcome

1. No venue-facing surface in the studio repository calls a Founding 25 venue a
   partner.
2. `prohibited-claims.v1.json` is read by a script that runs in CI, so the next
   occurrence fails a build rather than surviving to a review.
3. The record states what was deliberately not changed, and why, so a later
   reader does not read an exclusion as a miss.

## Implementation approach

Four passes, in this order, because the order matters:

1. **Derive** the true occurrence list independently and classify it.
2. **Sweep** venue-facing first, then internal, then re-baseline the experience
   hashes in the same commit.
3. **Build the reader** as a second stage inside
   `scripts/check-venue-edition-contract.mjs`, which is already inside
   `pnpm test` and therefore already blocking in both CI workflows. Pin the
   swept surfaces at their post-sweep counts, so the pins lock the work in
   rather than recording the debt.
4. **Verify by mutation**, in both directions, and record the real output.

**On route renames.** `rooms.ts` states as its first rule: *"`route` is
permanent. Rename `name` freely; never move URLs."* `rooms.test.ts` enforces it
in both directions. Renaming `/hq/partner-card` is therefore a fight with the
repository's own contract, for zero venue-visible benefit, since `/hq` is behind
a shared password and no venue sees the URL. The labels are changed; the URLs
are not. This is recorded rather than half-done, per the brief.
