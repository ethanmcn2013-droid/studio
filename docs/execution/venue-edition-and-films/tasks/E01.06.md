# E01.06 — Assign an accountable owner or lead agent to every epic while retaining founder approval over product, commercial and release decisions.

## Objective

Give every epic one accountable owner, and keep every decision that is the
founder's in the founder's hands regardless of who executes the work.

## Business reason

Two agent lanes and one founder. Without a named owner per epic, work either
duplicates across lanes or falls between them. Without the approval reservation,
delegation quietly becomes decision-making, which is the specific thing the
founder sign-off rule exists to prevent.

## Intended outcome

Every epic has an owner. Every task has an executor and an approver. The
separation between who does the work and who approves it is enforced by the tool,
not by good intentions.

## Deliverables

- [x] An owner on all 15 epics
- [x] An executor and an approver on all 211 tasks
- [x] The lane change the founder made in D-008: E11 drafting moves to Claude Code, execution stays with Ethan
- [x] An assessment of whether approval authority is enforced in code

## Scope

Epic ownership, task executor assignment, and the enforcement of founder approval
authority.

## Non-goals

- Not re-litigating the lane split. D-008 accepted it.
- Not the motion lane's internal allocation. That is Codex's.
- Not per-task effort or scheduling.

## Implementation approach

Assess. Ownership was assigned at import and ratified in D-019 and D-008. Verify
programmatically that no epic and no task is unowned, that the D-008 lane change
for E11 and the D-015 Q6 change for the three E13 engineering tasks are actually
reflected in state rather than only in the decision text, and that the tool
refuses to let anyone but the founder mark work Done.

## Acceptance criteria

1. All 15 epics carry an owner. No epic is unassigned.
2. All 211 tasks carry an executor and an approver. No task is `unassigned`.
3. The founder's D-008 lane change is reflected in state: E11 drafting tasks sit with Claude Code, and E11 execution remains the founder's.
4. D-015 Q6 is reflected in state: E13.04, E13.15 and E13.16 sit with Claude Code rather than the motion lane.
5. Founder approval authority is enforced in code, not convention: `transition` cannot set `done`, and `approve` refuses without criteria, evidence and founder-review status.
6. A test asserts point 5, so the enforcement cannot be removed silently.
7. The assessment names any epic whose recorded owner disagrees with the decision that assigned it.

## Validation plan

- Programmatic count of epics and tasks with a null or `unassigned` owner, executor or approver. Expect zero.
- Compare each E11 and E13 task's recorded executor against D-008 point 2 and D-015 Q6
- `node --test tools/project-control.test.mjs` and name the specific tests that defend the approval boundary

## Evidence required

- The owner and executor counts, real output
- The E11 and E13 executor listing against the decisions
- The named tests defending founder approval
