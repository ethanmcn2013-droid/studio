## Objective

Watch the six surfaces that carry the release, with a defined check, a defined
frequency and a defined action for each, for the duration of the first window.

## Business reason

A release window is the one period where a defect is both most likely and most
expensive. Twenty-five venues are looking at the same pages in the same fortnight.

## Intended outcome

A daily record exists for the release window showing what was checked, what was
seen, and what was done about it.

## Deliverables

- [ ] A one-page watch sheet naming the six surfaces, the check, the frequency
      and the action on failure.
- [ ] The daily records for the window.
- [ ] A short close-out naming what broke and what was changed.

## Scope

The product (app health, error rate), outbound email delivery, the landing and
proposal pages, the booking or reply path, the films, and the account surface.

## Non-goals

- Building a dashboard. A watch sheet a founder actually fills in beats a
  dashboard nobody opens.
- Adding third-party analytics to couple-facing surfaces while R-032 is open.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Six surfaces each have a named check, a frequency, a threshold and an action.
2. The health endpoint is checked on a schedule and a failure reaches a person.
3. Email delivery is measured by delivery and reply, never by opens. D-026's R4
   defines `opened` as a first-party load of the redemption page and never an
   email open.
4. Landing and proposal pages are checked for HTTP status daily during the
   window, because a 404 on a page named in a proposal is invisible from inside.
5. Error monitoring is confirmed live by an event that arrived, not by a DSN.
6. Every measurement of couple-facing behaviour respects the suppression rules;
   no venue-attributable count is produced below the floors.
7. The close-out names what broke. A window with nothing recorded is a window
   nobody watched.
