# E12.09 — Produce the concise one-page commercial proposal.

**Task ID:** E12.09
**Epic:** E12 — Commercial surfaces and sales assets

## Objective

Produce the one page a venue is sent after a walkthrough, carrying the whole
ratified commercial position in the fewest true words, as a real artefact that
can be printed and linked rather than a document describing one.

## Business reason

This is the last thing a venue reads before it decides. `E11.11-12` §2 already
fixed the words and stated plainly that "E12.09 designs it; the words are
these". Until the artefact exists, the proposal is a blockquote in an evidence
document, which means every send is a copy-paste and every copy-paste is a
chance for a price to travel without its conditions. Twenty-five venues, one
document, one price, one lock: the surface that carries them has to be built
once and imported everywhere.

## Intended outcome

The proposal exists as data with exactly one copy of the words in the
repository, rendered by a founder tool that fills the six slots, refuses to
look finished when a slot is empty, and prints to a single A4 page.

## Scope

**What artefact this is, decided and stated.** A **printable page behind the HQ
password gate**, not a public route and not a static document. The reasoning:
the proposal carries one named venue and a price, so it cannot be public; it
must be printable because `E11.11` §2 sends it as an attachment as well as a
link; and it must be filled from slots because the paid count is verified at
the moment of sending and never carried over from the previous proposal
(`FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS.md` §1).

It is not indexed: `robots: { index: false, follow: false }` on the route, plus
the standing `/hq/` disallow in `robots.ts`, plus absence from the sitemap.
Three mechanisms, because the page names a venue and a price.

## Non-goals

- **The commercial summary table as a separate asset.** `E11.11` §3 is rendered

## Implementation approach

The words live in one module as structured data, not in JSX. Two surfaces can
then render the same proposal without either restating it, and a test can sweep
the prose without parsing markup. A second copy of a price is how two prices
happen.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The proposal exists as a single module that is the only copy of the proposal
   words in the repository, and every rendering surface imports it rather than
   restating any sentence.
2. Both prices appear, both are stated as including VAT at the prevailing rate,
   and the founding rate is described as €500 a year less.
3. The founding lock is stated as holding while the agreement renews
   continuously without lapse, and no permanence wording appears anywhere in the
   filled or the unfilled proposal.
4. The number is stated as 01/25 through 25/25 assigned when payment clears, the
   fourteen-day hold is stated once, and no countdown or staleable scarcity
   claim appears.
5. Entitlement is every couple with a booking, with no seat count and no numeric
   entitlement anywhere.
6. The launch-scope sentence appears verbatim as its own block: "At launch you
   see your invitations and who opened them. Adoption figures follow later in the
   year." The negative half of the privacy block travels with the positive half.
7. The couple term always carries the grace rule; no bare eighteen months
   appears on any line.
8. Nothing states or implies legal approval, solicitor review, accountant
   verification or unqualified GDPR compliance.
9. No banned programme term (partner, member, investor, exclusive, guaranteed,
   certified), no em dash and no exclamation mark reaches the page, and this is
   asserted against the unfilled proposal as well as the filled one.
10. An unfilled slot makes the page visibly unsendable on screen and in print,
    rather than producing a proposal that looks finished.
11. The artefact is one page on A4 with 16mm margins, and is not indexed.
12. The route carries no third-party script and no client-side measurement of
    the reader.
