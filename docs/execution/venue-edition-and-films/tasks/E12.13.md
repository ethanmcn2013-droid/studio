## Objective

Produce the three things a venue uses after a couple books: a print-ready
welcome card, the email wording, and the onboarding sequence stated from the
running product rather than from a plan.

## Business reason

This is the first thing a sponsored couple reads, and it is written by a venue
rather than by Signal Studio. Everything that goes wrong at this moment goes
wrong in public, at the venue that gifted it. The two ways it goes wrong are a
price reaching a couple who was never part of the commercial arrangement, and a
promise about what happens after the wedding that the product cannot keep.

## Intended outcome

A venue can send one email and hand over one card, both correct, and can read
what actually happens next with each step marked built, done by hand, or not
built at all.

## Scope

**Couple-facing, and every rule follows from that.**

- **The couple never sees a price** (D-001). No figure, no currency, no value,
  no "normally", no "free". "Free" states that a price exists and that somebody
  paid it, which puts the couple inside an arrangement they were not part of.
  The only permitted statement is that there is nothing to pay.
- **The venue's name is the only venue thing and it appears once.** On the card
  it is `Compliments of <venue>`, which is the exact string the product already
  shows on first open. The printed object and the screen say the same four
  words. In the email it appears once, in the sign-off.
- **Nothing is promised that is not built** (D-016).

**The Keepsake, verified rather than assumed.** There is no Keepsake mode,
state, route or component in `app/src`, and no read-only state a workspace
enters at the end of its term. The two `keepsake` hits in that tree are code
comments in the Timeline print renderer using the ordinary English word; the
correction to an earlier, wrong grep is recorded in the verification run,
section 7. The only export that exists is
`app/src/app/api/account/export/route.ts`, an account-level machine-readable
file for a signed-in person. So the kit says one true thing about the end and
stops: the workspace does not switch off the day after the wedding, because the
term runs at least three months past it.

**The sequence is a description, not a plan.** Each of the eight steps carries
its state and the file or route the claim came from. Two are marked done by
hand, because R-043 is open and there is no venue login. The last is marked not
built.

**D-018.** The card ships as a print-ready file. The venue prints it, at its own
cost, or does not, so the email carries the whole message on its own.

## Non-goals

- **Building a Keepsake surface.** That is product work, not a kit.
- **A venue login or self-service issuing.** R-043, and D-027 point 4 puts it
  out of launch scope. The kit states that it does not exist.
- **Sending anything.** The route renders and prints. Nothing is stored and
  nothing is sent from it.
- **A venue-written welcome message.** D-027 point 3. There is no field for
  venue words and the kit does not invent one.

## Implementation approach

Three artefacts on one surface, in the order they are used, with the
couple-facing one first and printed alone. The sequence's state badges are
load-bearing rather than decorative: hiding the not-built row is exactly how a
venue ends up promising a Keepsake to a couple.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The kit produces a print-ready welcome card, email wording and an onboarding
   sequence from one module, with four slots and an explicit missing-slot state.
2. No couple-facing string carries a figure, currency, value word, "free",
   "complimentary", "sponsored" or "gift".
3. The only cost statement to a couple is that there is nothing to pay.
4. The card names the venue exactly once, as `Compliments of <venue>`, matching
   the product's own first-open string.
5. The email names the venue exactly once, in the sign-off.
6. Nothing carries a venue logo, colour, mark or venue-written message.
7. No couple-facing string promises a Keepsake, an export, a download, an
   archive or a read-only mode.
8. The one after-the-wedding claim is true today and does not describe what
   happens at the end of the term.
9. Both the card and the email carry the privacy sentence.
10. Nothing suggests the venue can be shown the plan through a setting.
11. The email carries no tracked link, image or button, and one subject.
12. Every onboarding step names an actor, a state of built, by hand or not
     built, and the file or route the claim came from.
13. The sequence marks issuing and adoption evidence as done by hand, and states
     that there is no login for the venue's team.
14. The last step is marked not built and its evidence names the Keepsake.
15. The editing rules forbid a coordinator adding a term, a value or a leaflet.
16. No prohibited claim, retired term, em dash or exclamation mark appears, in
     the filled kit, the unfilled kit, or the venue-facing half.
17. The route is registered in both registries and adds no new
     `experience:validate` failure.
18. The test is registered in `package.json` so CI runs it.

## Risks

- **The Keepsake gap is now written down in a venue-facing artefact.** A venue
  reading the sequence learns that the thing the agreement promises does not
  exist yet. That is the honest position under D-016 and it is a sales fact the
  founder should see before it is handed over.
- **R-043.** Two steps in the sequence depend on a founder answering an email.
  At twenty-five venues that is a real load and it is visible here.

## Decisions required

1. Does the card carry a QR code to the redemption address as well as the code?
   Nothing in this task assumes a destination that has not been confirmed.
2. Is the sequence, with its not-built row, handed to a venue as written, or
   kept as an internal document with a shorter version for the venue?
