# E03.06 — Finalise public Timeline terms, viewer privacy language, analytics disclosure and cookie requirements.

---

## Objective

Draft the terms and the privacy language for the pages a couple publishes, written against
the decided state of R-031 and R-032 and against the code as it actually runs, not against
the intended end state.

## Business reason

Every one of these pages carries other people's names. A wedding guest who follows a link
has no relationship with Signal Studio at all, and the venue that gifted the workspace is
the one whose reference is on the line if that page measures them, indexes them, or gives
them nowhere to go. E06.01 was held blocked on this task for exactly that reason.

## Intended outcome

One document that states, per surface, what is true today about tokens, indexing, caching,
framing, cookies, device storage and measurement, with a draft of the copy a viewer sees,
and with the two places the ratified sentence is not yet fully delivered named rather than
smoothed over.

## Deliverables

- [x] `evidence/E03.06-published-surface-terms-and-viewer-privacy.md` — the four-surface
      table, the published-page terms, the viewer privacy language, the analytics
      disclosure, the cookie position, six decision points, seven open legal points and a
      verification table.
- [x] This specification.

## Scope

`/p/[slug]`, `/embed/[slug]`, `/share/[token]` and `/s/[token]`. What a viewer is told.
What is measured and stored on their device. The analytics disclosure. The cookie and
ePrivacy position. The search-listing position.

## Non-goals

- Editing any route, header, CSP or component. Nothing was shipped.
- Deciding R-033. No founder decision was available and inventing one would settle it by
  accident.
- The couple's own terms and privacy notice. That is E03.05.
- The data-processing instrument. That is E03.04.
- Unpublishing on a dispute. That is E03.10.

## Implementation approach

Write the criteria first. Read every route, header and predicate rather than trusting the
evidence documents that describe them. Draft the copy to the shipped behaviour. Where the
decided position is only partly delivered, say which part and cite the file. Where an
ePrivacy question has two defensible readings, present both, take the one safer for the
viewer, and say that is what was done.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A DRAFT status block heads the document with the E09.10 Answer 2 sentence verbatim and
   the date.
2. The four surfaces are described individually, with token, indexing, header, caching,
   framing, cookie, storage and measurement state per surface, each traceable to a file.
   No published surface is given a security adjective, and the terms say in plain words
   that anyone with the address can open the page.
3. The analytics disclosure states R-032 Option A as it is actually built, naming GA4, the
   browser Sentry gate, the shared predicate and the enforced per-route CSP, **and names
   the part that is not closed**: server-side Sentry has no route exclusion and
   `redactSensitiveUrl` does not redact `/p/{slug}`.
4. The cookie section states that no cookie is set by Signal Studio on any of the four
   surfaces, with the proxy evidence, and identifies the one piece of device storage that
   does exist with its purpose, scope, retention and the open ePrivacy question, presented
   as a decision point rather than answered.
5. Search visibility describes what is true today: `/p` in the robots disallow, wedding
   workspaces `noindex` by default, `/embed`, `/share` and `/s` always `noindex`, the
   robots-versus-noindex interaction stated, and the couple opt-in named as having no
   storage, so there is no way to turn listing on.
6. The viewer-measurement description matches `qualified-view.ts`, `qualified-view-store.ts`
   and `viewer-count.ts` exactly: dwell bounds, the per-publication hash, the four stored
   columns, the thirty-minute retention, the floor of 5, and byte-identical output below it.
7. R-033 is stated as true today and undecided, and no answer is invented.
8. The removal route for a person named on a published page is described only as it exists,
   and the absence of any controller name, contact or removal link on the page is stated
   with the files that prove it.
9. Nothing restates the E03.04 data-processing position, and nothing states or implies GDPR
   or ePrivacy compliance, certification, audit or DPC approval.

## Risks

- **R-032 is not as closed as the register reads, in two places.** Client-side third-party
  measurement is gone from the four couple-facing routes. Server-side error reporting still
  fires on them and carries the `/p` slug. And **D-033's second sentence, dropping GA4 from
  marketing and commercial surfaces, is not built at all**: `gtag.js` still loads in
  production on every non-excluded route with no consent gate, and the component says so in
  its own comment.
- **R-033 stays open**, so a couple's published workspace remains framable on any site by
  anyone holding a slug, with no signal to the couple.
- The ePrivacy status of the `/s` view receipt is a genuine open question and the draft
  takes the safer reading rather than the settled one.
- No data-protection footer exists on any published surface, which the role map calls the
  cheapest and highest-value item in it.

## Decisions required

Seven, listed as DP-A through DP-G in §8 of the evidence document. The four that need an
answer before the Founding 25:

1. **DP-B.** Are sponsored wedding workspaces embeddable at all? E06.05 recommends no.
2. **DP-C.** Exclude server-side Sentry from the couple-facing routes, or at minimum add
   `/p` to `redactSensitiveUrl`.
3. **DP-D.** Whether the data-protection footer ships before the first couple redeems.
4. **DP-G.** Whether the second half of D-033 gets built before a venue reads a marketing
   page, or whether the ratified sentence is amended to what is actually shipping.
