## Objective

A venue-facing page that previews what a venue receives from the Venue Edition,
what it will never receive, why small figures are withheld, and the terms the
agreement renews on.

## Business reason

The trust boundary is the product. A venue is being asked to hand its couples a
planning workspace it cannot read, and the reason that is safe is the whole
reason a couple will use it. Said out loud, in advance, it is the strongest
thing in the offer. Left to the objection, it reads as a limitation discovered
late. D-032 R4 made stating the launch scope in writing a requirement rather
than a courtesy, precisely so a venue does not find out on 1 September.

## Intended outcome

A venue can read what arrives at launch, what arrives later in the year, the
figures that are held back and why, and what happens to its couples if the
agreement ends. It can also read, plainly, that there is no login, because
under D-032 point 1 there is not one and will not be one before 1 September.

## Scope

- The launch-scope sentence, verbatim and prominent.
- The E09.11 §5.1 privacy block, both halves, at the same weight.
- Suppression explained as the ratified rule, with the asymmetry D-032 R12
  ratified: access counts exact at any size, behavioural counts withheld below
  three, rates below five.
- The rhythm under D-032 point 1 Option C: codes arrive in a batch, Signal
  Studio keeps the register, the record comes back with the venue's name on it.
- Renewal, from the four permitted forms in E09.11 §4.1, plus change of control
  and the D-020 point 2 survival sentence stated unprompted.
- Keepsake, venue-facing, with the not-yet-built caveat.

## Non-goals

- **No depiction of a portal the venue reaches.** No login, no dashboard, no
  roster of people, no role picker, and no number that reads as an entitlement
  ceiling. R-043 verified there is no venue-authenticated route, and D-032
  point 1 confirms none is built before 1 September.
- **No promise of a read-only venue link.** A′ is held pending the first three
  venue conversations. Naming it would be a roadmap promise, which E09.11 §5.1a
  bans outright.
- **No description of the current suppression code.** R-027 and R-028 record
  that the shipped projector does not yet implement the two-sided floor. The
  page states the ratified rule; WP-07 owns making the code match it.
- **No adoption figure, sample chart or example report.** The figures do not
  exist at launch and a sample would be a fabricated screen.
- **No mention of a solicitor, an accountant or GDPR compliance.** D-016.

**The dependency needed re-reading and this is the finding.** E07.18 and E07.16
are both recorded `done`, approved by the founder on 2026-08-03 in the Wave 2
bulk approval. But E07.16's own spec document,
`evidence/E07.16-venue-identity-lab.md`, scores the audit **3 of 13 criteria
satisfied** and states at the top: "options for founder decision. Nothing here
is built. Nothing here is approved." Its recorded evidence is a list of things
that do not work. AC-1 reads "a venue user can authenticate as themselves" and
R-043 verifies there is no such route.

Both readings are correct, because "done" here means the **audit** is done and
the invitation-administration build is done, not that a venue portal exists.
D-032 point 1 then answered the open question by picking Option C. So E12.06 is
buildable, and it is buildable as a preview of what a venue **receives**. A
build agent that read only the task title and the `done` status would have
built a preview of a portal, and it would have been a false statement to a
venue about to pay €1,000.

## Implementation approach

Establish the launch position from the decision record rather than from the
task title, then write only what is true under it. Every sentence about what a
venue sees is traceable to a ratified decision or to E09.11's reusable blocks.
Every sentence about what a venue does not get is stated as a decision, not as
an apology, and the not-built section names three things by name.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The launch-scope sentence from E09.11 §5.1a appears verbatim, unparaphrased,
   in its own block rather than buried in a paragraph.
2. Both halves of the E09.11 §5.1 privacy block appear verbatim, rendered at the
   same visual weight, with neither half omitted or shortened.
3. The suppression rule is stated as a floor of three on counts and five on
   rates, with the D-032 R12 asymmetry stated: invitations issued and opened are
   exact at any size. The page never renders a "fewer than N" form, which would
   itself be a disclosure.
4. The page describes no login, no venue account, no roster of people, no role
   control and no entitlement ceiling, and states positively that there is no
   venue login.
5. Renewal uses only the four permitted forms from E09.11 §4.1. Both prices are
   VAT-inclusive wherever a price appears. No permanence wording anywhere.
6. The D-020 point 2 survival sentence appears verbatim and unprompted, in the
   renewal section rather than in an objection.
7. Keepsake is stated in its ratified form with the qualifier, and the fact that
   the Keepsake screen is not built is stated on the page.
8. Every capability not yet built is stated as not yet built, naming the login,
   the adoption figures and the Keepsake screen.
9. Zero hits across every rule in `prohibited-claims.v1.json`, zero banned
   programme terms, zero banned CTAs, zero em dashes and zero exclamation marks
   in rendered text.
10. Nothing states or implies legal, solicitor or accountant approval, and no
    fourth venue-visible thing is named beyond invitation administration,
    activation evidence and aggregate adoption evidence.
11. The route is registered in `experience/registry.json` and added to the
    commercial sweep list in the same change that created it.
