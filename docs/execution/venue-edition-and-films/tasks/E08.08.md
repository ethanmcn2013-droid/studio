# E08.08 — Complete audit logging, operational logging, error monitoring and alert escalation.

**Task ID:** E08.08
**Epic:** E08 — Engineering, security and quality

## Objective

Separate the audit log from the operational log, enforce that separation in
code, make the audit ledger's tamper-resistance real rather than documented,
and state plainly what error monitoring and alerting exist today.

## Business reason

Twenty-five venues will hand Signal Studio the couples who trust them. Three
things follow.

A **venue** will one day ask who issued a code, when a founding number was
assigned, or why a licence lapsed. The answer has to be a record, not a
recollection, and it has to be one nobody could have quietly edited.

A **couple's** planning workspace holds guests' names, suppliers' names, and —
per the shipped template — dietary notes, which are Article 9 special-category
data about people who agreed to nothing. That content must never end up in a
debugging log stream that nobody has a retention policy for.

And **one founder** operates this alone. If something breaks at 02:00 during
the Founding 25 onboarding, whether anyone finds out is not a detail.

## Intended outcome

- The two logs are defined, written down where they are enforced, and cannot
  be confused: an audit line is evidence; an operational line is a debugging
  aid that carries no content.
- The claim that the entitlements audit ledger is physically append-only is
  either true on a given database or reported as false by a command.
- Two real leaks of credentials and personal data into operational logs are
  closed, and a new one cannot be added without a reviewer seeing it.
- The state of error monitoring and alerting is recorded as fact, including
  the part that does not exist.

## Scope

The definition of the two logs; enforcement of the operational-log rule in the
app; the audit ledger's physical append-only enforcement in studio; an honest
statement of monitoring and alerting.

## Non-goals

- **Provisioning Sentry, PostHog or any alerting service.** Each is integrated
  in code and none is provisioned. Provisioning is a founder decision with a
  data-processor and cost dimension; a build agent must not take it.
- **Extending the app's audit log to every privileged action.** Today
  `workspace_events` records exactly `inviteSent` and `inviteAccepted`.
  Removing a member, changing a role, creating or revoking a share link,
  publishing a workspace and deleting an account are all unaudited. Those
  writers sit in files owned by other work packages this wave, and adding
  audit writes to them concurrently would collide. The gap is recorded rather
  than half-closed.
- Client-side `console.*`. Different exposure, different owner.
- Anything that would state or imply legal or GDPR approval (D-016).

## Inputs

  "integrated in app code, none provisioned".

Superseded: the `audit.ts` header sentence naming `migrate-access.mjs` as the
enforcement mechanism.

## Implementation approach

1. Write the distinction down in the file that enforces it, not in a doc
   nobody opens.
2. Make the sanctioned sink take scalars only, so spreading a database row
   into a log call does not type-check.
3. Gate with two mechanical rules rather than one judgement: a ratchet that
   forces review of any new logging site, and a denylist of expressions that
   have actually leaked or were one edit from leaking. A site that genuinely
   needs one carries a greppable `// op-log-safe:` waiver.
4. For the ledger, install the triggers idempotently by script rather than by
   migration, matching the convention already set in this repository, and make
   absence a failing check rather than a silent condition.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The audit log and the operational log are defined in one place in code,
   naming both audit tables, and a test fails if that definition is removed.
2. No server-scope `console.*` call may log an email address, a rendered email
   body, a task or workspace title, guest or dietary data, or a URL that can
   be a bearer token — proven by a test that is shown to fire on each of those
   seven shapes and shown not to fire on ordinary error logging.
3. A new server-scope logging site cannot be added without a change to a
   recorded list, proven by a ratchet test whose comparison direction is
   itself asserted.
4. The two identified leaks are closed at source: the CSP collector no longer
   writes `/share/<token>` to the log, and `sendEmail` no longer writes the
   recipient or a body preview outside `NODE_ENV === "development"`.
5. On a database built from the checked-in entitlements baseline, an UPDATE
   and a DELETE against `entitlement_events` are refused after
   `apply-audit-ledger-triggers.mjs` runs, and are demonstrably NOT refused
   before it runs.
6. A command reports the absence of append-only enforcement and exits
   non-zero; the integrity checker treats absence as a failing run.
7. No source comment claims an enforcement the schema does not deliver, held
   by a test that fails if the retired wording returns.
8. The state of error monitoring and alerting is recorded as fact, including
   naming what is not provisioned, and no surface implies monitoring exists
   that does not.

## Risks

- **R-017** — the operational-log rule is one control over Article 9 data; it
  does not address the template that asks the couple to collect it.
- **New, recorded here:** the app's audit log covers two event kinds. A venue
  or a couple asking "who removed this person" cannot be answered from the
  record today.
- **New, recorded here:** no error monitoring is provisioned, so an
  unhandled error in production is currently observed by nobody.

## Decisions required

1. **Provision error monitoring before 1 September, or accept that production
   errors go unobserved during the Founding 25 onboarding?** Sentry is
   integrated and scrubbed; it is not provisioned. The free tier costs
   nothing, which fits D-015 Q5, but it adds a data processor and that is the
   founder's call.
2. **Should the app's audit log cover member removal, role change, share-link
   creation and revocation, publishing and account deletion before Cohort 1?**
   Recommended yes, as a small follow-on once this wave's concurrent edits to
   those files have landed.
