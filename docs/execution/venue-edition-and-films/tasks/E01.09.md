# E01.09 — Create a project risk and issue register covering commercial, product, privacy, delivery, founder-capacity and launch risks.

**Task ID:** E01.09 · **Epic:** E01 · **Status:** in_progress
**Executor:** claude_code · **Approver:** Ethan McNamara · **Spec written:** 2026-08-03

---

## Objective

A risk and issue register that demonstrably covers all six named categories, with
a controlled vocabulary so a missing category is findable.

## Business reason

A register is only worth the categories it actually covers. This one was strong
on five of six and had **nothing** in launch, and nothing in the system could
have told anyone: `Type:` was free text and no tool reads the file.

## Intended outcome

Six categories, six populated rows, and a stated method for noticing when one
empties.

## Deliverables

- [x] A category index mapping the six named categories to entries
- [x] Three launch risks: R-023, R-024, R-025
- [x] Three governance issues found by the audit: I-007, I-008, I-009
- [x] The two structural limitations recorded rather than papered over

## Scope

`RAID.md`: risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies, and its coverage of the
six named categories.

## Non-goals

- Not re-scoring existing entries. Severity is judged, and re-judging 22 entries would be noise.
- Not building validation over `RAID.md`. That limitation is recorded, not fixed, because it is a bigger change than this task.
- Not resolving any risk. A register records; it does not mitigate.

## Dependencies

None recorded.

## Inputs

- `RAID.md` — 22 risks, 7 assumptions, 6 issues, 7 dependencies before this task
- The six categories named in the task title
- `PROJECT_STATE.json` `releaseGates` — what the gates cover, and therefore what they do not
- `PROJECT.md` §4, §12, §22 — the completion condition the launch gap threatens

## Implementation approach

Test the register against the exact words of the title: six named categories,
each its own criterion. Where a category is empty, find out whether the exposure
is genuinely absent or merely unrecorded — for launch it was unrecorded, and the
three entries that resulted are real. Add an index rather than relabel entries,
because the compound `Type:` strings carry more information than a single label.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The register covers all six named categories: commercial, product, privacy, delivery, founder-capacity, launch. No category is empty.
2. Both halves of the title are satisfied: risks and issues are separately tracked, with stable IDs and no deletions.
3. Every entry carries type, probability, impact, severity, owner, trigger, mitigation, what it affects, status and a last-reviewed date.
4. A controlled vocabulary exists mapping the six categories to entries, so an empty category is visible rather than inferable only by reading the whole file.
5. Risks accepted by founder decision are recorded as accepted with the founder's position, not quietly closed.
6. Where an exposure is carried by the release gates rather than by the register, that is stated, because a gate records required state and not what could go wrong.
7. The register's own limitations are recorded: that nothing validates the file, and that its escalation rule is not cross-checked against task blockers.

## Validation plan

- Map every one of the six categories to at least one entry and print the mapping
- Confirm every new entry carries all ten required fields
- Confirm no existing entry was deleted: count before and after
- Cross-check the launch claim by mapping each of the six gates to its supporting epics and identifying which epics no gate covers

## Evidence required

- `RAID.md` with the category index and R-023 to R-025, I-007 to I-009
- The gate-to-epic coverage mapping that produced the launch finding
- `evidence/change-requests/CR-002.md`, the change request I-008 requires

## Risks

The register now names an exposure the gates do not cover (I-008) and one that is
simply true today (I-007, the control root is untracked in git). Recording them
raises no new risk; not recording them was the risk.
