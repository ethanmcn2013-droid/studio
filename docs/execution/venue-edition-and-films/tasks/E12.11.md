## Objective

Produce the certificate that records a founding place, built so it cannot exist
before the payment clears and cannot imply that the place is permanent.

## Business reason

The number is the whole of what the Founding 25 gives beyond the rate. D-009
point 6 makes it real by tying it to the one event that cannot be walked back:
cleared payment. An artefact that can be produced at signature undoes that,
because a card handed over at signature either carries a number the venue has
not earned or carries a blank that invites somebody to write one in. Two venues
both believing they hold 07/25 is the one failure in this programme that cannot
be corrected afterwards.

## Intended outcome

There is exactly one way to produce a certificate and it requires a cleared
payment, a founding plan and a number the register already assigned. Every
other input produces a printed refusal with a reason, not a blank frame.

## Scope

**The three rules the artefact is built around.**

1. **No cleared payment, no certificate.** `buildFoundingCertificate` returns a
   refusal for every input that is not a paid founding venue holding a valid
   number. There is no code path that renders a blank number and none that
   renders a number shape at all when the input is refused.
2. **No permanence.** A certificate hangs on a wall and is the object in this
   programme most likely to be read as outliving the agreement. The rate
   appears with its conditions in the same block, and nine permanence words are
   refused by test rather than avoided by the author.
3. **A number is never reused.** The certificate records an event that
   happened. It carries no validity window and it is not reissued at renewal.

**The rate is on the artefact, with its conditions.** The task title requires
it. It appears in the terms block as the smallest type on the page, never as a
bare number, alongside the standard rate, what holds the lock, and what happens
on a change of control. Whether the rate belongs on a wall-hung object at all
is a founder call and is in the packet.

## Non-goals

- **The physical object.** Stock, colour, frame, foil, whether the number is
  set in mono or a numeral face, and whether this is A4 or a smaller desk piece
  are founder-taste calls. The exploration at `/hq/partner-card` holds four
  directions with a recorded shortlist and is not superseded by this task.
- **Reprinting anything.** No print run is started and no PDF is regenerated.
- **Assigning numbers.** That is `src/lib/entitlements-db/founding-numbers.ts`.
  This route is a renderer, and keeping it one is what stops it becoming a
  second place a founding number can come into existence.

## Implementation approach

A function that refuses, and a component that renders the refusal. The refusal
is given a visual treatment on purpose: an empty frame with a blank number
field is the failure mode, so there is no empty frame to reach.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A certificate cannot be produced without a cleared payment date, and the
   refusal says plainly that a number is assigned on cleared payment and never
   on signature.
2. A certificate cannot be produced for a venue not on the founding plan.
3. A certificate cannot be produced without a valid number in 01 to 25, and
   inputs outside that range are refused in both directions.
4. No refused state renders a blank number, a number shape, or any field a
   number could be written into.
5. The number renders zero-padded as `NN/25`.
6. The record block carries the date the payment cleared and no signature date.
7. The rate never appears without "including VAT at the prevailing rate", and
   the lock condition and the change-of-control rule appear in the same block.
8. No permanence word appears anywhere on the artefact.
9. The certificate states that it is not the agreement and does not grant
   access on its own.
10. No prohibited claim, retired programme term, em dash or exclamation mark
    appears, on the certificate or in any refusal reason.
11. Nothing implies review, accreditation, approval, verification or an award.
12. The route is registered in both registries and adds no new
    `experience:validate` failure.
13. The test is registered in `package.json` so CI runs it.
