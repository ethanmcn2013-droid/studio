## Objective

Derive each venue's renewal state, grace window and lapse from its recorded
term, encode exactly what a lapse does and does not do, and write down the
manual procedure for the parts that are not built.

## Business reason

The worst outcome this product can produce is a sponsored couple losing Signal
Studio before their wedding, in public, at the venue that gifted it. Lapse
behaviour is where that either happens or is made impossible. Everything else in
this task is administration.

## Intended outcome

A renewal date exists per venue and is derived, never calculated by hand. Grace
and lapse are states with boundaries rather than guesses. A lapse provably stops
new issuance only, and a test asserts a couple's access is unchanged across one.
Everything not built by 1 September is written down as not built.

- [x] `venueRenewalWorklist()` — one action per venue, per state

## Scope

The derived state machine, the lapse invariant, the operator worklist, and the
written procedure for the manual parts.

## Non-goals

- Building an invoice system or a payment integration for venues. There is no
  Stripe venue price id and no venue webhook. Twenty-five manually invoiced
  venues in 29 days does not justify one, and pretending otherwise is worse than
  not having one.
- Building an email sender. Studio has none.
- Touching entitlements on lapse. Deliberately impossible: see criterion 4.

## Implementation approach

The notice lead time and grace window are parameters, not constants, so the
machine is correct for whatever Ethan ratifies and no rework follows the
decision. They ship as `ratified: false` and a refusal function stops an
unratified number reaching an agreement, an order form, an email or the portal.

The lapse writer is defined by what it refuses to touch. It writes one audit
line. It does not write to `entitlements`, does not shorten any expiry and does
not withdraw a founding number.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A renewal date exists per venue and is derived from the recorded term without
   a manual calculation.
2. Grace and lapse are named states with defined boundaries, visible to the
   operator, never guessed from absence of data.
3. A lapse is derived from the term and the grace window and cannot be declared
   by hand.
4. **D-020 point 2 is provably preserved:** a lapse stops new issuance only, and
   every already-redeemed couple workspace keeps its full term plus Keepsake,
   proven by a test that lapses a venue and asserts couple access is byte-for-byte
   unchanged.
5. Branding removal on lapse is a checkable value, not a promise.
6. A lapsed founding venue keeps its number historically and its place shows as
   closed, not open.
7. No copy anywhere implies a couple can lose access because a venue did not pay.
8. The notice lead time and grace window are marked unratified and cannot reach
   a venue-facing surface while they are.
9. Whatever is not built by 1 September is stated as not built, in the runbook,
   rather than described as automatic.

## Risks

- Renewal notices depend on a person sending them. That is the honest process
  for twenty-five venues, and the runbook says so, but it is a person.
- No renewal falls due before 1 September, so this is exercised in production
  roughly a year after launch. The tests are the only proof until then.

## Decisions required

1. **Ratify the renewal notice lead time and the grace window.** Proposed: 30
   days notice, 30 days grace. Until ratified, no agreement or renewal email may
   state either number. This is the only thing blocking the state machine from
   being fully usable.
2. Confirm manual invoicing is the launch process (shared with E08.01).
