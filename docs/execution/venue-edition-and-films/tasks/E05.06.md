## Objective

Make the six task properties a couple actually manages — who owns it, when it
is due, what state it is in, how it is labelled, how urgent it is, and what to
do next — true of a real couple's workspace rather than of a demo one.

## Business reason

Tasks is the surface a sponsored couple lands on. `redeemCompCodeAction`
redirects to `/app/tasks?welcome=venue`, so the first thing anyone sees after a
venue gifts them the product is this board. If assigning a task offers five
people who do not exist, or a due date says nothing about how close the wedding
is, the venue's gift reads as someone else's software.

## Intended outcome

1. Assigning a task offers the workspace's real members and nobody else.
2. A due date is legible against the wedding date, in exact days, wherever the
   workspace has one.
3. It is written down, with evidence, that a redeemed couple's workspace has no
   wedding date at all in production, and what that costs.

## Implementation approach

Audit first. Every criterion below was written before the code was read, then
tested against the shipped tree with a file and a line. Only the criteria that
failed were built. Nothing that already worked was rewritten.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The people offered when assigning a task are the workspace's real members,
   resolved server-side, and no seed persona or design-lab fixture name can
   appear in a live workspace.
2. A workspace with one member offers one person, and a workspace with no other
   members says so rather than offering anybody.
3. The workspace's wedding date is a first-class stored field, and it is stated
   plainly in evidence whether a redeemed couple's workspace has one.
4. Where the workspace has a wedding date, a task's due date is shown against it
   in exact calendar days, with no rounding to weeks or months.
5. Where the workspace has no wedding date, no relation is shown and no date is
   invented.
6. Status, priority and tags are settable from the task detail and are recorded
   as already working, with the file and line, rather than rebuilt.
7. Every string added is declarative plain English with no em dash and no
   exclamation mark.
8. `pnpm test` passes, and the new behaviour is covered by tests that fail
   against the code as it stood.

## Risks

- **The read side is inert until a wedding date exists.** Recorded below and in
  the evidence document. This is the single most important finding of the task.
- R-015 / D-022: any future wedding-date write path must also recompute the
  access term. Adding an editable date without that would silently break a
  ratified commercial promise.

## Decisions required

1. **Where does the couple's wedding date get set?** It is required by the
   contextual-onboarding path, which is flagged off in production, and there is
   no field anywhere in the app to set or change it afterwards. D-022 point 1
   says capture it at redemption. That capture does not exist in the app.
2. **Who owns the write path?** Any wedding-date write must recompute
   `max(redemption + 548 days, wedding date + 90 days)` per D-022 point 3.
   `studio/src/lib/venue-lifecycle.ts` implements the rule and has no app-side
   caller. A setter built without that coupling is worse than none.
