## Objective

Fix the project's primary objective as signed and paid founding venues, and make
the reporting system structurally incapable of presenting invitations sent as
progress toward it.

## Business reason

The commonest way a founder-led sales programme lies to itself is by counting
activity. Twenty-five invitations feels like the number 25. It is not the number.
This task is the guard against that, and it has to live in the machinery rather
than in an intention.

## Intended outcome

The objective is stated once, authoritatively; every generated report separates
activity counts from outcome counts; and no report can summarise them together.

## Deliverables

- [x] The objective stated in `PROJECT.md` §2 and §4, and restated in `BRIEF.md`
- [x] Strict stage definitions in `REPORTING.md` §5
- [x] Separate, non-summable tracker rows in `PROJECT_STATE.json` `commercial`
- [x] An assessment recording which of the above genuinely holds and which does not

## Scope

The definition of the objective, its stage vocabulary, and the reporting
structure that keeps activity and outcome apart.

## Non-goals

- Not the Founding 25 sales process. That is E11.
- Not the onboarding definition itself. That is E15.10 and E15.11.
- Not the completion condition for the whole project. That is E01.03.

## Implementation approach

Assess rather than build. The objective and the stage separation were written at
import. Test whether they are enforced or merely asserted: read the tracker
fields, read the generated commercial table, and check whether any test prevents
activity and outcome being blended. Record what genuinely holds. Where the
separation is only prose, say so rather than counting the prose as the control.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The primary objective is stated in a durable, controlling document as 25 signed and paid founding venues, not 25 invitations or expressions of interest.
2. `PROJECT_STATE.json` carries `invitationsIssued`, `signedAgreements`, `paidAgreements`, `configuredVenueAccounts` and `onboardedVenues` as separate counters that no code path sums.
3. `REPORTING.md` defines each stage strictly, including that a researched venue is not a founding venue, an invited venue is not a signed venue, and a signed venue is not paid until payment is confirmed.
4. The generated commercial report presents paid agreements and onboarded venues as the outcome rows, distinct from activity rows.
5. The assessment states plainly which of the above is enforced by code or test and which rests on prose alone.

## Validation plan

- `project-control.mjs status commercial` — inspect the real tracker shape
- Read the commercial section of generated `STATUS.md`
- `node --test tools/project-control.test.mjs` — record which tests, if any, defend the separation
- Grep the tool for any summation across activity and outcome counters

## Evidence required

- The real `status commercial` output
- The relevant `PROJECT.md` and `REPORTING.md` sections
- The test-run result, with an honest note on what the tests do and do not defend

## Risks

The failure mode is accepting prose as a control. R-006 (founder capacity) makes
a self-congratulatory activity count attractive; this task is the counterweight.
