## Objective

Make the Founding 25 rate immutable in code, and make each term's price survive
a future price change without rewriting history.

## Business reason

D-009 promises twenty-five venues EUR 500 a year less for as long as the
agreement renews continuously. That promise is only as good as the record behind
it. If the standard price moves to EUR 1,800 and a founding venue's row is a
mutable column, the promise is one `UPDATE` away from being untrue, and the
venue has a written agreement saying otherwise.

## Intended outcome

A venue that joined at EUR 1,000 still reads EUR 1,000 after the standard price
moves, every past term keeps the amount actually charged, the founding number is
assigned on cleared payment and cannot be duplicated, and no code path can move
a venue off the locked rate while the agreement renews continuously.

## Deliverables

- [x] `sponsor_price_agreements`: one append-only row per term, effective period
- [x] Append-only enforced by SQLite `RAISE(ABORT)` triggers, probed on migrate
- [x] `planChangeRefusal()` — the guard that makes the lock immutable forward
- [x] Founding-number assignment called by the writer that records payment
- [x] `scripts/venue-billing-ddl.test.mjs` — schema-drift check
- [ ] Baseline `drizzle-entitlements/0000_init.sql` drift (reported, not fixed)

## Scope

## Implementation approach

Two separate guarantees, because one was mistaken for both. Append-only rows
protect the **past**: a recorded term cannot be edited or deleted, enforced by
the database rather than by the writer. `planChangeRefusal()` protects the
**next** term: a held founding lock cannot be renewed onto the standard plan,
because that would end the lock at the correct price for the plan named, with
every historical row intact and no lapse anywhere on record.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A founding venue carries an immutable record of the rate it locked: amount,
   date, and the basis, and no code path can lower or raise it while the
   agreement renews continuously. Proven by a test that attempts the downgrade.
2. A founding number 01/25 to 25/25 is assigned only when payment clears, is
   unique at the database level, and is never reused after a lapse.
3. The number-assignment path is actually called by whatever records payment.
   Assignment is not left as an uncalled library function.
4. A historical price survives a renewal: the amount charged in each term is
   retrievable, not overwritten by the current term. Proven with a term written
   at a hypothetical future EUR 1,800.
5. A recorded term cannot be edited or deleted, and that is a property of the
   database rather than a convention in the writer.
6. A payment reversal withdraws the number and returns the place to the pool
   with date and reason, in the audit ledger.
7. "Unlimited" is representable and the founding record carries no seat count
   (D-020, R-016).
8. No surface states the lock as "for life", "forever", "in perpetuity" or
   "lifetime" (D-009 point 3, D-021).
9. A test proves two venues cannot hold the same founding number, at the index
   level and not only in arithmetic.
10. The drizzle table the application queries and the DDL the migration executes
    declare the same columns, proven by a check that fails on drift.
