## Objective

Produce the approved wording a venue uses to describe the planning workspace to
a couple who has not booked yet, per channel, with a per-channel list of what
must not appear there.

## Business reason

R-020. Twenty-five venues will describe this product to couples with no
obligation attached, and a venue telling a couple "a free wedding planner,
yours forever" is a commercial practice under the Consumer Protection Act 2007.
It is enforceable by the CCPC and it folds into the venue's own contract with
the couple, so a Signal Studio discontinuation becomes the venue's consumer
problem and then the venue's claim against Signal Studio.

The automated sweep polices Signal Studio's strings. It cannot reach a venue's
brochure. The only mitigation that works is making the correct sentence the
easiest one to reach for, in the venue's own channels, before the first couple
is on a show-around.

## Intended outcome

A venue has, in writing, the sentence it may use in each of the five places it
would use one, in three lengths, with the reason that channel is different from
the others and the specific things that must not go in it.

## Scope

**Pre-booking is a different promise from post-booking, and that is the whole
reason this task is not a duplicate of the copy pack.**
`evidence/E09.11-venue-copy-pack.md` answers a couple who has already booked.
Pre-booking is conditional: every sentence is about what happens if they book,
and it has to be conditional without becoming an inducement the venue cannot
honour in two years.

**Five channels, because they fail differently.** The brochure is read without
the coordinator in the room. The website is edited by somebody who was not in
the conversation. The written proposal or quote folds into the contract and is
the highest-risk surface in the kit. The show-around is spoken and unprepared.
The wedding fair has ninety seconds and noise.

**One ban list, not two.** The kit points at `E09.11-venue-copy-pack.md`
section 4, which is generated from `evidence/copy/prohibited-claims.v1.json`.
Reprinting it here would create a second list, and a second list is how a term
survives on one surface after being retired on another. That is R-042 in one
sentence.

**The launch-scope sentence is in the team notes.** D-032 R4 makes it required
on any surface that tells a venue what it sees, and a coordinator reading this
kit forms a belief about what the venue sees.

## Implementation approach

Channels as data, sentences as strings, and the never-lists separated from the
swept prose by structure rather than by a heuristic. A never-list names banned
language, so it contains it; the exclusion is explicit and narrow, and those
lines are still swept for the retired programme term and for punctuation. A
heuristic that decides "this looks like a citation" once suppressed two real
defects on the live venues page, and it is not reinvented here.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Five channels exist, covering brochure, website, written proposal, the
   show-around conversation and a wedding fair, each with three lengths.
2. Every channel states its moment of use and the one rule that makes it
   different, and carries a never-list of at least three items.
3. No couple-facing block carries a figure, a currency, a value word, "free",
   "complimentary" or "sponsored".
4. Every channel states what it costs the couple, using the permitted phrasing.
5. The privacy sentence appears in the short and full block of every channel,
   unchanged from the copy pack.
6. No couple-facing block claims a Keepsake, a one-click export, a download or
   a read-only mode, because none of them is built.
7. The one after-the-wedding claim is true today and makes no claim about what
   happens at the end of the term.
8. Nothing states or implies that a venue can see, receive or check a couple's
   plan.
9. Nothing implies a venue's logo, colour or own words reach a couple.
10. The team notes carry the launch-scope sentence verbatim.
11. The kit points at the one prohibited-claims list rather than reprinting it.
12. No prohibited claim, retired term, em dash or exclamation mark appears in
    any block; the never-lists are still held to the retired term and to
    punctuation.
13. The route is registered in both registries and adds no new
    `experience:validate` failure.
14. The test is registered in `package.json` so CI runs it.
