# E01.12 — Establish a weekly operating review covering blockers, decisions, evidence, quality, pipeline and the next seven days.

**Task ID:** E01.12 · **Epic:** E01 · **Status:** in_progress
**Executor:** claude_code · **Approver:** Ethan McNamara · **Spec written:** 2026-08-03

---

## Objective

A weekly operating review that exists: six named sections generated from
canonical state, a cadence, a template and a procedure.

## Business reason

R-006 says founder capacity is the single constraint, and its mitigation is
batching decisions into this review. The mitigation for the project's most severe
capacity risk was a command that printed the status report and called it a
weekly review.

## Intended outcome

`status weekly` produces a real operating review, distinct from the status
report, and the ritual around it is written down.

## Deliverables

- [x] `renderWeekly` with the six sections the title names
- [x] `templates/WEEKLY_REVIEW.md`
- [x] `WORKFLOWS.md` §12, the procedure
- [x] An `answered` command so a closed question leaves the founder queue
- [x] Tests: all six sections, the seven-day window, determinism, and that it is not the status report

## Scope

The review's content, its cadence, its template and its procedure.

## Non-goals

- Not a new meeting. D-008 clause 4 folded it into the existing Friday brief deliberately.
- Not a second status report. If it duplicates `status overall` it has failed.
- Not automating the judgement. The generated part carries numbers; a person adds what the numbers do not say.

## Dependencies

None recorded.

## Inputs

- `DECISIONS.md` D-008 clause 4 — Friday morning, folded into the Friday brief
- `RAID.md` R-006 — the risk this review mitigates
- `REPORTING.md` — what every number means
- The six sections named in the task title

## Implementation approach

Build the six sections against the words of the title, and make each say
something `status overall` does not. A status report says where the project is.
An operating review says what needs a decision this week. The quality section
therefore reports In Progress without criteria and Founder Review without
evidence, rather than a completion percentage.

Two things were needed to make the numbers honest. **Decisions** was
overstating the founder's queue by 4, because four questions D-008 answered on
2026-08-02 still showed as open — hence the `answered` command. **The next seven
days** could not be computed at all, because no task carried a target date —
hence E01.05's field, which is why these two tasks were built together.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The review carries all six named sections: blockers, decisions, evidence, quality, pipeline, the next seven days.
2. It is demonstrably not the status report under another name, asserted by a test.
3. Its horizon is genuinely seven days and it names freezes and target dates landing inside that window.
4. Its decisions section reports only questions that are actually open. A question closed by a decision does not appear.
5. Its pipeline section never sums activity with outcome; paid and onboarded are marked as the outcome rows.
6. Its evidence section reports how much active work carries acceptance criteria, because work in flight with no criteria has no agreed definition of done.
7. Its quality section states plainly that `DECISIONS.md` and `RAID.md` are validated by nothing, so those are read with human eyes.
8. The cadence is recorded, and the review changes no state: decisions taken in it exist only once recorded by a command or a change request.
9. A template and a written procedure exist, so the review survives without the person who designed it.
10. The output is deterministic for the same state and clock.

## Validation plan

- `node --test tools/project-control.test.mjs` — the six-section test, the not-the-status-report test, the seven-day window test, the determinism test, and the answered-questions test
- `status weekly` — read the real output end to end
- Confirm the founder queue count drops by exactly the four questions D-008 answered
- Confirm `templates/WEEKLY_REVIEW.md` and `WORKFLOWS.md` §12 exist

## Evidence required

- The real `status weekly` output
- The test run
- The template and the workflow section
- The before-and-after founder queue count

## Risks

A review that is generated is easy to run and easy to not read. The template
forces three human additions — what the numbers do not say, what was decided,
what changes next week — because those are the parts a command cannot produce.

**Section 6 is currently empty of tasks.** That is honest, not broken: no task
has a target date inside the next seven days, and the first freeze is twelve days
out. An empty section 6 is itself a finding.
