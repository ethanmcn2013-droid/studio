## Objective

Establish what the public Venue Edition landing page has to be if the sponsored
couple is its organising idea, audit the page that is already live against that,
and hand the founder the gap with the replacement words written.

## Business reason

It is the page a Greater Limerick venue finds before it has spoken to anyone. A
venue owner is buying an outcome for the couples it hosts, and a page organised
around the venue's feature list sells the wrong thing to the wrong instinct.

## Intended outcome

E12.01 has written, checkable acceptance criteria for the first time, the live page
is measured against them, and every gap has a named owner and approved replacement
text rather than an intention.

## Deliverables

- [x] Ten acceptance criteria, written against the sponsored-couple organising idea
- [x] A claim-by-claim audit of `src/app/venues/page.tsx` against them
- [x] Four defects confirmed with exact replacement text, and two more raised
- [x] A recommendation on whether E12.01 is a second page or the page that exists

## Scope

Criteria, audit, gap, recommendation.

## Non-goals

- **Rewriting `src/app/venues/page.tsx`.** E12.04 is Done and founder-approved over
  that file, the page is live in production, and three separate mechanisms make an
  unrequested edit expensive: I-014 (the surface is registered and design-quality is
  unsatisfiable once its hash moves), the WP-06 copy sweep (the file is pinned at
  eight expected hits in `copy/prohibited-claims.v1.json`, and any edit moves that
  count into a merge conflict), and R-025 (a change-controlled price on a live
  public surface is not Claude's to publish).
- **A second public Venue Edition landing page.** Recommended against below. Two
  public pages carrying one price is the I-002 failure mode with a new URL.

## Implementation approach

Audit first (D-015 Q2), then build only the gap. The audit found that the gap is not
a page. The hero already leads with the sponsored couple, there is a dedicated
"What your couples get" section, and the co-branded workspace mock puts a couple's
workspace above the fold. What is missing is six specific things, four of which are
already recorded as E12.04's defects and two of which this audit adds.

So the honest deliverable is the audit and the replacement words, not a rewrite.
Building a second public page to satisfy a task title would put a second price on
the public internet, which is the exact defect I-002 exists to close.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The page's organising idea is what the couple receives, not what the venue
   administers: the first thing a reader meets is the couple's experience.
2. The offer appears in one of the three approved lengths, not a fourth written for
   this page.
3. The couple access term is stated as eighteen months or three months past the
   wedding, whichever is later, everywhere it appears, including the metadata.
4. Both prices appear, both are stated as including VAT at the prevailing rate,
   including in the meta description, and the founding rate is "€500 a year less".
5. The founding lock is described as holding on continuous renewal, with no
   permanence wording anywhere.
6. The launch-scope sentence appears verbatim.
7. Exactly three venue-visible things are named, and no fourth, and no consent
   mechanism that is unwired at launch is described.
8. The survival sentence appears unprompted wherever the end of the agreement is
   described.
9. Venue branding is described as the venue's name only, including in the
   OpenGraph description, and no logo or venue-written content is implied anywhere.
10. No banned programme term, no manufactured urgency, and nothing that states or
    implies legal, solicitor or accountant approval.

## Validation plan

Read the file against each criterion and record the line. Where a criterion fails,
write the replacement sentence from an approved source rather than inventing one.

  required on any surface that tells a venue what it sees. The page tells a venue
  what it sees and does not carry it.
