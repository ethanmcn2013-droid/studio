# E12.07 — Add the privacy explanation covering exactly what the venue sees and never sees.

## Objective

Publish one page that states the whole venue-visible list and the whole
never-visible list, in the same place, at the same weight, with every claim
traced to a shipped read path or a ratified decision.

## Business reason

Rung 4 of the venue-owner ladder (E09.10 §3.1) is where trust is won or lost,
and it comes before price. A venue owner asks "can you see the couple's plan"
in the first ten minutes of every conversation, and today the only written
answer in the repository was the superseded `VENUE_FAQ_OBJECTIONS.md:31`, which
names a fourth visible thing. Twenty-five venues will each ask this question
once. The answer needs to be the same every time and it needs to survive being
read by a venue's own adviser.

## Intended outcome

There is a public page a venue can be sent that answers the privacy question
completely, that names no capability the product does not have, and that
describes no published surface as private when it is not.

- [x] `src/app/venues/privacy/page.tsx` — the page, route `/venues/privacy`
- [x] Route added to `src/app/sitemap.ts`

      `currentCommercialSources`, so it is swept from the day it exists
- [x] `evidence/E12.07-venue-privacy-page.md` — claim-by-claim trace

## Scope

The venue-facing privacy boundary: the three permitted things and the absence of
a fourth, the definition of "opened", the full negative list, the suppression
rule, what happens when a couple shares or publishes, what the venue's name does
and does not do, and the four things Signal Studio will not claim.

## Non-goals

The couple-facing privacy notice (E03.05). The data processing agreement and
security schedule (E03.04). The site-wide privacy policy at `/privacy`. Visual
design sign-off. The analytics consent position, which D-032 R8 settles for
commercial pages but which nobody has yet implemented — recorded as a risk
rather than built here, because the wiring point is a single line in
`src/app/layout.tsx` that a concurrent package is also editing.

## Implementation approach

Audit-first (D-015 Q2). Nothing venue-facing existed on this subject except one
superseded document and one line on `/venues` that E09.11 §13 records as a
defect, so the gap was the whole page. Approved copy is reproduced verbatim
rather than paraphrased; where no approved sentence exists, the sentence is
written from a named decision or a named code path and the source is recorded in
the evidence document line by line.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The reusable venue-facing privacy block (E09.11 §5.1) appears in all three parts — what you see, what you never see, at launch — at the same visual weight, and the D-032 R4 launch-scope sentence appears verbatim.
2. Exactly three venue-visible things are named, and no fourth appears anywhere on the page, including any description of a couple consenting to share a specific field with the venue.
3. Every positive claim about what the venue is shown corresponds to a field the shipped read path actually produces, and anything not instrumented is stated as not existing yet rather than described as working.
4. The page states plainly that a couple's Notes, Tasks, unpublished Timeline and any private planning content are never visible to the venue, and that a wedding-date change is never surfaced anywhere.
5. The suppression floors are stated as the ratified rule — three for behavioural counts, five for percentages — attached to figures that do not exist yet, with no claim that the shipped implementation has been verified, and access counts are stated as exact at any size per D-032 R12.
6. No published or shared surface is described as private, secure, unlisted, token-bound, or visible only to the people the couple sends it to. The permitted findability sentence appears instead.
7. Nothing states or implies legal approval, solicitor review, accountant verification or unqualified GDPR compliance, and no document is described as existing when it does not.
8. No banned programme term appears (partner, member, investor, exclusive, guaranteed, certified), no permanence wording, no manufactured urgency, no em dash and no exclamation mark anywhere in the file.
9. No dietary, allergy, accessibility or health information about a named person appears, and nothing states that such information reaches the venue.
10. The route is present in `sitemap.ts` and in the commercial contract sweep's source list, and the OpenGraph description describes venue branding as the venue's name only.

## Risks

- **R-031, open.** `/p` is deliberately indexable while the privacy
  documentation models every published surface as token-bound. The page states
  only what is true today and makes no token-bound claim, but the underlying
  product decision is unmade.
- **R-032 / D-032 R8.** This page is a Venue Edition commercial page and D-032 R8
  removes GA4 from those. The carve-out is not implemented, so a page about
  privacy currently loads a third-party analytics script with no consent gate.
- **R-027, open.** The shipped behavioural floor is one-sided. The page states
  the rule rather than the implementation, and the figures it governs do not
  exist yet, so nothing untrue is published — but the gap is real.
- **I-014.** This route adds one unregistered discovered experience to
  `experience:validate --product=studio`.
- **Overlap with E12.06.** A concurrent package built `/venues/what-you-see` on
  the same subject. Two public pages describing the same boundary in different
  words is the two-sources failure E09.10 §0 exists to prevent.
