## Objective

Close the programme properly: what was learned, what is archived, what changes
in the business plan, and who owns each thing that keeps running.

## Business reason

The programme ends at 25 signed and paid venues. Everything it built keeps
running afterwards: 25 annual agreements, their renewals, the couples in their
terms, and the Keepsake tail that has no end date. If nothing transfers those
into operations, they transfer themselves into the founder's inbox.

## Intended outcome

A postmortem exists that is honest about what went wrong, the assets are
archived where they can be found, the business plan reflects reality, and every
ongoing obligation has an owner and a cadence.

## Deliverables

- [ ] The postmortem.
- [ ] The asset archive with an index.
- [ ] The updated business plan and market-entry documents.
- [ ] The operations transfer: every ongoing obligation, its owner, its cadence.

## Scope

The whole programme: decisions, risks, evidence, films, commercial assets, the
venue universe, and everything that continues after M6.

## Non-goals

- Rewriting history. Superseded decisions stay superseded and visible.
- Deleting anything. Archiving is moving and indexing, not removing.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The postmortem names what went wrong with dates and consequences, not only
   what went well. At minimum it addresses: the two concurrent implementations of
   the same ratified rule, the gap between integration branches and production at
   the go/no-go, the Keepsake promise shipping ahead of the Keepsake product, and
   the film schedule.
2. Every superseded position is traceable: what it was, what replaced it, and
   when. The superseded ledger is complete.
3. The business plan and market-entry documents state the actual outcome: the
   number of venues signed, the actual revenue, the actual timeline, and the
   conversion rate against the eligible pool.
4. Every ongoing obligation has a named owner and a cadence. At minimum:
   annual renewals, the founding rate lock, the annual founder call per venue,
   the founding-request intake, the couple access terms running to 2028 and
   beyond, and the Keepsake tail which has no end date.
5. The asset archive has an index that a person can use a year later without
   asking anyone.
6. The record shows what was judged at approval, not only that approval
   happened, for every decision taken after D-026 flagged the gap.
7. Nothing in any updated document states or implies legal approval or GDPR
   compliance.

## Risks

- **The Keepsake tail has no end date and no owner.** Free, read-only,
  indefinite access while the service exists is an obligation that outlives the
  programme by years and appears in no operating budget.
- A postmortem written by the people who did the work tends to be kind. The
  adversarial review pattern used elsewhere in this programme applies here too.

## Decisions required
