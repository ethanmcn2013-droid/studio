# E04.03 — Define invitation states from creation through sent, opened, redeemed, expired, revoked and replaced.

**Task ID:** E04.03
**Epic:** E04 — Venue Edition product architecture and workspace lifecycle
**Status:** Founder Review
**Executor:** claude_code

---

## Objective

Define the seven invitation states as an executable state machine with legal transitions, not prose.

## Business reason

The venue's whole administrative surface is invitation state. A venue that cannot tell 'sent' from 'redeemed' cannot answer a couple who says the link does not work.

## Intended outcome

The seven states and their transitions are in code and under test, and the two states that did not exist have a defined meaning.

## Deliverables

- [ ] studio/src/lib/venue-lifecycle.ts INVITATION_STATES and nextInvitationState
- [ ] studio/src/lib/venue-lifecycle.test.ts transition coverage
- [ ] studio/docs/architecture/ADR-008-venue-edition-lifecycle.md section 3, including the 'opened' definition

## Scope

The state vocabulary, the transition table, terminality, and the definition of 'opened' and 'replaced', neither of which exists in the shipped schema.

## Non-goals

Persisting the new states. Adding delivered_at or replaced_by columns. Building the venue UI. Those follow once the definition is approved.

## Implementation approach

Two disjoint machines exist today and neither matches the seven states in the title. Define one machine that does. Resolve 'opened' against D-013's ban on open pixels by defining it as a first-party load of the redemption page, which is a different fact from an email open and is honestly obtainable.

## Acceptance criteria

1. All seven states are defined with their legal transitions.
2. A redeemed code cannot be revoked, expired or replaced.
3. Re-sending and re-opening are legal and non-advancing, because both are ordinary.
4. 'opened' is defined as a first-party page load and explicitly not an email open, consistent with D-013 point 3.
5. Every transition and every refusal is covered by a test.
