## Objective

Produce Schedule 1 to the annual Venue Edition agreement: the document that turns a
founding place into contractual terms.

## Business reason

Twenty-five venues are being asked to prepay a year at a rate held indefinitely on
continuous renewal, and to accept a numbered place assigned on cleared payment rather than
on signature. Every one of those rules is decided and written down in strategy documents
that describe themselves as unapproved. Until they are in a schedule attached to the
agreement, the founding offer is a promise with no instrument behind it.

## Intended outcome

A founding venue can read one schedule and know exactly what its rate is, what holds it,
what ends it, what its number is, when it is assigned, what a founding place includes and
where each of those benefits stops. Nothing in it is new, and nothing in it depends on a
document that has not been approved.

## Deliverables

- [x] `evidence/E03.03-founding-venue-schedule.md` — Schedule 1 in twelve paragraphs, the

      points.
- [x] `evidence/E03.02-E03.03-copy-sweep.txt` — shared with E03.02.

## Scope

- The €1,000 rate, its VAT basis and its public description.
- The continuity conditions: what the lock covers, what holds it, what ends it, what it
  does not cover.
- Founding-place status: numbering, assignment on cleared payment, ordering, non-reuse,
  payment reversal, the 14-day hold and its extension, and programme closure.
- The six founding benefits with their boundaries in the same paragraph.
- Recognition as a separate revocable consent.
- What a founding place is not.

## Non-goals

- **No second agreement.** This is a schedule, and it says so. Where both documents must
  carry a term, this one cross-references the agreement clause by number.
- **No restatement** of the couple term, the entitlement rule, the survival sentence, the
  branding rule or the marketing controls. All five live in the parent agreement.
- **No renewal notice period or grace window.** Both are marked blanks in the parent
  agreement because the code refuses their publication.
- **No incorporation of the Benefits Charter by reference.** See the implementation
  approach.
- **No correction of `FOUNDING_25_PROGRAMME_MECHANICS.md`**, which contradicts itself.
  Reported, not edited.
- No percentage and no fraction anywhere.

## Implementation approach

1. Write acceptance criteria, then convert already-ratified rules into contractual
   language without inventing new words for them.
2. **State the six benefits operatively rather than incorporating the Benefits Charter by
   reference.** The charter still reads "Status: draft for founder approval" in its own
   header, and incorporating it would make an unapproved document contractual and put the
   same six rules in two places. The schedule carries the benefits with their boundaries,
   and the charter is named at paragraph 12 as the plain-English version that is not part
   of the agreement. This is a deliberate departure and it is founder question 1.
3. Where three sources disagree and none is ratified, record the disagreement and mark a
   decision point. Do not pick.
4. Express every boundary in plain words rather than by using a banned term to deny it,
   so the schedule sweeps clean rather than needing exemptions.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The same DRAFT status block and the E09.10 Answer 2 sentence verbatim head the
   deliverable.
2. It is drafted as a schedule to the E03.02 agreement, not as a second agreement, and it
   restates nothing the agreement already carries. Where both must carry a term, the
   schedule cross-references the agreement clause by number.
3. The rate is €1,000 a year, prepaid, inclusive of VAT at the prevailing rate, and the
   saving is described only as "€500 a year less, for as long as you stay" (E02.02 AC1).
   No percentage and no fraction appears anywhere (E02.02 AC2).
4. Continuity matches E02.03 exactly: the lock covers the base annual agreement only, it
   holds on continuous renewal without a lapse, a lapse ends it, and that is disclosed
   before signature rather than at the lapse (E02.03 AC1 to AC4).
5. Founding-place status matches the Programme Mechanics sections 2 and 3: numbers 01/25 to
   25/25 zero-padded, assigned on cleared payment and never on signature, ordered by the
   moment payment clears with the stated tie-breaks, never reused, and withdrawn if a
   payment reverses.
6. The 14-day hold is stated as one hold from the date of the proposal, extendable only by
   Ethan personally and recorded when extended (E02.11 AC2), with the signature-to-payment
   case covered.
7. Programme closure is stated: the programme closes when the 25th payment clears,
   outstanding holds are void, and those venues are told the same day and offered the
   standard rate (E02.11 AC4).
8. The six benefits appear with their boundaries intact, including the two that bite: first
   look is not a veto, and nothing is built for one venue (D-014 point 2, E02.09 AC4).
9. No protected area is granted, stated positively and before signature (E02.10 AC2).
10. Recognition is a separate, revocable, written consent to use the venue's name and logo
    in the founding programme, and nothing about the rate or the place depends on it
    (D-010 point 1). It is distinguished from D-027 point 3, which keeps a logo out of the
    couple's workspace.
11. The banned-term list is respected in full, meaning zero hits against prohibited-claims
    rules `d033-programme-terms`, `p1-permanence` and `p13-urgency` in the schedule text.
12. Anything the schedule cannot settle because E02.04 is backlog is carried as a visibly
    marked decision point rather than chosen quietly.
