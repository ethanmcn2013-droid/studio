## Objective

Let a couple take a date off their published page and have it genuinely leave
the payload, and state plainly which of the other "sensitive details" the
product can and cannot conceal.

## Business reason

The wedding day is the single value a couple most often wants withheld from a
page other people can see, and until this task it was fixed at publish: the only
way to remove it was to unpublish the whole artifact. A venue asking "can they
keep the date private" needed the answer to be yes.

## Intended outcome

Concealment is a control the couple can use on a live page, and concealing
removes the value from the serialised DTO rather than hiding it in the render.
The limits of "and other sensitive information" are written down instead of
implied by an absent control.

## Deliverables

- [x] `setAudiencePrimaryDate` — set or clear the main date on an existing
      publication, owner-scoped, both fields moving together.
- [x] `updateAudiencePrimaryDateAction` and an owner control in the artifact
      manager, with the copy that says what hiding does.
- [x] Tests proving the key disappears from the payload, including a
      fail-before mutation.
- [x] A written statement of what cannot be concealed and why.

## Scope

Concealment of the publication's main date. Audit and evidence for per-milestone
date concealment, which already shipped. The written refusal on structured
location.

## Non-goals

- **A structured location field. Deliberately not built.** There is no location
  column on `workspaces`, on `tasks` or on `timeline_publication_items`.
  Locations live in free text the couple wrote, so a "hide the location" toggle
  would be a control that cannot do what its label says.
- EXIF GPS stripping. It belongs to the photograph byte path, E06.03, and no
  photograph reaches a public surface today.
- The `/p` OpenGraph card. `/p` is a Tasks published workspace; its metadata is
  E06.05 and E06.12 territory.

## Implementation approach

Audit first. Per-milestone date concealment already works end to end and was
recorded rather than rebuilt: `updateAudiencePublicationItem` accepts a null
`calendarDate` on a live publication, and the DTO builder omits the key.

The genuine gap was the main date. `primaryDate` and `primaryDateLabel` were
written at creation and never updated again. A server function, an action and an
owner control were added. The two columns move together because the DTO only
emits `primaryDate` when both are present, so a half-set pair would look like a
change and do nothing.

The location half was assessed and refused. Writing the refusal down is the
deliverable.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Concealing a **milestone** date is proven on the serialised public DTO: the
   item carries no `date` key at all, rather than a blanked or placeholder
   string.
2. The **wedding day itself** has its own concealment control that works on an
   already published artifact, and concealing removes `primaryDate` from the
   payload entirely.
3. A **half-set pair** — a label with no date, or a date with no label — never
   publishes a partial main date.
4. Concealment is **owner-scoped**: the publication ownership is re-checked
   server-side and another workspace cannot clear someone else's date.
5. **Location is stated plainly.** There is no structured location field
   anywhere in the published model, so either a structured field is added or the
   refusal is written down. The criteria must not imply a control that does not
   exist.
6. A concealed value never appears in the **OpenGraph card or the page title**
   of the surface it was concealed on, asserted rather than assumed.
7. **EXIF GPS is cross-referenced to E06.03**, and the current position stated:
   no photograph reaches any public surface today, so there is no metadata path
   to leak through and no claim is made that one is handled.

## Risks

- The refusal on structured location is a product limit a venue may ask about.
  It is better stated than papered over, and E12.07 must carry it in the
  couple's own words.
- The main-date control lives on `/s`. If the founder decides the couple's
  keepsake is `/p`, an equivalent control has to exist there. Recorded, not
  assumed.

## Decisions required

1. None blocking. The location refusal is a recommendation; if the founder wants
   a structured location field it is a schema change on the Timeline database
   and inherits E06.02's migration blocker.
