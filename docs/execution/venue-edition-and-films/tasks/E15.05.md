## Objective

Verify the couple's public artifact end to end: sharing, the viewer count,
unpublishing, the export the couple owns, and the Keepsake state the programme
has promised in writing.

## Business reason

The shared Timeline is the part of the product a couple's guests and suppliers
see, and the part that carries other people's names. Keepsake is the promise
that makes the whole offer honest: the couple keeps what they made. A promise in
a signed charter that the product cannot keep is worse than no promise.

## Intended outcome

Each of the five behaviours has a recorded result: it works, it does not exist,
or it works with a stated limit. Nothing is left as an assumption.

## Deliverables

- [ ] A published shared Timeline, checked for indexing behaviour and for what
      it discloses.
- [ ] The viewer count, checked against its qualification rules.
- [ ] An unpublish, checked for immediate effect on every route that renders the
      workspace.
- [ ] An export produced and opened.
- [ ] A written, honest statement of the Keepsake position.

## Scope

`/p/[slug]`, `/s/[token]`, `/share/[token]`, `/embed/[slug]`, the audience
manager and qualified-view store, `unpublishWorkspaceAction`, the account export
path, and `src/lib/keepsake/*`.

## Non-goals

  a minimum Keepsake surface ships before 1 September, that is a separate task
  and a change request.
- Face detection, auto-tagging or face grouping. A standing no.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A published **wedding** workspace is served `noindex, nofollow` and `/p` is
   disallowed in the deployed `robots.txt`. Verified against production, not
   against the branch.
2. A token-bound share (`/s`, `/share`) is unreachable once revoked, on the next
   request, with no cached render surviving.
3. Unpublishing removes the workspace from every route that can render it,
   including `/p` and `/embed`, and the removal is visible without waiting for a
   revalidation window.
4. The viewer count shown to a couple counts qualified views only and cannot be
   inflated by the couple's own loads or by a crawler.
5. An export is produced, downloads, opens, and contains the couple's content in
   a form that is readable without Signal Studio. The couple owns it (D-010
   point 3).
6. The Keepsake position is stated honestly in one paragraph that matches what
   the product does today. **`src/lib/keepsake/*` has no production callers**, so
   the honest statement is that the library exists and the couple-facing state
   does not.
7. No Keepsake copy anywhere uses "forever", "for life", "in perpetuity" or any
   storage guarantee. The permitted formulation is free, read-only, indefinite
   access while the service exists, with an export the couple owns.
8. `/embed/[slug]` behaviour is decided and recorded. Today it renders a
   published workspace with no token on any third-party site (R-033).
9. Third-party analytics on couple-facing public surfaces is either absent or
   consent-gated, and whatever is true is what the privacy page says (R-032).
