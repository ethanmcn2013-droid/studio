## Objective

Produce the deck a venue is shown in a room, as a rendered artefact built from
the ratified copy module, carrying the whole commercial position with every
claim traced to the decision that ratified it.

## Business reason

The seated conversation is where a founding venue decides. Until now the only
venue deck in the repository is `public/brand/collateral/venue/venue-deck-screen.pdf`,
made on 1 July 2026, and it is wrong on three counts that all reach a venue:
it shows EUR 1,500 as the price a founding venue pays, it uses the programme
term D-033 retired, and it closes on a pilot ask that the ratified offer does
not contain. It is a PDF, so nothing could keep it current.

Twenty-five venues, one price, one lock. The surface that carries them has to
be a render of the source, not a copy of it.

## Intended outcome

The deck exists as data with no second copy of any ratified sentence, rendered
by a gated founder route that fills the one slot, and every price statement on
it carries its conditions. The unbuilt half of the product is stated on a slide
rather than left for a venue to discover.

## Scope

**What artefact this is, decided and stated.** A gated HTML route that prints
to A4 landscape, one slide per page. Not a public route, because the cover
names a venue and the deck carries the price. Not a PDF, because a PDF is how
the July deck became wrong and stayed wrong.

**The ladder it runs.** E09.10 section 3.1, trust before price. Slides 02 to 11
are the product and the boundary. Price is slide 12, after a venue knows what
it never sees and how long a couple has the thing. The test enforces the
ordering rather than trusting the author.

**One ask, and it is the walkthrough.** "See it, on your own screen." The deck
frames the demonstration; the proposal (E12.09) follows it. A deck that asks
for a signature is asking before the venue has seen the product.

**One slot, not six.** The venue name. The paid count and the next free number
are spoken from the register at the moment of the meeting and never printed,
because a printed count is a scarcity claim that goes stale between the render
and the room.

## Non-goals

- **The visual language.** Structure, hierarchy and the source trace are fixed
  here because they are correctness questions. Colour, type scale, the cover
  treatment and whether slides carry an image band are founder-taste calls and
  are options in the recommendation packet, not decisions taken here.
- **Replacing or deleting the July PDF.** It is another lane's artefact under
  `public/brand/collateral/venue/` and it is referenced by `/hq/venue-kit`.
  Recorded as defect C-3 with a recommendation.
- **Product screenshots.** E05.12 has not locked the interface. A deck showing
  a screen that then changes is worse than a deck showing none.

## Implementation approach

The E12.09 pattern, exactly: data module, executable refusal set, server
component, print stylesheet, gated route. The one addition is
`src/lib/venue-copy-refusals.ts`, because four artefacts each carrying their own
copy of a forty-nine-expression ban list is how a retired term survives on one
surface after being removed from another.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The deck exists as a rendered artefact at a gated route, not as a document
   describing one, and it prints one slide per A4 landscape page.
2. No ratified sentence is restated. Every price, term and promise is imported
   from `src/lib/venue-invitation/copy.ts`.
3. Every price statement on the deck carries "including VAT at the prevailing
   rate", and a test proves it over the rendered prose rather than the source.
4. Every slide traces to at least one ratified decision, and the trace is
   visible on the slide rather than only in a comment.
5. Price appears after the privacy boundary, after the launch-scope sentence
   and after the couple's access term, and a test enforces the ordering.
6. The launch-scope sentence appears verbatim, as its own slide.
7. The survival sentence appears unprompted.
8. The deck carries a slide stating what is not built, naming the Keepsake,
   adoption figures and the absence of a venue login.
9. No prohibited claim, banned programme term, em dash or exclamation mark
   appears, in the filled deck or the unfilled one.
10. The deck ends on exactly one ask and it is the walkthrough.
11. No numeric entitlement, no seat language, no places-left figure.
12. An unfilled deck says so rather than looking prepared.
13. The route is registered in the HQ room registry and the experience
    registry, and adds no new `experience:validate` failure.
14. The test is registered in `package.json` so CI runs it.
