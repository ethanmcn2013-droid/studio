## Objective

Build the invitation state of `/v/<token>`: the private page one of the twenty-five
invited venues reaches from the personalised film link, carrying the whole offer in
text and asking for one reply.

## Business reason

It is the first surface a Founding 25 venue sees that is theirs rather than the
public internet's. `E13.16-link-and-destination-contract.md` makes it the
destination of every cold touch in the outreach sequence, so nothing in the
programme reaches a venue until it exists. It is also where the price first appears
with its conditions attached, which is the only form in which the price may travel.

## Intended outcome

A venue holding a link opens one page, reads the complete offer whether or not the
film loads, sees exactly what its venue is called and nothing else about itself, and
has one thing to do. Nobody outside the venue can find the page, no third party
measures the visit, and the one thing recorded is that the page was opened.

## Implementation approach

One route, two states, one URL, because E13.16 section 3 settles it: "invitation
first, proposal after a conversation. The venue never gets a second link."

The venue's name is rendered on the page and appears nowhere else. Not in the URL
(D-032 R13), not in the metadata, not in a link preview. Section 1.1's reasoning is
that a name already sent cannot be recalled, and an OpenGraph description is fetched
and cached by every unfurling service a private link is pasted into.

Copy is imported from one module that quotes the approved sources. A claim written
in new words at a render site is the drift that put the retired commercial position
into roughly 140 files (I-002).

## Acceptance criteria

1. The route resolves a 24-character token server-side with no venue slug and no
   query parameter, and an unknown, malformed or revoked token produces the same
   404 while recording nothing.
2. The page is `noindex, nofollow`, `/v/` is disallowed in `robots.ts`, and no
   private path can reach the sitemap, each proven by a test rather than by reading.
3. No third-party script and no consent-requiring cookie loads on the route, proven
   by a request log on a real page load rather than by a code read.
4. The venue's name appears on the page and in no URL, no metadata field and no
   link preview; no logo, colour or other venue asset is rendered anywhere.
5. The offer is complete in text without the film: entitlement, couple term,
   privacy, what the team does, price with every condition, Keepsake, the survival
   sentence, and what being one of the 25 means.
6. The launch-scope sentence appears verbatim: "At launch you see your invitations
   and who opened them. Adoption figures follow later in the year."
7. Both prices are stated as including VAT at the prevailing rate, the founding
   lock is described as holding on continuous renewal, and no permanence wording
   appears anywhere on the route.
8. There is exactly one action on the page.
9. The click disclosure line appears verbatim, with a route to object.
10. The recorded event carries exactly the seven fields of E13.16 section 4.2, and
    an eighth is rejected rather than trimmed.
11. No banned programme term appears: partner, member, investor, exclusive,
    guaranteed, certified, or any urgency that can go stale.
12. Nothing on the route states or implies legal, solicitor or accountant approval,
    and no capability that is not built is described as if it were.

## Risks

- **I-014.** The route is a new registered-experience surface. The registry entry
  and the review are handed to the founder; design-quality is not satisfied here.
- **R-032.** The carve-out removes GA4 from `/v/*` and `/venues` under D-032 R8. It
  does not decide the position for the rest of the site and must not be read as
  doing so.
- **The event has no durable store.** `firstView` and `repeatViewCount` are computed
  from a per-process counter that does not survive a cold start.

## Decisions required

1. Whether the venue's name may appear in the page's metadata. Built as **no**, on
   E13.16 section 1.1's reasoning. Ratifying or reversing it is the founder's.
2. Where the runtime link document lives. Built as one environment variable, so no
   venue name and no token is committed. A table is the alternative.
