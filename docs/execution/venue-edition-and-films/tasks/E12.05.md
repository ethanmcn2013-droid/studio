## Objective

A venue-facing page that shows a venue exactly where its name appears on the
couple's side, in the words the couple actually reads, and states plainly what
its name never does.

## Business reason

A venue about to pay €1,000 is putting its name on a stranger's wedding
planning. "Your name on it, quietly" is the line on `/venues`, and it is the
part of the offer a venue owner will most want to see before signing, because
it is the part that touches their own reputation. Today there is nothing to
show them: `/venues/demo` walks through the four products with fictional
content and never renders a single sponsored moment. A venue asking "so what
does my name actually look like?" gets a verbal answer.

## Intended outcome

A venue can read, in one page, the four places its name appears, in order, with
the exact line at each one. It can also read the four things its name does not
do, each of which is a decision rather than a gap. Nothing on the page depicts
a screen that does not exist.

## Implementation approach

Audit first (D-015 Q2). Establish, by reading source rather than documentation,
every place a sponsored couple meets the venue name. Then render those places
as words. Where an existing product string is defective against a ratified
decision, describe the moment rather than reproduce the defective string, and
return the defect to its owning task. A preview that laundered a wrong string
into a commercial page would be worse than no preview.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Every venue-name moment the page shows is present in running source, and the
   evidence document names the file and line for each one.
2. The page depicts no screen, bar, banner or field that does not exist. In
   particular it does not depict a persistent venue identity bar in the couple's
   workspace, because there is none.
3. Venue branding is the venue name only. No logo, no colour, no theme and no
   venue-written message appears or is promised, and the page says so
   explicitly.
4. The couple-facing privacy line and the couple-facing and venue-facing
   collaboration lines appear verbatim from E09.11 §5.3 and §6.3.
5. The launch-scope sentence from E09.11 §5.1a appears verbatim, because the
   page tells a venue what it sees.
6. The couple access term is stated as eighteen months from opening or three
   months past the wedding, whichever is later, and never as a bare figure.
7. Every capability not yet built is stated as not yet built, naming Keepsake,
   published-page attribution, and the absence of product imagery.
8. Zero hits across every rule in `prohibited-claims.v1.json`, zero banned
   programme terms, zero banned CTAs, zero em dashes and zero exclamation marks
   in rendered text.
9. Nothing on the page states or implies legal, solicitor or accountant
   approval, and nothing describes a couple consenting to share a field with the
   venue.
10. The route is registered in `experience/registry.json` and added to the
    commercial sweep list in the same change that created it.

## Risks

- **R-024 / D-027 point 3.** The single largest risk on this page is implying
  more branding than the venue gets. Mitigated by stating the limit four times
  in its own section rather than leaving it to be inferred.
- **I-014.** A new route is a newly discovered experience. Registered by hand
  with a matching hash rather than by `experience:discover --write`, which
  would have destroyed the 2026-08-03 re-baseline.
- **R-032 / D-032 R8.** GA4 currently renders from the root layout on every
  route. This route must be inside the Venue Edition exclusion before it is
  published. That predicate is E12.14's and is in flight in the same worktree.
- **E05.12.** Real imagery is still owed after the UI freeze. The page is built
  so that adding it later is additive rather than a rewrite.

## Decisions required

   carrying the ratified term and the privacy boundary.
2. **R-042** — retire "founding partner" in favour of "Founding 25" everywhere,
   or define it as a distinct thing. Not touched by this task.
3. **The example venue name.** This page uses Glenmara House, which is the copy
   system's standing example. `/venues` uses Glenmara Estate for the same job.
