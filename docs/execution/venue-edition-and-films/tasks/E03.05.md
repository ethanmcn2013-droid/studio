## Objective

Draft the two documents a sponsored couple actually agrees to, consistent with the ratified
role map, and reconcile them against the four live legal pages that already govern them.

## Business reason

A founding venue is promised the couple privacy notice before it signs
(`studio/src/app/venues/questions/page.tsx`, E09.10 Answer 1). The venue is handing its own
couples to this product. If the notice a couple reaches is the one live today, it names the
wrong minimum age, carries a United States governing-law placeholder, and tells the couple a
six-name subprocessor list is "the entire list" when four more are running.

## Intended outcome

Two drafts exist that a founder can read end to end, that state only what is true of the
shipped product, that name every place they disagree with a live page, and that leave every
unresolved question visibly unresolved instead of quietly answered.

- [x] `evidence/E03.05-couple-terms-and-privacy-notice.md` — the two drafts, the

## Implementation approach

Write the criteria first. Read the role map in full. Verify every business fact in code
before asserting it. Where the role map records a question as UNRESOLVED, draft to the
reading that is safer for the couple, say which reading was taken and why, and carry it as a
numbered decision point. Where a fact could not be verified, leave a marked blank rather
than invent one.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A DRAFT status block heads the document, naming what it is (AI-drafted under D-016), what
   it is not (not legal advice, not a reviewed instrument, not a compliance statement), the
   date, and the E09.10 Answer 2 sentence verbatim: "No solicitor has reviewed these
   documents, and no accountant has verified the financial or VAT position."
2. No price, rate, percentage or euro figure appears anywhere in the couple-facing copy
   (D-001 point 1).
3. The couple access term is stated as eighteen months from opening or three months past the
   wedding, whichever is later, and never as a flat eighteen months. A prohibited-claims
   sweep over the drafted copy returns no `p7-bare-term` hit.
4. The three live-page defects are named as defects the drafts do not inherit: the
   governing-law placeholder in the app terms, the postal-address placeholder in the app
   privacy page, and "thirteen and older" at `app/src/app/privacy/page.tsx:165` against 16
   under s.31 DPA 2018.
5. An Article 14 section exists for people named by someone else, giving categories, the
   source, recipients, the objection route as it exists today, and stating plainly that no
   removal control exists on any published surface.
6. An Article 13 disclosure exists telling couples that activation data about them goes to
   the venue, naming the fields from the sponsor allowlist rather than making a general
   statement.
7. The subprocessor list matches what the code and environment prove, including Turso,
   Vercel Blob, Upstash and Anthropic, and no region or transfer claim is made for any
   vendor not verified in this programme.
8. Rights are described only as far as they are built. Export and deletion are named by
   route; no capability that does not exist is described as existing.
9. Retention is not given a sixth version: the draft states what the code enforces, records
   the five-way conflict, and carries the single answer as an open founder decision.
10. The R-017 position is stated at the mechanism, claims no Article 9(2) condition, and
    designs no structured field. Nothing anywhere states or implies GDPR compliance,
    certification, audit, solicitor review or DPC approval.

## Risks

- **R-013** stands and is amplified: this is a consumer contract drafted with no solicitor,
  and the liability cap is the clause most likely to be void.
- **R-017** is stated rather than solved. The notice takes a harm-reduction position with no
  Article 9(2) condition behind it.
- **New, and the largest thing this task found:** Anthropic receives workspace text through
  the writing features and appears on no privacy page anywhere. That is a gap in a live
  notice, not a drafting question.
- The wedding-date limb of the access term cannot fire in production, so a document is being
  drafted around a promise the code does not keep.

## Decisions required

Twelve, listed as DP-3 through DP-13 in §5 of the evidence document. The four that most
change the product rather than the wording:

1. **DP-6.** Disclose Anthropic in the notice and fix both live privacy pages, or disable
   the writing features for sponsored wedding workspaces.
2. **DP-12.** Which of the five retention statements survives. Nothing enforces any of them.
3. **DP-8.** Whether the data-protection footer ships before the Founding 25. The role map
   calls it the cheapest and highest-value item in it.
4. **DP-3.** Whether the wedding-date capture gap closes before the first redemption, or
   whether extending the term stays a manual undertaking.
