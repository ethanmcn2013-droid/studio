## Objective

Establish whether Signal's selection is genuinely selective for a wedding
workspace, and whether a sponsored couple's workspace contains anything for it
to select from.

## Business reason

"Only what needs your attention today" is the sentence the whole of Signal
rests on, and it is the sentence a venue will repeat to a couple. A briefing
that lists everything is a task list with a nicer heading. Worse, a briefing
that surfaces the wrong thing on day one is the couple's first impression of a
gift the venue paid for.

## Intended outcome

The claim is separated into its two halves and each is answered with evidence:
the engine filters (it does, and that is now pinned), and the couple's workspace
carries the dates the filter runs on (it does not, and the exact reason is now
recorded with a run rather than an opinion).

## Implementation approach

Audit first. No engine code changed. The engine is well built and the honest
finding is upstream of it, so the work was to prove the selectivity that exists
and measure the consequence of the missing input rather than to add triggers
that would have nothing to fire on.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A quiet wedding workspace produces an empty briefing, not a list, and the
   empty state states its denominator.
2. Given thirty items of which three carry real deadlines, exactly those three
   surface.
3. The three-per-block cap holds under load, and work held back by the cap is
   counted as held back rather than as clear.
4. One task that crosses several rules is reported once, not once per rule.
5. A dismissal removes an item from the read without shrinking the denominator.
6. The scope the briefing reads is the couple's workspace membership, so an
   invited spouse's briefing covers the shared wedding and nothing else of the
   couple's.
7. What a sponsored couple's briefing contains on the day they redeem is
   measured, not asserted.
8. Whatever the wedding template contains, no seeded task can be surfaced by
   date while the workspace has no wedding date.
